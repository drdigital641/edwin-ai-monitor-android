What Is the Difference Between a Gearbox and a Transmission?

Gearbox and transmission are often used for the same thing. What each word means, how an automatic differs, and what to say when you describe a fault to a workshop.

By Jacky Gearbox · 3 November 2026

In short: in everyday use there is no difference, and workshops in Singapore treat "gearbox" and "transmission" as the same part. Strictly, the gearbox is the set of gears and clutches in one housing that changes the ratio between the engine and the wheels, while the transmission can mean that gearbox plus the parts that carry its power on to the wheels. If you use either word when you call a workshop, you will be understood.

Is a gearbox the same as a transmission?

For most drivers, yes. "Gearbox" is the word commonly used in Singapore, the UK and Malaysia. "Transmission" is the word used in the United States and on many scanner tools, service manuals and part labels. The warning message in your car, a repair manual and a workshop quote may each use a different word for the same unit. Use whichever you like. What matters more is the type of gearbox your car has and what it is doing wrong.

What does the word "gearbox" strictly mean?

Strictly, the gearbox is the unit that holds the gears. It lets the engine run at a sensible speed while the car moves slowly or quickly, and it provides Reverse and Neutral. In an automatic, the same housing also holds the clutches or bands that change gear, the hydraulic parts that control them, and the gearbox oil (ATF) that cools and lubricates it all.

What does "transmission" cover?

"Transmission" is the wider idea of transmitting engine power to the wheels. Depending on the car, that can include the gearbox, the torque converter or clutch that joins it to the engine, the drive shafts and the differential. This is why people sometimes say "transmission" when they mean the whole drive system, and sometimes mean only the gearbox. A workshop will usually ask a follow-up question to find out which one you mean.

Does it change for an automatic, a CVT or a dual-clutch car?

The words stay the same, but the parts inside differ. A conventional automatic uses a torque converter and sets of gears, a CVT uses belts or chains and pulleys instead of fixed gears, and a dual-clutch uses two clutches. All of them are normally called a gearbox or a transmission. For how they differ and which one your car has, see CVT, dual-clutch or automatic.

What should you say when you describe a fault?

The words you pick matter less than the details. It helps to say:

  • the car make, model and year, and whether it is an automatic, CVT or dual-clutch;
  • what you notice: slipping, jerking, a delay, a noise or a warning light;
  • when it happens, such as cold start, uphill, or only in Reverse;
  • any transmission warning light or fault code you have seen.

Where does gearbox oil come into it?

Whichever word you use, an automatic gearbox depends on its oil. Old, low or wrong oil is a common cause of trouble. See how often to change gearbox oil and how to check automatic gearbox fluid.

Questions

Is a gearbox the same as a transmission?
In everyday use, yes. Gearbox is the common word in Singapore and the UK, and transmission is the common word in the US and on many scanner tools. Strictly, transmission can also include the parts that carry the power to the wheels.
What is the difference between an automatic gearbox and an automatic transmission?
Usually none. Both words describe the same automatic unit. Some people use transmission for the whole drive system, including the torque converter, drive shafts and differential.
Which word should I use when I call a workshop?
Either one. Say what the car is doing, the make and model, and whether it has an automatic, CVT or dual-clutch gearbox, because those details help a workshop more than the word.
Does a transmission warning light mean the gearbox is faulty?
It means the car has detected a problem in the gearbox or its controls, but the light alone does not say what. A scan and a test are needed to find the cause.
