Trade-in, consignment or direct sale: which way should you sell your car?

Sell directly to a dealer if you want the fastest, simplest sale. Trade in if you are buying your next car from the same dealer and want one transaction. Consign if you can wait for the right buyer and want the dealer to handle viewings and paperwork. The right choice depends on time, effort and price.

Direct sale to dealerTrade-inConsignment
SpeedFastestSame day as your purchaseDepends on the buyer
Effort for youLowLowest: one dealLow: dealer handles viewings
PriceDealer's offerOffer set against your next carOften higher, not fixed
Best forYou need the money or space nowYou are buying from the same dealerYou can wait for the right buyer

Direct sale

The dealer buys your car, settles any loan with the bank and pays you the balance. It is quick and certain, and you know the figure up front.

Trade-in

Your car's value goes towards the car you buy from the same dealer. You avoid a gap without a car and handle one set of paperwork. In August 2026 one of our customers came only to sell his car and drove home in a BMW 520i M Sport three days later.

Consignment

The dealer markets your car and finds the buyer while you keep ownership. It can fetch more but takes longer, and the terms (fees, duration, viewings) should be agreed in writing first.

Frequently asked questions

Which way of selling gets the highest price?

Consignment often gets more because the car is sold to a private buyer, but it is not guaranteed and takes longer.

Can I sell a car with an outstanding loan?

Yes. The loan is settled with the bank as part of the sale and you receive the balance.

What details does a dealer need for an offer?

Model, registration date, mileage, COE expiry, service history and a few photos.
