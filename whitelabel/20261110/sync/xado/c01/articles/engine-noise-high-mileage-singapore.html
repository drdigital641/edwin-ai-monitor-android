Engine getting noisier at high mileage: what it means and what to check

By XADO Singapore · Updated 9 November 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • More engine noise at high mileage usually has a cause you can check first: oil level and grade, a ticking exhaust leak, a loose belt, a worn chain or valve train.
  • A deep knock, metallic grinding or an oil pressure warning means stop driving and get the car inspected.
  • XADO says its Twin Turbo engine treatment is designed to reduce noise and vibration in worn engines. It does not repair broken parts.

An engine that sounds louder than it used to is common in a high-mileage car, and not always serious. The type of sound, and when it happens, tells you a lot. Below are the usual causes, what to check yourself, when to stop, and what XADO says its engine treatment is designed to do.

What can make a high-mileage engine noisier?

  • Low or old oil. Thin, dirty or low oil gives less cushioning between moving parts, and the engine sounds rougher or ticks, especially when cold.
  • Valve train noise. A light tick from the top of the engine can come from worn lifters, rockers or valve clearance.
  • A stretched timing chain or worn tensioner. This often shows as a rattle for a few seconds at cold start.
  • An exhaust leak. A ticking near the manifold that fades as the engine warms up and the metal expands.
  • A worn belt or accessory bearing. Squeals, chirps or a whine from the alternator, water pump or tensioner pulley.
  • Worn internal parts. A deeper knock under load can point to bearing wear and is the one to take seriously.

What to check first

  1. Check the oil level on a level surface with the engine off, using the dipstick. Top up to the mark with the grade in your owner's manual.
  2. Look at the oil. Very dark, gritty or thin oil, or a service that is overdue, is an easy first fix.
  3. Note when it happens. Cold start only, warm only, at idle, or under acceleration each points to different causes.
  4. Note where it comes from. Top of the engine, front belts, underneath or the exhaust side.
  5. Check for warning lights and for oil or coolant leaks under the car.

When should you stop driving?

Stop and get the car inspected, or towed, if you hear a deep knock under load, metallic grinding, or if the oil pressure warning light comes on. Driving on can turn a repairable fault into engine damage.

FAQs

Why is my engine getting noisier as the car gets older?

Common causes are low or old oil, a ticking valve train, a stretched timing chain, an exhaust leak, a worn belt or accessory bearing, and in serious cases worn internal parts. The sound's type and timing help narrow it down.

Is a ticking noise from a high-mileage engine serious?

It depends. A light tick that fades as the engine warms up is often minor, such as an exhaust leak or valve train noise. A deep knock under load, a metallic grinding or an oil pressure warning is serious: stop driving and have the car towed or inspected.

Can an oil change make the engine quieter?

Fresh oil at the right grade and level can reduce noise if old or low oil was the cause. Check the level and condition first, and follow your owner's manual for the grade.
