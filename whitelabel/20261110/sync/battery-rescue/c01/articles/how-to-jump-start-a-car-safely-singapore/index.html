How do you jump-start a car safely?

By Battery Rescue SG (Motoserv) · Last updated 7 Nov 2026 · First published 7 Nov 2026

QUICK ANSWER
To jump-start a car, connect the red clamps to the positive (+) terminals of the dead battery and the donor battery, then the black clamp to the donor battery's negative (-) terminal, and the last black clamp to bare unpainted metal on the dead car, away from the battery. Start the donor car, wait a few minutes, then start the dead car. Remove the clamps in the exact reverse order. Check your owner's manual first, especially for hybrid and start-stop cars.

What you need before you start

  • A set of jumper cables in good condition, or a portable jump starter.
  • A donor car with a healthy battery and the same 12-volt system. Park it close enough for the cables to reach, but not touching your car.
  • Both cars switched off, handbrake on, in park or neutral, with lights, air-conditioning and the radio off.
  • Your owner's manual, in case your car has a marked jump point.

Steps to jump-start a car

  1. Open both bonnets and find both batteries. Look for + (red cover) and - markings.
  2. Clip one red clamp to the positive (+) terminal of the dead battery.
  3. Clip the other red clamp to the positive (+) terminal of the donor battery.
  4. Clip one black clamp to the negative (-) terminal of the donor battery.
  5. Clip the last black clamp to bare, unpainted metal on the dead car, such as an engine bolt or a bracket, away from the battery and moving parts.
  6. Start the donor car and let it run for a few minutes.
  7. Try to start the dead car. If it does not start, wait a little longer and try again. Do not keep cranking for long.
  8. Once it runs, take the clamps off in reverse order: black from the dead car's metal, black from the donor, red from the donor, red from the dead car. Do not let the clamps touch each other.

When you should not jump-start

  • The battery case is cracked, leaking or bulging.
  • You smell rotten eggs near the battery, or see white or green crust that is wet.
  • The car is a hybrid or electric vehicle and the manual does not describe jump-starting. Many have a designated jump point, so follow it.
  • The cables are damaged, or you are not sure which terminal is which. Stop and call for help.

What to do after the car starts

Drive for a while instead of switching off straight away, so the alternator can recharge the battery. If the car does not start again the next day, the battery is probably worn out or something is draining it. See battery or alternator to tell the two apart, and signs your battery is dying for the usual warning signs.

Frequently asked questions

Which cable do I connect last when jump-starting?

The last clamp, the black one from the donor car, goes on bare unpainted metal on the dead car, such as an engine bolt or a bracket, away from the battery. It is not put on the dead battery's negative terminal. This keeps any spark away from the battery.

Can I jump-start a hybrid or start-stop car?

Many hybrids and start-stop cars have a marked jump point under the bonnet and specific instructions in the owner's manual. Follow the manual. If you are not sure, call us on 8125 9169 (24 hours) before you connect anything.

How long should I drive after a jump-start?

Drive for a while rather than switching off straight away so the alternator can recharge the battery. If the car will not start again the next day, the battery is probably worn or something is draining it, and it needs testing or replacing.

When should I not jump-start a battery?

Do not jump a battery that is cracked, leaking, bulging or frozen, or if you smell rotten eggs. Call for help instead.
