What are the risks of a vending machine business in Singapore?

The main risks of a vending machine business are a weak location, sales lower than you hoped, machine faults, stock running out and rules you did not check. Nobody can promise sales. You can limit the rest by checking the site, the warranty, the support and the licence position before you pay.

What is the biggest risk?

The location. A machine only sells where people pass with a few minutes to spare. AI Printbox machines sit in malls, MRT linkways and Changi Airport Terminal 3; Topi Express machines sit at motorcycle workshops. Agree the placement with the host in writing, including whether they charge rent or a share of sales. Our guide to the best locations for a vending machine shows what to look for.

Can anyone promise how much a machine will earn?

No. Sales depend on the site, the hours and the price. Be wary of any listing that states a return and shows nothing behind it. This guide does not give an earnings figure, and neither should a seller. Work out your own costs (location, stock, the monthly support plan and electricity) and ask what you would need to sell to cover them.

What if the machine breaks down?

Faults are a normal business risk, so ask what protects you. AIVS refurbished machines carry a hardware warranty: 1 year on AI Printbox and 6 months on Topi Express (terms apply), so ask for the terms in writing. The optional support plan from S$99 a month includes 24/7 technical help. Our checklist for buying a refurbished machine covers the questions to ask.

What if the stock runs out?

An empty machine earns nothing. Owners buy phone cases (AI Printbox) or disinfectant (Topi Express) from AIVS at cost price, and the owner dashboard shows revenue, device status and inventory on your phone, so you can restock before a slot runs empty.

Is there a cash or theft risk?

AIVS machines are cashless, so there are no coins or notes inside to steal or count. You still choose a placement where the machine is in view and the host looks after the space. See how cashless vending machines work.

Do you need a licence?

It depends on what the machine sells. SFA licenses food vending machines that prepare food inside or sell raw meat or seafood; our guide explains whether you need a licence for a vending machine. Check the official agency pages for your own product before you buy.

What should you check before you buy?

  • The location: who hosts it, what they charge, and for how long.
  • The warranty and what it covers, in writing.
  • Who supports you when something fails, and the cost of that support.
  • Where stock comes from and what it costs.
  • Your own monthly costs, set against sales you can reasonably expect.
  • Any licence your product needs.

Frequently asked questions

Is a vending machine business risky?

Every business has risk. For a vending machine the main ones are the location, sales that fall short of hopes, machine faults and stock running out. Each can be checked or reduced before you buy.

No income or return figure is promised on this site. Sales depend on the location, the hours and the price, so judge the site yourself and ask for every term in writing.
