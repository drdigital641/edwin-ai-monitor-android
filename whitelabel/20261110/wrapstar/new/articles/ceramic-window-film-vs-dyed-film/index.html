What is the difference between ceramic window film and dyed film?

By Wrapstar Autostyle · Last updated 30 October 2026

The short answer

Dyed film gets its darkness from dye, which mainly absorbs sunlight, while ceramic film uses ceramic particles to reject heat, so a lighter film can still cut a lot of heat. The nano ceramic solar film we posted has 93% to 96% IR rejection. Any film must still meet Singapore's 70% front and 25% rear light limits.

What is dyed window film?

Dyed film is coloured with dye. It looks dark and cuts glare, but it works mostly by absorbing sunlight, so the film itself warms up. It is a simple, familiar kind of tint. Because darkness is what you see, many people assume a darker film must keep the cabin cooler.

What is ceramic window film?

Ceramic film uses fine ceramic particles in the film to reject infrared (IR) heat. The posted offer for our nano ceramic solar film quotes 93% to 96% IR rejection to cut cabin heat. Because the heat rejection comes from the particles rather than from how dark the film is, a ceramic film does not need to be very dark to work.

Does a darker film mean a cooler car?

Not necessarily. How dark a film looks (the share of visible light it lets through) and how much heat it rejects are two different measures. In Singapore the visible light limits are fixed: at least 70% through the front windscreen and front side windows, and 25% at the rear (Ministry of Transport, 7 April 2026). Since you cannot simply go darker on the front windows, heat rejection is the number worth asking about. Our guide to the tint rules in Singapore explains which windows the limits cover.

Can window film affect phone or GPS signal?

Films that contain metal can interfere with signals, which is why many people ask for a non-metallic film. This is general information, not a promise about any one film, so ask the studio which film it uses and check your phone and GPS once it is fitted.

What should you ask before choosing a film?

  • Which type of film is it: dyed, ceramic or something else?
  • What is the IR rejection, and who measured it?
  • What visible light does it let through on each window, and does that meet the rules?
  • Is the old film being removed, and is that included?
  • What are the warranty terms, in writing?

Questions

Is ceramic film darker than dyed film?

Not necessarily. How dark a film looks and how much heat it rejects are separate things, so a lighter ceramic film can reject more heat than a darker dyed one.
