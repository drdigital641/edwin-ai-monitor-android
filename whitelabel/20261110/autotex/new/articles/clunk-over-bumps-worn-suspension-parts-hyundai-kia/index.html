Clunk over bumps: worn suspension parts on a Hyundai or Kia

A clunk or knock over bumps on a Hyundai or Kia usually comes from a worn suspension part: a stabilizer link, strut top mount, lower arm bush, ball joint, tie rod end or a tired shock absorber. Where and when you hear it narrows the list, but a workshop should confirm the part before you buy.

By Autotex · Published 10 Nov 2026 · Last updated 10 Nov 2026

Why does my Hyundai or Kia clunk over bumps?

Suspension parts move all day, and the rubber bushes and joints that hold them wear out. When a joint or bush gets loose, metal knocks against metal as the wheel moves over a bump. That is the clunk. It is a warning that something has play in it, not a sign of one specific part.

Which parts usually cause it?

  • Front stabilizer links: a light rattle or clunk over small bumps and uneven roads.
  • Strut top mounts and strut top bearings: a knock from the top of the front wheel arch, sometimes with a creak when turning the steering.
  • Lower arm bushes and control arms: a dull clunk when braking or pulling away.
  • Ball joints and tie rod ends: a knock with loose or vague steering.
  • Shock absorbers and bump stops: a bouncy ride, or a thump when the suspension bottoms out.
  • Rear absorber top mounts: a knock from the rear over bumps.

How can I narrow down where it comes from?

Note when it happens. A clatter over small bumps at low speed often points to stabilizer links. A knock when turning the wheel points to top mounts or bearings. A clunk on braking or accelerating points to bushes or control arms. A noise from the rear points to rear mounts, stoppers or absorbers. Tell the workshop exactly this; it saves time.

Is it safe to keep driving?

A light rattle can wait for your next visit to the workshop, but do not ignore it. Worn joints get worse, and ball joints and tie rods affect steering. If the steering feels loose, the car pulls, or the noise is getting louder, have it checked soon. This guide describes common signs and cannot diagnose your car.

Should I replace parts in pairs?

Many workshops replace suspension parts such as absorbers, links and bushes as a left and right pair, so the two sides behave the same. Ask your workshop what they recommend for your car. After some suspension and steering work, a wheel alignment may be needed.
