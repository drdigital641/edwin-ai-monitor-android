THINKTOOL Master X2 vs Expert 391: which scanner suits your workshop?

By CSKA Auto Enterprise · Updated 5 November 2026

Choose the THINKTOOL Master X2 if your workshop also services 24V trucks or commercial vehicles, or wants a built-in TPMS module: it covers 12V and 24V and has 128 GB, J2534, DoIP, D-PDU and CAN FD. Choose the Expert 391 if online programming is the priority: it covers 120+ passenger brands, and the EV package adds 70+ EV brands while the HML package adds 150+ heavy-duty brands.

What do the two tablets list side by side?

These are the specifications CSKA posts for each tool, taken from THINKCAR's own descriptions:

  • THINKTOOL Master X2 10.1-inch, 128 GB, J2534, DoIP, D-PDU and CAN FD; 12V and 24V; built-in TPMS module; video remote diagnosis.
  • THINKTOOL Expert 391 10.1-inch; online programming; 120+ passenger brands, 70+ EV brands with the EV package, 150+ heavy-duty brands with HML; supports J2534 and DoIP.

When is the Master X2 the better fit?

The Master X2 suits a workshop whose bays are not all passenger cars. It covers 12V and 24V vehicles, so a mixed workshop that also takes trucks and commercial vehicles can use one tablet for both. Its built-in TPMS module activates, programs and relearns sensors, which saves buying a separate TPMS tool if tyre work is part of your day. J2534, DoIP, D-PDU and CAN FD cover the interfaces newer cars use, and video remote diagnosis lets a colleague or a technical contact look at a problem with you.

When is the Expert 391 the better fit?

The Expert 391 is the one to look at when online programming is a regular job. It lists 120+ passenger brands, and the packages are how it grows: the EV package adds 70+ EV brands and the HML package adds 150+ heavy-duty brands. That makes it a flexible base if your work moves towards EVs or you want heavy-duty coverage added later, instead of paying for it up front. CSKA also lists the Expert 391 as supporting J2534 and DoIP.

What should you check before you decide?

Decide on your jobs, not the model number:

  • 24V work if trucks or commercial vehicles come in, the Master X2 lists 12V and 24V, and the Expert 391 reaches heavy-duty through the HML package.
  • Online programming the CSKA listing names it for the Expert 391, so ask CSKA to confirm it for the makes you program before assuming the same on the Master X2.
  • EV coverage the Expert 391 lists 70+ EV brands with the EV package, so check which EV makes you see and whether they are covered.
  • TPMS the Master X2 has the TPMS module built in; for the Expert 391, ask CSKA what TPMS options suit your tyre work.
  • Updates each maker sets its own update terms, so ask what your model includes and when it ends.

Where can you compare them?

Both tools are listed on the CSKA diagnostic scanners page, with the other THINKTOOL, Autel and TOPDON models beside them. For which one fits your workshop, WhatsApp CSKA with the cars you service and the jobs you want to do.

Questions

What is the difference between the THINKTOOL Master X2 and the Expert 391?

Per CSKA's listing, both are 10.1-inch tablets. The Master X2 has 128 GB, J2534, DoIP, D-PDU and CAN FD, covers 12V and 24V and has a built-in TPMS module. The Expert 391 has online programming and covers 120+ passenger brands, with 70+ EV brands from the EV package and 150+ heavy-duty brands from HML.

Which one is better for 24V trucks?

The Master X2 is listed for 12V and 24V vehicles. The Expert 391 reaches heavy-duty coverage through THINKCAR's HML package, so check what you need with CSKA.

Which one has online programming?

CSKA lists online programming for the Expert 391. Ask CSKA to confirm coverage for the makes you program.

Do both support J2534?

Yes. CSKA lists J2534 support for both the THINKTOOL Master X2 and the Expert 391.

Which one has a built-in TPMS module?

The Master X2. Its TPMS module activates, programs and relearns sensors.
