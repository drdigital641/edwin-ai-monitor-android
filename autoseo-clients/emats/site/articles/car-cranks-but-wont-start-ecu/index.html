Car cranks but won't start, and the power and wiring check out: when to suspect the ECU

15 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · EMATS, Johor Bahru

WhatsApp us Call +60 10-820 2063

When a car cranks but won't start, the ECU is only one of several suspects. Fuel, spark, the crank and cam sensor signals, the immobiliser and the wiring all produce the same symptom. Once you have confirmed power, earth and the wiring at the ECU, and the sensors and supplies it depends on are good, the module itself becomes a fair suspect. This note covers what to rule out first and when to send the ECU in.

> Short answer

If a car cranks but won't start and the ECU's power, earth, fuses and wiring are confirmed good, check the crank and cam sensor signals, the immobiliser and the fuel and ignition supplies next. If those are good and the ECU still does not fire the injectors or coils, the module is worth checking on the bench.

01

What a cranking, non-starting car needs

An engine needs three things to start: fuel, spark and the right timing signals. The ECU controls the first two, so a fault anywhere in that chain can look like a dead ECU. Sort the symptom first:

Cranks, no fuel pressure
Check the fuel pump fuse and relay, the pump supply and the fuel pressure before the ECU.
Cranks, no spark
Check the coils, their supply and the ignition signal. A coil or driver fault is not always the ECU.
Cranks, scan tool shows no engine speed signal
Check the crank sensor, the cam sensor and their wiring. The ECU cannot time the engine without them.
Cranks, immobiliser light flashing or staying on
Check the immobiliser and key system first. The ECU may be cutting fuel or spark on purpose.

02

The checks to finish before you blame the ECU

These are standard checks and most only need a multimeter and the wiring diagram for the car:

  1. 01Battery voltage while cranking: a weak supply can make the ECU reset mid-crank
  2. 02The ECU fuses and main relay, tested under load rather than by eye
  3. 03Power and earth at the ECU connector itself, with the connector plugged in so you can back-probe it
  4. 04The crank and cam sensor signals while cranking, with a scope or the live data
  5. 05Fuel pressure, and spark at the plug or a signal at the coil
  6. 06Whether the scan tool talks to the ECU, and any codes it stores or keeps losing

03

Signs the ECU itself is the fault

When the supplies, sensors and wiring all pass, these signs point back to the module:

  • Good power and earth at the connector, good sensor signals, but no injector or coil drive from the ECU
  • No communication with the ECU while other modules answer normally
  • Water damage, corrosion or burnt parts visible on the board or in the case
  • A no-start that comes and goes with heat, vibration or a tap on the case
  • A previous repair, a jump start or a short on the wiring just before the fault began

Questions workshops ask

Can a bad ECU make a car crank but not start?

Yes, if it stops driving the injectors or coils. But a bad crank or cam sensor signal, the fuel supply, the immobiliser and wiring faults give the same symptom, so rule those out first.

How do I know if the ECU is getting power?

Check the fuses and main relay, then measure power and earth at the ECU connector with the connector plugged in. A missing supply or earth is a wiring fault, not a dead module.

Do you need the whole car to check the ECU?

No. Your workshop removes the ECU and sends or drops it off. We check it on the bench and tell you the fault and the cost before any repair.
