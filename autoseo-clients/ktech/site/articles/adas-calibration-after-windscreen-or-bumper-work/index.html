ADAS calibration after windscreen or bumper work: when a workshop needs it

ADAS calibration after windscreen replacement, bumper repair or other body work makes sure the camera and radar see the road the way the car expects. A forward camera sits behind the windscreen and a radar sensor often sits behind the bumper, so any job that moves them can throw the aim off. This note sets out which jobs call for a calibration and how a workshop checks it.

Short answer

Calibrate the ADAS camera or radar after any job that removes, replaces or moves it or the part it sits behind: windscreen replacement, bumper removal or repair, collision work, or a camera or radar replacement. Follow the car maker's procedure for that model, which is static, dynamic or both, and scan for ADAS fault codes before and after.

01Jobs that move the camera or radar

The sensors are calibrated to a fixed position on the car. If the position changes, the car can misjudge distance or lane position, even when no warning light is on.

  • Windscreen replacement, because the forward camera is mounted behind the glass
  • Front or rear bumper removal or repair, because radar sensors often sit behind the bumper cover
  • Collision or body repair around the front end, the pillars or the mirrors
  • Replacing a camera or radar sensor
  • Suspension or wheel alignment work that changes the ride height or the thrust line

02Checks before the calibration

  1. C1
    Scan firstRead ADAS fault codes with a diagnostic tool before you start, and note what is stored, so you can tell old faults from new ones.
  2. C2
    Prepare the carCheck tyre pressures, a level floor, a full-enough fuel level and an empty boot where the procedure asks for it, and make sure the glass and sensor area are clean.
  3. C3
    Look up the procedureThe method and the target position depend on make, model and year. Use the maker's procedure shown on the tool for the car in front of you.

03Static, dynamic or both

Car makers use different methods, and some require more than one:

  • Static: the car is placed in front of calibration targets at set distances and positions
  • Dynamic: the car is driven on a suitable road at a set speed while the system learns
  • Both: a static step followed by a road drive

After the calibration, scan again for ADAS fault codes and confirm the system reports as calibrated. If a fault remains, check the sensor mounting and the target setup before repeating.

Questions

Is ADAS calibration needed after a windscreen replacement?

Often, yes, when the car has a forward camera behind the windscreen. The camera is aimed to a fixed position, and a new windscreen can change it. The car maker's procedure for that model says how.

Do I need calibration after a bumper repair?

If a radar sensor sits behind the bumper, or it was removed or knocked, the aim can change. Check the procedure for the model, and scan for ADAS fault codes before and after.
