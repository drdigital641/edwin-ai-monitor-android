Nippon AI-PPS, PPF or ceramic coating: which paint protection for Johor?

Johor's sun, rain and highway grit are hard on car paint. Here is what each kind of protection actually does, where it falls short, and how to choose the right one for the way you use your car.

By MCS Auto Spray Painting · Updated 22 November 2026 · 5-minute read

WhatsApp us a photoCall

What Johor weather does to paint

Cars here face strong sun all year, heavy rain, and long hours parked outdoors at home, at work or in open mall car parks. Ultraviolet light slowly breaks down the clear coat, the thin transparent layer that protects the colour, so the roof, bonnet and boot fade first. Rain that dries on hot paint leaves water spots, and bird droppings and tree sap dry quickly in the heat and can etch into the clear coat if they are not washed off. On the highways, especially on daily runs to Senai, Pasir Gudang or the Second Link, small stones thrown up by lorries chip the front bumper and bonnet.

Protection products deal with these problems in different ways. Some are a chemical layer that makes the surface slick and easier to clean; others are a physical film that takes the hits instead of the paint. None of them repairs damage that is already there, which is why polishing comes first.

Wax: a short-term shine

Wax is the simplest and cheapest option. It adds gloss and makes water bead for a while, but in Malaysian heat it wears off quickly and needs to be reapplied often. It is a good top-up between washes, not long-term protection.

Ceramic coating: gloss and easier washing

A ceramic coating is a liquid that bonds to the clear coat and hardens into a thin, slick layer. It deepens the gloss, makes water and dirt run off, helps the paint resist UV and chemical marks such as bird droppings, and makes the car much easier to wash. It is not a shield against stone chips or deep scratches, and it still needs regular, gentle washing.

Because a coating seals in whatever is underneath, the paint should be polished first to remove swirl marks and haze. Coating unpolished paint locks the marks in. If your paint is already dull or chalky, read our guide to faded paint before deciding.

PPF: a physical film against chips

Paint protection film (PPF) is a thick, clear urethane film laid over the paint in pre-cut or hand-cut sheets. It physically absorbs stone chips, light scratches and scuffs, and many films are made to self-heal light marks in heat. Because it is applied as sheets, there are edges and joins, usually tucked around panel edges, and it is most often used on the high-impact areas: bonnet, front bumper, wings and mirrors.

Nippon AI-PPS: spray-on protection film

Nippon AI-PPS is a paint protection film that is sprayed on instead of wrapped. Because it goes on as a spray, there are no seams to lift and no glue line at the edges. It can be clear, to keep the original colour, or coloured, to change the look of the car and protect it at the same time. It is there to take the stone chips and light scratches of daily driving, and light marks on the film can be polished out.

Our Facebook reel shows AI-PPS being applied on a Toyota Alphard, in both clear and coloured versions. If you choose a coloured AI-PPS that changes the car's main colour, remember that a colour change has to be updated with JPJ; our respray guide explains how.

Side by side

  • Stone chips and light scratches: PPF and Nippon AI-PPS help; ceramic coating and wax do not
  • Gloss and easy washing: ceramic coating is strongest; PPF and AI-PPS give a glossy surface; wax gives a short-lived shine
  • UV and chemical marks: ceramic coating, PPF and AI-PPS all help; wax helps only briefly
  • Seams and edges: PPF has edges and joins; AI-PPS is sprayed on with no seams; coatings and wax have none
  • Changing colour: coloured AI-PPS can change the look; PPF and coatings are usually clear

Which one suits your car?

A new car that does a lot of highway driving, for example a daily commute from Bandar Dato' Onn or Senai: put physical protection on the front end, with AI-PPS or PPF on the bonnet, bumper and mirrors, and consider a coating on the rest.

A city car that is parked outdoors all day: polish the paint, then add a ceramic coating to cope with sun, rain spots and bird droppings, and keep it washed.

An older car with dull paint: polish first. If the gloss comes back, a coating keeps it. If the clear coat is already peeling, protection will not help and the panel needs a respray first.

A car that needs a new look as well as protection: coloured AI-PPS can do both in one job. Choose the colour from real samples rather than a screen.

A freshly resprayed or repaired car: this is the best time to protect the paint, while it is at its best. Ask the painter when the new paint is ready to be coated or filmed.

Looking after any protection

  • Wash regularly with a pH-neutral car shampoo and soft wash mitts
  • Avoid harsh brushes at automatic car washes, which leave swirl marks
  • Rinse off bird droppings and sap as soon as you see them
  • Park in shade when you can, especially at midday
  • Ask how long to wait before the first wash after a coating or film is applied

Questions about this topic

Is ceramic coating the same as PPF?

No. A ceramic coating is a thin hard layer for gloss and easier washing. PPF is a thick film that physically takes stone chips and scratches.

What is different about Nippon AI-PPS?

It is a protection film that is sprayed on rather than wrapped, so there are no seams or glue lines, and it comes in clear or coloured versions.

Should I polish before coating or film?

Yes. Protection seals in what is underneath, so swirl marks and haze should be polished out first.
