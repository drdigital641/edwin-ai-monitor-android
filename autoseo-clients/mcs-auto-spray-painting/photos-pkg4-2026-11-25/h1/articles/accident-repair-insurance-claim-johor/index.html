Car accident repair and insurance claims: a guide for JB drivers

What to do after a bump in Johor, how own-damage, knock-for-knock and third-party claims work, and where the workshop fits in. Based on insurers' published claim guides; your own policy wording always decides.

By MCS Auto Spray Painting · Updated 22 November 2026 · 5-minute read

Right after the accident

Make sure everyone is safe first. Switch on your hazard lights, move the car out of traffic if it can still be driven, and call 999 if anyone is hurt. If the car cannot be driven, call your insurer's towing hotline instead of accepting help from a tow truck that turns up uninvited; insurers' claim guides warn about this, and an unauthorised tow can make the claim harder.

Then collect the evidence while you are still at the scene. It takes a few minutes and saves a lot of trouble later:

  • Photos of the damage on every vehicle involved, close up and from further back
  • The number plates of the other vehicles, and of the tow truck if one is used
  • The other driver's name, phone number, IC number and insurer
  • The place and time, and a simple sketch of how the accident happened

Stay calm and polite, and avoid agreeing to a private settlement under pressure before you know how much the repair will be.

Lodge a police report within 24 hours

Malaysian law requires you to report a road accident to the police within 24 hours, and insurers ask for the original police report with the claim. Takaful Malaysia's claim guide notes that late reporting can result in a fine. Keep the report and its number safe, because every later step needs it.

Tell your insurer quickly

Each insurer sets its own deadlines. AmAssurance, for example, asks to be notified within 7 days of the accident and for the claim form and documents within 14 days. Others are similar but not identical, so check your policy schedule and call your insurer or takaful operator as soon as you can.

Which kind of claim is it?

Own damage (OD): you claim from your own comprehensive policy for the damage to your car. This is the usual route when the accident was your fault, and your no-claim discount (NCD) is forfeited at the next renewal.

Own damage knock-for-knock (OD-KFK): if the other driver was at fault, you can claim from your own insurer without losing your NCD, provided the police investigation outcome establishes that the other party was at fault. There are exceptions, for example when the other vehicle was uninsured or used for hire and reward, or when someone was injured, as Berjaya Sompo explains.

Third-party claim: you claim from the other driver's insurer when they caused the accident.

If your policy is third-party only, or third-party, fire and theft, it does not pay for accident damage to your own car. In that case you pay for your own repairs unless you can claim from the other driver's insurer.

What the claim usually needs

  • The insurer's claim form, completed and signed
  • The original police report
  • A copy of the policy or cover note
  • A copy of the vehicle ownership certificate (VOC)
  • Copies of the owner's and driver's IC and driving licence
  • Photos of the damage

Commercial vehicles and company-registered cars need extra documents, so ask your insurer for its checklist.

Panel workshops, adjusters and approval

Most insurers want repairs done at one of their approved or panel workshops, and using a non-panel workshop usually needs the insurer's agreement first. Ask your insurer which workshops you can use before the car goes in, so the claim is not delayed.

Once the car is at the workshop, the insurer usually appoints an adjuster to inspect the damage and agree the repair with the workshop. After approval, the workshop repairs the car and the insurer pays the workshop directly. You pay only your excess, any betterment that applies to older cars when old parts are replaced with new ones, and anything your policy does not cover.

Approval times are set by the insurer and the adjuster, not by the workshop. Be wary of anyone promising a fixed claim time before they have seen your policy and the damage.

Windscreen claims are different

A cracked windscreen is usually handled separately from accident damage. If your policy has windscreen cover, often called Endorsement 89, you can claim the repair or replacement without affecting your NCD. Great Eastern's windscreen FAQ also notes that a police report is not required for a windscreen claim. After a replacement, the cover normally ends unless you pay to reinstate it. Without windscreen cover, the glass is claimed as own damage and your NCD is lost.

Questions about this topic

Do I need a police report for a small accident?

Malaysian law requires a police report for a road accident within 24 hours, and insurers ask for the original report with an accident claim.

Will an insurance claim affect my NCD?

An own-damage claim forfeits your NCD at renewal. A knock-for-knock claim, when the police find the other driver at fault, and a claim under windscreen cover do not.

Can I choose any workshop for an insurance repair?

Most insurers want a panel or approved workshop, and a non-panel workshop usually needs their agreement first. Check with your insurer before the car goes in.

How long does a claim take?

The insurer and its adjuster set the approval time, so we do not promise a fixed time. We keep you updated on WhatsApp as the claim moves.
