The 4-room plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    The living and dining area is the largest open space on the 4-room plan and carries the household shelter. Put a shoe cabinet at the entry, give the dining bench the wall nearest the kitchen, and run the TV and storage wall at the living end so the walkway from the door stays clear. With no beams in the internal spaces, a full-height joinery wall can run cleanly to the ceiling.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The kitchen is enclosed and opens to a service yard. One run carries the hob and the other the sink on the service-yard side, with a tall fridge and pantry column at the end. Take the washing machine and a laundry cabinet to the yard so the kitchen stays for cooking.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom has its own bath. A recessed bed-head wall with lit niches replaces bedside tables, and a full-height wardrobe on the longest wall keeps the floor clear. Check that the bath door does not clash with the wardrobe doors.

  4. Common bedrooms

    The two common bedrooms suit a platform bed with drawers and a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space. One can be a child's room and the other a study or guest room.

  5. Common bath

    The common bath serves the common bedrooms and guests. For the first three years, toilet finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed, so plan tile overlays, a vanity cabinet, niches and hooks rather than hacking.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. HDB does not allow them to be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall. We confirm with your approved plan and the permit rules before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Parchment Shoji Oak Living

A full-height pale parchment oak TV wall with a shoji-inspired timber grid, cove lighting and a floating console, a built-in dining bench along the kitchen-side wall, and handleless storage. Window view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the living and dining with a household shelter on HDB's 4-room plan. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Oyster Inset Compact Kitchen

Oyster inset-panel cabinets, pale stone counters, a hob on one run and a sink on the service-yard side, sized for the enclosed 4-room kitchen that opens to a service yard.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the enclosed kitchen that opens to a service yard. A compact HDB kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single matte colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Fawn Linen Cove Suite

A recessed fawn-linen headboard wall with lit bedside niches, a built-in platform bed, and a full-height pale oak wardrobe with linen-tinted glass doors. Evening view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the main bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Duck-egg Ash Desk Berth

A platform bed with drawers, a pale ash wardrobe with duck-egg accent doors, and a wall-to-wall study desk with open knee space under the window.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a common bedroom of the official 4-room plan. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the approved plan from HDB.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Choose a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors so the permit can be applied for properly.
  5. After keys, report defects within 30 days, then book the start of works.

Planning your Merpati Meadow renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Report defects within 30 days of key collection, before renovation starts (HDB move-in guide). The Defects Liability Period runs 1 year from key collection.
  • Check floor and wall tiles for hollow or chipped pieces, plaster for cracks, and paint for stains.
  • Test every door, window, lock, tap, floor trap, light point and power socket.
  • Report through the QR code in your Welcome Kit or your project's Building Service Centre, with photos.

What the rules allow

  • Use a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors; they can apply for the permit for you.
  • A permit is needed for works such as removing floor finishes, raising floor levels in wet areas, or hacking or altering walls.
  • For the first 3 years, toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed. New tiles can be overlaid.
  • General works: Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm. Noisy works: Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. No works on Sundays or public holidays.
  • Household shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled. Windows need a BCA-approved window contractor; air-conditioners need a BCA-trained installer.

About Merpati Meadow

Merpati Meadow is a Plus HDB BTO project in Geylang, along Merpati Road, adjacent to Aljunied Park, with a common green opposite Jalan Anggerek. HDB offered 438 Plus flats in the November 2026 BTO exercise, in three residential blocks of 18 storeys: 33 2-room Flexi Type 1 flats (approx. 40 sq m), 134 2-room Flexi Type 2 flats (approx. 48 sq m) and 271 4-room flats (approx. 89 sq m). The 4-room flats are on a 99-year lease; 2-room Flexi flats are on a 99-year lease or, for eligible seniors, a short lease of 15 to 45 years. HDB's published subsidy recovery rate is 8%. HDB estimates a 39-month wait from the median of the March to June 2027 booking period, completion in 3Q 2030 and delivery possession on 31 March 2032. Those are estimates, not a confirmed key date. Residents will be served by bus services and Mattar MRT Station on the Downtown Line.
