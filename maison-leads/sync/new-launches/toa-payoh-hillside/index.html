Toa Payoh Hillside flat types and renovation ideas

Community Care Apartment

256 apartments, approx. 39 sq m (36 sq m internal), per HDB

Community Care Apartments are for senior owners, so the brief is comfort and safety: step-free floors, slip-resistant tiles, good lighting at night, blocking for grab bars and lever-type handles. Built-in storage keeps the walkways clear. Use your HDB documents for the rules that apply to this flat type.

2-room Flexi

Keep the flat open and light. A full-height wardrobe wall, a bench seat with drawers and a sliding or fluted-glass partition give a bedroom feel without closing the space. For senior owners, plan step-free floors, slip-resistant tiles and blocking in the walls for grab bars.

Toa Payoh Hillside floor plan and space planning

These are HDB's typical 4-room plans. HDB has not said which stacks have the balcony, and individual key appointments are not public. Layout Ideas for the 2-room Flexi types and Community Care Apartments are on the HDB Flat Portal.

The 4-room plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    Living and dining form one open area, with the household shelter on the plan. Put the entry cabinet and shoe storage near the door, a full-height TV and storage wall on the living side, and a built-in bench along the dining side so the walkway stays clear. On the balcony plan, keep the balcony door free of fixed furniture.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The kitchen has a service yard behind it. A single continuous run with a tall fridge and pantry column suits the compact room, and the yard takes the washing machine and laundry storage.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom is the quiet end of the plan. Build the bed head, side niches and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set so the room reads as a suite.

  4. Common bedrooms

    The two common bedrooms work as a child's room with a study wall and a flexible room for a guest, parent or home office. A platform bed with drawers and a window desk keep the floor clear.

  5. Common bath

    There are two bathrooms on the plan. Plan a vanity cabinet, wall niches and hooks, and leave wall and floor finishes alone where HDB's rules say they must stay.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. HDB does not allow them to be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall. We confirm with your approved plan and the permit rules before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Cinnamon Reeded Hearth

A full-height cinnamon reeded teak TV wall with cove lighting and a floating stone console, handleless walnut storage and a built-in window-seat bench. Styled on the official 4-room living and dining. Neighbourhood view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the open living and dining area of the official 4-room plan. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Celadon Brass Inset

Celadon matte lacquer drawers with brushed-brass inset pulls, a pale quartz counter and upstand, and a lit walnut open shelf. Sized for the compact kitchen and service yard on the official 4-room plan.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the kitchen and service yard on the official 4-room plan. A compact HDB kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single matte colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Ink Velvet Hill Suite

A full-width ink-blue velvet headboard wall with lit bedside niches, a built-in platform bed and a smoked-oak wardrobe with bronze glass doors. Evening view of neighbouring blocks is illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the main bedroom of the official 4-room plan. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Apricot Cane Study Nook

A platform bed with drawers, a full-height apricot cane wardrobe and a wall-length study desk under the window with open knee space.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a common bedroom of the official 4-room plan. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the approved plan from HDB.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Choose a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors so the permit can be applied for properly.
  5. After keys, report defects within 30 days, then book the start of works.

Planning your Toa Payoh Hillside renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Report defects within 30 days of key collection, before renovation starts (HDB move-in guide). The Defects Liability Period runs 1 year from key collection.
  • Check floor and wall tiles for hollow or chipped pieces, plaster for cracks, and paint for stains.
  • Test every door, window, lock, tap, floor trap, light point and power socket.
  • Report through the QR code in your Welcome Kit or your project's Building Service Centre, with photos.

What the rules allow

  • Use a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors; they can apply for the permit for you.
  • A permit is needed for works such as removing floor finishes, raising floor levels in wet areas, or hacking or altering walls.
  • For the first 3 years, toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed. New tiles can be overlaid.
  • General works: Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm. Noisy works: Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. No works on Sundays or public holidays.
  • Household shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled. Windows need a BCA-approved window contractor; air-conditioners need a BCA-trained installer.

About Toa Payoh Hillside

Toa Payoh Hillside is a Prime HDB BTO project in Toa Payoh, bounded by Toa Payoh Rise and Toa Payoh Link. The November 2026 sales exercise offered 1,432 flats in four residential blocks of 31 to 43 storeys: 256 Community Care Apartments, 156 2-room Flexi (Type 1), 436 2-room Flexi (Type 2) and 584 4-room flats, some with a balcony. A fifth block holds 232 rental flats, which are not for sale. HDB's estimated waiting time is 45 months. Individual key appointments are not public. Community Care Apartments are a distinct HDB product on short leases for seniors, so use your HDB documents for the rules that apply to them.
