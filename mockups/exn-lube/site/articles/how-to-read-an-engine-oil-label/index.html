How to Read an Engine Oil Label

By the EXN Lube technical team · 8 November 2026

An engine oil label tells you four main things: the viscosity, such as 5W-30; the API service category, such as SN; any ACEA sequence, such as A3/B4 or C3; and the maker approvals. Match all of these to your owner's manual, not only the viscosity number.

What does 5W-30 mean on an engine oil?

5W-30 is a SAE viscosity grade that describes how thick the oil is when cold and when hot. The number before the W (for winter) shows cold flow. A lower number flows more easily at start-up. The second number shows thickness at normal operating temperature. A higher number means a thicker oil when hot.

So 0W-20 is thinner than 5W-30, and 10W-40 is thicker than both. Your manual names the grade your engine was designed for.

What do API SN, SP and CF mean?

API categories show the level of performance tests an oil has passed. The first letter tells you the engine type. "S" is for petrol (spark ignition) and "C" is for diesel (compression ignition). The second letter shows the generation, and a later letter usually means a newer standard.

  • API SN and SP are petrol categories. SP is newer than SN.
  • API CF is an older diesel category. Check your manual if you drive a modern diesel.
  • SN/CF is a dual rating. It means the oil meets both a petrol category and a diesel category, so one oil can serve petrol and diesel passenger engines that call for those levels.

What are ACEA sequences such as A3/B4 and C3?

ACEA sequences are European performance standards that sit beside API. The letter shows the use, and the number shows the type of oil.

  • A/B sequences are for petrol (A) and light-duty diesel (B) engines. A3/B4 is a high-performance level for demanding use.
  • A5/B5 is for low-viscosity, fuel-economy oils in engines made to use them.
  • C sequences are for engines with exhaust after-treatment such as particulate filters. They use low or mid levels of SAPS (sulphated ash, phosphorus and sulphur), which are chemicals that can clog filters over time. C3 is one of these.

What are OEM approvals?

OEM approvals are listings from a car maker that confirm an oil passed its own tests. If your manual asks for a specific approval, the label must show it. A matching viscosity and API rating alone may not be enough in that case.

Does the base oil type matter?

Base oil type tells you what the oil is made from: mineral, semi-synthetic or fully synthetic. Fully synthetic oils are made from engineered base stocks and are often used where heat resistance matters. The type is not a replacement for the grade and specification. The right oil must still match the manual.

How do I read a label step by step?

  1. Open your owner's manual and note the viscosity grade, API category, ACEA sequence and any approval.
  2. Find the viscosity on the pack, for example 5W-30.
  3. Check that the API category meets or exceeds the manual's level, and that it covers petrol or diesel as needed.
  4. Check the ACEA sequence. If you drive a diesel with a particulate filter, look for the C-sequence the manual names.
  5. Check for any maker approval your manual requires.
  6. Choose the base oil type that suits your driving, such as heavy traffic or long trips.

If anything on the label does not match, ask your workshop before pouring it in.

Questions and answers

What does 5W-30 mean?

5W-30 is a SAE viscosity grade. The 5W shows how well the oil flows when cold, and the 30 shows its thickness at running temperature. A lower first number means easier cold starts, and a higher second number means a thicker hot oil.

What does API SN/CF mean on an oil label?

SN/CF is a dual rating. SN is a petrol engine category and CF is a diesel category, so the oil meets both. Always confirm that the ratings match what your owner's manual requires for your engine.

What is the difference between ACEA A3/B4 and C3?

A3/B4 is a high-performance level for petrol and light diesel engines. C3 is for engines with exhaust filters and uses reduced ash, phosphorus and sulphur to protect them. Choose the one your owner's manual names.
