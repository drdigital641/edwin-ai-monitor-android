DOT 4 vs DOT 5.1 Brake Fluid: What Is the Difference?

By the EXN Lube technical team · 8 November 2026

DOT 4 and DOT 5.1 are both glycol-based brake fluids and are compatible with each other. DOT 5.1 has a higher boiling point than DOT 4. DOT 5 is silicone-based, is a different type, and must not be mixed with either. Use the grade printed on your reservoir cap or in your owner's manual.

What is the difference between DOT 4 and DOT 5.1?

The main difference is the boiling point. DOT 5.1 has a higher minimum boiling point than DOT 4, so it handles more heat before vapour forms in the brake lines. Both have a glycol base and similar uses. Brake fluid works because it does not compress, and vapour does. If fluid boils, the pedal can feel soft and braking can fade.

How do DOT 3, 4, 5 and 5.1 compare?

The table shows the minimum boiling points set by the US standard FMVSS 116. Real products often exceed these figures.

"Dry" means fresh fluid. "Wet" means fluid that has absorbed some moisture.

Can I mix DOT 4 and DOT 5.1?

Yes, DOT 4 and DOT 5.1 are both glycol-based and can be mixed. The mix will behave closer to the lower-rated fluid, though. If your maker specifies DOT 4, DOT 5.1 is generally an acceptable upgrade, but check your owner's manual first. Do not go the other way and use a lower grade than the one specified.

Why can't DOT 5 be mixed with DOT 4?

DOT 5 is silicone-based, and silicone does not blend with glycol. Mixing can leave pockets of different fluid in the system and damage seals. DOT 5 also does not absorb water, which changes how moisture collects in the system. Most road cars use glycol fluid, so do not add DOT 5 unless your manual says so.

Why does brake fluid need to be replaced?

Glycol brake fluid absorbs moisture over time, and moisture lowers its boiling point. Water enters slowly through hoses and seals, even in a closed system. Wet fluid is more likely to boil under heavy braking, and it can also cause corrosion inside calipers, lines and ABS parts. Replacing the fluid restores its boiling point and protects these parts.

How often should I replace brake fluid?

Replace it at the interval your maker gives. Many makers suggest about every two years, but some use a different time or distance, so check your owner's manual. Malaysia's humid climate can add moisture faster, so do not push the interval longer. A workshop can also test the fluid's moisture level.

What are the signs of old brake fluid?

A soft or spongy pedal, longer stopping distance, or a pedal that sinks under steady pressure can point to old or wet fluid. These signs can also come from other brake faults, so have a workshop inspect the system promptly rather than guessing.

How do I check and replace brake fluid?

  1. Read the grade on the reservoir cap and confirm it in your manual.
  2. Check the replacement interval in the service schedule.
  3. Look at the fluid level between the MIN and MAX marks. Dark fluid is a sign it is ageing.
  4. Ask a workshop to test the moisture level if you are unsure.
  5. Have the system bled with fresh fluid from a sealed container, so old fluid is fully removed.
  6. Keep unused fluid tightly capped, because an open bottle absorbs moisture too.

Brake fluid can damage paint, so wipe off any spills right away. Brakes are a safety system, so if you are not trained, let a workshop do the job.

Questions and answers

Can I use DOT 5.1 instead of DOT 4?

DOT 5.1 is glycol-based and compatible with DOT 4, and it has a higher boiling point. It is generally an acceptable upgrade, but check your owner's manual or ask your workshop first. Never use a lower grade than specified.

Can I mix DOT 4 with DOT 5 brake fluid?

No. DOT 5 is silicone-based and does not mix with glycol-based DOT 4 or DOT 5.1. Mixing can harm seals and affect braking. Only use DOT 5 if your manual specifically names it.

How often should brake fluid be changed?

Follow your maker's interval. Many suggest about every two years, because brake fluid absorbs moisture that lowers its boiling point. Malaysia's humid climate can speed this up, so a workshop moisture test can help.
