ATF vs CVT Fluid: What Is the Difference?

By the EXN Lube technical team · 8 November 2026

ATF and CVT fluid are not the same, and you must not mix them. A conventional automatic uses ATF to work with a torque converter and clutch packs. A CVT uses a belt or chain on pulleys and needs CVT fluid with specific friction properties. Always use the fluid your owner's manual names.

What is ATF?

ATF (automatic transmission fluid) is the fluid used in conventional automatic gearboxes with a torque converter and planetary gear sets. It does several jobs at once. It transmits power inside the torque converter, lubricates gears and bearings, carries heat away, and gives the clutch packs the right grip when they engage. Makers specify the fluid by their own specification names, so match the one in your manual.

What is CVT fluid?

CVT fluid is made for continuously variable transmissions, which have no fixed gears. Instead, a steel belt or chain runs between two pulleys that change size to vary the ratio. The fluid must let metal grip metal without slipping, while still protecting parts from wear. This needs a different friction behaviour from ATF, so CVT fluid is formulated separately.

What is the main difference between ATF and CVT fluid?

The main difference is the friction behaviour the fluid must provide. ATF is tuned for smooth clutch engagement and torque converter operation. CVT fluid is tuned to give the belt and pulleys reliable grip. The table summarises the points.

Can I mix ATF and CVT fluid?

No, you should never mix ATF and CVT fluid. The wrong friction properties can cause belt slip, juddering, overheating and faster wear. In a CVT this can damage the belt and pulleys, which are costly to repair. In a conventional automatic, CVT fluid may not give the clutches the right engagement.

If the wrong fluid has been added, avoid hard driving and ask a workshop to drain and refill with the correct one. Do not wait for symptoms.

What about DCT gearboxes?

A dual-clutch transmission (DCT) needs its own specified fluid, so neither ATF nor CVT fluid is automatically right. Some DCTs use a dedicated DCT fluid, and others use a fluid close to ATF or a gear oil type. The right choice depends on the make and model, and on whether the clutches run wet or dry. Check your owner's manual or ask your workshop.

How often should I change transmission fluid?

Change it at the interval your car maker gives. Some makers publish a distance or time interval, and others call the fill "lifetime". Even then, many workshops advise a check, because fluid still ages with heat. Stop-go traffic, hot weather, towing and hilly roads are usually treated as severe use, so ask your workshop whether an earlier change makes sense for you.

How do I pick the right fluid?

  1. Find out which transmission you have: conventional automatic, CVT, DCT or manual. Your manual or a workshop can confirm this.
  2. Read the fluid specification in the owner's manual.
  3. Match that specification to the label on the pack, not just the word "ATF" or "CVT".
  4. Check the fill amount, which differs between a drain-and-fill and a full flush.
  5. Have the work done by a workshop that follows the maker's procedure and fluid level checks.

Questions and answers

Can I use ATF in a CVT?

No. A CVT needs fluid made for belt and pulley friction, and ATF is tuned for clutches and torque converters. Using ATF in a CVT can cause slip and wear. Use the CVT fluid specification given in your owner's manual.

What happens if I mix ATF and CVT fluid?

Mixing can change the friction behaviour the transmission relies on. This can lead to slipping, juddering, overheating and wear. If it happens, avoid hard driving and ask a workshop to drain and refill with the correct fluid.

How often should I change CVT or ATF fluid?

Follow the interval in your owner's manual. If the maker says lifetime fill, ask your workshop about checking it, especially if you drive in heavy traffic or hot weather, which are usually treated as severe conditions.
