How to buy genuine DENSO spark plugs in Singapore

Fake spark plugs can look almost identical to the real thing. Here is how to tell, and where to buy plugs you can trust.

Why it matters

A spark plug fires thousands of times a minute. A poor copy can misfire or wear out early, and a misfiring engine wastes fuel and can damage the catalytic converter.

5 checks before you buy

  1. Buy from an authorised channel

    The only sure way. Authorised workshops buy from KohDen and can show you the invoice.

  2. Check the price

    Iridium Power lists at S$24 a plug, Iridium TT from S$26. A set of four at a fraction of that is a red flag.

  3. Look at the box

    Genuine boxes have sharp print, correct spelling and a part number that matches the plug. Blurred printing or a loose, unsealed box is a warning.

  4. Look at the plug

    Markings on the metal shell and insulator should be crisp and match the box. DENSO's iridium plugs have a very fine centre electrode, 0.4mm on Iridium Power.

  5. Match the part number

    Check the number against DENSO's application chart for your engine. A workshop can do this in seconds.

Questions

Are cheap DENSO spark plugs on marketplaces fake?

Not always, but you cannot check where they came from. KohDen's list price for Iridium Power is S$24 a plug; far below that is a warning sign.

What happens if I fit a fake spark plug?

Copies can misfire, foul or wear out early. A misfiring engine wastes fuel and can damage the catalytic converter, which costs far more than a set of plugs.
