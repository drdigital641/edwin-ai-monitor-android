# Interior design for Towner Residences BTO, Kallang/Whampoa

**A home planned around your actual layout — before the renovation starts.**

Collecting keys at Towner Residences? Start with the decisions that matter: how the kitchen connects to dining, where your storage fits, which room becomes a study, and how your windows affect light and heat. This guide brings the project's launch layouts together with a warm contemporary concept inspired by Maison Interior Design's published work.

HDB currently lists Towner Residences among its latest MyNiceHome roadshow resources, but that does not establish a handover date. ## Three-room layouts: two bedrooms, practical flexibility

The brochure's three-room illustration has a living/dining area, main bedroom and one common bedroom, two bathrooms, a combined kitchen/utility arrangement, an air-con ledge and a household shelter beside the entrance/kitchen zone. The bedrooms sit together off the living area. The drawing illustrates a flexible open kitchen/utility arrangement, with suggested partitions to separate utility from kitchen, wet from dry kitchen, or kitchen from living/dining. Those suggested partitions are options, not a promise that every unit arrives enclosed. [2, printed p.10]

**Renovation ideas for approximately 65 sqm internal:**

- Make one dining table do daily dining and occasional laptop duty; measure chair pull-back space before ordering.
- Start storage with a slim entrance cabinet and a full-height bedroom wardrobe rather than filling every living-room wall.
- If you cook frequently, assess a lightweight glazed kitchen enclosure against the actual openings, ventilation and applicable rules.
- Keep the household shelter door, its swing and ventilation usable. Never hack or drill shelter walls or tamper with its door. [7]
- Keep the second bedroom usable as a bedroom if your household may grow; a loose desk is easier to change than an irreversible whole-room workstation.

## Four-room layouts: two different ways to plan 90 sqm

Four-room flats form the majority of the launch's sale flats: 226 of 316. Each illustrated arrangement provides three bedrooms, a living/dining area, kitchen, separate service yard, two bathrooms, air-con ledge and household shelter. Both drawings show the kitchen connecting to the dining/entrance zone rather than an island-sized room. [2]

### Conventional arrangement — stacks 219, 225, 227 and 229

The brochure's first four-room illustration places the three bedrooms along one side of the living/dining circulation route. The kitchen and service yard sit beside the bathrooms; the household shelter is beside the main entrance and opens towards the entrance/living side. The matching block plan identifies the conventional arrangements in these stacks. [2, printed pp.12 and 16–17]

**What we would explore:** keep a compact living zone by the windows, dining close to the kitchen, and storage outside the bedroom circulation path. Retain the common bedrooms for a child, guest or enclosed study. Before adding cabinetry at the entrance, mark both the front-door and shelter-door swings on the measured plan.

### More central living arrangement — stacks 221 and 223

The brochure's second four-room illustration places living/dining more centrally, with one common bedroom on the opposite side from the main-bedroom grouping. The household shelter sits alongside the kitchen with its access from that kitchen zone, so this wall cannot be treated as uninterrupted cabinet frontage. A separate service yard adjoins the kitchen. The common bathroom is also close to the kitchen area. [2, printed pp.12 and 16–17]

**What we would explore:** a dining table positioned around the kitchen access, an enclosed study in the separated common bedroom, and a calmer sleeping zone for the remaining bedrooms. Confirm which kitchen walls are provided and whether an enclosure is appropriate; do not assume a wall shown in a render can be removed.

**For both arrangements:** approve furniture and appliance dimensions first. An attractive image is not evidence that an island, king bed, oversized sofa or walk-in wardrobe fits. Wall alterations require HDB approval where applicable; structural walls and household-shelter structure are not design opportunities for hacking. [7]

## Windows, orientation and the view: design for your stack

Towner Residences sits between schools, existing housing, roads and an expressway edge. It is not a project where every unit can honestly be sold on the same “unblocked view”. The following is a reading of the **2021 brochure site/block plans**, not a live view survey. [2]

| Block 153A location | What the plans support | What to consider inside |
|---|---|---|
| Stacks **219 and 221**, on the Towner Road side | Windows towards the road/school side. Published site analysis describes these as broadly east-facing; treat that compass description as a secondary-source interpretation, not an HDB sun study. [2, 4] | Morning glare: adjustable blinds and a desk/TV position that avoids direct reflection. |
| Stacks **225 and 227** | Their living/bedroom side faces the precinct's internal central-green/play/fitness side on the site plan. [2] | On lower floors, think about activity noise and privacy as well as greenery. A blind with independent daylight/privacy control can help. |
| Stacks **233 and 235**, three-room wing | The plan positions this wing towards Towner Road and Hong Wen School. The view depends on floor, window and nearby building height. [2] | Study placement matters if school activity is audible; visit during the day rather than assuming quiet from a photograph. |
| Stacks **229 and 231**, at the MSCP/pavilion end | The site plan places these beside the parking/pavilion part of the precinct. [2] | Check lower-floor outlook and access/activity noise before planning a favourite reading seat or work corner. |

The brochure specifically notes that units **at and above the tenth storey are higher than the main roof-garden level of Block 152**. This is a relative-height note, **not a guarantee of an unobstructed panorama**. Its 33rd-storey communal roof garden is also not a private balcony belonging to your flat. [2, printed pp.16–21]

### Sun and heat choices worth making early

- For any window that receives strong late-afternoon sun, combine adjustable shading with a low-glare wall finish; avoid making a desk face the brightest pane.
- Check the actual window orientation at different times. Singapore's sun direction varies through the year; “north-facing” is not a no-heat guarantee.
- Keep air movement and air-conditioner maintenance access clear. Do not add deep ceilings or oversized full-height furniture that make a modest room feel smaller.
- Select blinds and curtains after measuring window openings and handles. Window replacement and alterations must follow the applicable HDB requirements. [7]
- No reservoir, city-skyline, permanent park or guaranteed unblocked view is claimed here. The brochure marks a nearby site as reserved for park, but this is a historical planning annotation, not confirmation that a completed park is visible from a given window. [2]

## One Maison-inspired direction: warm wood, graphite and quiet light

Maison describes its approach as **“Effortless Luxury”** and **“Space Consciousness”**. Its published interiors range from light, practical family homes to stronger charcoal-and-stone schemes. For this sample, the direction is restrained warm contemporary — not an unsupported claim that Maison only designs in Japandi or any single style. [9–12]

## From keys to move-in: a realistic sequence

This is a planning example, not a guaranteed Maison programme. Start with your confirmed HDB appointment; the final schedule depends on defects, approvals, scope, material availability and contractor capacity.

1. **Before keys:** share your exact floor plan, household needs, appliance sizes and budget; develop the concept and itemised scope. Do not fix a move-in day from a third-party project completion estimate. HDB itself recommends planning your renovation before key collection. [1, 7]
2. **First days after keys:** photograph the bare flat and inspect doors/windows, plumbing, finishes and electrical points. Submit defects to the precinct Building Service Centre or HDB's online service **before affected surfaces are covered by renovation**. HDB provides a **one-year Defects Liability Period**; it is not a blanket one-month expiry. Leave time for rectification. [6]
3. **Survey and approvals:** verify site measurements, OCS inclusions and block-specific restrictions. Engage a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors. Obtain permits for applicable work before starting; the owner remains responsible for compliance. Do not assume an ID's marketing page itself proves contractor registration. [7, 8]
4. **Fit-out:** a provisional working allowance for a moderate new-flat fit-out is **about 8–12 weeks after site readiness and approvals**, not a project-specific promise or an HDB service standard. A simpler carpentry-only scope may differ; heavy changes, rectification or procurement delays can extend the sequence. HDB says new-flat renovation should be completed within **three months after approval**; your permit conditions govern the actual approved period. [7]
5. **Checks and handover:** test fittings and appliances, inspect joinery, rectify snags, clean thoroughly, and allow time for furniture delivery before moving in. Recheck the fire alarm and restore anything temporarily protected during dusty work. Keep documents and maintenance instructions.

**Rules that change the design:** HDB's guide restricts general renovation to Monday–Saturday, **9am–6pm SGT**, and noisy work to Monday–Friday, **9am–5pm SGT**; no noisy works on weekends, public holidays or eves of major public holidays. New BTO bathroom wall/floor finishes cannot be removed during the first three years. Household-shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled and its door must not be tampered with. Always check current and block-specific rules. [7]

## Questions Towner Residences owners ask

### When will I collect keys?

Late 2026 is a third-party completion expectation, not a confirmed appointment. Check your HDB application status and the SMS/email or letter HDB sends you. A roadshow listing or virtual tour does not confirm your keys. [1, 3]

### Is a four-room flat here 90 sqm or 93 sqm?

The brochure states approximately **90 sqm internal floor area** and **93 sqm including the air-con ledge**. Three-room flats are approximately 65 sqm internal and 68 sqm including the ledge. The ledge is not living space available for cabinetry or a study. [2]

### Are all the four-room layouts the same?

No. The brochure illustrates a conventional bedroom grouping and a more central living/dining arrangement, with different household-shelter access. Check your stack and whether your plan is mirrored before adapting any concept. [2]

### Can I copy these concepts exactly?

No. They show an aesthetic direction only. Actual windows, columns, ceiling beams, shelter details, services and clearances must be checked. Maison should develop a unit-specific measured layout and approve the final proposal with you before construction.

### Can I change the kitchen or hide the household shelter?

Assess your actual kitchen enclosure and any proposed alterations with your contractor; permits are needed for applicable works. Do not hack or drill the shelter structure, tamper with its door or obstruct its operation. This sample makes no promise that any wall can be removed. [7]

### What should I send for a useful concept discussion?

Send your HDB unit plan, flat type, preferred style, household size, cooking habits, storage priorities, budget and confirmed key timing if known. A stack number helps assess orientation without making a view promise. You need not share NRIC, Singpass details or purchase documents for an initial design conversation.
