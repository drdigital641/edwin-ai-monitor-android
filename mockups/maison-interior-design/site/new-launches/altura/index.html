Planning your Altura interior

Bukit Batok sits just west of the Dairy Farm and Hillview belt and is a short hop from other new homes we already cover, including Lumina Grand on nearby Bukit Batok West Avenue 5. New EC owners here typically live in the unit for the long stretch after the minimum occupation period, so the first fit-out is worth doing properly. The work that usually pays off is built-in: a shoe cabinet that actually takes the household’s shoes, a living-room joinery wall instead of a loose television bench, full-height bedroom wardrobes, and a kitchen of fitted cabinets and a tall pantry rather than assembled boxes. Maison is an interior designer. We specify carpentry that is built in, not IKEA-style loose furniture, and we are happy to work in close, material-led photographs as well as a full-room view.

Without a published official floor plan on this page, we will not describe room sizes, window positions or a typical unit type. Those details change by stack and by type, and inventing them helps no one. Send the approved plan that came with your sale documents, plus photographs of the existing wet areas and any built-in appliances that must stay. From that, we can plan an entry run, a dining position that does not block the corridor, and bedroom wardrobes that respect the window and the air-conditioning ledge.

After handover, the useful order is always the same: defects inspection and access for rectification, a measured survey, then the management corporation’s renovation rules, deposit and permitted hours, and only then fabrication. A moderate carpentry scope is often allowed about six to ten weeks on site after approvals; that is a planning figure, not a promise. If your keys are already in hand, write to us with the plan and we can start the design conversation straight away.

Planning your Altura renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Your sale and purchase agreement sets the defects liability period, commonly 12 months from vacant possession. Check yours.
  • Inspect before your contractor starts, so new marks are not blamed on the renovation.
  • Check floors, walls, doors, windows, balcony glass, sealant, appliances, taps, water heater and air-conditioning.
  • Submit a list with photos through the developer's defects process and keep a copy.

What the rules allow

  • Renovation approval comes from the condo's management (the managing agent, then the MCST). Expect a form, a deposit and contractor registration.
  • Working hours are set by the condo's by-laws and are usually shorter than HDB's. Check them before booking work.
  • Changes seen from outside, such as balcony screens, grilles and the main door, need approval and must follow the approved design.
  • Structural changes need a professional engineer and building approvals.
  • Executive condominiums follow the condo's rules, not HDB's renovation permits.
