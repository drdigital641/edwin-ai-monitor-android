Planning your Thomson Reserve interior

In a large Thomson-belt condominium, unit types will vary, and neighbours will be fitting out on different calendars. What does not vary is the work that usually makes a new home liveable: an entry cabinet, a living-room joinery wall, full-height wardrobes, and a fitted kitchen with a tall pantry. If you have a study or a flexible common room, a built-in desk and wardrobe run is cleaner than a loose table. Maison designs those pieces as custom carpentry, not as flat-pack furniture, and we will not publish a typical layout for this estate because no official floor plan is on file. Send your approved plan and we will design to that drawing.

Those are nearby links for readers, not a shared typologies list or a shared management handbook. We will not invent walking times to Thomson, to Ang Mo Kio, or to any MRT station. Check your own stack, facing and the estate rules before you plan blinds, a balcony cabinet or a window bench, especially if your living room is long and open.

When a key date is in writing, leave time for defects inspection, measurement and renovation approval before fabrication. Planning your Thomson Reserve renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Your sale and purchase agreement sets the defects liability period, commonly 12 months from vacant possession. Check yours.
  • Inspect before your contractor starts, so new marks are not blamed on the renovation.
  • Check floors, walls, doors, windows, balcony glass, sealant, appliances, taps, water heater and air-conditioning.
  • Submit a list with photos through the developer's defects process and keep a copy.

What the rules allow

  • Renovation approval comes from the condo's management (the managing agent, then the MCST). Expect a form, a deposit and contractor registration.
  • Working hours are set by the condo's by-laws and are usually shorter than HDB's. Check them before booking work.
  • Changes seen from outside, such as balcony screens, grilles and the main door, need approval and must follow the approved design.
  • Structural changes need a professional engineer and building approvals.

Rules change, so we check the current version for your unit before we quote. Full new condo renovation guide
