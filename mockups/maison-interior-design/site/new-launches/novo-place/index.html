Planning your Novo Place interior

District 24 is a growth corridor where new housing, new roads and new amenities have been arriving in stages. That matters for renovation only in practical ways: you may be planning a first home while the neighbourhood is still settling, and you will want storage that works from week one — school bags, bulk groceries, and a place to drop shoes and helmets — without waiting to “see how the estate feels”. The joinery that usually does that job is an entry cabinet, a living-room storage wall, full-height wardrobes, and a fitted kitchen with a tall pantry. Maison designs those as built-in carpentry, not as loose, flat-pack furniture.

We do not have an official floor plan on file for Novo Place, and we will not invent a typical unit type. EC estates often mix family typologies, but the only safe starting point is your approved plan. With that in hand we can tell whether the living and dining share one long wall (a good place for a continuous media and storage run), whether a common bedroom can take a built-in desk, and how far the kitchen can go before it meets a structural wall or a supplied appliance bank. Close-up material studies — stone, veneer, laminate, profiled doors — are as useful as a wide room view when you are choosing a look.

If your expected handover is in 2027, you still have time to lock a design, but not an unlimited amount once fabrication lead times are included. The sober sequence is: confirm the key appointment, inspect defects, measure, submit the management renovation application, then build. A moderate carpentry scope is often planned at six to ten weeks on site after approvals, as an editorial allowance only. Planning your Novo Place renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Your sale and purchase agreement sets the defects liability period, commonly 12 months from vacant possession. Check yours.
  • Inspect before your contractor starts, so new marks are not blamed on the renovation.
  • Check floors, walls, doors, windows, balcony glass, sealant, appliances, taps, water heater and air-conditioning.
  • Submit a list with photos through the developer's defects process and keep a copy.

What the rules allow

  • Renovation approval comes from the condo's management (the managing agent, then the MCST). Expect a form, a deposit and contractor registration.
  • Working hours are set by the condo's by-laws and are usually shorter than HDB's. Check them before booking work.
  • Changes seen from outside, such as balcony screens, grilles and the main door, need approval and must follow the approved design.
  • Structural changes need a professional engineer and building approvals.
  • Executive condominiums follow the condo's rules, not HDB's renovation permits.
