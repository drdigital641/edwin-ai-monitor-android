Planning your Rivelle Tampines interior

Tampines is a mature, well-equipped new town, which is why EC owners here often plan the first fit-out as a long stay: school runs, groceries in bulk, guests, and a living room that has to work on weeknights. The joinery that usually carries that life is an entry shoe and drop cabinet, a living-room storage wall, full-height wardrobes, and a fitted kitchen with a tall pantry rather than a row of loose cabinets. A common bedroom often becomes a study or a child’s room; a built-in desk and wardrobe along one wall keeps the floor clear. Maison is an interior designer. We specify built-in carpentry, finished and photographed professionally, not IKEA-style furniture, and we will design from your approved plan only.

No official floor plan is on file, so this page does not list typologies or room sizes, and we will not guess a “typical Tampines EC layout”. Amenities in the town centre, parks and transport are part of the district’s character, but we will not invent a walking time from your stack to any of them. Check facing, afternoon sun and the management handbook before you decide on blinds, a balcony cabinet or a window bench.

A confirmed key date, when you have one, should drive the programme — not a launch-year rumour. After handover, inspect defects, measure, and obtain renovation approval before anything is fabricated. A moderate, non-structural carpentry scope is often planned at six to ten weeks on site after access and approvals; that is an editorial allowance, not a guarantee. Planning your Rivelle Tampines renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Your sale and purchase agreement sets the defects liability period, commonly 12 months from vacant possession. Check yours.
  • Inspect before your contractor starts, so new marks are not blamed on the renovation.
  • Check floors, walls, doors, windows, balcony glass, sealant, appliances, taps, water heater and air-conditioning.
  • Submit a list with photos through the developer's defects process and keep a copy.

What the rules allow

  • Renovation approval comes from the condo's management (the managing agent, then the MCST). Expect a form, a deposit and contractor registration.
  • Working hours are set by the condo's by-laws and are usually shorter than HDB's. Check them before booking work.
  • Changes seen from outside, such as balcony screens, grilles and the main door, need approval and must follow the approved design.
  • Structural changes need a professional engineer and building approvals.
  • Executive condominiums follow the condo's rules, not HDB's renovation permits.
