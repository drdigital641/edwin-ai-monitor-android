Planning your Watten House interior

Shelford Road is a quiet, low-rise corridor rather than a high-street launch. Owners who buy here usually want the interior to feel considered and residential, not like a hotel suite dropped into a new tower. That is a good brief for built-in carpentry: a living room with a proper joinery wall and, where the plan allows, a window seat; a kitchen of fitted cabinets rather than loose units; and bedrooms with full-height wardrobes and a headboard wall instead of a freestanding bed against a bare plaster wall. Maison will design those elements to the approved plan, in materials that can take a closer, more luxurious photograph — stone, timber veneer, fluted panels — as well as a wide room view. We do not specify IKEA-style flat-pack furniture as the backbone of the home.

No official floor plan is on file, so typologies and room sizes are not listed. We will also not invent a unit count or describe a “typical Watten House layout”. When you send the approved drawings we can talk about circulation, the dining position, and whether a common bedroom should be a guest room, a study or a child’s room with a built-in desk.

If you are working towards a 2027 handover, use the time to freeze the design before keys, then follow the usual order: defects, measurement, management approval, fabrication. A moderate carpentry scope is often planned at six to ten weeks on site after access and approvals, as an editorial figure only. Planning your Watten House renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Your sale and purchase agreement sets the defects liability period, commonly 12 months from vacant possession. Check yours.
  • Inspect before your contractor starts, so new marks are not blamed on the renovation.
  • Check floors, walls, doors, windows, balcony glass, sealant, appliances, taps, water heater and air-conditioning.
  • Submit a list with photos through the developer's defects process and keep a copy.

What the rules allow

  • Renovation approval comes from the condo's management (the managing agent, then the MCST). Expect a form, a deposit and contractor registration.
  • Working hours are set by the condo's by-laws and are usually shorter than HDB's. Check them before booking work.
  • Changes seen from outside, such as balcony screens, grilles and the main door, need approval and must follow the approved design.
  • Structural changes need a professional engineer and building approvals.
