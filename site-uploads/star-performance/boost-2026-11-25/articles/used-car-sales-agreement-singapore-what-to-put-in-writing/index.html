Used car sales agreement in Singapore: what to put in writing

A used car sales agreement in Singapore should list the car details, the agreed price, every promised add-on or repair, itemised fees, deposit terms, the handover date and what after-sales support covers. If it was promised, put it in writing.

Why put it in writing?

Verbal promises are easy to forget and hard to prove. A written agreement turns what was said into something both sides can check. At Star Performance Motor we say it simply: a signature is not just paperwork, it is our promise.

What to write down

  • The car details: make, model, variant, plate number and any other details that identify it.
  • The agreed price, and what is included in it.
  • Every add-on, repair or service that was promised before handover.
  • All fees, itemised, so nothing appears later as a surprise.
  • Deposit terms, including when it is refundable.
  • The handover date and what condition the car will be in.
  • What after-sales support covers, and what it does not.
  • Trade-in terms, if you are trading in your current car.

Get promised extras into the agreement

In August 2026 a first-time buyer chose an Avante Hybrid and asked for a polish and for some exterior parts to be dechromed. Rather than leave that as a spoken promise, we wrote every agreed item into the sales agreement. If you are thinking about that model, read our guide to the used Hyundai Avante. The same idea applies to any car: if it was agreed, it goes on paper.

Deposit and inspection terms

Before you pay a deposit, ask what happens if the inspection finds problems. A clear agreement says whether the deposit is refunded, whether the dealer fixes the issue, or whether the price changes. Our article on the pre-purchase inspection explains how to arrange one.

Sometimes trust helps too. In August 2026 we declined a deposit on a Honda Civic FC1 and reserved it on a gentleman's agreement until the repairs were done. That worked because we had told the buyer everything first. You should still ask for the terms in writing whenever money changes hands.

Inspections can also help you negotiate. In July 2026 a family had an inspection done on a Toyota Sienta at another dealer, and it found wear items. We helped negotiate so the family paid only half the repair cost, and the work was done before handover.

Describe the car's condition honestly

Ask the agreement to reflect what you were told about the car. If the seller said the car has no known accident history, or that the service records are complete, that should be clear in the paperwork too. If you have had the car inspected, attach or refer to the report. This protects you, and it protects an honest dealer, because both sides know what was agreed at the time of sale.

Read the whole document before you sign, including the small print. If a line is unclear, ask what it means and have the answer added in plain words. A dealer who is comfortable with this will not mind the questions.

Trade-in terms

If you are trading in your current car, the agreement should say what value is offered, what condition it depends on, and when you hand your old car over. It should also say what happens if the car turns out to be different from how it was described. Keep the trade-in and the purchase on one clear page so you can see the full picture in one place.

Fees and financing

Ask for every fee to be listed. In August 2026 a Lexus buyer told us that another dealer, which we will not name, wanted to charge him a $3,000 admin fee just because he asked for a more flexible down payment. That is what the buyer reported to us. At Star Performance Motor we do not charge unreasonable admin fees for different financing needs.

If you are planning a loan, work out what you can borrow before you sign. Our guide on how much car loan you can get for a used car is a good start. Car loan limits are set by MAS, so check the current rules on its official website.

After-sales support in writing

Ask exactly what after-sales help covers. In July 2026 a Mitsubishi Attrage buyer found a small issue after collection. As promised, we arranged complimentary pick-up and drop-off, fixed it at our workshop and returned the car in working order. Having the promise clear from the start made that simple for everyone.

Red flags

  • Surprise admin fees that were never mentioned earlier.
  • Promises that stay verbal and never reach the paper.
  • Pressure to sign before you have read everything.
  • A deposit with no stated refund conditions.
  • No copy of the signed documents for you.

Keep your copies

Keep a signed copy of the agreement, receipts, inspection reports and any messages that confirm what was agreed. Frequently asked questions

What should a used car sales agreement include?

It should include the car details, the agreed price, every add-on or repair, itemised fees, deposit and refund terms, the handover date, after-sales support and any trade-in terms.

Is a verbal promise from a dealer enough?

It is safer to have every agreed item written into the agreement. A verbal promise is hard to prove later.

What if a pre-purchase inspection finds problems?

Ask the dealer to state in writing whether the deposit is refunded, the fault is fixed before handover, or the price changes.
