# Home hydroponic tower in Singapore: how much time does it really take?

A home hydroponic tower in Singapore takes about 3.5 hours of your time per growing cycle, and most weeks it is only a 10-minute check of the water and nutrient levels. The lights run on their own for about 14 hours a day, so the work is concentrated at the start and the end of each cycle.

This guide shows where those hours go, so you can decide whether a tower fits your week. If you would like to see the system itself, start with our [home page for households](/home/).

## What is the Urban Eco tower?

It is a 5-tier indoor hydroponic grow system that grows plants in water instead of soil. It stands 811 x 433 x 1805 mm (L x W x H), so it fits in a corner of a flat. Four of the five layers are planting layers with 18 plants each, and the fifth is a seedling layer with 76 positions. The planting layers can be assembled or taken apart freely, and the frame is food-grade PP.

Full-spectrum LED lighting runs for about 14 hours a day, so you do not need a sunny window.

## Where do the 3.5 hours go?

One growing cycle takes about 4 to 7 weeks depending on the crop. Across that cycle, the hands-on time adds up to about 3.5 hours:

- About 1 hour for seeding and transplanting
- About 10 minutes a week checking water and nutrient levels (about 50 minutes in total)
- About 1 hour for harvesting
- About 1 hour for cleaning and resetting between cycles

The weekly check is the only regular job in the middle of the cycle. Everything else happens at the start and the end.

## What does the weekly 10-minute check involve?

You look at the water level and the nutrient level in the tank and top up when needed. The nutrient is a chemical fertiliser mix made for hydroponics, so you follow the instructions that come with it.

Every 2 to 3 months, the system needs a full cleanse.

## Is it easier to harvest all at once or a little at a time?

It depends on the crop. Whole-head crops, such as lettuce heads and pak choy, are harvested once and then replanted. Loose-leaf lettuce, kale, basil and other herbs are cut-and-come-again, so they keep producing after the first harvest. See [what you can grow](/what-you-can-grow/) for the crops we trialled.
