Thinking of hiring an SEO executive?

Add up the full cost first

A good SEO or digital marketing executive can be worth every dollar, if there's enough work for them. Before you post the job ad, count everything the role costs, not just the salary.

  • Salary. Typically around S$3,000 to S$5,000 a month for these roles, depending on experience (general range, not a quote).
  • Employer CPF. 17% on top for Singapore citizens and PRs aged 55 and below.
  • Tools. Professional SEO tools are usually paid monthly subscriptions.
  • Your time. Recruiting, training, managing and covering leave or a resignation.

Example only: a S$3,500 salary plus 17% CPF is about S$4,095 a month, or roughly S$49,000 a year, before tools or bonus.

Before you sign with anyone

Seven questions to ask any SEO provider

  • Who owns the website and the domain? It should be you. With Page One, your domain is registered in your name.
  • Is there a minimum contract or setup fee? Know what you're committing to before you start.
  • What exactly is done each month? Ask for a list, not "ongoing optimisation".
  • Will I get access to Google Search Console? It's Google's own free report, so you can check results yourself.
  • Do you guarantee rankings? If the answer is yes, be careful. Google decides rankings, so no one can promise them.
  • How do you get links? Bought links break Google's guidelines and can hurt your site.
  • Does it cover AI search? More people now ask ChatGPT or read Google's AI answers before they click. Ask what is done for that, and whether it costs extra.

Check before you trust

How to check any SEO provider's claims in 10 minutes

SEO pages are full of big numbers: "#1 ranked", "200+ clients", "300% more leads". Many come with no client name, no date and no way to check. Here's how to test them, whoever you are talking to, including us.

  • Look up the company. Search the company name or UEN on ACRA's Bizfile. A real Singapore company shows its registration date and status.
  • Ask for dated Search Console numbers. Search Console is Google's own report. Ask for clicks, impressions and average position with the date range shown, for a site you can visit.
  • Visit the client websites. If they name clients, open the sites and search for their services yourself.
  • Read the headline against the small print. A page that promises "Rank #1" but says in its FAQ that no one can guarantee rankings is telling you something.
  • Find the minimum term. Many SEO contracts have one. Know yours before you pay the first invoice.

SEO services in Singapore: your questions, answered

How much do SEO services cost in Singapore?

It varies a lot by provider and scope. As a general guide, small business SEO retainers from agencies are often somewhere around S$500 to S$3,000 or more a month, sometimes with a setup or audit fee. Hiring an in-house SEO or digital marketing executive typically means a salary of roughly S$3,000 to S$5,000 a month plus employer CPF and tools. These are typical ranges, not quotes. Page One's Get Found plan is S$20 a month (introductory price), with optional SEO Boosts at S$10 each, no setup fee and no contract.

Is an SEO agency better than Page One?

It depends on what you need. An agency can make sense if you have a larger budget, compete for national searches, sell in several markets or want ads and SEO handled together. Page One is built for new and small local businesses that want the essentials done properly at a low monthly cost: a website, Google and Google Maps setup, AI search setup, regular articles and a monthly technical audit.

Should I hire an in-house SEO executive or outsource?

Hire if you have enough marketing work to keep a full-time person busy, for example social media, ads, content and events as well as SEO. If what you mainly need is for your website to be found on Google and Google Maps, outsourcing the SEO groundwork usually costs far less, and you can still hire later when there's more to do.

How much does it cost to hire an SEO or digital marketing executive in Singapore?

Salaries for these roles typically range from around S$3,000 to S$5,000 a month depending on experience, as a general guide. On top of salary, employers pay CPF of 17% for Singapore citizens and PRs aged 55 and below, plus paid SEO tools, leave cover, training and your own time managing the role. For example, a S$3,500 salary plus 17% CPF is about S$4,095 a month before tools or bonus.
