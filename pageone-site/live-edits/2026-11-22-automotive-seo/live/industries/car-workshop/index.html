How customers find a car workshop

They search for the job, the car and the area

Drivers look for a workshop when something goes wrong: a warning light, a strange noise, an aircon that blows warm air, a servicing that's due. They type exactly that into Google, often with their car model or their estate. If your website has no page for that job, Google has nothing to show for you, however good your work is.

Most workshop websites we check have one services page with a list of 20 jobs and no prices. Google can't rank a list. It ranks a page that answers one search well. So we give every service its own full page, with your real prices, photos and a WhatsApp button.

What we set up

What a car workshop website needs to get found

A full page for each service

Servicing, aircon, brakes, absorbers, batteries, gearbox, diagnostics: each gets its own page with your price, how long it takes and real photos.

Area pages within 10 km

"Car servicing Bishan", "workshop Yishun": a real page for each nearby area you actually serve, with the roads and landmarks drivers know.

Car model pages

Pages for the cars you see most (Honda Vezel, Toyota Wish, Tesla Model 3, Mercedes), with their common faults and service intervals.

Google Maps that matches

The right main category, phone, hours and website link on your Google Business Profile, matching your website exactly.

Reviews, the honest way

A simple review link you send after every job. We never write, buy or reward reviews.

WhatsApp taps counted

Every WhatsApp and call tap on your website is counted on our dashboard, so you can see which page brings in jobs.

Lessons from our own workshop

Three things that moved Edwin Garage up

  1. 1

    Prices on the page

    Pages with a clear price table get the WhatsApp message; pages that say "call for price" get skipped.

  2. 2

    Articles that answer real questions

    Edwin Garage's #1 positions for aircon, brake pads and Vezel servicing came from articles answering drivers' questions, each with a price box and WhatsApp button.

  3. 3

    Clear walk-in rules

    Telling drivers exactly what's walk-in (battery, emergencies) and what's by appointment cuts wasted calls and builds trust.

What we found on our own Google profile: the website link pointed to an old page and the phone number was missing. Small things like that cost map-pack positions. We check them for every workshop we take on.
