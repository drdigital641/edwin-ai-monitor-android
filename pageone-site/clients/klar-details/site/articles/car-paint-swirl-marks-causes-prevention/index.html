Car Paint Swirl Marks: What Causes Them and How to Prevent Them

Published 14 Oct 2026 · Updated 1 Nov 2026 · Klar Details team

Swirl marks are fine circular scratches in the clear coat, caused mostly by poor washing and drying. They show up as a hazy web under sunlight or bright torchlight. Machine polishing removes them, and gentler washing habits prevent them from returning.

What causes swirl marks?

Swirls are tiny scratches, and they come from anything that drags grit across the paint.

  • Automatic car washes with brushes.
  • Dirty or worn wash mitts and sponges.
  • Dry wiping dust off the car.
  • Rough or dirty drying towels.
  • Careless polishing or old, aggressive products.

Dark paint shows them most, because it reflects light cleanly and any disturbance stands out. Light colours hide the problem but are not free of it.

How do you check if your car has swirl marks?

Look at the car in direct sunlight or under a strong single light, such as a torch. Swirls appear as circular or spiderweb-like lines that seem to move as you do.

Check the bonnet and doors first, since those panels get the most contact. In shade or under soft light, the paint may appear fine, so a proper check needs a bright, angled light.

Can swirl marks be removed?

Yes, in most cases. Paint correction levels the surface by removing a very thin layer of clear coat, so the scratches disappear rather than being filled.

The process is careful. The technician tests the paint, chooses pad and polish, works panel by panel, and inspects under proper lighting. Deep scratches that reach the colour coat may only be reduced. Our paint correction service starts with a free assessment so you know what is realistic.

How do you prevent swirl marks coming back?

  1. Never wash dry. Rinse first to float off grit.
  2. Use a clean wash mitt, and rinse it often.
  3. Use a pH-balanced shampoo for proper lubrication.
  4. Dry with a soft microfibre towel or a blower, patting rather than dragging.
  5. Avoid automatic brush washes.
  6. Protect the finish with a coating, which makes washing easier and cuts down contact.

A coating does not make paint scratch-proof, so good technique still matters.

What is a good routine?

Wash gently on a regular rhythm, keep towels clean, and have the paint inspected from time to time. If you are unsure whether your car needs correction, our guide to the 5 signs your car needs paint correction is a good starting point.

Questions

Do all cars get swirl marks?

Most cars that are washed regularly pick up some fine marks over time. Careful washing and drying keep them to a minimum, while automatic brush washes and dirty towels make them far worse. Dark paint shows them most clearly, so owners of dark cars notice them first.

Will a ceramic coating hide swirl marks?

It may add gloss that softens their look, but it does not remove them, and the marks stay visible underneath. The paint should be corrected first, so the coating seals in a clean finish. That order of work is why preparation takes much of the time in a coating job.

Does polishing damage the paint?

Polishing removes a very small amount of clear coat. Done carefully by a trained technician, who measures and tests the paint first, it is safe. What you want to avoid is repeated, heavy polishing over the years, so correction should be done only when the paint truly needs it.

Can I remove swirl marks by hand?

Light marks can sometimes be improved by hand, but the results are limited and uneven, and poor technique can add new scratches. Machine polishing with the right pad and polish is far more consistent. If you are unsure, a free assessment will show what your paint needs.

How do I know how deep the scratches are?

A technician can assess depth by inspection and test polishing on a small area. As a rough guide, a scratch that catches your fingernail is usually too deep to remove completely. It can often be reduced, though, and we will tell you honestly what to expect.
