Do you need jack stands as well as a floor jack in a workshop?

By CSKA Auto Enterprise · Updated 7 November 2026

Yes. A floor jack is for lifting a car and a jack stand is for holding it. Never work under a car supported only by a hydraulic jack: set the car on stands, on level, solid floor, at the lifting points the vehicle maker shows. Buy stands with a rating that covers the heaviest car you lift, and pair them with a low-profile jack if you service lowered cars.

What is the difference between a floor jack and a jack stand?

A floor jack lifts the car with hydraulic pressure and is made to raise and lower it. A jack stand is a fixed support that holds the car at the height you set. The jack does the lifting, then the stands take the weight, and the jack stays in place only as a back-up.

Why is a jack alone not enough?

A hydraulic jack can lose pressure, slip on its pad or be knocked while someone is working. A stand is designed to stay at its set height. That is why general workshop practice and most makers' instructions say not to get under a car held only by a jack. Follow your workplace safety rules and the instructions that come with each tool.

What should you check before you lift?

Run the same short check each time:

  • Floor work on level, solid ground, never on a soft or sloping surface.
  • Lifting points use the points the vehicle maker shows, and do not lift on suspension parts or the underbody unless the maker allows it.
  • Rating the jack and stands should be rated for more than the car's weight; read the maker's capacity and whether the rating is for each stand or a pair.
  • Stands in place lower the car onto the stands, then check that it is stable before anyone goes under it.
  • Wheels chock the wheels that stay on the ground.

When do you need a low-profile jack?

Lowered and sports cars can sit too low for a normal jack to slide underneath. A low-profile jack with a low minimum pad height, such as the TFJ 330 at 85 mm, gets under them. A stand still has to hold the car once it is up, so check that the stand's lowest setting also suits the cars you service.

Do you still need stands if you have a two-post lift?

A two-post lift holds the car on its arms, and its own safety locks must be engaged before work starts, as the maker describes. Jacks and stands are still used for jobs a lift does not do, such as raising one wheel or supporting a part. The related guides below cover lift choice and the full workshop equipment list.

Questions

Do I need jack stands if I have a floor jack?

Yes. A floor jack lifts the car but is not meant to hold it while you work underneath. Put the car on jack stands, on level solid ground, at the vehicle maker's lifting points.

Is a hydraulic jack safe to work under?

No. A hydraulic jack can lose pressure or slip, so do not work under a car held only by one. Use stands rated for the car and follow the tool maker's instructions.

What is the THINKCAR TFJ103?

CSKA lists it as a 3-ton jack stand that pairs with the 3-ton THINKCAR floor jack 330.

What is a low-profile floor jack?

It is a jack with a low minimum pad height so it fits under lowered or sports cars. CSKA lists the THINKCAR TFJ 330 with an 85 mm minimum pad height.

Can I use jack stands on a lift?

A two-post lift has its own safety locks that must be engaged as the maker describes. Stands and jacks are still used for jobs such as supporting a part or raising a single wheel.
