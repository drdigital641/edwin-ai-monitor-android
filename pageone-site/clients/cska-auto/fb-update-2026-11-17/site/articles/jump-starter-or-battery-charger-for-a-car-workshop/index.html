Jump starter or battery charger for a car workshop: which do you need?

By CSKA Auto Enterprise · 15 November 2026

A jump starter and a battery charger do different jobs. A jump starter gives a flat battery a short, strong burst so the engine can start now, in the bay or at the roadside. A battery charger refills the battery slowly over a longer time. Most car workshops end up with both: the jump starter for quick starts and call-outs, the charger for batteries that need a proper recharge. If you can only buy one first, choose by whether your day is more call-outs and moves, or more batteries sitting on the bench.

What does a jump starter do?

It stores enough energy to turn over an engine whose own battery is flat, without a second car. You clamp it on, start the engine and take it off. It is built to start the engine, not to recharge the battery, so the car's charging system or a charger still has to restore the charge afterwards.

What does a battery charger do?

It feeds current into the battery over a longer time until it is back to charge. That suits a customer's battery that went flat, a car that has sat for weeks, or a battery you want topped up before a test. It is slower than a jump start, but it actually refills the battery.

Which one should a workshop buy first?

  • Jump starter first if you do call-outs, move cars around the yard, or start flat cars all day. It saves the time of connecting a second vehicle.
  • Charger first if batteries sit in the workshop waiting for a proper recharge, or you service cars with batteries that need topping up before a test.
  • Both if you do a mix of the two, which is most workshops.

What should you check before you buy?

  • Battery type and voltage whether it suits the 12V cars you see, and whether you also need to handle other battery types.
  • Starting power the peak current against the engines you start. Check what the maker says for your cars.
  • Number of starts per charge how many jump starts you can do before you recharge the unit.
  • Recharge time how long the unit takes to be ready again, and how it charges.
  • Clamps and cable whether they are in good condition and long enough for the bay.
  • Battery types the charger supports for example AGM, GEL or EFB if your customers have them.

Questions

Is a jump starter the same as a battery charger?

No. A jump starter gives a flat battery a short burst so the engine can start. A charger refills the battery slowly over a longer time. Many workshops keep both.

Can a jump starter charge a car battery?

It is built to start the engine, not to recharge the battery. After a jump start the car's own charging system or a proper charger needs to restore the charge.

Does a workshop need both a jump starter and a charger?

Most do. The jump starter handles flat-battery call-outs and quick moves in the bay, and the charger handles batteries that need a proper recharge before they go back to the customer.
