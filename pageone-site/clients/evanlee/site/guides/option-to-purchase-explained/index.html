The Option to Purchase, explained

What the Option to Purchase (OTP) is in a Singapore property sale, the option fee, the exercise period, and what happens if a buyer does not exercise it.

In Singapore, most resale property deals are made with an Option to Purchase (OTP). It is the document that turns an agreed price into a commitment.

What it is

The seller grants the buyer an option to buy the property at the agreed price, for a fixed period. The buyer pays an option fee in return. During the option period the seller cannot sell to anyone else.

Exercising the option

If the buyer decides to go ahead, they sign the acceptance and pay the exercise fee within the option period. The deal is then binding on both sides, and stamp duty becomes payable within 14 days.

If the buyer does not exercise

The option lapses at the end of the period, and the seller usually keeps the option fee. Buyers should arrange their loan approval and valuation before the period runs out.

HDB and private property differ

HDB resale transactions use HDB's own OTP form with set amounts and periods. Private property OTPs are prepared by the seller's side, and the option fee and exercise period are agreed between the parties. Read every OTP carefully, or have Evan and your lawyer go through it with you.
