EFB vs AGM vs standard car battery: which does your car need?

QUICK ANSWER
A start-stop car (the engine switches off at traffic lights) needs the battery type it came with: EFB or AGM. A car without start-stop uses a standard battery. Delkor EFB lists from $196.20 to $272.50 and AGM from $250.70 to $468.70; the smallest standard is $119.90.

By Battery Rescue SG (Motoserv) · Last updated 2 Nov 2026 · First published 26 Oct 2026

Standard (flooded / maintenance-free)

Most Japanese and Korean cars without start-stop. Delkor examples: NS40ZL $99.90 walk-in, NS60L $110.80.

EFB (Enhanced Flooded Battery)

Many start-stop Japanese cars and some European cars. Delkor examples: EFB Q85L $208.90, EFB S95 $252.50 walk-in.

AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat)

Most European cars with start-stop and many premium models. One battery maker's guide says plainly for Audi and BMW: use only AGM where AGM was fitted from the factory. Delkor examples: AGM LN3 $317.90, AGM LN4 $394.20 walk-in.

Look at the label on your current battery (it says EFB or AGM), or type your car into our finder.

Same case size matters

A battery has to physically fit the tray and reach the terminals. That is why the finder works by size code and case size, not just by model name. In a few cases the nearest Delkor in the same case is an AGM, which is an upgrade on a standard battery: for example the finder pairs DIN80L with AGM LN4 and DIN100L with AGM LN5.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between EFB, AGM and a standard car battery?

A standard (flooded, maintenance-free) battery suits most Japanese and Korean cars without start-stop. EFB (Enhanced Flooded Battery) is used in many start-stop Japanese cars and some European cars. AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat) is used in most European start-stop cars and many premium models.

Can I fit a standard battery in a start-stop car?

We do not recommend it. A standard battery in a start-stop car shortens its life and can upset the car's charging system. Replace like with like: EFB with EFB, AGM with AGM.

How do I check which type my car has?

Look at the label on your current battery, which says EFB or AGM, or type your car into our finder. If the label is unreadable, WhatsApp us your car model and year.
