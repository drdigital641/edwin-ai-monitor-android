Salons and studios

Salon and Beauty Studio Fit-Out in Singapore

Salon and beauty studio fit-outs centre on workstations, mirrors, concealed storage, a welcoming reception and flattering lighting. This page suits owners of hair salons, nail bars, lash and facial studios who want a practical, easy-to-clean interior and a clear picture of the services involved.

What do salons and beauty studios need from their space?

Salons and studios need stations or treatment areas that suit the service, storage that keeps products out of sight, a reception that welcomes clients, and lighting that looks good in mirrors. Surfaces must also cope with products, water, heat styling tools and frequent cleaning.

A hair salon needs mirrors, chairs and basin positions. A nail bar needs comfortable tables and good seating. A lash or facial studio needs quiet treatment rooms with adjustable lighting. The mix of fixtures differs, so start from the services you offer.

Typical scope mix

Common elements in a salon or studio project include:

  • Station joinery: mirror frames, shelves, drawers and product storage under counters. See commercial carpentry.
  • Reception counter and retail shelves: a desk for bookings and payment, with a small display area for products.
  • Partitions and treatment rooms: private spaces separated from the main floor.
  • Electrical: power points at each station for dryers, tools and chargers, and appropriate lighting circuits.
  • Flooring and wall finishes: easy-clean surfaces that handle liquids and cleaning.
  • AVL: relaxing background music, a screen for menus or promotions, and warm mirror or ambient lighting.

Plumbing and basin installation are specialist matters that should be identified at the site assessment stage.

Station and layout considerations

These are starting points to confirm on site with your stylists, therapists and equipment.

  • Station spacing: allow enough room for chairs to turn, the stylist to move around the client and a trolley to sit nearby.
  • Mirror height: position mirrors so both seated and standing clients see themselves clearly.
  • Power: place sockets near each station, with cables hidden behind or within joinery to avoid trailing leads.
  • Wash area: keep it away from the entrance and from quiet treatment rooms, if possible.
  • Waiting zone: clients appreciate a small seating area where they can see the work in progress or relax.
  • Treatment rooms: provide privacy and soft lighting, with enough room for a bed and a therapist to move.

Concealed storage and surfaces that last

Products, towels and tools quickly create visual clutter. Concealed cabinets, drawers beneath mirrors and tall cupboards behind partitions keep stations tidy and help a small studio feel calm.

Surface choices should lean towards materials that handle moisture, colour products and cleaning agents. Laminate and solid-surface tops are widely used, while veneer and timber elements add warmth when used away from heavy splash zones. Rounded edges and sealed joints help cleaning.

Think about colour too. Hair dye and nail products can stain pale surfaces, so test samples, ask about care and consider darker or patterned finishes where staining is likely.

Floors should be easy to sweep and mop, with a surface that copes with hair, product spills and the weight of rolling chairs.

Lighting, mirrors and sound

Lighting in a salon has two jobs: it helps the work, and it flatters the client. Balanced, even light around mirrors reduces harsh shadows, while warm ambient light makes the whole room feel relaxed. Dimmable circuits are useful in treatment rooms.

Pair this with background audio chosen for the mood you want, with speakers placed so sound is even rather than loud in one corner. Treatment rooms may suit their own volume control. A wall screen can display service menus or promotions, see display and visual systems.

Cost and timing factors

Typical drivers include the number of stations and rooms, the amount of custom joinery, mirror size and quality, and the number of power points. Existing floor and wall condition, and whether partitions or plumbing changes are needed, can significantly affect scope.

Timing is shaped by material lead times, how many trades work in sequence and access rules in your building or mall. Opening dates are important for appointment-based businesses, so share yours early.

Booking systems and staff rosters make long closures costly, so ask whether works can be staged, such as completing treatment rooms first, to limit lost trading days.

Check with your landlord or mall management about approvals, permitted working hours and contractor rules.

What to prepare

An R&C project is planned to follow a conversation about your scope, a site assessment, a layout and material proposal and coordinated works. Some works may need submissions by qualified professionals; these should be identified at the site assessment stage.

  1. A floor plan and photos of the unit.
  2. Number of stations, chairs, basins or treatment beds.
  3. A list of electrical equipment used at each station.
  4. Storage needs for products, towels and tools.
  5. Style references, brand colours and your intended opening date.

The wider approach is described under design and build.

Frequently asked questions

Can stations be built to fit my stylists' equipment?

Yes. Share what each station must hold, such as dryers, tools, chargers and products, and the joinery can be planned with matching shelves, drawers and power access. Confirm sizes with your stylists before drawings are finalised.

How can I hide cables at hair stations?

Sockets can be placed behind or within the joinery, with cable routes planned before mirrors and panels are fixed. This avoids trailing leads and keeps the station tidy. It needs agreement early because it affects carpentry and electrical work.

What surfaces suit a busy salon?

Laminate and solid-surface tops are common for wet and product-heavy zones, and floors should be easy to clean. Choose for moisture, chemical contact and cleaning routine first, then for appearance.

Can a small studio feel spacious?

Light colours, mirrors, concealed storage and layered lighting all help. Careful layout matters most: avoid blocking circulation, and keep the entrance and reception tidy so the room reads as calm.

Do you handle plumbing for basins?

Plumbing and basin installation are specialist matters. They should be identified at the site assessment stage so the right professionals can be involved, and so joinery and flooring plans allow for them.
