Painting & finishing

Commercial Painting and Finishing for Shops and Offices

Commercial painting and finishing covers wall and ceiling painting, feature walls, surface finishes, touch-ups and the finishing work needed for lease-end reinstatement. It suits Singapore shops, offices, showrooms, cafes and salons that want a clean, consistent finish after other trades, or a quick refresh.

Who commercial painting and finishing suits

Paint and finishes are the most visible part of a fit-out and one of the easiest ways to refresh a space. They suit businesses at several stages.

  • New tenants finishing a unit after partitions, ceilings and joinery are in.
  • Shop owners wanting a refresh, rebrand or feature wall.
  • Offices and showrooms needing consistent walls and quick touch-ups.
  • Tenants nearing lease end who need surfaces returned to a neutral state.

Problems it addresses include scuffed walls, patchy colours, uneven repairs and joinery that looks unfinished beside bare walls. Many shop owners search for shop carpentry and painting together because the finish is where both trades meet.

Because paint is the final layer, it also exposes the quality of everything before it. Planning the painting stage alongside joinery, flooring and lighting avoids repeated touch-ups and keeps the finished space looking consistent.

What does a painting and finishing scope usually include?

A painting scope usually covers protecting floors and fixtures, preparing surfaces, applying primer and coats, and cleaning up. Surface preparation is normally the largest share of the effort, because paint shows whatever is underneath, so the scope should describe the preparation as clearly as the number of coats.

Paint types, sheen and finish: trade-offs

The paint you choose affects how the space looks, how well it handles cleaning and how visible marks and repairs will be.

  • Matt: hides wall imperfections and looks soft; marks can be harder to wipe off.
  • Satin or semi-gloss: easier to clean and tougher, but shows surface flaws and touch-up patches.
  • Colour depth: dark colours look rich but show scuffs and need more coats; pale colours feel larger and are easier to touch up.
  • Specialist finishes: textured or decorative coatings add character but are harder to repair seamlessly.

Ask for painted samples on your own wall and check them under your lighting at different times of day.

How do feature walls work in a commercial space?

A feature wall draws attention to one area, such as behind a counter, at an entrance or at a display. It works well where customers naturally look, and when its colour or texture relates to your brand and the surrounding finishes rather than competing with them.

  • Choose one focal wall rather than several.
  • Check how lighting will fall on texture or deep colour.
  • Allow for fixtures, signage and shelves that will cover part of the wall.
  • Consider how easily it can be repainted or reinstated at lease end.

Feature walls and lighting work together, so see feature lighting if you plan to highlight them.

What drives the painting budget?

Painting cost is driven mainly by surface area, the condition of walls and ceilings, the amount of preparation, the number of coats, the paint type, and difficulty of access, such as high ceilings or limited working hours. Colour changes and specialist finishes can add effort as well.

  • Extent of patching and repair before painting.
  • Colour changes, especially dark to light.
  • Specialist or textured finishes.
  • Protection of existing fixtures and floors.
  • Working at heights or around installed joinery.

No figures are quoted because they depend on your unit and finish.

Planning, sequencing and touch-ups

Painting usually comes late in the sequence, after dusty and heavy work, yet before delicate fixtures are installed. Timing depends on scope, preparation, drying between coats and access. No timeline is promised.

  • Finish ceilings and wall repairs before painting begins.
  • Confirm colours early so supply does not hold up the programme.
  • Protect new flooring and joinery during painting.
  • Plan a touch-up round after installation of fixtures, as moving items often leaves marks.

Coordinate with flooring and carpentry so each trade finishes cleanly before the next begins.

Coordination, site access and reinstatement

Check with your landlord or mall management about approvals, permitted working hours and contractor rules. Painting involves odour and materials in shared buildings, so confirm what is allowed and when.

For lease-end reinstatement, painting often restores walls and ceilings to the colour and condition your tenancy requires. Read your lease for the required colour and finish, keep a record of the original condition, and choose feature colours with repainting in mind.

Common painting mistakes to avoid

Paint faults tend to appear within weeks of opening. Most start with preparation rather than with the paint itself.

  • Painting over damp, dusty or unrepaired surfaces.
  • Choosing a colour from a small chip instead of a painted sample.
  • Selecting a matt finish for walls that customers brush against.
  • Painting before dusty trades have finished.
  • Skipping the touch-up round after fixtures are installed.
  • Choosing a bold colour with no plan for repainting at lease end.

Keep a record of the exact paint names and colour codes used, so touch-ups and reinstatement can match later.

The intended process and what to prepare

An R&C painting project is planned to follow these steps:

  1. Discuss colours, finishes and any reinstatement requirements.
  2. Inspect surfaces and agree preparation.
  3. Review colour and finish samples on site.
  4. Sequence painting with other trades and protect completed work.
  5. Apply, inspect and complete touch-ups before handover.

Frequently asked questions

Can painting and carpentry be planned together?

Yes, and it is usually sensible. Joinery needs to be finished or protected before walls are painted, and painted surfaces need touching up after installation. Planning both together helps avoid damage and extra rework.

What finish is easiest to maintain in a busy shop?

Satin or semi-gloss finishes are generally easier to wipe clean than matt, though they show wall imperfections more. Discuss samples and your cleaning routine, and consider tougher finishes in high-contact areas like counters and entrances.

Do I need to repaint when my lease ends?

It depends on your lease terms. Many tenancies require walls to be returned to a specified colour and condition. Read your agreement early so your colour choices and finishes can be planned with reinstatement in mind.

Can you paint over existing wallpaper or damaged walls?

It depends on the surface. Damaged or uneven walls normally need repair before painting, and wallpaper may need removal. A site check tells you what preparation is needed, since poor preparation shows through the finished paint.

Are touch-ups included after other trades finish?

A touch-up round after fixtures and equipment are installed is usually advisable, because moving items marks walls. Confirm in your scope that it is included, and when it will happen in the sequence.
