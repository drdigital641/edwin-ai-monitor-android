How to identify a car A/C compressor before ordering

Mega Power · draft for review by Mega Power's technical team · 29 Oct 2026

Read the label on the compressor body first. It usually carries the maker (for example Denso, Sanden or Valeo) and a part number. Then note the car's make, model, year and engine, and the refrigerant type shown on the label under the bonnet. Send all of these with your enquiry.

Where to find the part number

Most compressors carry a sticker or stamped plate on the body with the manufacturer's name and part number. A vehicle maker (OEM) number may also appear. Clean the label gently before taking a photo; do not scrape it.

What else identifies the compressor

  • Refrigerant type: most cars use R134a or R1234yf. The type is on a label under the bonnet.
  • The make, model, year and engine of the car.
  • The VIN or chassis number, which pins down the exact build.
  • A photo of the whole compressor, including the pulley, the pipe connections and the electrical plug.

Why the same model can use different compressors

One model can be built with different compressors for different engines, markets or years. Our own listings show this: the Volkswagen Golf 5 appears twice, once with a Denso Europe compressor (DCP32060) and once with a Denso Japan compressor (437100-8011). That is why we check the chassis number before quoting.

Sending the enquiry

  1. Search the catalogue by the part number on the label.
  2. If there is no exact match, search by vehicle.
  3. Add the part to your enquiry list, or send the chassis number if you are unsure.
