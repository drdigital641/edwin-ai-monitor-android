Why A/C part OEM numbers differ between markets

Mega Power · draft for review by Mega Power's technical team · 29 Oct 2026

A part can carry several numbers: the vehicle maker's OEM number, the part maker's own number and cross-references used by resellers. The same car model sold in different markets may also be built with different suppliers' parts, so one model can map to more than one number.

The three kinds of number

  • OEM number: the vehicle maker's number for the part.
  • Manufacturer part number: the part maker's own number, such as a Denso or Sanden reference.
  • Cross-reference: another number said to fit the same application. These need checking before you rely on them.

Why numbers change by market

Car makers may source a part from different suppliers for different factories or regions, and specifications change between model years. Refrigerant is one example: the European Union's mobile air-conditioning directive (2006/40/EC) moved new cars to lower-impact refrigerants such as R1234yf, so parts for the same model may differ by year and market.

How our search handles it

Our search ignores spaces, dashes and capitals, so 4M0 816 421B and 4M0816421B find the same part. It labels results as an exact reference match or a possible match, and never treats an unchecked cross-reference as interchangeable. Our team confirms fitment before quoting.
