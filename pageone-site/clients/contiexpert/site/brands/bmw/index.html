Why is my BMW check engine light on?

A check engine light means the engine management system has stored a fault. Common areas include ignition parts, sensors, small air or vacuum leaks and emissions-related components.

A steady amber light should be checked soon. If it flashes, or the engine runs roughly, stop safely and avoid driving further. Our guide on the BMW check engine light in Singapore goes into more detail.

A code is a starting point. We read it, then test the related parts, such as coils, spark plugs, sensors and hoses, so the repair we quote matches the actual fault and not just the message on the screen.

Why is my BMW aircon blowing warm air?

Warm air often comes from low refrigerant caused by a leak, but it can also be a compressor or clutch issue, a blocked condenser, a failed fan or a fault in the climate control. Topping up gas without finding the leak is usually a short-term answer.

Humid weather and stop-start traffic put steady load on the aircon, so cooling problems tend to show up quickly here. We test pressures, check for leaks and read the codes before explaining the options. See our car aircon repair page for the process.

How often should I service my BMW?

Follow your car's service indicator and handbook. BMW's condition-based system tells you when items are due, so the timing depends on how you drive.

If you want to understand what a service involves, see European car servicing. Brake pads, discs and tyres are checked at the same time, so you hear about wear before it becomes a safety issue. For gearbox fluid and shifting concerns, see gearbox and transmission service.
