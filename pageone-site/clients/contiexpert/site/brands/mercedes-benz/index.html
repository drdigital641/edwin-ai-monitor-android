Why is my Mercedes engine overheating?

Overheating usually points to the cooling system, such as a coolant leak, a weak water pump, a faulty thermostat, a failing fan or a blocked radiator. Low coolant is often the sign of a leak somewhere.

If the temperature warning appears, stop safely as soon as you can and switch off the engine. Do not open the cap on a hot system. Driving on can cause serious engine damage. Once the engine has cooled, we look at coolant level, hoses, the water pump, thermostat and fan to find where the problem sits. Our guide on Mercedes engine overheating in Singapore covers it in more detail.

What do warning lights on a Mercedes mean?

Warning lights come from the car's control units. An amber light usually means a fault has been stored and should be checked soon. A red light, such as for oil pressure or coolant, is more urgent.

We read the codes with a diagnostic tool, then test the related parts so we do not replace anything on a guess. See our diagnostics service.

How often should I service my Mercedes?

Follow your car's service indicator and the handbook. Modern Mercedes models calculate service timing from how the car is used, so two cars of the same age can be due at different times.

We can explain what a service on your model typically includes, and our guide to the European car service schedule gives general background. For the service itself, see European car servicing.
