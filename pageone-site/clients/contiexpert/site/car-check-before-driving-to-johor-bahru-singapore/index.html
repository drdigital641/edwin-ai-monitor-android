Car Check Before Driving to Johor Bahru: What to Look at First

Updated 11 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By the ContiExpert by Volt Autocare team, Newton Circus

Before a drive to Johor Bahru, check tyre pressure and condition, engine oil and coolant levels, brakes, battery, lights, wipers and aircon, and make sure no warning light is on. Do it a few days ahead so there is time to fix anything. If a warning light is on or the car feels different, book a workshop check before you go.

Why check the car before a Johor Bahru drive?

A drive across the Causeway or the Second Link mixes stop-go queues at the checkpoints with faster highway stretches. That is a hard mix for brakes, the cooling system, the battery and the aircon, and a breakdown in a queue or on a highway is stressful and costly. A short check a few days before leaves time to fix small things calmly.

None of these checks replaces a service. If the car is due for one, do the service first. Our European car service schedule guide explains how to read the maker's intervals.

What are the ten checks?

  1. Tyre pressure: check all four, cold, against the figure on the door-jamb label or in the handbook, and adjust for a full load if the label shows one.
  2. Tyre condition: look for cracks, bulges, nails and uneven wear. Replace tyres that are near the wear indicators before a long trip.
  3. Spare or repair kit: many European cars carry a sealant kit or a space-saver, or run-flat tyres. Know which yours has and that it is complete.
  4. Engine oil level: check it on level ground and top up only with the grade your handbook specifies.
  5. Coolant level: check the expansion tank when the engine is cold. A level that keeps falling points to a leak, so have it looked at.
  6. Brakes: note any squeal, pulsing or a longer pedal. Stop-go queues use the brakes a lot.
  7. Battery: slow cranking, start-stop that stops working, or an older battery are reasons to test it first.
  8. Lights and wipers: check headlights, brake lights, indicators and the wiper blades.
  9. Aircon: it should blow cold within a few minutes. Weak cooling is better found now than in a queue.
  10. Warning lights: the dash should show none after start-up. Do not ignore a light because the car still drives.

Which of these can you do yourself, and which need a workshop?

Tyre pressure, a visual tyre check, oil and coolant levels, lights and wipers are quick to do at home or at a petrol station. Brake noise, a weak aircon, a falling coolant level, battery doubts and any warning light need a proper look, because the cause is not visible from outside.

If a warning light is on, a diagnostic scan reads the codes the car has stored, so the cause can be found before you go. See our car diagnostics and warning lights page for how that works.

What should you carry in the car?

  • The car handbook, and the roadside assistance number from your insurer or the maker.
  • A charged phone and a charging cable.
  • The tyre sealant kit or the jack and tools that came with the car.
  • A warning triangle and a torch.
  • Drinking water, as the cabin gets hot if you are stuck in a queue.

Check the current checkpoint and vehicle entry requirements on the official Singapore and Malaysia sites before you travel, as these are separate from the car's mechanical condition.

Questions owners ask

What should I check on my car before driving to Johor Bahru?

Tyre pressure and condition, engine oil and coolant levels, brakes, battery, lights, wipers, aircon, and that no warning light is on. Do the checks a few days before so there is time to fix anything.

Do I need a service before a long drive?

Only if the car is due for one by the maker's schedule or has symptoms such as brake noise or a falling coolant level. These checks do not replace a service.

Can I drive to Johor Bahru with a warning light on?

It is best not to. Some lights are minor and some are not, and a breakdown at the checkpoint or on the highway is costly. Have the car scanned first so the cause is known.
