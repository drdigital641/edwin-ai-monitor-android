Pre-Purchase Car Inspection in Singapore: Buy With Confidence

Updated 5 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By the ContiExpert by Volt Autocare team, Newton Circus

A pre-purchase inspection is an independent check of a used car before you commit. It looks at body, engine, gearbox, suspension, brakes, tyres, electronics and service records, plus a test drive. Book it before paying a deposit so you can decide with facts.

What is a pre-purchase car inspection?

It is a structured check of a used car done by a workshop on your behalf. The aim is to find faults, past damage and upcoming costs before you buy, not after.

Photos and a short test drive show you how a car looks and feels. An inspection shows what is underneath, with the car lifted, scanned and examined part by part.

It is useful for any used purchase, and especially for European cars, where electronic systems and complex components can be costly to repair.

The report also gives you something concrete to talk about with the seller. Instead of arguing about a feeling, you can point to a worn part, a stored fault or a sign of repair and ask how it will be dealt with.

What is checked in a pre-purchase inspection?

AreaWhat the check looks for
Body and paintDents, rust, panel gaps, mismatched paint

GearboxShift quality, delays, fluid leaks

The exact scope depends on the car and the inspection you choose, so ask what is included before booking.

A visual check of the underbody on a lift is especially valuable. Leaks, damaged mounts, corroded parts and signs of impact are often only visible from below, and they are easy for a seller to leave out of a conversation.

What extra checks apply to hybrid and electric cars?

Hybrid and EV purchases have an additional concern: the high-voltage battery. Its state of health affects range, performance and future cost, and it cannot be judged by looking at the car.

A hybrid or EV inspection adds a battery state-of-health check, high-voltage system checks and a scan of the related control modules, alongside the usual body and mechanical checks.

The battery result should be explained in plain terms, including what it suggests for range or performance and what to ask the seller. Because replacement can be a significant expense, it is worth understanding before you commit.

When should you book the inspection?

Book it before you pay a deposit. Once money has changed hands, walking away can be difficult or costly, and the seller may have less reason to accommodate checks.

Here is a sensible order to follow:

  1. Shortlist a car after viewing and a first test drive.
  2. Ask the seller or dealer whether you may have the car inspected by a workshop of your choice.
  3. Book the inspection and agree a time that suits the seller.
  4. Read the findings and ask questions.
  5. Decide whether to proceed, negotiate or walk away.
  6. Only then pay a deposit and sign.

A seller who refuses any independent inspection is giving you information too.

Timing also matters in Singapore's market. Good cars move fast, so tell the seller early that you will need an inspection slot, and be ready to book quickly. A short delay is usually acceptable, and a seller with a sound car rarely minds.

What do you receive after the inspection?

You should come away with findings you can understand, not just a list of codes. A useful result includes:

  • What was checked and what was found, area by area.
  • Which issues are urgent, which are minor, and which can wait.
  • Signs of previous accident repair or neglected maintenance.
  • Any stored fault codes from the electronics scan.
  • A plain explanation of what the findings may mean for ownership.

Use the findings to negotiate, to ask the seller to rectify items, or to step away. The decision stays yours.

It helps to decide in advance what would make you walk away. For many buyers it is signs of structural repair or a significant unexplained fault. Having that line in mind makes the conversation after the report easier.

What else should you check when buying in Singapore?

An inspection works alongside other checks:

  • Ownership documents, COE expiry and the car's registration history.
  • The car's inspection status, as older cars need periodic LTA-mandated inspection.
  • Whether there are outstanding loans or recalls.
  • Cooling and aircon performance, which matter in Singapore's heat. Our guides on Audi aircon problems and engine overheating explain the warning signs.

Also think about running costs: a car near the end of its COE life, or with deferred maintenance, may need work soon after purchase.

Consider where the car has been kept, too. A car that has lived in a covered carpark will often look different from one left in the sun for years, and flood or water exposure, though uncommon, is worth asking about directly.

Is an inspection worth it for a car sold by a dealer?

Yes, it still gives you a separate view. A dealer's own checks serve the dealer's sale, while an independent report is prepared for you.

The same applies to cars sold privately. The more expensive or complex the car, the more value an independent look tends to add. A short, calm checklist before buying is far easier than a surprise afterwards.

Questions owners ask

Should I book the inspection before or after paying a deposit?

Before. Once a deposit is paid, backing out can be difficult. Arrange the inspection first so that the findings can inform your decision, your negotiation or your choice to walk away.

Can a pre-purchase inspection find accident damage?

It can often find signs of previous repair, such as repainted panels, uneven gaps or welds. It cannot always prove the full history, so use the findings alongside the car's documents and records.

Is there a different inspection for hybrid and electric cars?

Yes. Hybrid and EV inspections add a battery state-of-health check and high-voltage system checks to the usual mechanical and body checks, since battery condition strongly affects the car's value and future running costs.

What if the seller will not allow an independent inspection?

That is a reason for caution. A reasonable seller usually accepts a short inspection at an agreed time. If access is refused without a good reason, consider whether to continue with the purchase.
