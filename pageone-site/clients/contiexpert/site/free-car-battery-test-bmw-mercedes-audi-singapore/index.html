What does a car battery test check?

A battery test measures the battery's condition: how much charge it holds and how much starting current it can still deliver. A good test also looks at the charging system, because a battery that keeps going flat is sometimes a charging fault, not a worn battery.

  • Battery condition: whether it can still hold charge and start the engine reliably.
  • Charging output: whether the alternator is charging the battery properly while the engine runs.
  • Stored faults: a scan can show battery or charging messages the car has logged.

Ask the technician to explain the result in plain words, so you know whether the battery is fine, weak or due for replacement.

When should you get your battery tested?

Do not wait for a car that will not start. These signs are worth a quick test:

  • The engine cranks more slowly than it used to, especially on a cold start after the car has sat.
  • Start-stop stops working, or works less often than before.
  • Battery or charging warning lights or messages appear on the dash.
  • Headlights or interior lights dim at idle, or electrical features act up.
  • The battery is several years old. Heat shortens battery life, and many last only a few years in Singapore.
  • The car sits unused for weeks at a time.

One of these signs does not prove the battery is the problem, which is why testing first beats guessing.

What happens if the test says the battery needs replacing?

We fit and register batteries on BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Audi. Registration matters because many of these cars track the battery and adjust charging to suit it, so a new battery should be registered with a diagnostic tool. Our guide to BMW and Mercedes battery replacement and registration explains why.

The replacement should match the type your car was built for. Cars with start-stop usually need an AGM or EFB battery rather than a standard one, matched in size and terminal layout to the original.

What if the battery tests fine but the car still struggles?

A healthy battery with a car that still will not start or keeps going flat points elsewhere: a charging fault, a drain that runs when the car is off, a loose or corroded connection, or a starter problem. Further diagnosis finds the real cause before parts are replaced. See our car diagnostics and warning lights service, or the European car servicing page if the car is also due for a service.

How should you prepare before you visit?

  1. Note the symptoms: when the car is slow to start, and any dash messages.
  2. Know roughly when the battery was last replaced.
  3. Avoid repeated jump-starts before the visit, and turn off the air-conditioning and lights when you try to start.
  4. Tell us your make, model and year so we know what the car needs.

How do I know if my battery is weak?

Slow cranking, start-stop that stops working, battery warnings, dimming lights, or an age of several years are common signs. A test confirms it, because a charging fault or a drain can cause the same symptoms.

Can a good battery still leave my car not starting?

Yes. A charging fault, a drain while parked, a poor connection or a starter problem can stop a car with a healthy battery. That is why the charging system and the wider car are checked, not just the battery.
