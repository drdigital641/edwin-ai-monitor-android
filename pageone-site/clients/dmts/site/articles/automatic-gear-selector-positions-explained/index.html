What Do P, R, N, D, S, L and M Mean on an Automatic Gear Lever?

What P, R, N, D, S, L, 2, 1, M and B mean on an automatic gear lever, when to use the low and manual positions, and what to avoid so the gearbox lasts.

By Jacky Gearbox · 5 November 2026

In short: P is Park, R is Reverse, N is Neutral and D is Drive, and these four are on every automatic. S, L, 2, 1 and M are extra positions that let you hold a lower gear or choose gears yourself, for example on a steep descent or when overtaking. Exact names and behaviour differ by make and model, so the owner manual is the final word for your car.

What do P, R, N and D mean?

  • P (Park): locks the gearbox output so the car cannot roll. Stop the car fully, press the brake, then select P. Use the handbrake as well on a slope.
  • R (Reverse): drives the car backwards. Change into it only when the car is stopped.
  • N (Neutral): disconnects the engine from the wheels. The car can roll freely, which is why it is used for towing on a flatbed and for some car washes. Check the manual for your car before you tow or wash it in N.
  • D (Drive): the normal forward position. The gearbox chooses the gear for you.

What do S, L, 2 and 1 mean?

These positions limit the gearbox to lower gears. The letters differ by car: S usually means Sport, which holds each gear for longer, while L, 2 and 1 usually mean Low, which keeps the car in the lowest gear or gears. Lower gears give stronger engine braking and more pulling power at low speed, but the engine revs higher. Use them when the manual says so, such as a long downhill road, and go back to D afterwards.

What does M or the + and - sign mean?

M is manual mode. You move the lever, or use paddles behind the steering wheel, to ask for a higher gear (+) or a lower one (-). The car still protects itself, so it may change gear by itself at a very high or very low engine speed. Manual mode is useful on a long descent or when overtaking, and it is not a switch that turns the automatic into a manual gearbox.

What does B mean on a hybrid?

Many hybrids have a B position. It increases engine braking and battery charging when you lift off the accelerator, which is handy downhill. It is for temporary use in the situations your manual describes.

Can you shift positions while the car is moving?

Moving between D, S, L and M while driving is normal, because the gearbox is designed for it. Moving between D and R, or into P, while the car is still rolling is not. Come to a complete stop with your foot on the brake first. This is one of the habits covered in what driving habits damage an automatic gearbox.

What if the lever will not move or the car will not go?

A lever that will not leave P, or a car that does not move in D or R, is a fault to check rather than a position to try again and again. See lever stuck in Park, car stuck in gear and car will not move in Drive or Reverse. If the gearbox is limiting the gears by itself, read gearbox limp mode.

Does using the low gears or manual mode wear the gearbox?

Used for what they are made for, no. They are part of the design. Problems come from forcing a change at the wrong moment, holding high revs for long periods, or running on old oil. Keeping the gearbox oil in good condition helps, see how often to change gearbox oil.

Questions

What do D, S and L mean on an automatic car?
D is Drive, the normal forward position. S usually means Sport, which holds gears longer. L usually means Low, which keeps a low gear for strong engine braking on steep hills. Names vary by car, so check your owner manual.
Is it safe to shift from D to S or L while driving?
Usually yes, because the gearbox is designed for it, and the car may delay the change if the engine speed would be too high. Follow your owner manual for the speeds it allows.
Should I use M or L when going downhill?
Many owner manuals suggest a lower gear on long descents, because engine braking reduces the load on the brakes. Use the position your manual describes and go back to D on level road.
What does B mean on a hybrid gear lever?
B usually means a stronger engine braking and regenerative mode, useful downhill. It is for temporary use, and the manual explains when to use it in your model.
