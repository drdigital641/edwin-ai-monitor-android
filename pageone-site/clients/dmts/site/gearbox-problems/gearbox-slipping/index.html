Gearbox Slipping: Engine Revs but the Car Doesn’t Speed Up

You press the accelerator, the engine revs climb, but the car is slow to pick up speed, or the gear seems to slip out for a moment.

Common causes

These are the usual reasons for this symptom in an automatic gearbox. More than one can be involved, which is why we diagnose before quoting.

  • Low or burnt transmission fluid (ATF)
  • Worn clutch packs or bands inside the gearbox
  • Torque converter problems
  • Shift solenoid or valve body faults

What to do now

  • Check the floor where you park for red fluid stains
  • Drive gently and avoid hard acceleration and highways
  • Book a diagnostic early: slipping wears the gearbox faster the longer it goes on

Why it pays to check early

An automatic gearbox rarely fixes itself. A fault that starts as a sensor, a solenoid or low fluid can, if the car keeps being driven, wear the clutches and other internal parts, and a simple repair can turn into a full overhaul. Finding the cause early usually means a smaller, cheaper fix, which is why we diagnose first and quote before any work starts.

Questions about this problem

Can a slipping gearbox be fixed without an overhaul?
Sometimes. If the cause is low or old fluid, or a faulty solenoid, a service or a smaller repair may be enough. Worn clutch packs usually need an overhaul. Jacky tests the gearbox first and tells you which it is before any work starts.
