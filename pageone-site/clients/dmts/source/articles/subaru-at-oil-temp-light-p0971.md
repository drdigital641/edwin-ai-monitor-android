A Subaru Forester came to Jacky with the **AT Oil Temp** light on the dashboard. Many owners hear "gearbox light" and expect a full overhaul. In this case the scan pointed to one part, a **pressure control solenoid in the valve body**, and the car was put right by repairing that instead of overhauling the whole gearbox.

## What does the AT Oil Temp light mean on a Subaru?

On Subaru cars the AT Oil Temp light has two jobs. When it stays on, it usually warns that the gearbox fluid is running too hot. When it flashes after you start the car, it usually means the gearbox control unit has found a fault in the transmission system. Your owner's manual explains the light for your exact model, so check it, and in both cases have the gearbox checked soon rather than driving on.

## What the scanner showed

Jacky connected a diagnostic scanner to the Forester. The gearbox control unit had stored one code:

- **P0971: Pressure control solenoid "C" control circuit high**
- Status: **History**, meaning the fault was recorded by the control unit earlier, even if it was not active at that moment

A history code still matters. It tells you what the control unit saw when the light came on, and it is the starting point for testing, not the final answer.

## What is a pressure control solenoid?

An automatic gearbox, including the CVT used in most recent Subarus, is controlled by fluid pressure. The **valve body** is the part that directs that fluid, and electric **solenoids** on it open and close to set the pressure the gearbox needs. The control unit sends each solenoid a signal and checks what comes back.

"Control circuit high" means the reading on solenoid C's circuit was higher than the control unit expected. That can come from the solenoid itself, its wiring or connector, or the control unit. When a pressure solenoid misbehaves, the gearbox can shift or engage harshly, feel slow to respond, or go into [limp mode](/articles/gearbox-limp-mode/) to protect itself.

## Why this was a repair, not an overhaul

An overhaul means taking the gearbox apart and renewing its worn internal parts. It is the right job when the clutches, bands, belt or bearings inside are worn or damaged. It is not needed when the fault is in one control part and the rest of the gearbox is healthy.

Here the code, the testing and the road test all pointed to the valve body solenoid. So the workshop team repaired the solenoid fault, Jacky road-tested the car, and the customer did not pay for an overhaul the gearbox did not need. That is why the diagnosis comes first: it decides between a targeted repair and a bigger job. More on that choice in [gearbox overhaul vs replacement](/articles/gearbox-overhaul-vs-replacement/).

## Signs a solenoid or valve body may be the cause

- A warning light such as AT Oil Temp, or a check engine light
- Harsh or late engagement when you select Drive or Reverse
- Jerks, flare or hesitation when pulling away
- Limp mode, with the car holding one ratio and low power
- Symptoms that come and go, sometimes worse when the gearbox is hot

These signs can also come from low or old fluid, sensors or internal wear. Only testing tells them apart, so treat the list as a reason to check, not a diagnosis.

## What to do if your Subaru's AT Oil Temp light comes on

- **If it stays on:** stop somewhere safe and let the car cool. Do not keep driving hard or towing.
- **If it flashes:** drive gently to a workshop, or ask for a tow if the car jerks, slips or will not move.
- **Do not just clear the code.** The light may go off for a while, but the cause stays.
- **Get the fluid and codes checked.** Old or low fluid and a solenoid fault can show the same light.

## Questions
Q: What does a flashing AT Oil Temp light mean on a Subaru?
A: A flashing AT Oil Temp light usually means the gearbox control unit has found a fault in the transmission system, while a steady light usually warns of high fluid temperature. Check your owner's manual and have the gearbox tested.
Q: What does code P0971 mean?
A: P0971 means "pressure control solenoid C control circuit high". The control unit saw a higher than expected reading on that solenoid's circuit, which can come from the solenoid, its wiring or the control unit.
Q: Does a P0971 code mean my gearbox needs an overhaul?
A: Not always. In this Subaru Forester case the fault was the valve body solenoid, and it was repaired without a full overhaul. Testing decides whether the problem is one part or internal wear.
Q: Can I keep driving with the AT Oil Temp light on?
A: Only gently and as far as a safe place or workshop. If the car jerks, slips or will not move, stop and ask for a tow so a small fault does not become a bigger repair.
