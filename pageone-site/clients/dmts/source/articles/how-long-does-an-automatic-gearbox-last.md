**In short:** there is no single lifespan for an automatic gearbox. Makers do not promise a figure, and how long one lasts depends on its type, the oil it has run on, how hard it has worked and how the car is driven. A well-kept gearbox can run for a very long time, while a neglected or overworked one can fail early. The better question to ask is whether yours is showing warning signs, and what keeps it healthy.

## Why is there no fixed number?

Automatic, CVT and dual-clutch gearboxes are built differently, and each maker designs for its own targets. Two cars of the same model can end up with very different gearbox lives because of how they were used and looked after. For how the types differ, see [CVT, dual-clutch or automatic](/articles/cvt-dual-clutch-or-automatic-gearbox/). Be careful with any simple "lasts X kilometres" claim you read online: it is an average at best, not a promise for your car.

## What makes an automatic gearbox last longer?

- **The right oil, changed on time.** Gearbox oil (ATF) cools, lubricates and applies the clutches. Old or wrong oil is a common cause of early wear. Follow your car maker's schedule for your gearbox type. More in our [ATF guide](/articles/how-often-change-atf-singapore/).
- **Gentle driving habits.** Rocking between Drive and Reverse before the car has stopped, or flooring the pedal from cold, adds wear. See [driving habits that damage an automatic gearbox](/articles/driving-habits-that-damage-automatic-gearbox/).
- **Early attention to small faults.** A leak, a jerk or a warning light is cheaper to deal with early than after the damage has spread.
- **Heat under control.** Stop-and-go traffic, towing and heavy loads make the oil hotter, and heat ages the fluid.

## What shortens its life?

- **Old, burnt, dirty or contaminated ATF.** Water or coolant in the oil is serious: see [milky or pink gearbox fluid](/articles/milky-or-pink-gearbox-fluid/).
- **Low oil from a leak.** Running low can overheat the gearbox and wear the clutches.
- **Driving on a fault.** Continuing to drive a slipping gearbox, or one in limp mode, can turn a small repair into a big one. See [gearbox limp mode](/articles/gearbox-limp-mode/).
- **Electrical or sensor faults left alone,** including a weak battery that makes the control unit store faults.
- **Extra load.** Towing or power upgrades beyond what the gearbox was built for.

## Which warning signs mean get it checked?

- **Slipping,** where the engine revs rise but the car does not pick up speed. See [gearbox slipping](/gearbox-problems/gearbox-slipping/).
- **Harsh, delayed or jerking shifts.** See [jerking when changing gear](/gearbox-problems/car-jerks-when-changing-gear/).
- **A transmission warning light.** See [transmission warning light](/gearbox-problems/transmission-warning-light/).
- **Oil leaks, a burning smell or a whining or clunking noise** that is new.
- **The car will not move** in Drive or Reverse.

None of these proves the gearbox is near the end of its life: low oil, a sensor or a solenoid can cause the same signs, and some of those are one-part repairs. A test is the only way to know.

## Questions
Q: How many kilometres does an automatic gearbox last?
A: There is no single figure. It depends on the gearbox type, the oil and how often it was changed, how the car is driven and whether faults were dealt with early. Follow your car maker's schedule, and have a gearbox that slips, jerks or shows a warning light tested.
Q: Do automatic gearboxes last longer than manual ones?
A: Not as a rule. Each type has its own wear points: clutches and the hydraulic system in an automatic, the clutch and gears in a manual. How well it is looked after matters more than the type.
Q: Is "lifetime" gearbox oil really for life?
A: Some makers describe the oil as filled for life, but that is the maker's wording for the car's expected service life, and heat and hard use can still age the oil. Follow your car maker's schedule, and ask for the oil to be checked if the gearbox shows problems.
Q: Can a high-mileage automatic gearbox still be repaired?
A: Often yes. Whether a repair makes sense depends on what has failed, how the rest of the gearbox looks and the value of the car. A diagnostic shows whether the fault is one part or internal wear.
