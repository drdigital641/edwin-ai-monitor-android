Automatic transmission fluid (ATF) does more than lubricate. It cools the gearbox, carries the hydraulic pressure that makes it shift, and protects the clutches. As it ages and heats up, it loses those properties, and the gearbox starts to shift less smoothly.

## The short answer

Follow your car maker's service schedule first. Many makers give an interval for the gearbox fluid, and some describe the fluid as "lifetime" or "sealed for life". In practice, most of Singapore's driving is what owner's manuals call **severe conditions**: short trips, heavy stop-start traffic and high temperatures. Under those conditions it is sensible to have the fluid checked at every major service and changed earlier than the normal-use interval.

## What "lifetime fluid" really means

A "lifetime" label usually refers to the expected life under normal conditions, often the length of the original warranty, not the whole life of the car. Fluid that is never changed will still age. If your car has passed its warranty period and the fluid has never been changed, ask for it to be checked.

## Signs the fluid needs attention

- Harsher or later gear changes than before
- A short delay when you select Drive or Reverse
- A burning smell after driving in traffic
- Red or brown fluid stains where you park
- Dark fluid that smells burnt (a workshop can check this for you)

## What a proper ATF service includes

- Checking for leaks and fault codes before any fluid goes in
- Using the fluid type your gearbox is specified for (gearboxes are sensitive to the wrong fluid)
- Changing the filter or cleaning the pan where the gearbox allows it
- Filling to the correct level at the correct temperature
- A road test to check the shifting afterwards

## A word of caution on very old fluid

If a high-mileage gearbox has never had its fluid changed and is already slipping, a fluid change alone may not fix it, and the cause should be diagnosed first. That is one reason Jacky tests the gearbox before recommending a service or a repair.

## Questions
Q: Is it bad to change ATF on an old car that has never had it changed?
A: Changing the fluid itself is not the cause of problems. If the gearbox is already worn, though, new fluid will not repair the wear, so it is worth a [diagnostic](/services/gearbox-repair/) first if the car is already shifting badly.
Q: Can I just top up the fluid instead of changing it?
A: Topping up fixes a low level for now, but a low level usually means a leak. Find and fix the [leak](/gearbox-problems/transmission-fluid-leak/), then change the fluid if it is old.
Q: Does a CVT or dual-clutch gearbox need different fluid?
A: Yes. CVTs and dual-clutch gearboxes use their own specific fluids. Always use the fluid your gearbox maker specifies.
