**Neither method is better for every car. A drain and fill replaces only part of the automatic transmission fluid (ATF), because some stays in the torque converter, cooler and lines. A flush uses a machine to exchange most of it.** What matters more than the method is the right fluid for your gearbox, the right level at the right temperature, and checking the gearbox for leaks and fault codes first. Follow your car maker's procedure, and have the gearbox checked before choosing.

## What is the difference between a flush and a drain and fill?

- **Drain and fill.** The fluid is let out of the gearbox pan (or a drain plug) and new fluid is put back in. Only the fluid that drains out is replaced. A good share of the old fluid stays inside the torque converter, the cooler and the lines, so the new fluid mixes with it.
- **Flush (fluid exchange).** A machine is connected to the gearbox cooler lines and pushes new fluid through while the old fluid is pumped out. This replaces most of the fluid in one visit, including fluid that a plain drain cannot reach.

Both are accepted ways to service a gearbox. Which one is suitable depends on the gearbox design, how old the fluid is and what the maker specifies.

## Which method suits my car?

There is no one rule, but these points help:

- **Check what the maker allows.** Some gearboxes, including many CVTs and dual-clutch units, use their own fluid and a set procedure with a set fluid temperature. Use the method and the fluid your gearbox maker specifies.
- **Fluid that has been changed regularly** is usually fine with either method. Smaller, more frequent drain and fills are a gentle way to keep it fresh.
- **Fluid that has never been changed** is a case for care. If the gearbox is already shifting badly, new fluid will not repair worn parts, and the cause should be diagnosed first (see [what a proper ATF service includes](/articles/how-often-change-atf-singapore/)).
- **Dark fluid that smells burnt, or milky or pink fluid,** points to a problem that a service will not fix on its own. See [what milky or pink gearbox fluid means](/articles/milky-or-pink-gearbox-fluid/).

## What matters more than the method

Whichever way the fluid goes in, a good [gearbox service](/services/gearbox-servicing/) includes:

1. Checking for leaks and reading fault codes before any new fluid goes in.
2. Using the fluid your gearbox is specified for. Gearboxes are sensitive to the wrong fluid.
3. Changing the filter or cleaning the pan where the gearbox allows it.
4. Filling to the correct level at the correct temperature.
5. A road test afterwards to check the shifting.

## Questions
Q: Is a gearbox flush bad for an old gearbox?
A: Not by itself. Fluid flows through the gearbox either way. The risk is changing the fluid on a worn gearbox and expecting it to fix slipping or harsh shifts, which it cannot. If the car is already misbehaving, have it diagnosed before you service it.
Q: Does a drain and fill change all the gearbox oil?
A: No. Only the fluid that drains out is replaced, and part of the old fluid stays in the torque converter, cooler and lines. Repeating it at the right interval keeps the fluid fresh.
