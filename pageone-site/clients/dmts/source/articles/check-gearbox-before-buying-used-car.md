**To check the gearbox of a used car,** start it from cold, move through every gear while stationary, then drive it in town and on an expressway: shifts should be smooth and prompt, the revs should never rise without the car speeding up, and there should be no shudder, clunk, whine or warning light. Ask for the gearbox service records, and have the fault codes read if anything feels wrong.

## Why check the gearbox separately?

The gearbox is one of the most expensive parts of a car to repair, and many faults only show once it is warm or under load. A general inspection report may grade the car without driving it far enough for a gearbox fault to show. A few extra minutes on the test drive can save you from buying someone else's repair.

## What should I check before the test drive?

- **Ask to start the car from cold.** Some faults, such as a slow engagement into Drive, are easiest to feel when the gearbox oil is cold.
- **Look for warning lights.** The transmission, gearbox temperature or check engine light should come on briefly at start-up and then go off. A light that stays on means a stored fault. See [transmission warning light](/gearbox-problems/transmission-warning-light/).
- **Look under the car.** Fresh red or brown oil spots near the middle of the car can mean a gearbox leak. More on [ATF leaks](/gearbox-problems/transmission-fluid-leak/).
- **Ask for the service records.** Check whether the gearbox oil (ATF) has ever been changed, and when. Our [ATF guide](/articles/how-often-change-atf-singapore/) explains why it matters in Singapore traffic.

## How do I test the gearbox on the test drive?

1. **Engage each gear while stopped.** With your foot on the brake, move from Park to Reverse to Neutral to Drive. Each should engage within about a second, with a light nudge, not a long delay or a hard clunk.
2. **Pull away gently, then firmly.** Shifts should be smooth at light throttle and quick under firmer throttle. Watch the rev counter: if the revs flare between gears, or climb without the car speeding up, the gearbox may be [slipping](/gearbox-problems/gearbox-slipping/).
3. **Drive in stop-start traffic.** Jerky low-speed shifts and shudders often show here first. See [jerking or slipping between gears](/gearbox-problems/car-jerks-when-changing-gear/).
4. **Hold a steady speed on an expressway.** A vibration like driving over rumble strips at steady speed can point to the torque converter. Our [torque converter guide](/articles/bad-torque-converter-symptoms/) lists the signs.
5. **Try a carpark ramp.** Moving off uphill puts load on the gearbox. Rolling back further than usual or a slow take-up is worth noting.
6. **Listen.** A whine or hum that changes with speed, or a clunk when you change direction, should be checked.
7. **Check again when it is hot.** Some faults only appear after 20 minutes or more of driving, so do not judge the gearbox in the first few minutes.

## Do CVT and dual-clutch gearboxes feel different?

Yes, so judge each type on its own terms. A **CVT** has no gear changes: the revs stay fairly steady while the car speeds up, which is normal, but a shudder, a whine or revs that surge are not. A **dual-clutch** gearbox can feel slightly hesitant at walking pace, but it should not judder, clunk or hesitate badly when moving off. Not sure which gearbox the car has? See [CVT, dual-clutch or automatic](/articles/cvt-dual-clutch-or-automatic-gearbox/).

## When should I walk away?

- The transmission or gearbox temperature warning stays on.
- The car refuses to go into a gear, gets stuck in one gear, or drops into a limp mode. Our [limp mode guide](/articles/gearbox-limp-mode/) explains what that means.
- The gearbox clearly slips, or the oil smells burnt.
- The seller cannot say whether the gearbox has ever been serviced and will not allow a check.

None of these mean the car cannot be fixed. They mean the repair should be priced before you agree on the price of the car.

## Questions
Q: What is the best way to test an automatic gearbox on a test drive?
A: Start from cold, engage each gear while stopped, then drive in town, in stop-start traffic and on an expressway. Shifts should be smooth and prompt, with no slipping, shudder, clunk, whine or warning light, and the gearbox should still feel the same when hot.
Q: Should I buy a used car if the gearbox oil has never been changed?
A: It is a risk, not an automatic no. Old oil wears the gearbox faster, so check the shifts carefully and have the gearbox checked if anything feels wrong before you agree on a price.
Q: Can a fault code reader show gearbox problems?
A: Yes. The gearbox control unit stores fault codes, even when the warning light is off. Reading them is one of the quickest ways to find a hidden gearbox problem.
Q: Is a slight delay into Drive normal?
A: A short pause of under about a second, with a light nudge, is normal on many automatics. A long delay, a hard clunk, or a delay that gets worse when warm should be checked.
