When an automatic gearbox starts slipping, jerking or showing a warning light, most drivers are told one of three things: repair the faulty part, overhaul the gearbox, or replace it. The right answer depends on what has actually failed, which is why the first step is always a proper diagnosis, not a guess from the symptom.

## The three options, in plain words

### Repairing one part
Many gearbox faults start outside the gearbox's hard parts: a sensor, a shift solenoid, the valve body, wiring, or low and old fluid. If the diagnostic shows the clutches and gears are still healthy, fixing that one part (and changing the fluid) can be enough.

### Overhaul (rebuild)
The gearbox is removed, stripped down, cleaned and inspected. Worn clutch packs, seals, gaskets, bushes and any damaged parts are replaced, then it is rebuilt, refilled and road-tested. Your car keeps its own gearbox, and parts that are still good stay in.

### Replacement
The gearbox is swapped for another unit: a new one from the maker, a remanufactured one, or a used one from another car. Replacement makes sense when the case is cracked, internal damage is very severe, or parts for a rebuild are not available.

## How to decide

- **Find the actual fault first.** A slipping or jerking gearbox is not automatically a rebuild. Fault codes, a fluid check and a road test narrow it down.
- **Look at the damage, not just the mileage.** Two cars with the same mileage can have very different gearboxes, depending on how they were driven and serviced.
- **Ask what will be replaced.** For an overhaul, ask which parts are changed as standard (clutches, seals, filter, fluid) and which only if worn.
- **Ask about the warranty.** Whatever option you choose, get the warranty terms in writing.
- **Think about the car's future.** If you plan to keep the car for years, a proper fix matters more than the cheapest quote. If the car is near the end of its life, a smaller repair may be the sensible choice.

## Why stop-start driving matters in Singapore

Short trips, heavy traffic and hot weather keep an automatic gearbox working hard and its fluid hot. Heat is what breaks the fluid down and wears the clutches. That is why gearboxes here often show early signs such as harsh shifts or a slight delay into Drive, and why catching them early usually keeps the repair smaller.

## Questions
Q: Is a gearbox overhaul as good as a new gearbox?
A: A well-done overhaul replaces the parts that wear, such as clutches and seals, and checks everything else. For most cars that restores normal shifting. A new unit may be the better choice only when the case or major parts are badly damaged.
Q: Can I drive with a slipping gearbox until I decide?
A: It is better not to. Slipping creates heat and wears the clutches faster, which can turn a smaller repair into an overhaul. Drive gently and book a [diagnostic](/services/gearbox-repair/) soon.
