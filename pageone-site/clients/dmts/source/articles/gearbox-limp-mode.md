**Limp mode** is a protection setting. When the gearbox control unit detects a serious fault, it usually holds the gearbox in one gear, limits power and turns on a warning light, so you can get the car off the road without causing more damage. It is a sign to stop and have the gearbox checked, not a fault that goes away by itself.

## What does limp mode feel like?

- The car stays in one gear, often second or third, and will not change up
- Weak acceleration and a low top speed, with high engine revs on the expressway
- A transmission or check engine light on the dashboard, sometimes with a message
- A hard jolt when you select Drive or Reverse
- After switching the engine off and on, the car may drive normally for a while, then go back into limp mode

These signs can come from different faults, so treat them as a reason to test the gearbox, not as a diagnosis.

## Why does a gearbox go into limp mode?

The control unit switches to limp mode when a reading it relies on is missing or out of range. Common causes include:

- **Low, old or overheated gearbox oil (ATF).** The gearbox needs the right fluid level and condition to make pressure. More in our [ATF guide](/articles/how-often-change-atf-singapore/).
- **A faulty sensor.** Speed sensors and temperature sensors tell the control unit what the gearbox is doing.
- **Shift solenoids or the valve body.** These direct the fluid that changes gear.
- **Wiring, connectors or a weak battery.** Low voltage or a poor connection can make the control unit store faults.
- **A control unit fault.** On dual-clutch gearboxes this is often the mechatronic unit. See [CVT, dual-clutch or automatic](/articles/cvt-dual-clutch-or-automatic-gearbox/) for the differences.
- **Internal wear.** Worn clutches or a failing torque converter can make the gearbox slip, and the control unit reacts to it.

## Is it safe to drive in limp mode?

Only as far as a safe place to stop, and gently. Limp mode exists to protect the gearbox, so driving on in it can turn a small fault, such as a sensor or low fluid, into a bigger repair. Avoid the expressway if you can, because the car may not reach normal speed. If the car will not move, jerks badly or you smell burning, stop and do not force it. ## Will restarting the car fix limp mode?

Sometimes the car drives normally after a restart, but the fault code is still stored and the cause is still there. If it comes back, the gearbox needs testing. Clearing the codes without finding the cause only hides the warning until next time.
