**Often not safely, if the driven wheels stay on the ground.** In many automatic cars the gearbox oil pump only works while the engine runs, so a car towed with the engine off and its driven wheels turning can run the gearbox without proper lubrication. That can cause serious damage. The safest option is a flatbed truck, or a tow truck that lifts the driven wheels off the ground. Your owner's manual says what your car allows.

## Why can towing damage an automatic gearbox?

When the engine runs, it drives the oil pump that pushes gearbox oil (ATF) round the gearbox to cool and lubricate the gears and bearings. When a car is towed with the engine off, that pump is not running, but the wheels can still turn parts inside the gearbox. Without oil pressure, those parts can overheat and wear very quickly, and the damage is often not visible until later.

## Which wheels matter?

- **Front-wheel drive:** the front wheels are the driven wheels. They should be lifted, or the car carried on a flatbed.
- **Rear-wheel drive:** the rear wheels are the driven wheels. The same rule applies.
- **All-wheel drive and 4x4:** all four wheels are connected to the drivetrain, so a flatbed is normally the only safe choice.
- **Hybrid and electric cars:** some can generate power while the wheels turn, and the maker may say flatbed only. Check the manual.

Makers differ. Some manuals allow a short, slow tow with the gear lever in Neutral, and many say flatbed only. Follow your own car's manual rather than a general rule.

## What if the gear lever is stuck in Park?

The car cannot be towed on its wheels while it is locked in Park, and forcing the lever can break parts. Many cars have a small shift lock release near the lever, shown in the owner's manual, that lets the lever move to Neutral. If it still will not move, or the car will not move in Drive or Reverse, do not force it and ask for a flatbed. ## Should I turn the engine on to move it a short distance?

Only if the car moves normally and you can do it safely. If the gearbox is not engaging, is slipping or is in limp mode, driving on can turn a small fault into a larger repair. If the car will not move properly, ask for a tow instead. See [gearbox limp mode](/articles/gearbox-limp-mode/) if the car is stuck in one gear.

## What should I tell the tow driver?

- That the car is an **automatic**, and which wheels drive it
- That the gearbox may be faulty, if you think it is
- That you would like it **flatbed** or with the driven wheels lifted
- Where the car is going, so it is not moved twice

Q: Why is towing an automatic car with the wheels on the ground a problem?
A: The gearbox oil pump runs only with the engine, so a towed car with the engine off can turn gearbox parts without enough oil. That can overheat and damage them.
Q: Can I tow a front-wheel drive automatic with the front wheels on the ground?
A: This is usually not recommended, because the front wheels drive the gearbox. Lift the front wheels or use a flatbed, unless your manual says otherwise.
