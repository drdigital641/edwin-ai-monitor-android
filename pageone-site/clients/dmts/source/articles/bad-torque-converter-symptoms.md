**The common signs of a bad torque converter** are a shudder or vibration at steady low speeds, engine revs rising without the car speeding up, a whining or rattling noise in gear, an overheating gearbox, and in bad cases weak drive or none at all. These signs can also come from other gearbox faults, so the torque converter should be tested before anything is replaced.

## What does a torque converter do?

A **torque converter** is the fluid coupling between the engine and a conventional automatic gearbox. It lets the car stop in gear without stalling, multiplies the engine's pull when you move off, and on most modern cars locks up with an internal clutch at cruising speed so less power is lost. CVTs on many cars use one too; dual-clutch gearboxes do not. Not sure which you have? See [CVT, dual-clutch or automatic](/articles/cvt-dual-clutch-or-automatic-gearbox/).

## What are the signs of a bad torque converter?

- **Shudder at low or steady speed.** A vibration that feels like driving over rumble strips, often at light throttle, when the lock-up clutch engages. More on [vibration when accelerating](/gearbox-problems/car-vibrates-when-accelerating/).
- **Slipping.** The engine revs climb but the car does not speed up to match. See [engine revs but the car doesn't speed up](/gearbox-problems/gearbox-slipping/).
- **Noise in gear.** Whining, whirring or rattling that changes with engine speed, often louder when you move off.
- **Overheating.** A gearbox temperature warning, or fluid that smells burnt or looks dark.
- **Weak or no drive.** Slow pull-away, or the car will not move in Drive or Reverse even with the engine running.
- **A warning light.** The control unit can store a lock-up or slip fault and turn on a transmission or check engine light.

## How do you tell a torque converter fault from other gearbox problems?

You can't reliably tell by feel alone. Low or old gearbox oil, a worn clutch pack, a shift solenoid, the valve body or even worn engine mounts can feel much the same. That is why the fault codes, the fluid and a road test come first. A torque converter is usually only replaced together with removing the gearbox, so it pays to be sure before that work starts.

## Is it safe to drive with a bad torque converter?

Only gently and only as far as you need to. A slipping or overheating converter makes more heat, and heat breaks down the gearbox oil, which can spread the damage into the rest of the gearbox. ## What causes a torque converter to fail?

- **Old or overheated gearbox oil (ATF).** The converter works through the fluid, so worn fluid affects it directly. Our [ATF guide](/articles/how-often-change-atf-singapore/) covers how often to change it.
- **Wear in the lock-up clutch.** Stop-start traffic and heavy loads mean many lock and unlock cycles.
- **Worn bearings or seals** inside the converter, which can cause noise or leaks.
- **A control fault.** The lock-up is controlled by a solenoid and the gearbox control unit, so an electrical or valve body fault can look like a converter problem.

## Questions
Q: Can a bad torque converter cause shuddering?
A: Yes. A shudder at light throttle or steady low speed is one of the most common signs, often when the lock-up clutch engages. Old gearbox oil and worn engine mounts can feel similar, so it should be tested.
Q: Can changing the gearbox oil fix a torque converter shudder?
A: Sometimes, if worn fluid is the cause and the converter is not damaged. If the shudder comes back, the converter or its control needs testing.
Q: Do dual-clutch gearboxes have a torque converter?
A: No. Dual-clutch (DSG) gearboxes use clutches instead. Conventional automatics have one, and many CVTs do too.
