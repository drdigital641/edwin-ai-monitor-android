Questions to ask before choosing a columbarium niche

A simple checklist you can go through at your own pace, alone or with family.

By Benjamin Poon · Updated 2026-10-22 · 6 min read
Before choosing a niche, clarify what your family wishes for, who will visit, and where the niche is. Then ask the operator for written terms: what is included, what any fees cover, how the niche is cared for, and what happens if circumstances change. Visit in person before you decide.

Why prepare questions first?

Choosing a niche is a decision families live with for a long time. It is easier to think clearly when you have a list in hand, rather than trying to remember everything on the day.

The list below is general guidance. You do not need every answer at once, and you may add questions of your own.

What is the checklist?

Go through these in order. The first four are for the family; the rest are for the operator.

  1. What are the wishes of the departed or the family's elders? Has anything been said or written down?
  2. Which customs does the family follow? Religion and dialect group may affect what feels right.
  3. Who will visit, and how often? Think about older relatives and how comfortable the journey is for them.
  4. Who makes the decision? Make sure all the people who should be heard have been heard.
  5. Where exactly is the niche, and what is the surrounding environment like? Ask to see it, not only a picture.
  6. How many urns can it hold? Ask whether family members can be placed together later.
  7. What do the terms say about the arrangement itself? Ask the operator to explain the duration or tenure in writing, as this differs between venues.
  8. What do the fees cover, and are there any ongoing or future charges? Ask for a complete written breakdown.
  9. How is the niche cared for and maintained? Ask who is responsible and how the family is informed of any changes.
  10. What are the procedures for placing the urn, and what documents or approvals are needed?
  11. Can the arrangement be changed, transferred or cancelled? Ask what the written terms say before you sign anything.

How do I raise this with my family?

Choose a calm time, not a rushed one. You could begin by asking what each person would find comforting, rather than asking them to choose a product.

If family members disagree, that is common. It often helps to visit together, so everyone is looking at the same place.

What should I ask the operator?

Questions 5 to 11 are best answered by the operator in writing. If anything is unclear, ask again. A good explanation should not make you feel rushed.

Why visit in person?

Seeing the place helps you judge things photographs cannot show: the atmosphere, the walk in, and whether it feels right for your family. Take your time and bring the people who matter to the decision.

What if I am not ready to decide?

That is perfectly fine. Gathering information, visiting and talking things over carry no obligation. Take the checklist home and return to it when your family is ready.

Common questions

When should we start thinking about a niche?

There is no set time. Some families plan ahead calmly; others need to arrange in a short time. Either way, the same questions apply.

Should I sign on the first visit?

No. Take the written terms home, read them with your family and ask questions first. You should not feel pressured.

Can I bring my parents or elders along?

Yes, and it is often helpful. Let Benjamin know in advance so the visit can be planned around their needs.

What if the family cannot agree?

Disagreement is common. Visiting together and talking through each person's wishes often helps. There is no need to rush.
