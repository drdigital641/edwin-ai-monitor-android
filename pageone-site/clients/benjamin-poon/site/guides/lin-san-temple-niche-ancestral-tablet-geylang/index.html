Lin San Temple (灵山寺) niches and ancestral tablets: a guide for families around Geylang

Many families prefer a resting place close to where they live, so that elderly parents can visit often. This guide is for them.

By Benjamin Poon · Updated 2026-10-23 · 5 min read
Lin San Temple (灵山寺) is a Buddhist temple at 27 Lorong 12 Geylang, Singapore 399004. For families who live in Geylang, Aljunied, MacPherson, Sims or Kallang, it is a temple in their own part of the island. Benjamin Poon can talk you through niche and ancestral tablet questions in Chinese or English and help you prepare what to ask.

Why do families choose a temple near home?

For older parents, distance matters. A temple in the same neighbourhood means a shorter trip on Qing Ming, on anniversaries, or simply on a quiet afternoon.

A temple setting also suits families who want prayers and chanting to be part of remembrance, rather than a park setting.

  • Shorter journeys for elderly parents.
  • Easier to visit often, not only on festivals.
  • A familiar Buddhist setting.
  • Family members nearby can share the visits.

Niche or ancestral tablet: what is the difference?

A niche (骨灰位, also called 福位) holds the urn of ashes. An ancestral tablet (神主牌) carries the name of the departed and is where the family offers respect; it does not hold ashes. Some families choose one, some choose both.

Our longer guide, niche vs ancestral tablet, explains this step by step.

Questions to ask before deciding

Benjamin is a consultant, not the temple. He tells you plainly what he can arrange himself and what you should confirm with the temple.

  • Are niches, ancestral tablets, or both available at Lin San Temple right now?
  • Are there prayer sessions on Qing Ming, Zhong Yuan or anniversaries, and can the family join?
  • What are the visiting hours, and are there steps or lifts for elderly visitors?
  • What is included, and what are the ongoing fees, if any?
  • Can family members of other faiths visit and pay respects?

Getting there from nearby estates

The address is 27 Lorong 12 Geylang. The lanes in Geylang are narrow, so plan your drop-off point in advance, especially with elderly parents.

We have not listed bus or MRT routes, because the temple's own contact page does not give them and we only publish what we have checked. Ask Benjamin and he will share directions.
