Burning smell from your car: what it can mean

A burning smell usually comes from one of five things: rubber from a tyre or a dragging brake, hot oil, a slipping clutch, an electrical fault, or leaking coolant. The kind of smell and when you notice it help a workshop find the cause. If there is smoke, a warning light or the temperature gauge is rising, stop somewhere safe and switch off.

General guide in plain words. It is not a diagnosis and gives no prices: only a workshop that inspects your car can say what is wrong.

What the smell can mean

  1. Burning rubber

    Often a brake that is dragging or very hot, for example after a long downhill or with the handbrake left on, or a tyre rubbing on a part. A slipping drive belt can smell similar.

    Next: Tyre shops · Brake noise guide

  2. Burning oil or a sharp, acrid smell

    Oil dripping onto the hot engine or exhaust smells sharp, sometimes with light smoke from under the bonnet. Spilled oil after an oil change can smell for a short while, but a smell that keeps coming back points to a leak.

    Next: Servicing and repair workshops · Car overheating guide

  3. Clutch or gearbox smell

    A slipping clutch smells like burnt paper, often after slow stop-start driving or a hill start in a manual car. In an automatic or CVT, overheated gearbox fluid can also smell burnt.

    Next: Gearbox workshops · Gearbox or CVT jerking guide

  4. Hot plastic or burning wires

    A smell of hot plastic or wiring can come from a wiring fault, a fuse, the blower motor or an added accessory. Electrical faults can get worse, so have it checked soon by a workshop that does electrical diagnosis.

    Next: Servicing and repair workshops · Dashboard warning lights guide

  5. A sweet, syrupy smell

    A sweet smell, sometimes with steam or a damp floor mat, usually means coolant is leaking. Keep an eye on the temperature gauge.

    Next: Car overheating guide · Servicing and repair workshops

What to tell the workshop

  • What the smell is like: rubber, oil, burnt paper, plastic or sweet.
  • When it happens: after a long drive, when braking, on hills, in traffic, or with the aircon on.
  • Where it seems to come from: a wheel, under the bonnet, or inside through the vents.
  • Any smoke, warning lights or a rising temperature gauge.
  • Any recent work, such as an oil change, new brakes or a new accessory.
Stop driving when it is safe if: you see smoke, smell burning plastic or wires, a red warning light comes on, or the temperature gauge moves towards hot. Pull over, switch off and get help. If you see flames, get everyone out and away from the car and call 995. A faint smell that fades soon after a long drive is less urgent, but still have it checked.

Questions

Is a burning smell after a long drive normal?

A faint smell of hot brakes after a long downhill, or of spilled oil just after an oil change, can fade on its own. A smell that keeps coming back, gets stronger or comes with smoke needs checking.

Can I keep driving with a burning smell?

If there is no smoke, no warning light and the temperature gauge is normal, drive gently to a workshop. If any of those show, stop safely and switch off first.

Why does my car smell burnt when the aircon is on?

Dust on a heater or blower, a blower motor fault, or a smell drawn in from outside are possible. A smell of burning plastic from the vents should be checked soon.

What does a workshop check first?

Usually the brakes and tyres, oil leaks around the engine and exhaust, the clutch or gearbox fluid, the coolant level and the wiring. Telling them the kind of smell and when it happens saves time.
