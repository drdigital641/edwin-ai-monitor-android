What eCU tuning involves

The workshop reads the engine computer's software, changes settings such as fuel and boost maps, and writes it back, often checking the result on a dyno or a road test. Coding switches car features on or off. Tuning can affect warranty, insurance and the car's legal status, so check before you go ahead.

Signs you may need it

  • You want more power or a different throttle response.
  • You have fitted performance parts that need matching software.
  • You want features coded on or off on a European car.

What to ask the workshop

  • What changes they make and whether the original software is saved.
  • Whether the car is checked on a dyno or by data logging.
  • How it affects your warranty and insurance.
  • Whether the change meets LTA rules for your car.

More: ABS, ECU and module repair

Questions

Is ECU tuning the same as ECU repair?

No. Tuning changes the software. A faulty ECU is repaired or replaced; see the ABS and ECU module repair page for that.

Can a tune be reversed?

Often, if the workshop saves the original software. Ask before the work starts.
