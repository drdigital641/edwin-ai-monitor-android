Tesla servicing and repair

A Tesla has no engine oil to change, but its tyres, brakes, suspension, aircon and bodywork still need looking after. These notes cover what to ask a workshop, what to tell it, and the cautions that come with an electric car.

General guide in plain words. It is not a diagnosis and gives no prices: only a workshop that inspects your car can say what is wrong.

What to know and ask

  1. Ask which jobs the workshop does on Teslas

    Many jobs, such as tyres, brakes, suspension arms and wheel alignment, are ordinary mechanical work. Ask the workshop which jobs it does on Teslas and which it sends elsewhere.

  2. High-voltage work needs trained staff

    The main battery and its orange cables carry high voltage. Only staff trained on electric cars, with the right tools and safety steps, should work on them. Ask before you book.

  3. Tyres and suspension wear

    Electric cars are heavy and deliver power at once, so tyres and suspension parts such as control arm bushes can wear. A knock over humps or uneven tyre wear is worth checking.

    Next: Knocking over humps guide · Tyre shops

  4. Brakes still need checks

    Regenerative braking means the pads are used less, but discs and calipers can still rust or stick when they are used lightly. Ask for a brake check at each visit.

    Next: Brake noise guide

  5. Aircon on an electric car

    The aircon compressor is electric and may need a specific oil. Ask whether the workshop handles aircon on electric cars before you book a gas top-up.

    Next: Aircon not cold guide

  6. Body parts must match your exact version

    Panels, sensors and camera mounts can differ between model years and versions. Share the model, year and a photo of the part so fitment is checked before anything is ordered.

    Next: Car parts listings · Bodywork and spray painting

  7. Warranty, recalls and software

    Some faults are fixed by a software update, and some work can affect the maker's warranty. Check your warranty terms and any open recalls with the maker before you choose where to go.

What to tell the workshop

  • Model, year and version, for example Model 3 or Model Y and the year.
  • Mileage, and any warning message on the screen, with a photo.
  • Symptoms: noises, vibration, pulling, aircon not cold.
  • Whether the car is still under the maker's warranty.
  • For parts, a photo of the part and the car details so fitment can be checked.
Do not wait if: A battery or high-voltage warning, a burning smell, smoke, or a car that will not go into drive should be checked before you drive further. Never open or work on the high-voltage battery or orange cables yourself.

Questions

Can an independent workshop service a Tesla?

Many jobs, such as tyres, brakes, suspension and aircon, are ordinary mechanical work. Ask the workshop about its experience with Teslas and what it does not handle.

Does a Tesla need regular servicing?

There is no engine oil to change, but tyres, brakes, suspension, the cabin filter and fluids still need checks. The maker's maintenance advice for your model says what and when.

What should I check before buying a body part?

Check that it matches your model, year and version, and ask the seller to confirm fitment from photos before you pay.
