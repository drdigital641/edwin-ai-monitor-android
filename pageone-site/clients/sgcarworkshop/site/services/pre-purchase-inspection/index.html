What pre-purchase inspection involves

A mechanic checks the engine, gearbox, brakes, suspension, aircon and body, reads fault codes with a scan tool, and usually takes the car for a short drive. Some workshops add an underside check on a lift and a written report with photos.

Signs you may need it

  • You are buying a used car from a dealer or a private seller.
  • The car has a long or unclear service history.
  • You want a list of what needs fixing before you agree a price.
  • You are taking over a car from family or a friend and want a baseline check.

What to ask the workshop

  • What is checked, and whether the car goes on a lift.
  • Whether they scan for stored fault codes.
  • Whether you get a written report or photos.
  • How long it takes, and whether the seller has to bring the car in.

More: General servicing and repair

Questions

What does a pre-purchase inspection check?

Usually the engine, gearbox, brakes, suspension, aircon, body and fault codes, plus a short test drive. Ask each workshop for its own list, as it varies.

Can I bring a car that I have not bought yet?

Usually yes, with the seller's agreement. Arrange the time with both the seller and the workshop.
