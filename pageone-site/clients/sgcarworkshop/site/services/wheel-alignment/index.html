What wheel alignment involves

The workshop measures the angles of the wheels against the maker's settings on an alignment machine, then adjusts the steering and suspension links so the wheels point the right way. It is often done with new tyres or after suspension work.

Signs you may need it

  • The car pulls to one side on a straight road.
  • The steering wheel is not straight when driving straight.
  • Tyres wear faster on the inner or outer edge.
  • You have just fitted new tyres or had suspension parts replaced.

What to ask the workshop

  • Whether you get a before-and-after printout of the readings.
  • Whether they check for worn suspension parts first.
  • Whether alignment is front only or all four wheels for your car.
  • How long the job takes.

Related pages

Questions

Is wheel alignment the same as wheel balancing?

No. Alignment sets the angle of the wheels; balancing evens out the weight of each wheel and tyre to stop vibration. They are often done together with new tyres.

When should I do a wheel alignment?

When the car pulls, the steering is off-centre, tyres wear unevenly, or after new tyres or suspension work. Your owner's manual may also give an interval.
