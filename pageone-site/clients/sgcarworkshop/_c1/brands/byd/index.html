BYD servicing and repair

BYD cars such as the Atto 3, Dolphin and Seal are often seen on Singapore roads. They still need tyres, brakes, suspension, aircon and bodywork looked after. These notes cover what to ask, what to tell the workshop, and the cautions for an electric car.

General guide in plain words. It is not a diagnosis and gives no prices: only a workshop that inspects your car can say what is wrong.

What to know and ask

  1. Know whether your BYD is fully electric or a plug-in hybrid

    Some BYD models are fully electric and some are plug-in hybrids with a petrol engine too. A plug-in hybrid also needs engine oil and engine servicing, so tell the workshop which one you drive.

  2. High-voltage work needs trained staff

    The main battery and its orange cables carry high voltage. Only staff trained on electric cars, with the right tools and safety steps, should work on them. Ask before you book.

  3. Tyres, suspension and alignment

    Electric cars are heavy, so tyres and suspension parts can wear. A knock over humps, a pull to one side or uneven tyre wear is worth checking.

    Next: Knocking over humps guide · Tyre shops

  4. Brakes still need checks

    Regenerative braking means the pads are used less, but discs and calipers can still rust or stick. Ask for a brake check at each visit.

    Next: Brake noise guide

  5. Aircon on an electric car

    The aircon compressor is electric and may need a specific oil. Ask whether the workshop handles aircon on electric cars.

    Next: Aircon not cold guide

  6. Body parts for newer models

    Parts for newer models and versions may be less widely stocked. Share the exact model, year and a photo of the part so fitment is checked before anything is ordered.

    Next: Car parts listings · Bodywork and spray painting

  7. Warranty and recalls

    Some work can affect the maker's warranty, including the battery warranty. Check your warranty terms and any open recalls with the dealer before you choose where to go.

What to tell the workshop

  • Model, year and whether it is fully electric or a plug-in hybrid.
  • Mileage, and any warning message on the screen, with a photo.
  • Symptoms: noises, vibration, pulling, aircon not cold.
  • Whether the car is still under the maker's warranty.
  • For parts, a photo of the part and the car details so fitment can be checked.
Do not wait if: A battery or high-voltage warning, a burning smell, smoke, or a car that will not go into drive should be checked before you drive further. Never open or work on the high-voltage battery or orange cables yourself.

Questions

Can an independent workshop service a BYD?

Many jobs, such as tyres, brakes, suspension and aircon, are ordinary mechanical work. Ask the workshop about its experience with BYD cars and what it does not handle.

Does an electric BYD need servicing?

A fully electric BYD has no engine oil to change, but tyres, brakes, suspension, the cabin filter and fluids still need checks. A plug-in hybrid also needs engine servicing.

What should I check before buying a body part?

Check that it matches your model, year and version, and ask the seller to confirm fitment from photos before you pay.
