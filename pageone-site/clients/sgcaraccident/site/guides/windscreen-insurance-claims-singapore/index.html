Windscreen insurance claims in Singapore

Windscreen cover is usually separate from accident cover, and the excess and rules depend on your policy.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • Many policies have a windscreen excess, which differs by insurer.
  • Repair may cost you less than replacement under some plans.
  • Chips and scratches may not be claimable.
  • Check your certificate and ask your insurer before work starts.

Windscreen cover is its own thing

A cracked windscreen can happen without any collision, for example from a stone on the expressway. Many comprehensive policies treat windscreen damage as its own type of claim, with its own excess.

Terms differ by insurer and policy, so your certificate and policy wording come first.

Because the rules vary, two drivers with similar cracks can have different outcomes. That is normal, and it is why checking your own policy matters.

Check also whether the glass of other windows, such as side or rear windows, is treated the same way. Policies differ on this.

Examples of what insurers say

Here are examples from insurers' own pages. They are not a guide to your policy:

  • Income's Drivo full coverage mentions a windscreen excess of $100 per claim, or the amount on your certificate.
  • MSIG's MotorMax states a $100 windscreen excess that is waived if the windscreen is repaired and not replaced. It also says such claims do not affect your No Claim Discount.
  • MSIG's claims page says chipped or scratched windscreens are not claimable.

Other insurers may do things differently. Always check yours.

Do not assume your policy matches any of these examples. They show how different insurers can be.

Repair or replace

Some plans treat repair and replacement differently, as in the MSIG example. If a small crack can be repaired, ask your insurer whether that changes the excess. Ask before you approve any work.

If the crack is small, acting early may mean a simpler repair, since cracks can spread with heat and vibration. Still, wait for your insurer's guidance before you commit.

Steps to take

A simple routine helps:

  • Take clear photos of the damage and the whole windscreen.
  • Read your policy for the windscreen section and excess.
  • Contact your insurer to ask how to claim and which workshops to use.
  • Wait for approval before replacing, unless it is a safety emergency.

If the damage came from a collision with another vehicle, treat it as an accident and report it to your insurer.

Keep your invoice and any approval message from your insurer, in case there is a later question about what was covered.

Does it affect my NCD?

It depends on your insurer. MSIG says windscreen claims under its plan do not affect NCD, but another insurer may treat them differently. Ask yours before you claim.

Also ask whether the excess is charged per claim or in another way, since this may change what you pay.

If you cannot tell from your documents whether a windscreen claim counts toward your record, ask your insurer to confirm in writing or by message, so that you have it on file.

Questions people ask

How much is the windscreen excess?

It depends on your policy. Some insurers mention $100 per claim, but check your certificate for the actual amount.

Can I claim for a small chip or scratch?

Some insurers, such as MSIG, say chipped or scratched windscreens are not claimable. Check your own policy terms.
