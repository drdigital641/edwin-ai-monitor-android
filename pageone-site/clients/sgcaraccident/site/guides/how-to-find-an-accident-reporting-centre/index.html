How to find an accident reporting centre

Your insurer decides which reporting centres and workshops you should use, so start there.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • Reporting centres are linked to your insurer, so check with it first.
  • Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to the centre.
  • The centre helps you complete and submit the final accident report.
  • Bring your policy details, photos and the other driver's particulars.

What a reporting centre does

An accident reporting centre (ARC) assists you to complete and submit the final accident report. The General Insurance Association's framework asks you to take your vehicle, damaged or not, to the approved reporting centre or authorised workshop.

This step is part of reporting to your insurer. It is not the same as a Traffic Police report.

Using the wrong centre is a common source of delay, so a few minutes of checking can save you a trip.

Some insurers use more than one centre, and opening hours and booking rules can differ. This is another reason to confirm before you go.

How to find the right one

Which centre you use depends on your insurer. The simplest ways to find out:

  • Check your insurer's claims page or mobile app.
  • Look at your policy documents or claim guide.
  • Call your insurer's claims hotline, using the number on your policy.
  • Use our insurers page as a starting point, then confirm with your insurer.

We do not run or appoint any reporting centre. Details can change, so always confirm with your insurer before you drive over.

If you have more than one policy, such as one for each car, check the right one. The centre can differ between insurers.

What to bring

Having your documents ready saves time. Bring or have ready:

  • Your vehicle
  • Your driving licence and your policy details
  • The other driver's NRIC, name, telephone number, address, insurer and vehicle number
  • Photos of the scene and the damage
  • Your accident report or reference number, if you already reported

If you were not the driver, such as when a family member or colleague drove your car, bring their details too. The centre will want to know who was driving at the time.

Timing

Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

If you cannot meet the timeframe for a valid reason, tell your insurer as early as you can. GIA says you will not be penalised for late reporting if you have a valid reason, but insurers assess each case.

Plan the trip soon after the accident, ideally the same day if you can, so that you stay within the timeframe.

If your car cannot be driven

Call your insurer's hotline to arrange a tow truck. Avoid unauthorised tow-truck operators who approach you at the scene. Our tow page explains what to consider.

Keep your tow receipt, if any, and tell your insurer how the vehicle was moved. It can matter later if the cost is discussed.

Ask what the centre needs from you, such as photos or forms, and whether anything can be sent ahead online.

Repairs after reporting

Go to an authorised workshop of your insurer for repairs, even if you are claiming against the other party. Tell the workshop whether you are claiming under your own policy or against the third party's policy. Terms differ by insurer and policy.

If you are unsure which workshop is authorised, ask your insurer before you hand over the keys.

Questions people ask

Do I need to go to a reporting centre if my car looks fine?

The framework says to take your vehicle, damaged or not, to the approved reporting centre or authorised workshop.

Can SGCarAccident file my report for me?

No. We give general guidance only. You report through your insurer and its reporting centre.
