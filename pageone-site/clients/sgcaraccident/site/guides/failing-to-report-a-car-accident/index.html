What happens if I fail to report an accident?

Not reporting can cost you your cover, your No Claim Discount and even your renewal, so report as soon as you can.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • The insurer may refuse liability and you may lose policy protection.
  • Your No Claim Discount may be reduced or lost.
  • The insurer may end the policy or refuse renewal.
  • If you have a valid reason for delay, tell your insurer early.

Why reporting matters

Insurers rely on early reports to check facts, inspect vehicles and handle claims from the other side. If they hear late, or never, it can harm both you and them.

Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

Reporting is also how your insurer protects you if the other side makes a claim months later. Without a record, you may be left to deal with it alone.

What can happen if you do not report

The General Insurance Association's FAQ on non-reporting lists several possible results:

  • The insurer can repudiate liability, which means you lose protection under the policy.
  • The insurer can confiscate NCD entitlement for delay in reporting.
  • The insurer may end your policy or refuse to renew it.

Individual insurers add their own wording. AIG says your NCD may be reduced by 10% if you do not report. Income says failing to report within 24 hours reduces NCD by 10% even with NCD protection. Your policy terms apply, so check them.

The wording and the size of the effect differ by insurer. That is why it is worth reading the claims section of your policy before you ever need it.

These results are described by insurers in general terms. Whether any of them applies to you depends on the policy and the facts of your case.

Small accidents and private settlements

Insurer guidance is to report every accident, no matter how small. This also applies if you and the other driver settle privately. A private agreement is not a substitute for reporting.

It can feel easier to let a small accident go, but a quiet scrape can turn into a bigger claim later, and your insurer will want to know when you first learned of it.

A Traffic Police report is not enough

Some drivers think a police report covers them. GIA says a Traffic Police report is not equivalent to a report to your insurer. You may need both, depending on the accident.

If a Traffic Police report was required for your accident, make sure you complete that as well, as the two reports serve different purposes.

If you have already missed the timeframe

Do not wait longer. GIA says you will not be penalised for late reporting if you have a valid reason. The insurer decides whether your reason is valid, so explain what happened and why you were delayed, and keep any proof.

Then take your vehicle to the reporting centre or authorised workshop your insurer directs you to.

Keep proof of the reason, such as a hospital record or travel document, if that is why you were late. It makes your explanation easier to assess.

What to do now

Contact your insurer, tell them the facts, and follow their steps. SGCarAccident is not an insurer, law firm, reporting centre or claims representative, and cannot report for you or promise a result. Our report guide shows what to prepare.

The sooner you act, the easier it is to explain what happened and to show that you meant to follow the rules.

Questions people ask

Will I lose my NCD if I report late?

It can happen. Insurers say NCD may be confiscated or reduced for delay in reporting, but terms differ by insurer and policy.

What if I had a good reason for reporting late?

GIA says you will not be penalised for late reporting if you have a valid reason. Tell your insurer as soon as possible and explain.
