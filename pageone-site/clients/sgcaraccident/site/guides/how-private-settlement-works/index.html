How does private settlement work?

A private settlement is an agreement between the two drivers, and it does not remove your duty to report.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • It is a private matter between the two parties.
  • You must still report the accident to your insurer.
  • Do not sign or pay before checking your policy terms.
  • It does not replace the insurer reporting process.

What private settlement means

Sometimes two drivers agree to sort things out themselves, for example by one paying for the other's repairs. This is called a private settlement. It is often recorded in a short written agreement, sometimes called a memorandum of agreement.

The General Insurance Association describes it as a private matter between the two parties. It should not be regarded as a substitute for the reporting process.

Private settlements are common for small scrapes, but small does not always mean simple. Costs and injuries can grow after the day itself.

Some people prefer a private settlement to avoid hassle or a claim on their record. Whatever the reason, the reporting duty stays, and your insurer's terms still apply.

You still need to report

Insurer guidance is to report all accidents, even if settled privately. Take your vehicle to the approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

If you do not report, the insurer may refuse liability and you may lose protection under the policy. Insurers may also affect your No Claim Discount or renewal.

Think of reporting as protection for you, not a penalty. It keeps your policy in force if the agreement falls apart later.

Risks to think about before agreeing

A quick agreement at the roadside can look easy, but problems often show up later. Consider the following:

  • Hidden damage may appear after repairs start.
  • Injuries can show up days later.
  • Your policy may require you to get your insurer's consent before you settle.
  • Insurers advise against admitting liability, negotiating, or making offers to a third party.
  • A signed paper may be hard to undo.

Check your own policy terms. They differ by insurer and policy.

If the other driver's car is repaired at a workshop of their choice, you may have little control over the final bill. This is one more reason to speak to your insurer before you agree to pay anything.

If you still decide to settle privately

Write down the facts clearly: date, place, vehicle numbers, both drivers' details and what each side agrees. Take photos of the damage first. Keep a copy of everything you sign and any payment receipts.

Do not rely on this page as legal advice. If a large sum or an injury is involved, think about getting proper legal advice.

Pay by a traceable method where possible, so there is a record, and avoid agreeing to anything you do not fully understand.

If you are not sure the agreement is fair, do not sign on the spot. A short delay costs little, and signing too soon can cost a lot.

Questions people ask

If we settle privately, can I skip reporting to my insurer?

No. Insurer guidance is to report all accidents even if settled privately, and a private agreement is not a substitute for reporting.

Is a private settlement agreement legally binding?

We cannot give legal advice on that. If it matters, speak to a lawyer, and tell your insurer before you sign.
