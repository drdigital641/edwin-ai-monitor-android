What to do after a car accident in Singapore

The first day matters most, so this page gives you the big picture of what to sort out and in what order.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • Check for injuries first. Call 995 for an ambulance and 999 for the Police if needed.
  • Collect details and photos before the vehicles move, unless someone is hurt.
  • Tell your own insurer, even if the accident was small or settled privately.
  • Do not admit liability or sign anything with the other party.

Think of the first 24 hours in three parts

Most accidents in Singapore are minor, but the first day still decides how smoothly things go later. It helps to split it into three parts: safety, evidence and reporting.

Safety comes first. Evidence is what you collect at the scene. Reporting is what you do afterwards with your insurer and, in some cases, the Traffic Police. This page explains the overall picture. If you are standing at the scene right now, use our step-by-step checklist instead.

Keeping this order in mind helps when you feel rattled. Nobody expects you to remember every rule on the spot, so it is fine to check this page on your phone.

Safety and emergencies

Check yourself and your passengers. If anyone is injured, call 995 for an ambulance. Call 999 if there is danger or if the situation needs the Police.

The Government Insurance Association guidance says not to move an injured person or the vehicles when someone is hurt. Wait for help to arrive.

Evidence you should keep

Insurers ask for the same basic information again and again, so it is worth getting it right at the scene. Their guidance suggests taking the following from the other driver:

  • NRIC and full name
  • Telephone number and address
  • Insurer name
  • Vehicle numbers of every car involved
  • Digital photos of the scene and the damage to all vehicles

Take photos before you move your vehicle where it is safe to do so. A few clear pictures can save a lot of back and forth later.

Who you need to tell

You should tell your own insurer about every accident, no matter how small. Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

A Traffic Police report is only required in certain situations, such as a death, an injury that needs hospital transport or medical leave, damage to government property, a foreign vehicle, a pedestrian or cyclist, or a hit-and-run. A Traffic Police report does not replace your report to your insurer. They are separate steps.

Things to avoid

Do not admit liability, make offers or sign promissory notes at the scene. Fault is for the insurers and, where needed, the proper authorities to assess. Be polite, but keep to facts.

Be careful with tow trucks that appear without being called. Insurer guidance is to use your insurer's hotline for a tow truck and to avoid unauthorised operators at the scene.

Even if you and the other driver agree to settle privately, you should still report to your insurer. A private agreement is not a substitute for reporting.

After the first day

Take your vehicle to the reporting centre or authorised workshop your insurer points you to, even if it looks undamaged. Terms differ by insurer and policy, so check your own policy documents.

Questions people ask

Do I need to report a very minor accident?

Insurers advise reporting every accident to your insurer, no matter how small. Reporting late without a valid reason can affect your cover or your No Claim Discount.

Should I call the Police for every accident?

Not always. A Traffic Police report is required only in specific cases, such as injury needing hospital transport or medical leave, a pedestrian or cyclist, or a hit-and-run. Otherwise you exchange particulars and report to your insurers.
