How to report a car accident in Singapore

Most drivers must report to their insurer, and only some accidents also need a Traffic Police report.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • Report every accident to your own insurer, however small.
  • A Traffic Police report is required only in specific cases.
  • A Traffic Police report does not replace your insurer report.
  • Take your vehicle to the approved reporting centre or authorised workshop.

Two different reports

People often mix up the two reports. One goes to your insurer. The other goes to the Traffic Police. They serve different purposes, and one does not replace the other.

Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

Think of the insurer report as the one almost everyone needs, and the Traffic Police report as the one only some accidents need.

Reporting to your insurer

Under the General Insurance Association's Motor Claims Framework, you report every accident to your insurer, no matter how small. You also take your vehicle, damaged or not, to the approved reporting centre or authorised workshop.

The reporting centre helps you complete and submit the final accident report. Insurers have their own hotlines and procedures, so follow your insurer's instructions. Terms differ by insurer and policy.

If you are unsure how to start, look on your insurer's claims page or app, or check your policy documents for the claims contact.

Do not guess at details when you report. If you are not sure about something, say so, and give what you know. Honest and complete information helps the insurer more than a confident guess.

When a Traffic Police report is required

The Singapore Police Force says a Traffic Police report must be made when the accident involves:

  • A death
  • An injury where someone was taken to hospital by ambulance, or got outpatient medical leave of 3 days or more
  • Damage to government property
  • A foreign vehicle
  • A pedestrian or cyclist
  • A hit-and-run

You can report online through the SPF e-services Traffic Accident Report using Singpass, or at a police station. In other cases, the parties exchange particulars and report to their insurers.

What to prepare before you report

Have these ready: the other driver's NRIC, name, telephone number, address and insurer, vehicle numbers, and your photos of the scene and the damage. Write down what happened while it is fresh.

Our statement helper can help you organise your account in plain words. It does not file anything for you.

Keep a copy of anything you submit, and note the date and time you reported. If a question comes up later, you can show when you acted.

Private settlement does not remove the duty to report

Some drivers agree to settle between themselves. The GIA framework says a private settlement memorandum is a private matter between the two parties and should not be regarded as a substitute for the reporting process. Report all accidents even if settled privately.

Even a friendly agreement can fall apart later if repair costs turn out higher than expected. Reporting protects your position whatever the other driver decides.

If you reported late

GIA says you will not be penalised for late reporting if you have a valid reason. Insurers decide each case, so explain your situation clearly and early. SGCarAccident is not an insurer, reporting centre or claims representative, and cannot report for you.

If you are not sure whether your accident needs a Traffic Police report, check the list above, and if still unsure, ask your insurer.

Questions people ask

Is a police report the same as telling my insurer?

No. A Traffic Police report is not equivalent to a report to your insurer. You still need to tell your insurer.

Where do I make a Traffic Police report?

Online through the SPF e-services Traffic Accident Report with Singpass, or at a police station.
