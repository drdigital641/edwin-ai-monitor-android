What happens if the other driver is at fault?

Even when you believe you were not to blame, you still have steps to take, starting with your own insurer.

Updated 22 Nov 2026 · Sources: GIA, SPF

In short

  • Report to your own insurer, even if you think you were not at fault.
  • Keep photos, particulars and repair documents.
  • Your insurer does not make the third-party claim for you.
  • Use your insurer's authorised workshop for repairs.

Believing you are not at fault is only the start

It is natural to feel the other driver is responsible. But fault is assessed by insurers and, where needed, the proper authorities. We do not decide fault, and nobody can promise a claim will be accepted.

What you can do is protect your position by acting early and keeping good records.

Staying calm and organised is the best way to help your case, whatever the final view on fault turns out to be.

Also keep in mind that the other driver may see things differently. Your account should stick to what you saw and did.

Report to your own insurer

Insurer guidance is to report every accident, no matter how small. Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

Failing to report can cost you protection under your policy, so do not skip this because the other driver caused the accident.

Make the report as soon as you can, and keep your photos safe in more than one place, such as your phone and a cloud backup.

Keep your evidence

Evidence helps everyone understand what happened. Keep:

  • Photos of the scene and the damage to all vehicles
  • The other driver's NRIC, name, telephone number, address and insurer
  • Vehicle numbers
  • Witness contacts, if any
  • A short written account made soon after

Our statement helper can help you write your account clearly.

If the other driver gives you their details by phone message, keep the message. Do not rely on memory for numbers and names.

Making the claim

The General Insurance Association explains that your insurer does not make the third-party claim for you. You may lodge a damage claim under your own policy, or you may approach the other party's insurer.

Tell the workshop which route you are using. Tokio Marine advises saying whether you are claiming from your own policy or against the third party's policy. The other party's insurer may also survey your vehicle.

Ask your insurer in plain terms what happens to your policy record under each route, then make your choice. Keep notes of what you are told.

Repairs

GIA advises going to an authorised workshop of your insurer for repairs, even if you are claiming against the other party. Ask your insurer before you start any work.

Do not agree to a quick cash deal that you have not checked against your insurer's advice, as the real repair cost may be higher.

If the other side disputes it or delays

If the other party's insurer gives trouble, GIA says you can approach the insurer concerned directly. FIDReC exists for disputes with insurers. Do not sign promissory notes or agree to terms you do not understand. Terms differ by insurer and policy.

Keep a log of calls, emails and messages, with dates. If the matter drags on, a clear record helps you stay organised and helps anyone you ask for advice.

Questions people ask

If the other driver is at fault, do I still need to tell my insurer?

Yes. Insurers advise reporting every accident, and failing to report can affect your cover.

Will the other driver's insurer definitely pay?

No one can promise that. The other insurer will assess the claim, and you can approach it directly if there are problems.
