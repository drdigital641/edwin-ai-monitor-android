How to make a third-party insurance claim

A third-party claim means you seek compensation from the other driver's side, and your own insurer does not do that for you.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • Report to your own insurer first, even if you were not at fault.
  • Your insurer does not make the third-party claim on your behalf.
  • Tell the workshop whether you claim under your policy or against the other party.
  • Disputes with an insurer may go to FIDReC.

What a third-party claim is

A third-party claim is made against the other driver, usually through their insurer, when you believe they are responsible for your loss. It is different from a claim under your own policy.

We do not decide who is at fault. That is for the insurers, and the proper authorities where needed.

Many drivers use the term loosely, so it helps to be clear: a third party is simply the other person involved, not you and not your insurer.

In everyday talk, people say a third-party claim when they want the other driver's insurer to pay for their repairs or other losses. That is the sense used on this page.

Start by reporting to your own insurer

Even if you think the other driver caused the accident, report to your own insurer. Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

Go to an authorised workshop of your insurer for repairs, even if you plan to claim against the other party.

Reporting protects you even if the other side later disputes what happened. It also lets your insurer know that a claim may follow.

Your insurer does not claim for you

The General Insurance Association explains that your insurer does not make the third-party claim for you. You may lodge a damage claim under your own policy, or you may pursue the other party.

Which route is better depends on your policy and your situation. Check your policy wording, and ask your insurer how each option may affect your record. Terms differ by insurer and policy.

If you are unsure which route to pick, ask your insurer to explain how each might affect your policy, then decide. Take notes of what you were told.

Dealing with the other driver's insurer

The other party's insurer may survey your vehicle, as noted in Sompo's claim procedure. Keep your photos, the exchange of particulars and any repair documents together.

Tokio Marine advises telling the workshop whether you are claiming from your own policy or against the third party's policy. This avoids confusion over who is billed.

Respond to the other insurer promptly and politely. Slow replies can delay everything, including your repairs.

Documents worth keeping

Keep the following in one place:

  • Photos of the scene and the damage
  • The other driver's particulars and vehicle number
  • Your accident report reference
  • Repair quotations and invoices
  • Messages with insurers and the workshop

Do not sign promissory notes or admit liability to the other side. If you are unsure, speak to your insurer first.

Check every document for the correct dates, vehicle numbers and names before you send it, since small mistakes can slow a claim down.

It also helps to keep a simple timeline: when the accident happened, when you reported, when you got quotes and when you spoke to each insurer. A clear timeline makes any later conversation easier.

If the other insurer gives trouble

GIA says you can approach the insurer concerned directly. FIDReC exists for disputes between consumers and insurers. We are not a law firm or claims representative, and we cannot promise any claim will be accepted.

Questions people ask

Can my insurer claim from the other driver for me?

GIA says your insurer does not make the third-party claim for you. You may lodge a claim under your own policy or pursue the other party.

Do I still report to my insurer if the other driver was at fault?

Yes. Insurers advise reporting every accident, and you should still use an authorised workshop of your insurer for repairs.
