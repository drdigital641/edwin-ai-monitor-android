What happens if I am at fault in a car accident?

If you think you caused the accident, report promptly and let your insurer guide the next steps.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • Report to your insurer on time, even if you feel responsible.
  • Do not make offers or settle with the other party yourself.
  • What is covered depends on your policy type and terms.
  • A third-party-only plan may not pay for your own repairs.

First, who decides fault?

Feeling responsible at the scene is not the same as a decision on fault. Insurers, and where needed the proper authorities, assess what happened. We do not decide fault, and this page cannot tell you who is at fault in your case.

Many drivers blame themselves quickly, only to find later that other factors played a part. That is why you should not rely on your own first impression, and should leave the assessment to the people whose job it is.

Report to your insurer

Report the accident to your own insurer, no matter how small. Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

Late or missed reporting can put your cover and your No Claim Discount at risk. Take your vehicle to the approved reporting centre or authorised workshop.

Reporting on time also keeps your options open. If you have a valid reason for any delay, tell your insurer early.

Do not admit liability or make offers

AIG, MSIG and Sompo all say the same in their claims guidance. Do not admit liability, negotiate, make offers, or sign promissory notes with the other party.

Be polite and share facts. If the other driver asks for money on the spot, say you will pass the matter to your insurer.

It is also wise not to post about the accident on social media or in group chats, because your words can be read back later.

What your policy may cover

Cover depends on your policy. Two examples help show the range:

  • Comprehensive policies may include cover for your own vehicle's damage and for damage you cause to others, subject to the terms.
  • A third-party-only plan, such as the one Direct Asia describes, only pays when you have damaged another person's car. You pay your own repairs, and there is no fire and theft cover.

Check your policy schedule and ask your insurer what applies. Excesses and conditions differ.

If you are ever unsure what kind of policy you hold, the policy schedule usually says. Your insurer can also confirm it when you call.

How it may affect your record

A claim can affect your No Claim Discount or renewal terms. The effect depends on the insurer and your policy. Do not skip reporting to avoid this, because failing to report can have worse results, including the insurer refusing liability.

Ask your insurer how a claim may affect your renewal, so you can plan ahead without surprises.

If the other driver or their insurer contacts you directly, be polite, take their details, and tell them you will respond after speaking to your insurer.

Where to go next

Keep copies of everything you send and receive, and write down the names of anyone you speak to at your insurer.

Questions people ask

Should I pay the other driver at the scene?

No. Insurer guidance is not to admit liability, negotiate or make offers to the other party. Refer the matter to your insurer.

Will my own repairs be covered if I caused the accident?

It depends on your policy. A third-party-only plan generally does not pay for your own vehicle, while other policy types may, subject to their terms.
