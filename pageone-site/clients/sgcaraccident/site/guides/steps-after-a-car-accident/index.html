Steps after a car accident: a scene checklist

Follow these steps in order while you are still at the scene, and keep the list on your phone.

Last checked 2026-11-21

In short

  • Stop, stay calm and check for injuries before anything else.
  • Photograph everything before the cars move, if it is safe.
  • Swap NRIC, contact, address, insurer and vehicle numbers.
  • Say what happened, but do not admit liability or sign anything.

Step 1 and 2: stop and check for injuries

Stop your vehicle and switch on your hazard lights. Take a breath. Check yourself and your passengers, then the people in the other vehicles.

If anyone is hurt, call 995 for an ambulance. Call 999 if you need the Police. Do not move an injured person or the vehicles. Wait for help.

If you feel dizzy or unwell, say so. Some injuries are not obvious at first, and it is better to get checked than to assume you are fine.

Stay out of moving traffic while you check on people. Your safety matters more than the car.

Step 3: take photos first

Insurer guidance says not to move your vehicle before taking photos. If nobody is hurt and the road is safe, take pictures before anything shifts.

Try to capture the following:

  • All vehicles from a distance, to show their positions
  • Close-ups of the damage on every vehicle
  • Vehicle number plates
  • Road markings, signs and traffic lights nearby

If the cars are blocking traffic and you must move them, take a photo or note their positions first.

Photos are quick and cost nothing, so take more than you think you need. Include a wider shot that shows where the vehicles ended up, because a close-up alone can be hard to place later.

Step 4: exchange particulars

Ask the other driver for their NRIC, full name, telephone number, address and insurer. Note the vehicle numbers. Give them your details too.

If there are witnesses, ask if they are willing to share a contact number. Keep the exchange calm and short.

Write down the other driver's details somewhere safe. A photo of their NRIC or licence may not be acceptable to them, so ask first and note the details by hand if needed.

Step 5: be careful what you say

Describe what happened if asked, but do not admit liability, negotiate, make offers or sign promissory notes. Insurers such as AIG, MSIG and Sompo say the same in their claims guidance.

You may feel pressure to settle on the spot. You can always say you will speak to your insurer first.

Step 6: arrange a tow if you need one

If your vehicle cannot be driven, call your insurer's hotline for a tow truck. Avoid unauthorised tow-truck operators who approach you at the scene.

Never accept a tow from someone who simply turns up. Using the hotline your insurer gives you keeps the process clear and avoids surprise charges.

Step 7: decide who to report to

Plan to report to your own insurer, no matter how small the accident. Check our overview page for the timeframe. A Traffic Police report is needed only in certain cases, and it does not replace your insurer report.

This checklist is general guidance. Your policy terms may differ, so refer to your own insurer. We are not an insurer, law firm, reporting centre or claims representative.

When you get home, write down what happened while it is fresh, including the time, the road and the weather. Our statement helper can help you do this in plain words.

Questions people ask

Should I move my car after a minor bump?

Insurer guidance says not to move your vehicle before taking photos. If it blocks traffic and it is safe, record the positions first, then move it.

What if the other driver refuses to give their details?

Note their vehicle number and take photos of their car. You can then report to your insurer and, if the case needs it, the Police.
