Windscreen claims

Chipped or cracked glass? Check your cover first.

What cover do you have?

Many comprehensive policies include windscreen cover, but terms differ by insurer and plan. Check your policy schedule for the windscreen excess, whether chips are covered, and whether a windscreen claim affects your NCD.

This usually doesn't cover accidental damage to your own car. Check your policy for any windscreen add-on. If none, a private repair or replacement quote is the option.

Third-party-only cover pays for damage you cause to others, not repairs to your own car. Your windscreen repair would be at your own cost.

Your certificate of insurance or policy schedule names your cover type. Your insurer can also tell you.

Chipped or cracked: what to do

  • Take photos: the whole windscreen with the plate in view, a close-up of the damage, and the view from inside
  • Don't wait if the crack is in the driver's line of sight or growing
  • Tell your insurer before repairs if you want to claim

How excess and claim conditions can apply

Insurers set their own terms. For example, MSIG's MotorMax page lists a $100 windscreen excess, waived when the glass is repaired instead of replaced, and says such claims don't affect the NCD; MSIG also says chipped or scratched windscreens are not claimable. Income's Drivo page lists a $100 windscreen excess per claim.

Your policy may differ. Read your own schedule or ask your insurer before you decide.

When a private quotation may make sense

  • You have third-party-only cover, or no windscreen cover
  • The excess is close to the repair cost
  • You'd rather not make a claim

Questions people ask

Is every comprehensive policy's windscreen cover the same?

No. Excess, limits, what counts as damage and the effect on your NCD differ by insurer and plan. Check your own policy.

Is my windscreen covered under third-party-only insurance?

Usually not. Third-party-only cover pays for damage to other people's property and injuries, not your own car.
