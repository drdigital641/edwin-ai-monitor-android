1. Report to your insurer

Report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop. (GIA Motor Claims Framework)

At the reporting centre, staff help you complete and submit the final accident report.

2. Police report: only in these cases

A Traffic Police report is needed when the accident involves any of these:

  • Someone died
  • Someone was taken to hospital by ambulance, or got 3 or more days of medical leave
  • Government property was damaged
  • A foreign vehicle was involved
  • A pedestrian or cyclist was involved
  • Hit and run

Make the Police report online through the SPF e-services Traffic Accident Report (Singpass needed), or at any police station. A Police report does not replace your report to your insurer.

Insurer reporting and Traffic Police reporting are not the same. A Police report does not replace your insurer report.

3. What to bring

  • Your vehicle, damaged or not, if it can be driven
  • Your NRIC and driving licence
  • Your certificate of insurance
  • The other driver's name, NRIC, phone number, address, insurer and vehicle number
  • Photos of the scene and all vehicles
  • Dashcam footage
  • Police report, if you made one

Insurers' document lists vary a little. Your reporting centre will tell you if they need anything else.

Questions people ask
Must I report an accident if there is no damage?
Yes. GIA says all motorists have to report any accident to their insurer, no matter how small, and take the vehicle, damaged or not, to the reporting centre.
Does making a statement here submit my report?
No. It only helps you prepare. You still report to your insurer at a reporting centre, and to the Police if your case needs it.
What if I report late?
Report as soon as you can. GIA says you won't be penalised for late reporting if you have a valid reason; otherwise late or no reporting can affect your claim and NCD.
