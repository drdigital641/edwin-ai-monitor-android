How to report a car accident in Singapore

Three quick screens, then a checklist.

Do you also need a Police report?

Make a Police report if the accident involves any of these:

  • Someone died
  • Someone was taken to hospital by ambulance, or got 3 or more days of medical leave
  • Government property was damaged
  • A foreign vehicle was involved
  • A pedestrian or cyclist was involved
  • Hit and run

Report online through the SPF e-services Traffic Accident Report (Singpass needed) or at any police station. A Police report does not replace your report to your insurer.

If none apply, you report to your insurer only.

Report to your insurer

The GIA Motor Claims Framework says: report the accident to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day. Take your vehicle, damaged or not, to your insurer's approved reporting centre or authorised workshop.

Checklist

  • Photos of all cars, plates and the scene
  • The other driver's name, contact, plate and insurer
  • Your driving licence, NRIC and insurance certificate
  • Your vehicle, if it can be driven
  • Camera footage saved

Questions people ask

Do I need to report every car accident to the Police in Singapore?

No. A Police report is needed for the cases listed in this guide, such as injury or death. Every accident must still be reported to your insurer.

How long do I have to report an accident to my insurer?

Within 24 hours or by the next working day, under the GIA Motor Claims Framework.

Where do I report my accident?

At your insurer's reporting centre or authorised reporting point. See our list of insurer reporting centres.
