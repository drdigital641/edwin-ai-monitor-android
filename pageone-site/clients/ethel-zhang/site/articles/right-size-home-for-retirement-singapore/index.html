How do I right-size my home for retirement in Singapore?

By Ethel Zhang · Associate Division Director, Huttons Asia Pte Ltd · Updated 16 October 2026

Right-sizing for retirement means choosing a home that fits your actual daily life, not the one you needed twenty years ago. In Singapore, that often means unlocking equity from a larger property, reducing upkeep costs and ensuring CPF and cash reserves work together to support a comfortable, sustainable retirement.

What does right-sizing actually mean in a Singapore context?

Right-sizing is not simply downsizing. It is the deliberate process of matching your home to where you are in life — your health, your mobility needs, proximity to family, and the monthly costs you are comfortable carrying. For some retirees, that means moving from a five-room HDB flat to a three-room or smaller unit. For others it means shifting from a private condominium to a resale HDB flat to free up cash, or moving closer to children and grandchildren.

The goal is not to own the smallest or cheapest property. The goal is to own the right property — one that supports your wellbeing and gives you financial breathing room for the decades ahead.

How does selling my current home affect my CPF Ordinary Account?

When you sell a property that was partly funded by CPF savings, the CPF funds used plus the accrued interest must be returned to your CPF account upon sale. This is a common surprise for people who assume they will pocket the full sale proceeds. The amount returned goes back into your Ordinary Account and counts towards your retirement balances.

Before you commit to any plan, log in to the CPF Board portal to check your current CPF usage and accrued interest on your property. This figure directly affects how much cash you will have available after the sale. Confirm the exact rules and your personal position with CPF Board directly.

What are the HDB rules I need to know when right-sizing?

If you are moving into an HDB flat — whether for the first time in years or returning from private property — HDB eligibility rules apply. These include citizenship requirements, household nucleus rules, the income ceiling for new flats, and the minimum occupation period of your existing HDB flat if you own one. There is also a wait-out period if you are moving from a private residential property to an HDB resale flat; confirm the current duration with HDB directly as rules are updated periodically.

Seniors considering right-sizing may also want to explore HDB's lease buyback scheme or the Senior Priority Scheme, which are designed to support older owners. Check the HDB website for the latest eligibility criteria and scheme details before making any plans.

  • Check if your current HDB flat has met its Minimum Occupation Period before selling.
  • Confirm the private-to-HDB wait-out period on the HDB website before committing to a timeline.
  • Review the Lease Buyback Scheme if you prefer to stay in your current flat and monetise part of the lease.
  • Verify income ceiling and flat-type eligibility if purchasing a new HDB flat.

How do I work out whether the numbers actually make sense?

A rough framework is to ask three questions. First, after returning CPF accrued interest and paying seller's stamp duty if applicable, how much cash will you have from the sale? Second, what will the replacement property cost, and how much of that can be funded by CPF and a mortgage — bearing in mind that loan limits for borrowers near or past retirement age may be tighter? Third, will the remaining cash and CPF balances, combined with any other income or savings, support the lifestyle you want?

On mortgage borrowing, the Total Debt Servicing Ratio and Loan-to-Value limits set by MAS mean that borrowing capacity typically reduces as you age. A licensed mortgage broker or your bank can give you a specific assessment. Never assume you can borrow the same amount you could at forty.

Do not overlook ongoing costs: maintenance fees, property tax, and the practical costs of moving and renovating. These can add up to a meaningful sum and should be factored into your cash-flow planning before you decide.

What lifestyle factors matter as much as the finances?

Proximity to healthcare facilities, access to public transport, and the layout of the home itself — lifts, step-free access, single-storey living — become increasingly important as you age. A property that saves you money on paper but adds stress to daily life is not a good right-size.

Think about your social life too. Being near family, friends, community centres or places of worship contributes to wellbeing in retirement in ways that are hard to quantify but very real. The best financial plan is one you actually feel good living inside.

  • Single-level or lift-accessible unit for ease of mobility.
  • Proximity to MRT or bus interchange.
  • Nearby polyclinic, hospital or specialist clinic.
  • Community or recreational facilities that match your interests.
  • Distance from children or close family if that matters to you.

How do I decide what is right for my own situation?

Every household is different. A couple with grown children living abroad has a different set of priorities from a single retiree who wants to stay near siblings, or a family where one spouse is still working while the other has retired. There is no universal answer.

A useful starting point is to write down what a good day looks like in retirement — where you are, what you are doing, who you are near — and then ask which type of home supports that picture. From there, the financial planning becomes a way of making that vision viable, rather than the other way around.

Speaking with an experienced property adviser who works through your goals before your options can help you avoid the common mistake of choosing a property first and fitting your life around it afterwards. The adviser's role is to help you ask the right questions, map out a realistic timeline, and connect the property decision to your broader retirement and legacy plans — not to push you towards any particular transaction.

Questions people also ask

Can I buy an HDB flat if I have been living in a private property?

Yes, but a wait-out period applies after you dispose of your private residential property before you can purchase an HDB resale flat. The duration of this wait-out period has been adjusted over time, so always verify the current rule on the HDB website or with HDB directly before planning your timeline.

Will right-sizing affect my CPF LIFE payouts?

Returning CPF funds from a property sale replenishes your Ordinary Account balance. Depending on your age and the amounts involved, there may be options to top up your Retirement Account to enhance your CPF LIFE monthly payouts. Confirm the transfer rules and thresholds with CPF Board, as these are subject to change.

Is there stamp duty when I buy a smaller replacement home?

Buyer's Stamp Duty applies to all residential property purchases in Singapore. Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty may also apply depending on your residency status, how many residential properties you own at the time of purchase, and other factors. Check IRAS for the current rates and your specific situation before committing.

What if I want to stay in my flat but still access some of its value?

HDB's Lease Buyback Scheme allows eligible flat owners to sell a portion of their remaining lease back to HDB and receive proceeds that are channelled into CPF LIFE. This can be a way to monetise your property without moving. Eligibility criteria apply; check the HDB website for current details.

How far in advance should I start planning a right-sizing move?

Planning at least two to three years ahead gives you time to understand your CPF position, check eligibility rules, monitor the resale market without pressure, and coordinate the sale and purchase in a way that suits your household. Starting early also means you have the option to wait for the right replacement property rather than making a rushed decision.
