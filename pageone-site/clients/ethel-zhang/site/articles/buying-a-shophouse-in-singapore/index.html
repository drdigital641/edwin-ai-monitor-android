Buying a shophouse in Singapore: what to check first

By Ethel Zhang · Associate Division Director, Huttons Asia Pte Ltd · Updated 16 October 2026

Before buying a shophouse, decide whether it is for your own business or for investment, then check the zoning and allowed use, any conservation rules, the tenure, the existing tenancy and the full cost, including stamp duty and GST. A shophouse that cannot be used the way you plan is an expensive mistake.

Own use or investment?

An owner-occupier cares about layout, frontage, approved use and whether the space suits the business. An investor cares about the tenant, the lease terms, rental demand in the street and the exit. The same unit can be right for one and wrong for the other.

Check the zoning and allowed use

What a shophouse may be used for depends on its zoning and approved use. Uses such as food and beverage often need specific approvals. Check with URA before relying on a planned use, and ask for the approved floor plans.

Conservation shophouses

Many shophouses are conserved buildings. Restoration and alteration works must follow URA's conservation guidelines, which can affect renovation cost and timing.

Tenure and the existing lease

  • Freehold, 999-year or leasehold, and how much lease is left.
  • Whether there is a sitting tenant, the rent, the remaining lease and any renewal options.
  • Who is responsible for repairs and the building's condition.

Costs people forget

Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty applies to residential property, not to commercial property, but Buyer's Stamp Duty still applies, and GST may be charged if the seller is GST-registered. Commercial loans are set by each bank, so check financing early. Renovation and fitting-out can also be significant.

Plan the purchase

  1. Agree the purpose: own use, investment or both.
  2. Confirm zoning, approved use and any conservation conditions.
  3. Check financing and the full cost, including GST.
  4. Review the tenancy and building condition.
  5. Negotiate, then complete with your lawyer.

Questions people also ask

Is ABSD payable on a shophouse?

Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty applies to residential property. For commercial property, Buyer's Stamp Duty still applies, and GST may be charged if the seller is GST-registered. Mixed-use properties can be treated differently, so check with IRAS and your lawyer.

Can I run a café in any shophouse?

No. It depends on the zoning and approved use, and F&B usually needs specific approvals and licences. Check the allowed use before you commit.

Should my business buy or rent its premises?

It depends on your cash flow, how long you will stay, and whether the property also makes sense as an investment. Ethel compares both routes with you.
