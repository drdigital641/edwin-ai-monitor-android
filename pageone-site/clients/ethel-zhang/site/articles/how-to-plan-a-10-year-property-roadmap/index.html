How to plan a 10-year property roadmap

By Ethel Zhang · Associate Division Director, Huttons Asia Pte Ltd · Updated 16 October 2026

A 10-year property roadmap starts with what you want your life to look like, checks your real financial position, compares every realistic option, makes sure the choice fits your family and career, and ends with a clear sequence of steps. Ethel calls this the D.R.E.A.M. Method.

D: Discover the dream

Describe your life in 10 years: where you live, who lives with you, how you work and what you want property to do for you. Lifestyle, wealth, income, a life transition and legacy are all valid purposes, but they lead to different decisions.

R: Review your financial reality

List your cash, CPF, income stability and existing commitments. Work out a comfortable ceiling, not the maximum a bank would lend, and keep a buffer.

E: Evaluate all viable options

Put staying, renovating, upgrading, right-sizing and adding an investment property side by side. Compare cost, risk and how each one serves the purpose from step one.

A: Align property with your whole life plan

Check the shortlist against your family plans, career or business, timing and legacy. The right option is the one that pulls in the same direction as everything else.

M: Masterplan the execution

  1. Order the steps (for example, sell, then buy, then renovate).
  2. Set target dates and the decision points in between.
  3. Decide what would make you pause or change course.
  4. Review the plan once a year or when life changes.

Questions people also ask

Why plan property over 10 years?

Buying and selling are costly, so most homes are held for years. A 10-year view helps you choose a property that still fits after a marriage, a child, a job change or caring for parents.

How often should I review my property plan?

Once a year, and whenever something big changes, such as income, family or government rules.

Do I need an agent to make a property plan?

No, but an experienced adviser can help you see options and risks you may miss, and connect the plan with the market.
