Should I upgrade my home or stay put? A 10-year way to decide

By Ethel Zhang · Associate Division Director, Huttons Asia Pte Ltd · Updated 16 October 2026

Upgrade when a new home clearly does something your current one cannot, such as space for a growing family, a better location for work or school, or a long-term plan, and when you can afford it with a buffer left over. If the main reason is that "everyone is upgrading" or a launch looks attractive, staying put is often the stronger choice.

Start with what the next home must do

Most upgrade conversations start with a project, a location or a price. Ethel starts somewhere else: what should the next home do for your life over the next 10 years? Write down the two or three things your current home cannot give you. If you struggle to name them, that is useful information.

  • More space for children, parents or working from home.
  • A shorter commute or a school you want to be near.
  • A home that also supports a longer-term wealth or retirement plan.

Check your financial reality, not just your loan limit

A bank may lend you more than is comfortable. Look at the full picture: cash and CPF available for the down payment, stamp duty, legal and renovation costs, and the monthly instalment if interest rates rise. Leave a buffer for a job change, a new baby or caring for parents. CPF's home ownership pages explain how CPF can be used for housing.

Compare more than one route

Upgrading is not one decision. Compare at least three realistic routes side by side:

RouteGood whenWatch out for
Stay and renovateLocation still works and the gap is mostly space or layoutRenovation cost versus value added
Sell, then buyYou need the sale proceeds for the next purchaseTemporary housing and timing
Buy, then sellYou want to move once, with the next home readyStamp duty rules and carrying two loans

Stamp duty can change the numbers a lot when you own more than one property, even briefly. IRAS sets out the current Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty rules and remission conditions.

Align it with the rest of your life

A good upgrade fits your career, your family plans and your timing. If you might change jobs, start a business or support parents in the next few years, factor that in before choosing a bigger commitment.

Plan the execution

  1. Agree the purpose and the budget ceiling as a family.
  2. Get your loan position confirmed in principle.
  3. Decide the sequence: sell first, buy first, or renovate.
  4. Set a timeline with a fallback if a step takes longer.

Questions people also ask

Is it better to upgrade when prices are high?

Price level matters less than whether the move fits your plan. When you sell and buy in the same market, both sides tend to move together; the bigger risks are overcommitting on the loan or getting the timing between sale and purchase wrong.

How do I know if I can afford to upgrade?

Add up cash and CPF for the down payment, stamp duty and other costs, then test the monthly instalment at a higher interest rate. If the plan only works when nothing goes wrong, it is too tight.

Can I keep my current home and buy another?

Sometimes, but owning two homes usually brings Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty and stricter loan limits. Check IRAS and your bank before deciding.
