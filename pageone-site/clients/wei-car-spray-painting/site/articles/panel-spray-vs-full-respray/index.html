Panel spray or full respray: which does your car need?

Most cars only need a few panels painted. Here is how to tell when that is enough and when a full respray makes more sense.

By Wei Car Spray Painting · Updated 1 November 2026

What is the difference?

A panel spray repaints only the damaged panels, such as one door or a bumper. A full respray repaints every exterior panel of the car. Panel work costs less and keeps the original factory paint on the rest of the car; a full respray gives one even colour and finish everywhere.

When panel spray is enough

Choose panel spray when the damage is local: a scratched door, a scuffed bumper corner, a dented fender. Paint is mixed from your car's paint code and blended into the next panel where needed, so the repair matches.

  • One to a few damaged panels
  • The rest of the paint is still glossy
  • You want to keep as much original paint as possible

When a full respray makes sense

A full respray is worth it when the problem is everywhere, not in one spot.

  • Clear coat peeling on the roof, bonnet and boot
  • Paint faded all over after years in the sun
  • Many chips, dents and scratches all around the car
  • You want a new colour (check LTA rules on OneMotoring first)

A quick test you can do

Wash the car and look at it in daylight from a few metres away. If the damage is in two or three places and the other panels still shine, panel spray is usually the answer. If the whole car looks dull or patchy, ask about a full respray.
