Key Takeaways

  • Prioritise people and scene safety before documenting vehicle damage.
  • Photograph the scene and exchange driver and vehicle details when it is safe.
  • Report promptly to your insurer; minor damage or private discussions do not automatically remove reporting duties.

A collision can leave you shaken before you have even looked at the damage. First, check for injuries and make the scene safe

Before taking photographs or discussing damage, check whether anyone is hurt. If there is an injury, immediate danger, fire, a vehicle blocking a live lane, or another urgent risk, get to a safe place and contact the appropriate emergency service. Do not stand in a traffic lane to take pictures. If the vehicles can be moved safely and doing so will not create a hazard, move them out of the way; if not, keep a safe distance and follow the authorities’ instructions.

Switch on hazard lights when appropriate. Do not attempt to drive a vehicle that has leaking fluid, a damaged wheel, steering or braking problems, deployed airbags, a warning that indicates it is unsafe, or significant structural damage. Arrange suitable assistance instead. For an unroadworthy vehicle, see our guide to breakdown and accident towing in Singapore.

Record the scene before vehicles are moved, if it is safe

Once everyone is safe, preserve accurate evidence. Take wide photographs showing the vehicle positions and road layout, then closer images of damage to every affected vehicle. Capture number plates, lane markings, traffic signals, nearby signs and any visible debris. If there are witnesses, ask politely for their names and contact details. Keep the original images with their timestamps, and do not edit or annotate the only copies.

Exchange details with the other driver: name, contact information, vehicle registration number and insurer, where available. Be factual and civil. Do not argue at the scene, make threats, sign a blank form, or promise that you will pay a particular amount. Avoid admitting liability or speculating about fault before the circumstances have been assessed. A clear record is more useful than a rushed conclusion.

For example, imagine a driver from Hougang is involved in a low-speed rear impact on the CTE while travelling in a Honda Vezel. Even if the bumper looks lightly marked, the driver should first check for injuries, photograph both vehicles and the scene safely, exchange details, and follow the insurer’s reporting instructions. A visible scratch alone does not establish the full extent of damage or who is responsible.

Report the accident to your insurer on time

In Singapore, the Motor Claims Framework generally requires motorists to report an accident to their insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day, including accidents that appear minor and cases where the parties are discussing a private settlement. Your motor policy may set specific reporting instructions. Do not assume that an informal promise from the other driver removes your reporting obligation; check with your insurer and follow its process promptly.

Use your insurer’s accident-reporting channel or authorised reporting centre and keep the report reference, documents and communications. Ask what they need next and whether the car must be brought to a reporting centre, an approved repairer, or another inspection location. If the incident involves injury, a hit-and-run, a foreign-registered vehicle, damage to government property, or another reportable circumstance, follow the current police-reporting requirements as well. When unsure, ask the insurer or relevant authority rather than guessing.

What should you send to the workshop?

When you are ready to discuss repair, provide the vehicle make, model and registration, the date and location of the incident, a short description of what happened, clear damage photographs, and any insurer or claim reference. Mention whether airbags deployed, warning lights appeared, the car pulls to one side, doors or boot no longer close properly, or you noticed fluid, vibration or unusual sounds. Do not conceal damage or authorise dismantling before you understand how it may affect the insurer’s assessment.

A repair workshop’s role is to inspect the vehicle, explain visible damage and the likely repair steps, prepare an estimate or assessment as appropriate, and coordinate with the insurer or claims process where authorised. The actual process depends on the policy, liability position, insurer requirements and repair arrangement. In our experience, prompt and complete documentation helps prevent avoidable delays. A written estimate should identify the work proposed and any items that remain subject to inspection or insurer approval.

Damage is not always limited to the panel you can see. A bumper impact may affect brackets, sensors or mounting points behind the cover. A door scrape may need panel work and refinishing. The workshop should inspect relevant areas and explain what is confirmed versus what needs further assessment. Common causes of delay—and how to avoid them

  • Late reporting: waiting to see whether the other party pays can conflict with policy reporting rules. Report promptly and keep a record.
  • Incomplete evidence: missing photographs, contact details or claim references can make later clarification harder. Keep a simple folder with the originals.
  • Unclear repair authority: do not assume repair can start before the insurer’s inspection or approval. Confirm the required sequence first.
  • Driving an unsafe car: damaged steering, brakes, wheels, lights or safety systems need assessment. Arrange towing rather than taking a risk.
  • Unrecorded conversations: keep messages, receipts and written instructions. If a settlement is discussed, understand what it covers and do not let it replace insurer reporting.

Repair cost factors after an accident

There is no responsible one-price answer without inspecting the vehicle and knowing the claim arrangement. Costs vary with the vehicle’s make and model, parts availability, the number of affected panels, whether clips or structural components are damaged, sensor calibration needs, paint blending, labour access and whether hidden damage appears after disassembly. A small cosmetic repair and a collision involving safety structures are not comparable jobs.

Insurance excess, policy terms, liability, approved-repairer rules and whether the claim is own-damage or third-party can also change what the owner pays and how long authorisation takes. Ask the insurer to clarify excess and claim conditions. Ask the workshop to separate confirmed work from provisional items and to explain any additional findings before they proceed. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to report a minor car accident in Singapore?

Yes, do not decide that a minor-looking accident can be ignored. The Motor Claims Framework generally requires reporting to your insurer within 24 hours or by the next working day, even if damage appears slight or a private settlement is being discussed. Check your policy and use your insurer’s required reporting channel.

What is the first thing to do after a car accident?

Check for injuries and immediate danger first. Get to safety, contact emergency services when needed, and avoid standing in traffic. Once safe, document the scene and exchange details, then report the incident to your insurer promptly and follow its instructions.

Should I accept a private settlement at the scene?

Do not rush into an agreement while shaken. A private arrangement does not necessarily remove the duty to report the accident to your insurer. Preserve evidence, understand what is being proposed, and check your policy and insurer’s instructions before deciding.

Can I drive my car after an accident?

Only if it is safe and roadworthy. Do not drive if the collision has affected steering, brakes, wheels, lights, airbags, visibility or structural integrity, or if there is a fluid leak or warning that makes the car unsafe. Arrange suitable towing and get the vehicle inspected.

When should I make a police report?

Some accidents require a police report, including certain cases involving injury, hit-and-run, a foreign-registered vehicle or damage to government property. Requirements can depend on the circumstances and current rules. Check the Singapore Police Force’s current guidance and follow your insurer’s reporting instructions promptly.

How much does accident repair cost in Singapore?

There is no reliable estimate without knowing the vehicle, damage, parts and claim route. Costs can range from modest cosmetic work to substantially more when sensors, hidden components or structural areas are affected. Request an inspection and itemised estimate, and confirm insurer approval, excess and policy terms before authorising repairs.

Misconceptions that can cause trouble

“There is only a scratch, so I do not have to report it.” Visible damage does not determine reporting requirements. Notify your insurer within the applicable timeframe and follow its process.

“If we agree privately, there is no need to keep evidence.” Keep photographs, driver and vehicle details, messages and receipts. Circumstances and repair needs can become clearer later.

“A bumper that looks fine means nothing underneath is damaged.” Covers can conceal broken mounts or affected sensors. An inspection is the way to assess the relevant components.

When to seek help immediately

  • Anyone is injured, feels unwell, or may need medical attention.
  • The vehicle is blocking traffic or there is fire, smoke, leaking fluid or another immediate hazard.
  • Steering, brakes, wheels, lights, airbags or doors are damaged, or the car does not feel safe to drive.
  • The accident involves a hit-and-run, a foreign-registered vehicle, government property or facts you are unsure how to report.
  • The other party disputes what happened, asks you to sign incomplete paperwork, or pressures you to skip insurer reporting.
