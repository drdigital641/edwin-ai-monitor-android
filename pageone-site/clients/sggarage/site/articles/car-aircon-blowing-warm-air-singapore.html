Symptoms

Symptoms include air that is cool only while moving, one side warmer than the other, weak airflow, clicking behind the dashboard, compressor cycling, a wet smell or no change when temperature controls move. Warm air after a top-up strongly suggests a leak or compressor problem. A clogged cabin filter reduces airflow but does not by itself remove refrigerant.

Root Causes

Low gas is not a normal consumable; it indicates leakage or a previous incomplete charge. Leaks occur at O-rings, hoses, condenser, evaporator or service valves. Compressor clutch, control valve and bearing failure affect pressure. Condenser fins can be blocked or damaged, and an electric fan may not operate. Electrical fuses, relays, sensors and climate-control modules add other possibilities.

Prevention

Run the system regularly, keep the condenser face clear, replace the cabin filter and report new smells or noises. Avoid the temptation to use stop-leak products. During routine maintenance ask for fan, belt and electrical checks. A cooling system fault can also contribute to engine overheating, so do not ignore a fan that never runs.

FAQs

Q: Why is my aircon cold only when driving?

A: Airflow over the condenser may hide a weak fan or poor heat rejection at idle. Pressure and fan tests are needed.

Q: Can I just top up gas?

A: Only as part of a proper diagnosis. Repeated top-ups waste money and release the symptom of a leak.

Q: How often should gas be topped up?

A: A sealed system should not need routine topping up. A declining charge warrants a leak check.

Q: Does a cabin filter cause warm air?

A: It can make airflow weak and feel warmer, but it does not usually cause loss of refrigerant cooling.

When to Seek Help

Switch off the aircon if the compressor is grinding, smoke appears, the belt smells burnt or the engine temperature rises. Seek help when cooling disappears suddenly, a warning light appears or a leak is suspected.
