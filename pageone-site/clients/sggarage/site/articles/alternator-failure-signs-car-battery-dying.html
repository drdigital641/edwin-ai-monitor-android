Alternator Failure: Signs Your Car Battery Keeps Dying

Edwin Garage·Ang Mo Kio·4 July 2026

In our Ang Mo Kio workshop, we’ve seen countless cases of car batteries failing repeatedly. Customers often assume it’s a battery issue, but the real culprit is often the alternator. Your alternator is the unsung hero that keeps your car’s electrical system running smoothly, and when it starts to fail, your battery is the first to suffer. Here’s what you need to know to spot the signs and get it fixed before it leaves you stranded on the PIE or in your HDB carpark.

What does the alternator do, and why is it important?

The alternator's job is simple but crucial: it generates electricity to recharge your car battery and power your electrical systems while you drive. Think of it as the lifeline for your car’s battery. Without it, your battery can only run for a limited time before going flat. In Singapore’s stop-and-go traffic and humid climate, the alternator faces extra stress, especially if you’re running the aircon and other electronics constantly.

What are the common signs of alternator failure?

Here are some telltale signs your alternator might be failing:

  • Dim or flickering headlights: If your headlights are noticeably dim at night or flicker when you accelerate, your alternator might not be providing enough power.
  • Battery warning light: Most modern cars display a battery-shaped warning light when there’s an issue with the charging system. Don’t ignore this light—it’s a sign to get your car checked ASAP.
  • Dead battery: If your new or recently replaced battery keeps dying, it’s a strong indication that the alternator isn’t doing its job.
  • Unusual noises: A failing alternator can produce a whining or grinding noise, often caused by a worn-out bearing or pulley.
  • Burning smell: Overheating alternators can emit a burning rubber or electrical smell, which is a clear sign of trouble.

Case Study: A Toyota Camry with a Dying Battery

We recently had a customer bring in a Toyota Camry that couldn’t hold a charge. The owner had already replaced the battery twice in the past year, but the problem kept coming back. After running a few tests, we found that the alternator was only producing half the voltage it should. The heat and constant aircon use in Singapore had caused the alternator to overheat and fail prematurely. We replaced it with an OEM part and tested the entire charging system to ensure everything else was in top shape. Problem solved, and the customer was back on the road within a day.

Can you keep driving with a failing alternator?

Short answer: no. A failing alternator gives you some warning signs before it completely dies, but once it goes, your car will stop running. You might get stranded in the middle of the CTE or at the worst possible time. It’s always better to address a failing alternator early rather than risk a breakdown.

How much does it cost to repair or replace an alternator in Singapore?

Alternator replacement is quoted after inspection, because the cost depends on your car make and model. For example, a Hyundai Avante or Honda Jazz alternator replacement might cost less compared to a premium model like a Toyota Camry. At Edwin Garage, we confirm the price before any work starts and only use high-quality replacement parts. We also perform a full charging system check to ensure your battery and alternator work perfectly together.

Can a failing alternator damage other parts of my car?

Yes, it can. When the alternator isn’t functioning properly, your car’s electrical systems start to rely solely on the battery. This extra strain can drain your battery faster and potentially damage electrical components like the ECU or even the aircon compressor.
