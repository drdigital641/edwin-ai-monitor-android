How do I know if my car battery needs replacing?

In our experience, most drivers don’t think about their car battery until it’s too late. Here are some common signs to watch out for:

  • Slow engine crank: If your car is struggling to start, especially in the morning, it could be a weak battery.
  • Dashboard warning light: A battery or alternator light on your dashboard shouldn’t be ignored.
  • Electrical issues: Dim headlights, weak aircon performance, or flickering interior lights can all point to a dying battery.
  • Battery age: On average, car batteries in Singapore last 18–24 months due to our climate. If yours is older, it’s time to check it.

We recently had a Honda Vezel come into our workshop with a no-start issue. The owner thought it was the starter motor, but after a quick diagnostic, we found the battery was completely drained. Why do car batteries fail so quickly in Singapore?

The short answer: heat and usage. Singapore’s hot and humid weather is tough on car batteries, causing faster evaporation of the battery fluid and shortening their lifespan. Add to that the constant stop-and-go traffic on roads like the PIE and CTE, and your battery is working overtime to power your car’s systems.

Another factor is exposure. If your car is parked in an HDB outdoor carpark for long hours, direct sunlight can further weaken the battery. Regular checks and a proactive replacement schedule can save you from unexpected breakdowns.

Can I replace my car battery myself?

Technically, yes. But in our experience, it’s not always straightforward. Modern cars, like the Mazda 3 or Hyundai Avante, often have electronic systems that require proper resetting after a battery change. Disconnecting the battery incorrectly can lead to issues like engine warning lights or loss of radio presets.

We’ve had cases where DIY replacements went wrong, costing the owner more in the long run. For example, a Toyota Camry owner once tried swapping the battery themselves but accidentally shorted the system, damaging the fuse box. It’s always safer (and less hassle) to let a professional handle it.

What should I do if my car battery dies unexpectedly?

If your battery dies while you’re out and about, don’t panic. Here’s what we recommend:

  • If that’s not an option, contact a towing service to bring your car to our workshop. We’ve detailed everything you need to know in our breakdown & towing guide.

Battery, alternator or starter motor? Test before you replace

A car that will not start is not always a battery problem. If a new battery keeps going flat, the alternator may not be charging it.
