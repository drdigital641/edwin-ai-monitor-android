Burning Smell from Your Car in Singapore? What Each Smell Means and When to Stop

Edwin·Master Automotive Technician·November 8, 2026·Edwin Garage, Ang Mo Kio

A burning smell can involve oil, coolant, brakes, a belt or hose, or wiring, but odour alone cannot diagnose the cause or establish driving safety. A new strong or persistent smell needs inspection. Smoke or flames, a fuel smell, overheating warnings, reduced braking, severe vibration or power loss mean stop safely, switch off if safe and do not continue driving. A burning-plastic or electrical smell also warrants stopping and not continuing until assessed.

We are Edwin Garage, a car workshop in Ang Mo Kio. This guide helps you work out what the smell probably is, how urgent it is, and what to tell the workshop so the cause can be found quickly.

Smoke, flames or suspected vehicle fire: emergency action

  1. Pull over safely and switch off the vehicle if safe to do so.
  2. Evacuate everyone immediately to a safe place away from the vehicle and traffic. Do not return for belongings.
  3. Do not open the bonnet if fire is suspected. Do not restart or touch hot or leaking components. Do not investigate fuel or high-voltage systems yourself.
  4. Contact SCDF 995 for fire or immediate danger from a safe place and give your location. Keep others away without entering traffic or putting yourself at risk.
  5. Arrange recovery rather than driving; do not restart until the vehicle has been professionally assessed. For a suspected EV/hybrid battery fire, keep clear and do not attempt to extinguish it yourself.

SCDF’s Civil Defence Emergency Handbook advises stopping, switching off, evacuating and dialling 995 for a vehicle fire, and not attempting to extinguish a suspected EV battery fire. Emergency services and recovery are separate from nonurgent workshop bookings.

When should you stop driving straight away?

Pull over safely and do not continue if any red flag below is present. Switch off if safe; if fire is suspected, follow the emergency box above rather than inspecting the vehicle yourself.

  • Smoke from under the bonnet, from a wheel or from inside the cabin.
  • The temperature gauge climbing towards hot, or a coolant warning light.
  • A red warning light such as oil pressure, brake or battery. Our guide to dashboard warning lights explains each one.
  • A smell of burning plastic or hot wiring, especially from the dashboard or fuse box area.
  • A strong fuel smell. See car fuel smell: when to stop driving.
  • Reduced braking, severe vibration or power loss. Stop safely and arrange recovery rather than continuing.

If fire is suspected, do not open the bonnet. Do not restart, touch hot or leaking components, or investigate fuel or high-voltage systems yourself. For a stopped vehicle with steam, a coolant leak or uncertain safety, keep clear and arrange recovery rather than driving. Our breakdown and towing guide covers recovery planning; fire or immediate danger requires SCDF 995, not a workshop appointment.

Burnt oil

Oil that drips or seeps onto the exhaust burns with a sharp, smoky smell, often strongest when you stop at a traffic light or park after a drive. Common sources are a worn valve cover gasket or another engine seal. Look for dark spots on the ground where you park. Our articles on Mercedes E200 oil leaks and BMW N20 oil leaks show what this looks like on two common cars.

Sweet smell (coolant)

Coolant has a sweet smell when it is hot. If you smell it from the engine bay, there may be a leaking hose, radiator or water pump. If you smell it inside the car, often with a misty windscreen, the heater core may be leaking. Low coolant can lead to overheating, so read coolant leak signs and engine overheating causes before you drive far.

Brakes

Brakes can become hot on long multi-storey car park descents, and some replacement pads may produce an odour during their specified bedding procedure. Neither a smell fading nor recent pad replacement establishes safety. A new strong, recurring or persistent smell needs inspection. Reduced braking, pulling, smoke or severe vibration means stop safely and arrange recovery; do not touch a wheel to compare temperatures or drive to test whether the smell clears. See brake wear and when to replace brake pads.

Burning rubber

A drive belt that slips on its pulleys can smell like hot rubber, often with a squeal. A hose or a loose plastic undertray touching the exhaust can smell the same. Our guide to belt squeal at startup explains the belt side.

Burning plastic or electrical smell

This is the one not to ignore. A wiring fault, a blower fan motor or resistor running hot, or an aftermarket accessory wired in badly can all smell like hot plastic. Switch off the engine and don't keep driving until it has been checked. If the smell only comes through the aircon vents and is musty rather than burnt, it is more likely a dirty cabin filter or evaporator: see why car aircon smells.

What to do in the first few minutes

  1. Find a safe place to stop, switch on your hazard lights and switch off the engine.
  2. Keep clear rather than investigating. Do not approach smoke or leaks, open a bonnet when fire is suspected, touch hot components, or inspect fuel/high-voltage systems. Move everyone away from the vehicle and traffic.
  3. Check the dashboard. Note any warning lights and where the temperature gauge was before you stopped.
  4. Note when it happens. Cold start, after a long drive, after braking, only with the aircon on, or only when parked.
  5. Do not continue with red flags or uncertain safety. Arrange recovery, not a test drive. For fire or immediate danger, contact SCDF 995 from a safe place.

Burning smell after servicing or repairs

Residual spilled oil or replacement-brake materials can produce an odour, but a recent service does not establish that a smell is harmless. There is no universal safe waiting period of one or two days. Notify the workshop promptly about a new strong, persistent or recurring smell; do not keep driving to burn it off. Smoke or flames, a fuel smell, overheating warnings, reduced braking, severe vibration or power loss mean stop safely, switch off if safe and do not continue driving. A burning-plastic or electrical smell also warrants stopping and not continuing until assessed. Arrange recovery instead of driving back when safety is uncertain.

What a workshop checks

To find the cause, a mechanic will usually:

  • Look for oil and coolant leaks around the engine, hoses, radiator and exhaust.
  • Check the coolant and oil levels and the condition of the drive belt.
  • Compare wheel temperatures and check whether any brake is dragging.
  • Look for loose undertrays, plastic or debris touching the exhaust.
  • Check for signs of hot or damaged wiring, and test the fan and charging system if needed.

The fix and its cost depend on what is found, so ask for the cause and a quote before approving any work. Our guide to what a diagnostic fee covers explains how this works at Edwin Garage.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to drive with a burning smell from my car?

Odour alone cannot establish driving safety. A new strong or persistent smell needs inspection. Smoke or flames, fuel smell, overheating warnings, reduced braking, severe vibration or power loss mean stop safely and do not continue. A burning-plastic or electrical smell also warrants stopping until assessed. For suspected fire, switch off if safe, evacuate away from the vehicle and traffic, do not open the bonnet, and contact SCDF 995. Arrange recovery rather than driving when safety is uncertain.

Why does my car smell like burning oil after driving?

Oil contacting hot engine or exhaust parts is one possible cause, including a leak or residual oil after servicing. Smell alone cannot confirm this or show that continued driving is safe. A recurring, persistent or new strong smell needs inspection; smoke, an oil-pressure warning or other stop-driving red flags require stopping safely and arranging recovery.

What does a sweet burning smell mean?

A sweet smell can be a clue to a coolant leak, but odour alone does not confirm the source. Arrange assessment for a new strong or persistent smell. Stop safely for steam, overheating warnings or other red flags; do not open a hot cooling system, and arrange recovery if safety is uncertain.

Why do my brakes smell after driving down a car park ramp?

Braking on a descent can generate heat, but a smell that fades does not prove the brakes are safe. A new strong, persistent or recurring smell needs inspection. Reduced braking, pulling, smoke, severe vibration or suspected dragging means stop safely and arrange recovery. Do not touch a wheel to assess temperature or drive to see whether the smell clears.

Is a burning smell after servicing normal?

Residual oil or new brake materials can cause an odour, but recent servicing does not establish that it is harmless. There is no universal one-to-two-day safe waiting period. Notify the workshop promptly about a new strong, persistent or recurring smell. Do not keep driving to burn it off; stop for smoke, flames, fuel smell, overheating warnings, reduced braking, severe vibration or power loss and arrange recovery.

What should I tell the workshop about a burning smell?

Tell them what the smell is like (oil, sweet, rubber, plastic, sharp brakes), when it happens (cold start, after a long drive, after braking, with the aircon on), where it seems to come from, and whether any warning light or smoke appeared. Photos of drips under the car also help.
