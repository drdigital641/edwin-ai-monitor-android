How to check a used car's mileage and service history in Singapore

Compare the odometer reading with how worn the car looks inside, ask the seller for the service records and invoices, and check that the dates and mileage on them rise together without long gaps. Then have a workshop you trust inspect the car before you pay in full, because a paper record alone does not show the car's condition.

Why do mileage and service history matter?

Mileage and service history tell you how the car was used and looked after. They sit alongside age and remaining COE when a seller sets the price, as explained in our guide to depreciation on a car listing. A low mileage reading with no records is harder to trust than an average one with a full file.

How do you check the mileage?

  1. Read the odometer, then look at the wear the car shows: the driver's seat edge, steering wheel, pedal rubbers and gear knob. Heavy wear on a low-mileage car is a question to ask.
  2. Compare the reading with the registration date. Divide the mileage by the years since registration to see roughly how much the car was driven each year.
  3. Match the figure with the listing, the records and what the seller tells you. All three should agree.
  4. Ask how the previous owner used the car: daily commute, short trips or occasional weekends.

What should the service history show?

  • Invoices or stamped records from the workshop or agent, with a date and mileage on each visit.
  • Regular servicing, with dates and mileage that rise in step and no unexplained long gaps.
  • Records of larger repairs and replaced parts such as tyres, brakes and the battery, so you know what has been done and what may be due soon.
  • Whether servicing was done at one workshop or several. One long relationship is easier to verify with a phone call.

What are the red flags?

  • No records at all, and no clear reason.
  • Mileage on the records that is higher than the odometer, or does not rise in order.
  • A seller who will not let you check the car at your own workshop.

These are general checks and no single one proves anything; use them to decide what to ask next.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a used car's mileage is genuine?

Compare the odometer with the wear on the seat, steering wheel and pedals, and with the service records and the registration date. If the figures do not agree, ask the seller to explain and get the car inspected.

What service records should I ask for?

Invoices or stamped records with a date and mileage on each visit, plus records of larger repairs such as tyres, brakes and the battery.

Is a service history enough without an inspection?

No. Records show what was done, not the car's condition today. A pre-purchase inspection at a workshop you trust checks the car itself.
