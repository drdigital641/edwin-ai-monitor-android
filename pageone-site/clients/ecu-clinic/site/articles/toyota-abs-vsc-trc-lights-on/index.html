Why Are the ABS, VSC and TRC Lights On in My Toyota?

Three warning lights at once usually means one fault: what it can be, what to check first, and when the ABS unit needs repair.

By The ECU Clinic · 16 Oct 2026

Short answer: in a Toyota, the ABS (anti-lock brakes), VSC (Vehicle Stability Control) and TRC (traction control) systems work through the same brake actuator and control unit, so one fault often turns on all three lights. Common causes are a wheel speed sensor, wiring, a switch or sensor the system relies on, or the ABS unit itself. The fault code tells you which.

What do the ABS, VSC and TRC lights mean?

  • ABS is the anti-lock braking system. It stops the wheels locking when you brake hard.
  • VSC is Toyota’s stability control. It brakes single wheels to help the car hold its line in a skid.
  • TRC is traction control. It stops the wheels spinning when you pull away. On many Toyotas the light reads “TRC OFF” when the system has switched itself off.

VSC and TRC both need the wheel speed signals and the brake actuator that ABS uses. When the ABS side has a fault, the car usually switches VSC and TRC off too, which is why the lights come on together.

Is it safe to drive with the ABS, VSC and TRC lights on?

In most cases your normal brakes still work, but anti-lock braking, stability control and traction control may be switched off until the fault is fixed. The wheels can then lock under hard braking, and the car will not correct a skid on a wet road. Leave more distance and avoid hard stops. If the red BRAKE warning light is also on, or the brake pedal feels different, stop driving and get the car checked first. Our pick-up and delivery service can collect it.

Why is the check engine light on as well?

On many Toyota models, VSC and TRC are also switched off when the engine or hybrid system reports a fault, so the VSC and TRC lights can come on with the check engine light even when the brakes are fine. That is one more reason to read all the stored codes, not just the ABS ones, before any part is changed.

What are the common causes?

  • Wheel speed sensor. Each wheel has one. A dirty, damaged or failed sensor is a common cause.
  • Wiring and connectors. Sensor wiring runs close to the wheels and can be damaged or corroded.
  • Other inputs the system relies on. Stability control also uses signals such as steering angle, yaw rate and the brake light switch, so a fault there can turn on the VSC light.
  • Low brake fluid or a blown fuse. Simple causes worth ruling out first.
  • The ABS unit itself. The control module or the pump motor in the brake actuator can develop internal faults.

How do we find which part is at fault?

  1. Read the fault codes. The ABS and skid control system stores codes that point to the circuit at fault.
  2. Rule out the simple causes. Sensors, wiring, switches, fluid level and fuses.
  3. Check the unit. If the codes and tests point to the module or pump, the ABS unit is the problem.
  4. Tell you the options. You decide before any work starts.

Questions

Will the ABS, VSC and TRC lights go off by themselves?
Sometimes they go off after a restart if the fault is intermittent, but the code stays stored and the lights usually come back until the cause is fixed. Read the code first.
Can a weak battery turn on the VSC light?
Low voltage can make some control units store faults, so it is worth checking the battery and charging. If the lights stay on, the stored codes show where the fault really is.
