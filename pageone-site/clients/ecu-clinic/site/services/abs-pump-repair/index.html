About this service

ABS pumps and modules are our main work. The ABS unit sits in the engine bay and combines a hydraulic pump with an electronic control module. When the module or pump develops a fault, the ABS warning light comes on, and on many cars the ESP or traction control light comes on with it.

We start with the fault code, so we know whether the problem is the unit itself or something around it, such as a sensor or wiring. Then we tell you the options: repair your unit, or fit a reconditioned, used or new one.

Many car workshops send their ABS and module repairs to us. Come direct and deal with the technician who does the repair.

Is it safe to drive with the ABS light on?

In most cases your normal brakes still work, but the anti-lock function may be switched off until the fault is fixed. That means the wheels can lock under hard braking, especially on wet roads, so leave more distance and avoid hard stops.

If the red brake warning light comes on together with the ABS light, or the brake pedal feels different, stop driving and get the car checked first. Our pick-up and delivery service can collect it. More in our guide: ABS warning light causes.

Sensor, wiring, pump or module: how we find the fault

  1. Read the fault code. The ABS control module stores a code that points to the circuit at fault.
  2. Rule out the simple causes. Wheel speed sensors, their wiring and connectors, brake fluid level and fuses.
  3. Check the unit itself. If the code and tests point to the pump motor or the module, the ABS unit is the problem.
  4. Tell you the options. You decide before any work starts.

This order matters: the same warning light can come from a wheel speed sensor or from the ABS unit, and replacing parts without the code can mean paying for parts you did not need.
