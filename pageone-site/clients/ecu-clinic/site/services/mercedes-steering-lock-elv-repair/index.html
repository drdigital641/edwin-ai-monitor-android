About this service

Many Mercedes-Benz models use an electronic steering lock, often called the ELV or ESL. It is a small motorised unit on the steering column that locks the steering when the car is off and unlocks it when the key is turned or the start button is pressed.

If the ELV does not unlock, the car cannot start. We test the unit, repair it, and get your car driving again. WhatsApp us your model and what the car is doing, and we will tell you what we can do.

Steering lock, key or battery: what we check first

  1. The simple causes. A flat key battery, a weak car battery or an ignition switch fault can look just like a steering lock fault.
  2. The fault codes. The car’s control units store codes that show whether they can talk to the steering lock.
  3. The steering lock itself. We test the unit to confirm it is the part that is failing.
  4. Your options. Repair the steering lock, or fit an ELV emulator. We tell you what we can do before any work starts.

Your options: repair the lock or fit an ELV emulator

Repair the steering lock

We repair your car’s own electronic steering lock, so it keeps locking the steering when the car is off.

Fit an ELV emulator

A small electronic unit that takes the place of the steering lock. It gives the car the signal it expects from the lock, so the car starts. The original lock comes off, so the steering no longer locks when you switch off.

An emulator is used instead of repairing the lock when the lock itself has failed and repairing it is not the right fix for your car. We test the lock first and explain both options before any work starts.
