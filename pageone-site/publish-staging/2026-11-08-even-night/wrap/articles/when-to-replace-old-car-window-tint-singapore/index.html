When should you replace old car window tint in Singapore?

By Wrapstar Autostyle · 8 November 2026

The short answer

Replace old window tint when it bubbles, peels at the edges, looks hazy or no longer keeps the cabin cool. The old film has to come off before new film goes on, and the new film must still let through at least 70% of visible light at the front and 25% at the rear. Send us a photo of the glass and we will tell you what we would do.

What are the signs old tint needs replacing?

These are common signs, and one is usually enough to justify a look:

  • Bubbles or blisters under the film.
  • Edges that lift or peel, especially at the corners of the glass.
  • A hazy or blurry look that makes it harder to see out, particularly at night.
  • Film that has changed colour or looks patchy from the outside.
  • A cabin that feels hotter than it used to, as the film has stopped doing its job.

What happens to the old film?

It is removed first, so the new film sits on clean glass. Putting new film over old film is not a fix. Removal is a job for a studio: leftover adhesive has to be cleaned off carefully, and a rear windscreen has heater lines that careless scraping can damage. Removal of existing film was included in our opening offer for nano ceramic film; ask us for today's terms.

Does replacement tint still need to follow the rules?

Yes. Film on the front windscreen and front side windows must let at least 70% of visible light through, and at least 25% on the rear windscreen and rear side windows. The Ministry of Transport restated this on 7 April 2026. OneMotoring lists tinted films and tinted glass under modifications that do not need LTA approval, as long as the light rules are met. Read car window tint rules in Singapore for the full picture.
