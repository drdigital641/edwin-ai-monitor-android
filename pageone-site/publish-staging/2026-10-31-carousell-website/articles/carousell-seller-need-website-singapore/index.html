Do You Need a Website If You Sell on Carousell in Singapore?

Carousell is a good place to sell, but your listings and reviews sit on its platform. Here is what your own website adds and how to get one without a big bill.

Published 31 October 2026 · By Page One Singapore

You list on Carousell, reply to chats, collect ratings and get regular sales. Then a customer says they could not find you again, or a friend asks for your website. So do you need a website if you sell on Carousell in Singapore, or is the marketplace enough?

The short answer is that Carousell is enough to start, but it is not yours. This guide explains what the marketplace gives you, what it leaves out, and how to add a simple website beside it.

Do you need a website if you sell on Carousell?

No, you do not need one to sell. Carousell is built for listing items and services and chatting with buyers. But your shop there is an account on a platform you do not control. A website gives you an address of your own that customers can find on Google, save, and share, and it keeps working whatever happens to your account.

Key takeaways

  • Carousell is a good place to find buyers. Keep using it while it works for you.
  • Your profile, listings and ratings stay on the platform. You cannot take them with you.
  • A website lets people find you on Google by service, area or business name.
  • Repeat customers can come to you directly instead of searching the marketplace again.
  • AI assistants read web pages, so a website gives them something to quote about your business.
  • A site can sit beside your Google Business Profile, Facebook and Instagram, and tie them together.

What does Carousell do well for a seller?

A marketplace solves the first problem for a new seller: finding people who are already looking to buy. It is good at a few jobs:

  • Putting your listings in front of buyers browsing the app.
  • Letting you chat with buyers and answer questions quickly.
  • Showing ratings from past buyers on your profile.
  • Letting you start with no website and no setup cost.

For one-off items and quick sales, that may be all you need.

What does a Carousell shop not give you?

If you are building a real business, a few gaps show up:

  1. You compete on the same page. Buyers see your listing next to other sellers' listings. A website has only your business on it.
  2. It is hard to be found by name. A customer who remembers your business but not which app they used has little to search for. A website and Google Business Profile give your name a place to appear.
  3. You follow the platform's rules. The platform decides how listings, accounts and fees work, and it can change them. A website you own does not depend on that.
  4. Your reviews stay behind. Ratings on the platform do not move to Google or to a website if you ever leave or lose access.
  5. Little for AI tools to read. AI assistants answer from pages they can read. A website describes what you do in your own words.

The same point applies to social accounts. If you want to see how fragile a platform account can be, read what happens if your Instagram business account gets banned, and do I need a website if I have a Facebook page.

Should I stop selling on Carousell once I have a website?

No. Treat them as two jobs. Carousell brings buyers who are browsing. Your website is where people check who you are, see what you offer in one place, and contact you directly. Many small sellers use both. If you decide to add links to your website anywhere on the platform, check the platform's own rules first so your account is not put at risk.

What should a Carousell seller put on a first website?

Keep it simple. A first website only needs a few things:

  1. One clear sentence on what you sell and who it is for.
  2. A page for each main product or service, with your own photos.
  3. Prices or a 'from' price, if you are comfortable sharing them.
  4. Delivery, meet-up or service area details.
  5. Your WhatsApp number as a tap-to-chat button.
  6. Your business name, phone number and address if you have one, matching your Google Business Profile.

How do I get found on Google as a Carousell seller?

Three things work together. A website gives Google pages to read about what you sell. A Google Business Profile puts you on Maps if customers can visit you or you serve an area. Search Console shows which searches bring visitors. Nobody can promise a ranking, but these give Google something to work with that a marketplace shop does not. Frequently asked questions

Can I run my business only on Carousell?

Yes. Many Singapore sellers do, and it is a good way to start. The limit is that your profile, listings and reviews stay on the platform, so people searching Google for your business name or service may not find you, and you depend on the platform's rules.

Will a website replace my Carousell shop?

No. Keep selling on Carousell while it brings you buyers. A website is your own home base that sits beside it, so people who hear about you elsewhere can check who you are and contact you directly.

Do my Carousell reviews move to my website?

No. Reviews belong to the platform and do not transfer. A website can only show reviews you are allowed to quote, and Google reviews on your Business Profile are a separate record you can build alongside Carousell.

What should a Carousell seller put on a first website?

A short description of what you sell, your service area or delivery details, prices or a 'from' price if you are comfortable sharing it, photos of your own work or stock, and a WhatsApp button so customers can message you.
