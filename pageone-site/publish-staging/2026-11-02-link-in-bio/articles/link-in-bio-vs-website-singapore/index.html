Is a Link in Bio Page Enough for Your Business in Singapore?

A link-in-bio page is a handy signpost, but it is not a website. Here is what it does well, where it stops, and how to add a real site behind it without a big bill.

Published 2 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

Your Instagram or TikTok bio has one tappable link, so you put a link-in-bio page there with your WhatsApp, your menu or your booking form. It works, and customers do tap it. Then someone asks for your website, or a customer cannot find you on Google, and you wonder: is a link in bio enough for a business in Singapore?

The short answer is that it is enough to point followers somewhere, but it is not enough to be found. This guide sets out what a link-in-bio page does, what it leaves out, and how a simple website sits beside it.

Is a link in bio enough for a small business?

A link in bio is enough to send people who already follow you to one or two places, such as WhatsApp or a booking form. It is not enough to be found by people who do not know you yet. The page usually sits on the address of the tool that hosts it, holds only a few buttons and little text, and gives Google and AI assistants little to read about what you offer. A website covers that gap.

Key takeaways

  • A link-in-bio page is a signpost for people who already follow you.
  • It usually lives on the hosting tool's address, not a domain in your business name.
  • With only a few buttons and little text, it gives search engines and AI assistants little to read.
  • A website lets people find you by service, area or business name, and check you before they message.
  • Keep the link in bio. Point its main button to your website or WhatsApp.
  • A simple managed site can cost less than most people expect.

What does a link in bio do well?

For a business that lives on Instagram or TikTok, a link-in-bio page solves a real problem: a profile with one clickable spot. It is good at a few jobs:

  • Putting your WhatsApp chat, booking form or latest offer one tap away.
  • Being quick to set up, with no design or hosting to manage.
  • Letting you change the buttons whenever your offer changes.

If most of your customers already follow you and message you, that may be all you need today.

What does a link in bio not give you?

If you want to grow beyond the people who already know you, a few gaps show up:

  1. It is not an address in your name. The page sits on the tool's domain. A website on your own domain is something you can print on a card, say to a customer and keep if you change tools.
  2. There is little for Google to read. A page of buttons has few words about your service, your area or your prices. A website has a page for each thing you offer.
  3. There is little for AI assistants to quote. AI tools answer from pages they can read. A website describes your business in your own words.
  4. It does not answer questions before the chat. A new customer often wants to see what you do, where you work and a 'from' price before messaging. A website answers that so your chats start warmer.
  5. It depends on one platform's profile. If your account is restricted or lost, your link goes with it. Our guide on what happens if your Instagram business account gets banned explains the risk.

The same logic applies to a Facebook page, which we cover in do I need a website if I have a Facebook page.

Should I replace my link in bio with a website?

No. Use them for different jobs. Your link in bio helps followers who are already on your profile jump to the next step. Your website helps everyone else: a person who searches Google for your service, hears your name from a friend, or asks an AI assistant who to hire. Many small businesses use both, with the link in bio pointing to the website or WhatsApp.

What should a first website include if I am active on social media?

Keep it simple. A first website only needs a few things:

  1. One clear sentence on what you offer and who it is for.
  2. A page for each main service or product, with your own photos.
  3. Prices or a 'from' price, if you are comfortable sharing them.
  4. Your service area, or the place customers can find you.
  5. Your WhatsApp number as a tap-to-chat button.
  6. Your business name, phone number and address if you have one, matching your Google Business Profile.

If many customers already praise you online, a site can sit beside your Google reviews and give them somewhere to land.

How do I get found on Google if I run my business from social media?

Three things work together. A website gives Google pages to read about what you offer. A Google Business Profile puts you on Maps if customers can visit you or you serve an area. Search Console shows which searches bring visitors. Nobody can promise a ranking, but these give Google something to work with that a page of buttons does not. Frequently asked questions

Is a link in bio the same as a website?

No. A link-in-bio page is a short list of buttons that points people elsewhere, usually on the address of the tool that hosts it. A website is a set of pages on a domain in your own name, where you describe what you sell, where you work and how to contact you.

Can I use a link in bio instead of a website for my business?

You can, and many small businesses start that way. The limit is that it is a signpost, not a place that explains your business, so people who search Google for your service or business name have little to find.

Should I remove my link in bio once I have a website?

No. Keep it, and point its main button to your website or WhatsApp. It stays useful for followers who tap through from Instagram or TikTok, while the website does the job of being found and checked.

What should my link in bio point to?

Pick one main action, such as WhatsApp chat or a booking page, and add a link to your website once you have one. Too many buttons make it harder for a new customer to know what to tap.
