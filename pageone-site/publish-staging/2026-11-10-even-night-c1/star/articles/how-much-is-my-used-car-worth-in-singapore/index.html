How much is my used car worth in Singapore?

Your used car is worth what a buyer will pay for it today. In Singapore that depends mainly on the remaining COE, the car's age, mileage, condition and service history, and how much buyers want that model. Compare listings of similar cars, then ask a dealer for an offer and how they arrived at it.

What decides what a used car is worth in Singapore?

No single number sets the price. These are the things buyers and dealers look at, roughly in the order they matter:

  • Remaining COE. A car with more COE left gives the next owner more years on the road, so buyers usually pay more for it. See buying a used car with little COE left and what a COE is.
  • Age and depreciation. The yearly depreciation figure on a listing is how buyers compare cars of different ages. See what depreciation means on a car listing.
  • PARF rebate. Whether the car still has a rebate, and how much, can change what it is worth to the next buyer or to an exporter. See the PARF rebate explained.
  • Mileage and service history. Lower mileage with records is easier to trust. See how to check mileage and service history.
  • Condition. Paint, interior, tyres and any faults found at inspection.
  • The model and its specification. Some models and options are simply in more demand than others.

How do you get a realistic number?

  1. Write down your car's details: model, registration year, mileage, COE expiry date, number of owners and any modifications or known faults.
  2. Look at similar cars for sale. Match the model, year, mileage and COE left. Remember these are asking prices, not what the cars sold for.
  3. Ask for an offer from a dealer. A real offer after an inspection is worth more than a guess from a website.
  4. Ask how the figure was reached. A good dealer explains it. That also lets you compare two offers properly.

Why is a dealer's offer different from an asking price?

An asking price is what a seller hopes for. A dealer's offer has to allow for the inspection, any work the car needs before it can be sold, the time it waits for a buyer and the cost of selling it on. That is why an offer is usually below the prices you see in listings, and why it comes quickly with little effort from you. If you want to aim higher and can wait, compare it with trade-in, consignment and direct sale, and see how long consignment takes.

Does a loan change what the car is worth?

No, but it changes what you take home. The loan has to be settled with the bank before the ownership transfer, and you receive the balance after that. When you sell to Star Performance Motor, we settle any outstanding loan with the bank as part of the deal, so tell us your loan balance when you ask for an offer. The paperwork is covered in what you need to sell your car in Singapore.

We make you an offer and explain how we arrived at it, inspect the car in person to confirm its condition, and handle the ownership transfer. Frequently asked questions

How do I find out how much my used car is worth in Singapore?

Compare listings of the same model, year, mileage and remaining COE, then ask a dealer for an offer. Star Performance Motor gives you a figure and explains how it arrived at it.

Why is a dealer's offer lower than the asking prices I see online?

An asking price is what sellers hope to get. A dealer's offer reflects the inspection, any work the car needs, and the time and cost of reselling it. Ask the dealer how the figure was worked out.

Does a car with more COE left sell for more?

Usually, because the buyer gets more years of use before the COE expires. Our guide on buying a used car with little COE left shows how buyers think about it.

What should I tell a dealer to get an offer?

The model, registration year, mileage, COE expiry and a few photos. Tell us your loan balance too if there is one, because a loan is settled with the bank as part of the sale.
