Do you need to tell LTA or your insurer about a car wrap?

By Wrapstar Autostyle · 10 November 2026

The short answer

Check with LTA and your insurer before the job, and ask for the answers in writing. LTA's modification page does not list wraps among the changes that need approval first, but the rules are LTA's to confirm. Insurers advise telling them about modifications, so a quick message to yours is the safe step.

What does LTA say about modifications?

LTA's OneMotoring page on vehicle modifications (last updated 6 August 2026) describes modifications as changes from the manufacturer's original specification, including cosmetic ones. It says some changes, such as engines, exhaust systems and transmissions, need LTA approval before you make them. It does not mention wraps or colour changes, so it does not tell you either way. Window film is covered separately, and the light rules for it are in our guide to car window tint rules in Singapore.

What about the colour on your vehicle record?

This is the part to confirm. Some owners say they updated the colour on their vehicle record through OneMotoring after a colour change. We cannot tell you the current process or whether it applies to your car, so ask LTA or check OneMotoring before your job and keep the reply.

Should you tell your insurer?

Yes, it is the safer course. The General Insurance Association of Singapore advises owners to notify their motor insurer of modifications, and insurers generally ask you to declare changes to how a car looks or runs. A wrap changes how the car looks, so ask your insurer whether a colour change needs to be recorded and whether it affects your cover or premium.

What should you ask, and what should you keep?

  1. Ask LTA, through its website or hotline, whether a wrap colour change needs anything from you.
  2. Ask your insurer whether they need to be told, and when.
  3. Keep the replies in writing, with the date.
  4. Keep your quote and the colour or film name from the studio, which you may be asked for.

Does a wrap stay reversible?

Yes. A colour change wrap is a thin vinyl film over the painted panels, the factory paint stays underneath, and the film can be peeled off later. We check the paint before we start, because badly resprayed panels can lift paint when film is removed. Many owners wrap cars they plan to return or resell; see wrapping a leased or company car and removing a wrap before selling.

What else should you ask a studio before booking?

The film, the finish, which parts are covered and how it will be removed. Our list is in what to ask before booking a car wrap in Singapore. To start, see our colour change wrap service or WhatsApp a photo of your car for a quote.

Source: LTA OneMotoring, vehicle modifications (last updated 6 August 2026, read 10 November 2026). General information, not legal advice. Confirm with LTA and your insurer.

Questions

Telling your insurer is the safer course. The General Insurance Association of Singapore advises owners to notify their motor insurer of modifications. Ask your insurer whether a colour change needs a note on your policy.

Do I need to update my car's colour on the LTA record?

We can't confirm the current process for your car. Some owners have reported updating the colour through OneMotoring, so ask LTA or check OneMotoring before the job and keep the answer in writing.

Yes. The film can be peeled off later, and the factory paint stays underneath. We check your paint before we start and tell you if there is a risk.
