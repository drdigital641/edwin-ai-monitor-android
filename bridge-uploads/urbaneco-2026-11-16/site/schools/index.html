What children do

Seed to plate within a term.

The full cycle fits inside a school term, so one class sees the whole story, from a tiny seed to a salad they grew.

  • Plant about 2 seeds in each sponge cup
  • Check water and nutrient levels each week (about 10 minutes)
  • Compare crops: pak choy in 4 weeks, lettuce in 5, herbs in 7
  • Weigh, harvest and taste what they grew
  • Clean, reset and start the next cycle
  1. Wk 0

    Seed & transplant

    About 1 hour for the class.

  2. Wk 1–4

    Watch & record

    Roots, leaves and water levels, checked weekly.

  3. Wk 4–5

    Harvest day

    About 1 hour. Weigh the greens, then eat them.

  4. Reset

    Clean & replant

    About 1 hour between cycles.

Learning themes

Lessons that grow on their own.

01

Plant science

Seeds, roots, light and nutrients, seen through the clear logic of soil-free hydroponics.

02

Food & health

Where vegetables come from, and how fresh greens taste when picked the same day.

03

Sustainability

Growing food indoors, in a small space, and harvesting only what is needed.

04

Measuring & maths

Count cups, time the cycles, weigh the harvest and compare crops.

05

Two languages

Our crop list carries English and Chinese names, from 生菜 to 香港菜心.

06

Teamwork

Weekly duty rosters for checking water, and a shared harvest day.

Fits Eco Stewardship and ALP.

MOE's Eco Stewardship Programme takes a whole-school approach through the 4Cs: Curriculum, Campus, Culture and Community. A grow corner touches all four: a science lesson, a green space on campus, a weekly duty roster, and a harvest shared with the school.

Many schools teach hydroponics through their Applied Learning Programme (see the public examples below). ESP and ALP are programmes, not grants. The funding options are in the next section.

How schools fund this.

The system is suitable for White Area Grant, ESP, ALP and SG Eco Fund projects. Schools apply for these grants themselves, then buy from a vendor. This is not an MOE-subsidised product.

Schools already growing indoors.

Public examples from school pages and news reports. They are not Urban Eco installations.

  • Jing Shan Primary, Sprout Spot. Pupils grow baby bak choy, lettuce, mustard greens and kale indoors as part of the ALP "Green Explorers @ JSPS" and Farm-to-Table. Harvests go to canteen stall holders, salad days, guests or home (school page). The Straits Times reported at least eight harvests a year (ST).
  • North Vista Secondary. Its ALP is "From Farm to Table": all Secondary 1 students grow edibles with a hydroponics system (school page).
  • Chua Chu Kang Primary. Primary 3 students transplant and harvest local vegetables such as Milk Cabbage and Chye Sim, observing the plants' life cycle (school page).

Urban Eco is the indoor classroom option: 72 plants on 4 layers plus 76 seedlings, in an 811 × 433 mm footprint. In our growing trial, F1 Hybrid Special Pak Choy gave 7.77 kg per trial system in 5 weeks (trial results).
