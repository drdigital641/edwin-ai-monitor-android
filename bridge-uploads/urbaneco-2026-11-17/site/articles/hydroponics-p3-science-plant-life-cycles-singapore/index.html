Hydroponics for P3 Science: Life Cycles of Plants in Singapore

Published 17 Nov 2026

See seed, roots, leaves and harvest in one term, indoors.

WhatsApp Elaine

Growing a life cycle, not just reading about it

P3 hydroponics in Singapore lets pupils watch the plant life cycle happen instead of reading about it. In an indoor, soil-free grow system, they plant seeds, see roots and leaves form, measure growth and harvest what they grew. Leafy greens such as Nai Bai (milk cabbage) and choy sum finish in about 4 to 5 weeks, so one class sees the whole cycle within a term.

Because nothing grows in soil, pupils can lift a cup and look at the roots. LED lighting runs about 14 hours a day, so the plants do not depend on a sunny window.

A week-by-week lesson plan

A suggested plan for one class. Exact timing depends on the crop.

  • Weeks 1–2: seedlings. Pupils sow about 2 seeds in each sponge cup on the seedling layer, then talk about what a plant needs: water, light and nutrients. Look for the first roots and leaves.
  • Weeks 3–4: transplant and measure. Move the seedlings to the planting layers. Each week, measure height and count leaves. The water and nutrient check takes about 10 minutes.
  • Weeks 4–7: harvest and taste. Weigh the greens, compare crops and taste them: a Farm to Table lesson in the classroom. Harvest day takes about 1 hour.
  • Reset. Clean and replant in about 1 hour, ready for the next class.

Why leafy greens suit P3

Leafy greens change visibly from week to week, which keeps a P3 class interested. In our growing trial:

  • Nai Bai (milk cabbage)about 4 weeks
  • Tong San Pak Choyabout 4 weeks
  • Hong Kong Choy Sumabout 5 weeks
  • Local Lettuceabout 5 weeks

Whole-head crops such as pak choy are harvested once and replanted, which shows the full cycle from seed to seed tray. Herbs take about 7 weeks and keep producing, so they suit a longer project. See what you can grow for all 20 crops we trialled.

A public example

At Chua Chu Kang Primary, Primary 3 students transplant and harvest local vegetables such as Milk Cabbage and Chye Sim, observing the life cycle of the plants (school page). This is a public example, not an Urban Eco installation. Many schools run this kind of growing through their Applied Learning Programme or as part of the Eco Stewardship Programme, sometimes alongside a school edible garden.

FAQ

How long does a P3 hydroponics cycle take?

In our growing trial, Nai Bai (milk cabbage) and Tong San Pak Choy took about 4 weeks, and choy sum and lettuces about 5 weeks. One cycle fits inside a school term, and the system is replanted for the next class.

Which crops suit a P3 class best?

Leafy greens. They change quickly from week to week and are ready in about 4 to 5 weeks. Herbs take about 7 weeks, so they suit a longer project better.

Is the grow system subsidised by MOE?

No MOE scheme subsidises this; schools apply for grants themselves. The system is suitable for White Area Grant, ESP and ALP projects.
