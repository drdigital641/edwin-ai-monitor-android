SG Eco Fund Sprout closes 31 Dec 2026: how schools fund indoor hydroponics

Published 16 Nov 2026

Sprout is open until 31 Dec 2026: up to $30,000, up to 80% of supportable costs. Schools apply themselves.

WhatsApp Elaine

The short answer

The SG Eco Fund's Sprout category is open now and closes at 23:59 on 31 December 2026. Sprout is for grants of $30,000 or less, and the fund can support up to 80% of supportable cost items (MSE apply page, MSE FAQ). Schools apply themselves. A classroom hydroponic system can be one part of a community sustainability project that students run and share.

Urban Eco Solutions sells the grow system; we are not a grant body. The system is not an MOE-subsidised product. It is suitable for SG Eco Fund, White Area Grant, Eco Stewardship and ALP projects that a school plans and applies for itself.

  • Deadline31 Dec 2026, 23:59

Sources: MSE, SG Eco Fund apply page (last updated 4 May 2026) and MSE, SG Eco Fund FAQ. The SG Eco Fund is a $50 million fund set up in 2020 (MSE, about the fund).

Who can apply

Singapore-registered organisations can apply. The project must improve environmental sustainability in Singapore, involve the community, and not have obtained funding from other government sources (MSE eligibility criteria). That last point matters if your school plans to pay for the same project from another government grant, so check with MSE before you apply.

Has the SG Eco Fund backed hydroponics at a school before?

Yes. In the Sprout awards announced on 29 December 2021, one project was "The Green Wall of Singapore", to pilot an automated vertical hydroponics set-up at Jurong Pioneer Junior College and train students to conduct urban farming. It was awarded to named individuals for work at the college. Another project, an urban farming edu-tainment programme, included a hydroponics setup at Nurture Tots Preschool (MSE media release). Sprout's cap at that time was up to $10,000; today it is $30,000.

A Sprout proposal is about community impact, not equipment. Students could run the grow corner, track the harvest, and share it with the school community. MSE decides which cost items are supportable.

What else is open for schools now

SG Eco Fund Climate Adaptation Package

A new $5 million package under the SG Eco Fund. It funds up to 90% of supportable cost items, capped at $1 million. Applications are open year-round from 1 May 2026 to 30 April 2028, and outcomes come in 3 to 6 months. One of its four focus areas is supporting local produce: raising awareness of local farms and promoting consumption of local produce (MSE, Climate Adaptation Package).

SG Partnerships Fund, Sprout tier

A new $50 million fund announced in Budget 2026. Sprout gives up to $50,000, up to 80% of supportable programme costs, over up to two years. The current listed Sprout cycle is October to November 2026, then January to February 2027. A Seed tier of up to $5,000 (up to 100%) is open year-round (SGPO, SG Partnerships Fund). The fund does not fund commercial projects (SGPO FAQ, Q5), so a school or community group would apply for a non-commercial project that uses the system.

MOE School White Area and Canteen Grant

MOE announced a $64 million School White Area and Canteen Grant from 2024 for primary schools, secondary schools and junior colleges/Millennia Institute that are not part of upcoming rebuilding or upgrading plans. MOE says schools can use it "to transform learning spaces, such as by creating a makerspace or an environment hub with indoor farm" (MOE press release). There is no public apply-by date, so ask your school leaders whether your allocation is still available. The Straits Times reported per-school amounts of $170,000 to $270,000, and that Greendale Secondary used $20,000 of the grant for an edible community garden (The Straits Times).

Environment Fund for Schools

Smaller and project-based: Tier 1 $1,500, Tier 2 $1,000 and Tier 3 $500, for MOE and MOE-registered independent primary and secondary schools, JCs and centralised institutes. Apply by 31 December of the year the activities took place (CGS, Environment Fund for Schools). It suits a first trial.

Eco Stewardship Programme and ALP

MOE's Eco Stewardship Programme is a whole-school approach built on the 4Cs: Curriculum, Campus, Culture and Community. Many schools also teach hydroponics through their Applied Learning Programme. These are programmes, not grants with closing dates, but they are where a grow corner fits into lessons.

A public example: Jing Shan Primary's Sprout Spot

Jing Shan Primary runs Sprout Spot, an indoor hydroponics space where pupils grow baby bak choy, lettuce, mustard greens and kale. Harvests go to canteen stall holders, salad days, guests or home. It is part of the school's ALP, Green Explorers @ JSPS, and its Farm-to-Table initiative (school page). The Straits Times reported at least eight harvests a year (The Straits Times). This is a public example, not an Urban Eco installation.

FAQ

When does SG Eco Fund Sprout close?

The current Sprout window runs from 1 September to 31 December 2026. Forms are due by 23:59 on 31 December 2026. Results are estimated for March 2027, with projects starting around 1 April 2027. The next Sprout window is 1 January to 30 April 2027, according to the MSE apply page.

How much can SG Eco Fund Sprout give?

Sprout is the category for grants of $30,000 or less. The SG Eco Fund can support up to 80% of supportable cost items, capped at $30,000 for Sprout, according to the MSE FAQ.

Is the Urban Eco grow system an MOE-subsidised product?

No. Urban Eco Solutions is a vendor, not a grant body. The system is suitable for SG Eco Fund, White Area Grant, Eco Stewardship and ALP projects, but each school plans and applies for its own funding.
