Car battery keeps going flat? The causes besides the battery

When your car battery keeps going flat, the natural move is to buy a new battery. Sometimes that is right, but not always. The battery is only one of three places the problem can come from, and a new one will not help if the cause is somewhere else.

Short answer

A car battery that keeps going flat is not always a bad battery. The cause can also be a charging problem, where the battery is not topped up while you drive, or something drawing power while the car is off. Replacing the battery without finding which one it is can leave you stranded again.

The three places a flat battery can come from

A car that will not start looks the same whichever one it is, so it helps to know the difference:

The battery itself
An old or worn battery can no longer hold its charge, so the car starts fine after a drive and is flat after sitting.
The charging side
While you drive, the car is supposed to top the battery back up. If it does not, the battery slowly runs down even though it may be healthy.
Power drawn while the car is off
Something keeps using power after you lock the car, such as a light left on, a faulty part or a wiring fault, and the battery is empty by morning.

What you can notice before you come in

You do not need tools to gather useful clues. These are worth jotting down:

  • Whether the car is fine after driving but flat after sitting overnight, or flat even after a long drive
  • Whether the battery or warning lights show up while you are driving
  • Whether fuses keep blowing
  • Whether anything was fitted to the car just before the problem began
  • Whether the car often sits for days without being driven
  • Whether the battery posts look loose or covered in white or green crust

None of these proves the cause on its own, but they help the person checking your car know where to start.

Why swapping the battery does not always work

If something is drawing power overnight, or the car is not charging properly, a new battery simply gets drained the same way. You pay for a part and the problem comes back.

A battery that is flattened again and again also tends to wear out sooner, so guessing can cost you twice. Our own workshop video shows exactly this: batteries that kept dying after two other workshops had not found the cause, until the electrical fault behind it was traced.

FAQ

Questions about this topic

How do I know if it is the battery or something else?

You often cannot tell by looking. A battery that is worn out, a charging problem and something drawing power while the car is off can all look the same: the car will not start. A proper check tests each one so the cause is found before parts are replaced.

Will a new battery fix a battery that keeps going flat?

Only if the old battery was the cause. If the real problem is charging or a power drain, the new battery can go flat again, so it helps to find the cause first.
