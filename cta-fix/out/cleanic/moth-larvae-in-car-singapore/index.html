Why Do I Have Larvae in My Car?

Many car owners in Singapore are surprised to find small, crawling larvae in their vehicle. Often, these are the offspring of clothes moths. These insects are attracted to organic materials found in car interiors, such as natural fabric blends, wool carpets, or even trapped food particles and pet dander.

Moths prefer dark, humid, and quiet environments. If your car sits idle for long periods or has neglected food spills, it provides the perfect breeding ground. The larvae feed on protein-rich fibres, potentially causing hidden damage to your floor mats, headliners, or seat cushions.

  • Attracted to natural fibre upholstery
  • Thrive in dark, undisturbed areas like under seats
  • Commonly associated with residual food spills or pet dander
  • Can cause structural damage to fabric linings over time

How to Identify and Address the Infestation

If you see tiny, worm-like creatures in your car, it is important to act quickly before they spread further into the cabin's ventilation or deeper upholstery foam. Initial steps include removing all trash, vacuuming under floor mats, and checking hidden pockets.

However, simple vacuuming may not catch all eggs or larvae hidden deep within the seat fabric. For a total solution, professional intervention is often required to ensure every corner of the car is treated.

  • Thoroughly vacuum the entire cabin, including boot spaces
  • Inspect and clean under seat rails
  • Avoid using harsh pesticides that leave oily residue
  • Consider professional interior steam cleaning to destroy eggs

FAQs

Can I just vacuum the larvae out?

Vacuuming helps remove visible larvae, but it rarely eliminates eggs hidden deep in the upholstery fibres. Professional steaming is necessary to kill the eggs on contact.

Will these larvae ruin my leather seats?

Moth larvae typically prefer fabric. However, if your car has leather seats with fabric inserts or stitched seams, they can still nest in the crevices. Early professional cleaning is recommended.
