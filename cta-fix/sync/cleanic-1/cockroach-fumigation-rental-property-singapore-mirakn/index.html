Why Cockroaches Show Up in Rental Units Between Tenants

It is common in Singapore for a landlord or agent to walk into a unit for a handover inspection and find cockroaches, droppings, or an oily grease smell in the kitchen — even if the outgoing tenant kept the place reasonably tidy day to day.

The reason is usually timing. A unit that sits vacant for even one to two weeks with the aircon off, windows shut, and no daily cooking or cleaning activity becomes an ideal environment: warm, humid, and undisturbed. Cockroaches that were already living in wall gaps, behind the fridge, or under the sink cabinet during the tenancy become far more visible once there is no human activity to keep them away.

  • Grease build-up inside kitchen cabinets and behind the stove, often invisible until the unit is emptied
  • Damp bin corners, shared rubbish chutes, or a blocked floor trap in the kitchen or bathroom
  • Boxes, cardboard, and old newspapers left behind by the outgoing tenant during packing
  • A vacant unit with the aircon off, raising humidity and encouraging pest activity
  • Common corridor or riser pipe access points shared with neighbouring units

What Landlords and Agents Can Check Before Assuming It's a Serious Infestation

Not every sighting means a full infestation. Before booking any treatment, it helps to do a quick walkthrough so you can describe the situation accurately when asking for quotes.

  • Check inside kitchen cabinets, under the sink, and behind the fridge and stove for droppings or grease trails
  • Look at the bin corner, floor traps, and any exposed pipe risers for moisture or food residue
  • Note whether the sightings are isolated to the kitchen or spread to bedrooms and living areas
  • Check if neighbouring units share a rubbish chute or riser that could be a source
  • Take photos and note the approximate size of the unit (studio, 2-room, condo, landed) — this affects how a treatment is quoted

What Not to Do Before the Next Tenant Moves In

It is tempting to grab a can of insecticide spray or a few bug bombs before a viewing, especially under time pressure. This usually creates more problems than it solves for a rental handover.

Spraying visible cockroaches without addressing the source (grease, moisture, entry points) tends to scatter the population rather than resolve it — tenants sometimes report seeing more activity a few days after a DIY spray, not less. Aerosol residue can also leave a chemical smell in a tightly closed unit, which is exactly the kind of thing a prospective tenant notices on a viewing.

  • Avoid heavy aerosol spraying right before a viewing — the smell often lingers longer than the treatment helps
  • Do not seal up cabinets or vents without treating what is inside them first
  • Do not assume a one-off wipe-down of the kitchen resolves an infestation that has had weeks to establish
  • Avoid mixing multiple over-the-counter pest products, which can be counterproductive

When Professional Fumigation Makes Sense for a Rental Unit

Professional treatment is worth arranging when there are repeated sightings (not a single stray cockroach), when a tenant has raised it as a complaint, or when you are working to a fixed handover date and cannot afford a wait-and-see approach.

This is also where the choice of method starts to matter for a rental property specifically. Many landlords need the kitchen cabinets, countertops, and any furniture left in the unit to be presentable for photos and viewings almost immediately after treatment — not left with a greasy film that needs a separate clean-up.

  • Multiple sightings across more than one room, not an isolated case
  • A tenant complaint logged before move-out
  • A fixed handover or viewing date within days, leaving no time for a slow DIY approach
  • Visible grease trails or droppings inside cabinets that a wipe-down has not resolved

FAQs

Does MIRAKN fumigation leave an oily residue on rental furniture or cabinets?

MIRAKN is specifically a non-oily treatment, which is the main reason it comes up for rental handovers — the concern with some traditional fumigation methods is the oily surface film left on cabinets, countertops, and furniture afterward, which then needs extra wiping down. MIRAKN is used because it avoids that particular clean-up step.

How soon can a unit be shown to a new tenant after treatment?

This depends on the extent of the infestation and the specific product instructions followed during treatment. Because MIRAKN does not leave the oily film some other methods do, there is generally less follow-up cleaning needed on visible surfaces, but you should confirm the exact re-entry and viewing timeline with whoever carries out the treatment for your unit.

Is it safe to fumigate a unit with furniture, mattresses, or a tenant's belongings still inside?

This should be assessed on a case-by-case basis against the product's normal-use instructions at the time of treatment. Let the provider know exactly what is left in the unit — furniture, mattresses, appliances — so they can advise accordingly rather than assuming a blanket answer.

Do I need to fumigate every single time a tenant moves out?

Not necessarily. A quick inspection for droppings, grease trails, or repeated sightings will tell you whether it is a genuine infestation or just a one-off stray cockroach from a vacant, humid unit. Fumigation is more relevant when sightings are repeated or a tenant has raised a complaint.

What's the practical difference between MIRAKN and traditional fumigation for a rental unit?

The core practical difference discussed here is the non-oily nature of MIRAKN versus the oily surface film some traditional fumigation methods can leave on furniture, cabinets, walls, and fixtures. The non-oily principle behind MIRAKN is relevant wherever an oily residue on furniture, shelving, or fixtures is undesirable after treatment.
