Moth Infestation in Car Singapore: Signs, Causes and Removal

Moths in cars are often attracted to fabric, food crumbs, or moisture. If left untreated, they can lay eggs in hidden upholstery areas, requiring thorough cleaning and professional fumigation.

Oct 4, 2026 · Cleanic Detailing Team · Singapore
Short answer: A moth infestation in your car is usually caused by food remnants, damp upholstery, or clothing items stored in the cabin. To eliminate them, you should clear all debris, vacuum thoroughly, and engage professional car fumigation to reach hidden crevices where larvae may reside.

Why Are There Moths in Your Car?

While it may seem unusual, moths can thrive inside a vehicle's interior. They are typically attracted to dark, undisturbed spaces where organic materials are present.

Common culprits include food crumbs trapped in seat crevices, floor mats that haven't been vacuumed, or even moisture that encourages mould growth, which some moth species feed on. If you carry groceries or textiles regularly, you might be accidentally introducing them into your car.

  • Trapped food debris and organic matter
  • Damp conditions and hidden moisture
  • Accumulation of fabric fibres and dust
  • Undisturbed storage areas under seats

Signs You Have a Moth Problem

Early detection is crucial to preventing an infestation from spreading. Look for small holes in fabric upholstery, silk-like webs in corners or under seats, or the presence of larvae in dark pockets.

If you notice tiny flying insects around your dashboard or headliner during your commute, it is a sign that they have established a breeding cycle within your vehicle.

  • Small, irregular holes in fabric seat covers
  • Fine, cobweb-like structures in upholstery crevices
  • Visible larvae casings or droppings
  • Frequent sightings of flying moths while driving

FAQs

Can moths damage my car interior?

Yes, moth larvae feed on natural fibres. If left unchecked, they can cause visible damage to seat upholstery, carpet linings, and even roof headliners.

Is car fumigation safe for my family?
