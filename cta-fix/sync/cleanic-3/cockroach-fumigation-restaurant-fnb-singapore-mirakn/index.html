Why Cockroaches Are a Recurring Problem in Singapore F&B Outlets

If you run a restaurant, cafe, hawker stall or food kiosk in Singapore, cockroach sightings are one of the fastest ways to lose customer trust, trigger a bad review, or draw attention during an audit. Commercial kitchens create ideal cockroach conditions: constant food residue, warmth from cooking equipment, moisture near sinks and grease traps, and dense cardboard or plastic storage that cockroaches use for shelter and egg-laying.

Unlike a home, an F&B kitchen usually cannot simply close down to deal with a pest problem — service continues, deliveries keep arriving, and storage areas keep getting used. This is why F&B business owners searching for a solution are often looking for something that works around their operating hours and does not disrupt food-contact surfaces.

  • Grease build-up around stoves, extraction hoods and floor traps
  • Cardboard boxes and delivery packaging stored near walls
  • Gaps around plumbing, drainage and equipment bases
  • Shared refuse or grease trap access with neighbouring units
  • Standing water near dishwashing or ice machine areas

What Usually Causes the Infestation

Cockroach activity in F&B premises is rarely caused by one single thing — it is usually a combination of an entry point (drainage, service ducts, shared walls with neighbouring units) and a food/moisture source that lets a small population grow quickly given how fast cockroaches reproduce in warm kitchen conditions.

In older shophouse or food court units, shared drainage and ducting with adjacent tenants is a common but often overlooked contributor, meaning your own cleaning standards alone may not fully solve the problem.

  • Poor grease trap maintenance or infrequent degreasing
  • Cardboard and paper packaging stacked in storerooms
  • Cracks or gaps behind fixed kitchen equipment
  • Shared drainage/ducting with neighbouring F&B units
  • Inconsistent night-time cleaning of food prep surfaces

What You Can Do Yourself First

Before booking any treatment, a few operational changes can reduce cockroach activity and are worth doing regardless of whether you proceed with professional fumigation.

  • Increase grease trap cleaning frequency and check floor traps for buildup
  • Store cardboard and packaging off the floor and away from walls
  • Seal visible gaps around pipes, ducting and equipment bases where practical
  • Improve nightly cleaning of prep surfaces, floors and under-equipment areas
  • Keep bin areas covered and cleared more frequently during warmer months

What Not to Do in a Commercial Kitchen

Some common DIY responses create bigger problems in F&B settings than they solve. Spraying generic insecticide directly onto or near food-contact surfaces, counters, or open food storage areas raises contamination and food-safety concerns and can leave chemical residue exactly where it should not be. Similarly, some traditional fumigation methods leave a noticeable oily film across whatever surface they contact — a real issue in a kitchen where every counter, wall and piece of equipment may need to be food-safe and easy to wipe down afterward.

  • Avoid spraying insecticide directly on or near food prep surfaces
  • Avoid DIY fogging that is not designed for enclosed commercial kitchens
  • Avoid ignoring repeated sightings — a small population grows quickly in warm kitchens
  • Avoid choosing a treatment without asking what residue, if any, it leaves behind

When Professional Treatment Is the Right Call

A one-off sighting after a delivery of packaging might be resolved with better storage habits. But repeated sightings, activity near food prep zones, sightings reported by staff or customers, or preparation ahead of an audit or franchise inspection generally call for a proper fumigation treatment rather than more intensive daily cleaning alone.

  • Repeated sightings over more than one week
  • Activity seen during service hours, not just at night
  • Preparing for an audit, inspection, or franchise standards check
  • New tenancy where the previous occupant's pest history is unknown
  • After a break in cleaning routine (e.g. renovation, long closure)
