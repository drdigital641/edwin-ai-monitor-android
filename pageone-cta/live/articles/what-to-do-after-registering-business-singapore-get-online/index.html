What to Do After Registering a Business in Singapore to Get Online

You have registered your business. Now here is exactly what to do next to get online, show up on Google and start getting customers in Singapore.

Published 3 November 2026 · Updated 11 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

You have just registered your business with ACRA. Congratulations. Now comes the part most new owners do not expect: getting online so that customers can actually find you. The question of what to do after registering a business in Singapore to get online has a clear answer, and this article walks you through every step.

What to do after registering a business in Singapore to get online

Register your domain name, set up a website, create a Google Business Profile, connect Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and make sure your business details are marked up so Google and AI assistants can read them. Do these five things in the first two weeks and you have a solid foundation. Everything else builds on top.

Key takeaways

  • A domain name in your brand is the first thing to buy, usually S$15-30 a year.
  • A Google Business Profile is free and puts your business on Google Maps immediately.
  • A website gives Google and AI tools like ChatGPT a place to read your details and rank you.
  • Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools let you measure what is working.
  • Structured data and an llms.txt file help AI assistants find and recommend your business.
  • You can set all of this up yourself, or have it done for S$100 a month with no setup fee.

Step 1: Buy your domain name

A domain name is your address on the internet, for example yourbusiness.com.sg or yourbusiness.com. Buy it in your own name so you always own it. Most .com.sg domains cost S$15-30 a year through a domain registrar. Pick something short, close to your business name, and easy to type. This is the only thing you must pay for separately; a good web setup will include everything else.

Step 2: Set up a Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is free. It is what puts your business on Google Maps and in the 'near me' results that Singapore customers search every day. Go to business.google.com, enter your business name, category, address or service area, phone number and opening hours. Google will verify your listing, usually by postcard or phone. Once it is live, you appear in local searches straight away.

For more detail on this step, read how to appear on Google Maps for small business Singapore.

Step 3: Get a website live

A Google Business Profile alone is not enough. When someone searches beyond Maps, for example 'best aircon servicing Tampines' or 'HDB home painter near Bedok', Google looks for a website it can read and rank. Without one, you miss those searches entirely.

Your first website does not need to be complicated. It needs:

  1. A clear homepage that says who you are, what you do and where you serve.
  2. A page for each main service.
  3. Your phone number, WhatsApp link and contact details.
  4. Fast loading speed and an SSL padlock (https://).
  5. Mobile-friendly layout, because most Singapore customers browse on their phones.

Step 4: Connect Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools

Google Search Console is a free tool that tells Google your website exists and shows you which searches are sending you visitors. Bing Webmaster Tools does the same for Bing, and because Bing feeds ChatGPT search results, being verified there helps AI assistants find your business too.

To connect them:

  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and add your website.
  2. Verify ownership by adding a small code to your site or uploading a file.
  3. Submit your sitemap (a list of all your pages) so Google can find them quickly.
  4. Repeat the same steps at bing.com/webmasters.

Once connected, you can see exactly which searches bring people to your site.

Step 5: Add structured data and an llms.txt file

Structured data is a small piece of code that labels your business details, opening hours and services so that Google and AI assistants can read them precisely. Without it, a search engine has to guess what your page means. With it, your details can appear directly in Google search results as rich snippets.

An llms.txt file is a newer addition. It is a plain text file that tells AI tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity what your business does and how to describe it. Think of it as a briefing note for AI assistants. For more on this, see how to get my business recommended by ChatGPT Singapore.

Both are technical tasks. If you are doing this yourself, most website builders have plugins or settings for structured data. If you use a managed setup, this should be included as standard.

Step 6: Publish content that answers real questions

Once your site is live, the fastest way to be found on Google for more searches is to publish pages that answer the questions your customers actually type. For a renovation contractor in Jurong, that might be 'how much does HDB painting cost' or 'best time to renovate before Chinese New Year'. For a home-based baker in Punggol, it might be 'custom birthday cake Punggol delivery'.

Each page targets a different search. Over time, those pages add up and your business appears for a wider range of customers. This is why a new article every two days matters: more pages, more searches covered, more potential customers finding you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a website if I already have a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile puts you on Google Maps, but it does not help you rank in regular Google search results. A website gives Google pages to index and rank for the specific services and questions your customers search. Most businesses need both working together.

How quickly can I get my new Singapore business online?

A Google Business Profile can be created in a day, though Google verification may take a few days. A basic website can be live within a week if the content is ready. Page One typically gets a new site live within seven days of starting.

What is the difference between a domain name and hosting?

A domain name is your web address, for example yourbusiness.com.sg. Hosting is the server where your website files are stored so people can access them. You need both; they are usually bought separately, though some managed plans bundle hosting in the monthly fee.

Does my business need to appear on Bing as well as Google?

Bing has a smaller share of Singapore searches than Google, but it matters for one important reason: Bing feeds ChatGPT search results. Connecting Bing Webmaster Tools helps AI assistants find and describe your business accurately, which is increasingly how customers discover local services.

How long before my new website appears on Google?

Google can index a new site within days if Search Console is set up and a sitemap is submitted. Appearing on page one for competitive searches takes longer, often several months, because Google needs to build trust in a new domain over time. Results build up gradually.

What structured data does a new Singapore business need?

At minimum, add LocalBusiness schema marking up your business name, address, phone number, opening hours and service area. If you have FAQs on the page, add FAQ schema too. These help Google display your details as rich results and help AI tools describe you accurately.
