How to Get My Business Recommended by ChatGPT Singapore

ChatGPT and other AI assistants are increasingly the first stop for Singapore customers searching for local services. Here is how to make sure your business gets recommended.

Published 10 October 2026 · Updated 11 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

If you have ever typed a question into ChatGPT and noticed it recommended specific businesses, you are not imagining things. AI assistants are increasingly answering questions like "best car workshop near Ang Mo Kio" or "who does sofa cleaning in Singapore" — and some businesses show up while others do not. This article explains how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT in Singapore, what signals matter, and what practical steps you can take today.

How do I get my business recommended by ChatGPT in Singapore?

Make your business easy for AI to find and trust. ChatGPT search draws on Bing, so verify your website in Bing Webmaster Tools. Complete your Google Business Profile, describe each service and area in plain words on a fast website, add LocalBusiness structured data and an llms.txt file, and keep your details identical everywhere. There is no paid shortcut.

Key takeaways

  • ChatGPT's web-browsing and search features rely heavily on Bing, which means getting your site into Bing Webmaster Tools is essential.
  • A complete, accurate Google Business Profile still matters because AI assistants cross-reference it.
  • Structured data (schema markup) on your website helps AI assistants understand exactly what your business does and where you are located.
  • An llms.txt file on your website tells AI crawlers which pages to read — a newer but increasingly important signal.
  • Plain, specific language on your site (services, location, opening hours) is more useful to AI than vague marketing copy.
  • You do not need a big budget. A well-structured S$100/month plan can cover most of these foundations.

Why do ChatGPT recommendations matter for Singapore businesses?

Singapore customers are early adopters. Many already use ChatGPT to shortlist service providers before they open Google Maps. A homeowner in Tampines might ask ChatGPT "who does mattress cleaning in Singapore" and then click the first result it suggests. If your business is not visible in those AI answers, you are invisible to a growing slice of your potential customers — even if you rank well on Google.

The good news is that most Singapore small businesses have not optimised for AI search at all. The competition is low right now, which means acting early gives you a real advantage.

How does ChatGPT decide which businesses to recommend?

ChatGPT does not have a secret directory of Singapore businesses. When it answers a local question, it draws on a mix of sources:

1. Bing search results

OpenAI's ChatGPT search feature is powered by Bing. If your business appears in Bing search results and has a Bing Webmaster Tools listing, you are much more likely to be surfaced. Most Singapore businesses only think about Google — Bing is an overlooked opportunity.

2. Your website content

AI assistants read your website like a very literal reader. They look for clear answers to questions: What do you do? Where are you? Who should contact you? Vague taglines do not help. Specific pages — "Car aircon servicing in Ang Mo Kio" — do.

3. Google Business Profile

Even though ChatGPT is not Google, AI assistants cross-reference Google Business Profile data when forming local recommendations. An incomplete or unverified listing is a trust signal missed.

4. Structured data and llms.txt

Structured data (also called schema markup) is code added to your website that labels your business type, address, phone number, services and opening hours in a format machines can read without guessing. A newer standard, llms.txt, works similarly — it is a plain-text file that tells AI crawlers exactly what your site covers. Both are technical steps most small business owners skip, but they are increasingly how AI decides whether to trust and cite your business.

What steps get a business recommended by ChatGPT?

  1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Fill in every field — category, services, opening hours, photos, your website URL. An incomplete listing signals uncertainty to both Google and AI.
  2. Submit your site to Bing Webmaster Tools. Go to webmaster.bing.com, add your site and verify it. This directly feeds ChatGPT's search. Most Singapore businesses have never done this.
  3. Write clear, specific content on your website. Each service should have its own section or page. Use plain language: "We service and repair car aircon systems in Ang Mo Kio and surrounding areas." Avoid jargon. Include your neighbourhood and district names — AI uses these for local matching.
  4. Add structured data to your site. At minimum, add LocalBusiness schema with your name, address, phone, opening hours and service type. If you use a website builder this may require a plugin; if you have a developer, ask them to add it manually.
  5. Add an llms.txt file. This is a plain-text file at yourdomain.com/llms.txt that lists your key pages and what they cover. It is a small step that signals AI-readiness.
  6. Build consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) citations. Make sure your business name, address and phone number are identical across your website, Google Business Profile, Facebook, and any directories. Inconsistency confuses AI.
  7. Keep your content updated. AI assistants distrust stale information. A website last updated in 2021 is less likely to be recommended than one with recent content. Regular Google Business Profile posts help too.

What mistakes stop Singapore businesses being recommended?

  • Only optimising for Google. Bing powers ChatGPT. Ignoring Bing means ignoring AI.
  • Having no website, or a website with no text. A beautiful image-only website is invisible to AI. Every service needs words.
  • Inconsistent business details. If your phone number on Google differs from your website, AI lowers its confidence in your listing.
  • Waiting. Early movers in AI search have an advantage right now that will shrink as more businesses catch up.

Frequently asked questions

Does ChatGPT use Google to find Singapore businesses?

Not directly. ChatGPT's search feature is powered by Bing, not Google. However, Google Business Profile data is cross-referenced by many AI assistants, so maintaining both is important.

Do I need a website to get recommended by ChatGPT?

Yes, in almost all cases. AI assistants need a page to read and cite. A Google Business Profile alone is not enough; you need a website with clear, specific text about your services and location.

What is an llms.txt file and do I really need one?

An llms.txt file is a plain-text file on your website that tells AI crawlers which pages to read and what your site covers. It is a newer standard but increasingly used by AI assistants to decide which businesses to trust and recommend.

How long does it take before ChatGPT starts recommending my business?

There is no guaranteed timeline — AI assistants update their knowledge at varying intervals. Getting your technical foundations in place (Bing submission, structured data, complete GBP) is the first step, and visibility typically improves over weeks to a few months.
