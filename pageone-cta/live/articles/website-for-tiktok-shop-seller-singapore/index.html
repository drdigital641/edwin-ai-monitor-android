Do You Need a Website as a TikTok Shop Seller in Singapore?

You sell on TikTok Shop Singapore, but do you need a website too? Here is an honest answer and a practical guide to help you decide.

Published 11 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

If you sell on TikTok Shop in Singapore, you already have something most small businesses spend months building: an audience and a sales channel. But a question keeps coming up among active sellers: do I need a website for my TikTok Shop, or is the platform enough?

This guide gives you an honest answer, explains what a website adds, and shows you how to get one without a large upfront cost.

Do TikTok Shop Sellers in Singapore Need a Website?

Not immediately, but yes, eventually. TikTok Shop handles payments and product listings well. The problem is that it owns the channel. If your account is restricted, your sales stop. A website gives you a second home that you fully control, lets customers find you on Google, and builds trust with buyers who search for your brand before they purchase.

Key Takeaways

  • TikTok Shop is a powerful sales tool, but you do not own it and can lose access overnight.
  • A website lets customers find you on Google Search and Google Maps, not only inside TikTok.
  • Buyers often search a brand name on Google before they trust it enough to buy.
  • A website that ranks on Google can bring you enquiries every day, even when you are not posting.
  • You can get a website built and managed in Singapore for S$100 a month, with no setup fee and no contract.
  • The two channels work together: TikTok drives discovery, your website builds credibility and captures Google traffic.

What Does a Website Add That TikTok Shop Does Not?

TikTok Shop is built for in-app buying. It is excellent at impulse purchases driven by video. What it cannot do:

  • Appear in Google Search results when someone types your product name or category.
  • Show up on Google Maps when someone searches 'near me'.
  • Build a permanent home for your reviews, your story and your full product range.
  • Keep working when TikTok limits your reach or restricts your account.

A website sits on a domain you own. It is indexed by Google. It can be found by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity when customers ask those tools for recommendations. TikTok content, by contrast, is mostly invisible to Google Search.

The same risk applies to Instagram and Facebook sellers. If you have ever wondered what happens if your Instagram business account gets banned, the answer is the same: you lose everything on that platform overnight. A website is your insurance.

How Does a Website Help a TikTok Shop Seller Get More Sales?

Think about the buyer journey. A customer sees your product on TikTok. Before buying, some of them open Google and search for your brand name or your product category. If nothing comes up, they may not trust you enough to complete the purchase.

A website that appears on page one of Google for your brand name, or for searches like 'handmade candles Singapore' or 'Korean skincare Singapore', catches those buyers at exactly the right moment.

It also captures buyers who never saw your TikTok at all. These are people searching Google for what you sell. Without a website, you are invisible to them entirely. The article how to get customers from Google instead of only social media explains this well for Singapore sellers.

Is It Risky to Rely Only on TikTok Shop?

Yes, and here is why.

TikTok Shop accounts can be restricted for policy reasons, payment disputes or algorithm changes. When that happens, your entire storefront disappears. You lose your product listings, your follower base as a sales tool and your income from that channel, all at once.

A website you own cannot be switched off by a platform. Your domain, your content and your customer relationships stay with you. If TikTok changes its rules, you still have a channel that works.

This is exactly the same risk Carousell sellers face. The article website for a Carousell seller in Singapore covers how sellers in that position got an independent home online.

What Should a TikTok Shop Seller's Website Include?

You do not need a complicated e-commerce store. A simple, well-built website for a TikTok Shop seller in Singapore typically needs:

  1. A clear homepage that explains what you sell and who you are.
  2. A product or category page for each main line you carry.
  3. Your contact details and a WhatsApp button so buyers can message you directly.
  4. A link to your TikTok Shop so the two channels work together.
  5. Some social proof: screenshots of reviews, customer photos or a short 'about' section.
  6. Fast loading on mobile, since most of your buyers are on their phones.
  7. An SSL padlock so the site shows as secure in the browser.

Over time, adding articles about your niche (ingredients, care guides, how-to posts) gives Google more pages to index and brings in more search traffic.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a website alongside my TikTok Shop, or do I have to choose one?

You can and should use both. TikTok Shop handles in-app sales driven by video. A website captures buyers who search on Google and builds a channel you fully own. The two work well together.

Will a website replace my TikTok Shop?

No. A website is a separate channel, not a replacement. Most TikTok Shop sellers use a website to add Google visibility and reduce their reliance on a single platform. Your TikTok Shop keeps running exactly as it does now.

How long does it take for a website to show up on Google?

Google typically indexes a new site within days if it is set up correctly with Search Console and a sitemap. Ranking for competitive searches takes longer, often weeks to months. Results build up as more pages are added and indexed.

Do I need a physical address in Singapore to get a website or Google Business Profile?

You do not need a physical shopfront to have a website. A Google Business Profile does require an address, but home-based sellers in Singapore often use their home address with the display option set to hide the full address.

What if I already have a TikTok following but no website at all?

That is exactly the situation this article is written for. Your following is an asset, but it lives on a platform you do not control. A website gives that audience and new Google visitors a permanent home to find you.
