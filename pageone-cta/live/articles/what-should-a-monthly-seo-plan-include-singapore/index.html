What Should a Monthly SEO Plan Include? A Singapore Small Business Checklist

Before you sign up with any SEO provider, know exactly what you should be getting every month. Here is an honest checklist built for Singapore small businesses.

Why 'Monthly' Matters in SEO

SEO is not a one-time task. Google and AI assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity reward businesses that consistently publish fresh, relevant content and maintain a technically healthy website. A business that was set up correctly six months ago but has had nothing done since will slowly lose ground to competitors who keep publishing and updating.

For a heartland HDB shopfront, a home-based business or a neighbourhood service provider in Singapore, this ongoing work is what turns a Google search for 'aircon servicing Tampines' or 'best car workshop near me' into a phone call or WhatsApp message to you.

The Core Checklist: What Every Monthly SEO Plan Should Include

Here is what you should expect from any monthly SEO plan — and what you should ask about before signing anything.

1. Regular New Articles or Content

Every month, new pages or articles should be published on your website answering questions your customers actually search for. These are not generic blog posts; they should target specific, lower-competition searches — the kind of 'long-tail' queries where a ready-to-buy customer in your neighbourhood is looking for exactly what you offer.

A good content plan answers questions like 'how much does sofa cleaning cost in Singapore' or 'what to check when buying a used car in Ang Mo Kio' — the specific things real people type into Google or ask ChatGPT. Over time, more pages means your business shows up for more searches.

2. A Monthly Technical SEO Audit

Every month, someone should be checking that Google and AI assistants can actually find, read and understand your site. This includes:

  • Page speed on mobile (most Singapore customers search on their phones)
  • SSL padlock and site security
  • Structured data (the behind-the-scenes markup that tells Google your business name, address, opening hours and services)
  • A sitemap and an llms.txt file so AI tools like ChatGPT can reference your business
  • Fixing any errors that could stop Google from indexing your pages

If your provider never mentions a technical audit, ask why.

3. Google Business Profile Management

For most Singapore small businesses, Google Maps is where the first call comes from. Your Google Business Profile — the listing that appears when someone searches your business name or looks for your service 'near me' — needs to be complete, accurate and regularly updated.

This should be part of your monthly plan, not an optional extra. Learn how to appear on Google Maps for your small business if you want more detail on what a well-managed listing looks like.

4. Measurement and Reporting

Google Search Console should be connected to your website from day one. Every month, you should be able to see how many clicks your site is getting, what position you rank at for key searches, and whether those numbers are going up.

Page One connects Google Search Console (and Bing Webmaster Tools, which feeds ChatGPT search results) as part of setup. Real Search Console data — not estimates or vanity metrics — is the honest way to measure SEO progress. You can see real examples of what this looks like on the Page One results page.

5. AI Search Visibility

This is newer but increasingly important. When a Singapore customer asks ChatGPT or Perplexity 'who is a good property agent in Bukit Timah' or 'find me a reliable car workshop in Ang Mo Kio,' you want your business to come up. This requires structured data, an llms.txt file, fresh content and authoritative on-page signals — exactly the same things that help Google, but they need to be set up intentionally. Red Flags to Watch For in Any SEO Proposal

Before you commit to any provider, watch out for these:

  • Guaranteed rankings — nobody can guarantee a position on Google. Any provider who does is either lying or planning to use tactics that will eventually get your site penalised.
  • No reporting — if they cannot show you Search Console data, you have no way to know if anything is working.
  • Vague deliverables — 'we will optimise your site' is not a deliverable. Ask specifically: how many articles per month? What does the audit check? How is Google Business Profile managed?
  • Long lock-in contracts — a confident provider does not need to lock you in for a year. Read more about what no-contract SEO looks like for Singapore businesses.
  • Price with no explanation — a big monthly fee without a clear breakdown of what is included is a sign the pricing is not transparent.

How to Judge the Proposal You Have Been Sent

Use this quick checklist when reviewing any SEO proposal:

  1. Does it list specific monthly deliverables (articles, audit, Maps management)?
  2. Is Google Search Console included and will you get access?
  3. Is there a report each month showing what was done?
  4. Is AI search visibility (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) mentioned?
  5. What is the contract length and cancellation policy?
  6. Is there a setup fee on top of the monthly price?
  7. Can you speak directly to the person doing the work?

If a proposal cannot answer all seven questions clearly, ask before you sign.
