How to Show Google Reviews on a Business Website in Singapore

You've earned great Google reviews. Here's how to show them on your business website in Singapore, and what to do if you don't have one yet.

Published 13 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

You've worked hard for every five-star review on Google. Customers searched for you, tried your service, and took time to say something kind. Those words are powerful. The question is: are they working as hard as they could for your business? Knowing how to show Google reviews on a business website in Singapore is the difference between a review that sits quietly on Google Maps and one that convinces every new visitor to your site to get in touch.

How do you show Google reviews on a business website?

You can show Google reviews on a website by embedding a Google reviews widget using a third-party plugin or script, or by manually quoting individual reviews as testimonials. The simplest approach for most Singapore small businesses is a free or low-cost widget that pulls your reviews automatically and displays them in a clean section on your site.

Key takeaways

  • Google does not give businesses a single official 'embed' button, so you need a widget or manual method.
  • Several free and paid tools exist; most work by connecting to your Google Business Profile.
  • Manually quoting a review is always allowed and costs nothing, but it needs updating by hand.
  • If you do not have a website yet, your reviews are only visible on Google Maps and cannot do more work for you.
  • A website lets your reviews appear in Google Search results, not just Maps, giving them far greater reach.
  • Building a website does not have to be expensive or complicated.

Why showing Google reviews on your website matters

When someone searches for your type of business in Singapore, they may find you on Google Maps first. Your star rating is right there. But the moment they click through to your website, those stars disappear unless you have brought them across.

A review displayed on your website does several things at once. It tells a new visitor that real people have used and trusted you. It signals to Google that your site is about a real, active business. And it keeps the visitor on your site long enough to contact you, instead of jumping back to Maps to compare competitors.

For neighbourhood businesses, from home-based bakers in Tampines to car workshops in Ang Mo Kio, a page full of genuine reviews is often more convincing than any marketing copy you could write.

Three ways to show Google reviews on your site

1. Use a free or paid widget

This is the most common method. A widget is a small piece of code or a plugin that connects to your Google Business Profile and pulls in your reviews automatically.

Popular options include Elfsight, EmbedSocial and Trustmary. Most have a free tier that shows a limited number of reviews, and a paid plan if you want more control over the design. The basic steps are:

  1. Create a free account with the widget provider.
  2. Connect your Google Business Profile (you will need to log in with the Google account that manages it).
  3. Choose a display style, such as a carousel, grid or list.
  4. Copy the code snippet they give you.
  5. Paste the snippet into the relevant page on your website, usually in a 'Testimonials' or 'Reviews' section.
  6. Publish the page and check that the reviews load correctly on mobile.

Most widgets update automatically when new reviews come in, so you do not need to touch the code again.

2. Manually quote reviews as testimonials

If you are not comfortable with code, you can simply copy the text of a Google review and add it to a testimonials section on your site. Include the reviewer's first name and star rating. You cannot copy their profile photo, but you can use a star icon graphic.

This method is free and always works. The downside is that you have to update it yourself when you receive new reviews.

3. Use Google's own review badge (limited option)

Google provides a small reviews badge for some business categories through its Customer Reviews programme, but this is primarily for e-commerce. For most Singapore service businesses, a widget or manual quote is the practical choice.

What if I don't have a website yet?

This is the situation many Singapore business owners find themselves in. You have ten, twenty, maybe fifty Google reviews. Customers find you on Maps. But there is no website for those reviews to live on, and no page for Google Search to show when someone types your business name or your service plus your area.

Without a website, you are invisible to anyone who searches outside of Maps. You also have no place to put a contact form, a price list, photos of your work, or anything else that turns a curious visitor into a paying customer. If you rely only on Google Maps and social media, you are one algorithm change away from losing that visibility.

What a website with Google reviews actually looks like

A well-built small-business website in Singapore typically has:

  • A homepage that explains what you do and where you are.
  • A services or menu page.
  • A reviews or testimonials section showing your best Google feedback.
  • A contact page or WhatsApp button so customers can reach you immediately.
  • A blog or articles section that answers questions your customers search for.

The reviews section is usually near the bottom of the homepage or on a dedicated testimonials page. Some businesses put a short quote and star rating near the top of the page, right next to their headline, so it is the first thing a visitor sees.

Does showing reviews on your site help with Google Search?

Yes, indirectly. Reviews displayed on your site, especially when marked up with structured data (also called schema), can appear as star ratings in Google search results. This is called a 'rich snippet' and makes your listing stand out visually. Click-through rates tend to be higher when stars are visible in search results.

Frequently asked questions

Can I copy my Google reviews and paste them onto my website?

Yes, you can manually quote the text of a Google review on your website as a testimonial. Include the reviewer's first name and their star rating. You should not copy their profile photo without permission.

Is there an official Google widget to embed reviews on a website?

Google does not offer a universal official embed widget for business reviews. Most businesses use a third-party widget such as Elfsight or EmbedSocial, which connects to your Google Business Profile and pulls in reviews automatically.

Will showing reviews on my site improve my Google ranking?

Displaying reviews with structured data (schema markup) can earn star ratings in Google search results, which tends to increase click-through rates. It is one useful signal among many; no single step guarantees a ranking improvement.

What happens to my Google reviews if I build a website?

Your Google reviews stay on your Google Business Profile regardless of whether you have a website. A website lets you display those reviews to visitors who land on your site, not only people browsing Google Maps.

How do I get my Google reviews to show as stars in Google Search results?

This is done through structured data, also called schema markup, added to your website's code. It tells Google how to read and display your review information. A website provider should handle this as part of building or managing your site.

I only have a Google Business Profile and no website. Can I still show reviews somewhere?

Your reviews are already visible on your Google Business Profile and on Google Maps. To show them on a dedicated page you control, you need a website. Without one, you also miss out on appearing in Google Search results beyond Maps.
