Already Have a Website That Isn't Working? Your Old Domain May Be Your Biggest SEO Asset

An old website that gets few customers may be sitting on a mature domain. See the common mistakes, a real case from Singapore and what to check first.

Published 16 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

You already have a website, but it brings in almost no customers. It was built a few years ago, it loads slowly on a phone, and nobody has touched it since. Before you pay for a brand new site, read this. An old website that is not working often has one thing a new site cannot buy: a mature domain. In this guide you will see why an old website not working for SEO in Singapore can be your best starting point, what usually holds it back, and what a recent case shows.

Is an old website that is not working worth fixing?

Yes, often more than starting again. A domain that has been online for years has history with Google, and a badly built site leaves that history unused. Fixing the technical mistakes can bring it out. A brand new domain usually needs two to three months to show its potential. Rankings are never promised.

Why does an old domain matter for SEO?

A domain is the web address you own, such as yourbusiness.com.sg. An old domain has been online for years, so Google has seen it many times. It may have been listed, linked to and crawled for a long time. A brand new domain has none of that history, so Google has to learn about it from zero.

Think of two shops on the same street. One has traded for ten years, the sign is faded and the door sticks. The other opened last week with a perfect shopfront. People already know the first shop. The fix is paint and a clear sign, not a new address.

That is why an old site that was never set up properly can have more potential than a new one. The history is there. The technical work was never done.

What mistakes hold an old website back?

Most old websites fail for plain technical reasons, not because the business is weak. These are the ones we fix most often:

  1. Slow mobile pages. Most Singapore customers search on a phone. Heavy images and old code make the page slow, and visitors leave.
  2. Missing page titles and H1 headings. Google reads the title and the main heading to understand a page. If they are empty or repeated, the page is unclear.
  3. No schema. Schema is hidden code that tells Google your business name, services and answers. Without it, search and AI tools have to guess.
  4. A broken sitemap. A sitemap is the list of pages you want Google to see. If it is missing or wrong, pages go unnoticed.
  5. No internal links. Pages that do not link to each other are hard to find and rank.
  6. No tracked WhatsApp button. Customers want to message you. Without a tracked button, you cannot see which pages bring enquiries.

What should you do first with an old website?

Start with a short review before you spend money. These steps take one evening:

  1. Open your site on your phone. Note how long it takes to load and whether the text is easy to read.
  2. Search for your business name and service on Google. See which of your pages show up, and what the title and description say.
  3. Check your Google Business Profile. Make sure the name, phone number and area match your website.
  4. List the pages that matter. Home, services, contact and any page that already gets visits.
  5. Run the free website check. It points out the Google basics, mobile, speed and AI-search readiness without a sign-up.
  6. Message us for a free site check. We tell you what is holding the site back and what to fix first.

Keep your domain and your old pages while you do this. Deleting an old site to start again throws away the history you want to keep.

How is this different from buying a new website?

A new website is a new build on a new address. It looks fresh, but Google starts learning about it from the beginning, and the first two to three months are usually quiet. An existing website on a mature domain keeps its history. The work is repair, not replacement.

Repair is also faster to see. You are fixing known errors, so the changes are easy to measure in Google Search Console. Our guide to how long SEO takes for a new small business explains the wait for brand new sites in more detail.

Is this you? A quick checklist

Tick the ones that fit your website:

  • It is more than a year old and rarely updated
  • It loads slowly on your phone
  • Some pages have no title or the same title as other pages
  • You never added schema or a sitemap
  • Pages do not link to each other
  • Your WhatsApp button is missing, or you cannot see which pages bring messages
  • You get few visits even though your business has good reviews

Two or three ticks mean a mature domain is likely being wasted. Our website service covers fixing an existing site.
