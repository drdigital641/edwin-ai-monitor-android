Website for a New Cafe in Singapore: What You Actually Need Before You Open

Opening a new cafe in Singapore? Here is what your first website needs, what it costs, and how to get customers finding you on Google from day one.

Published 21 October 2026 · Updated 11 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

Opening a cafe in Singapore is exciting. You have picked a spot, sorted the menu and painted the walls. But before the first customer walks in, you need something else: a website for your new cafe in Singapore that helps people find you, trust you and decide to visit.

This guide covers exactly what your first cafe website needs, what it costs, and how to set it all up without spending weeks on it.

Do I Need a Website for My New Cafe in Singapore?

Yes. Even if you already have an Instagram account, a website is the one place you fully own. Instagram can restrict your reach or change its algorithm overnight. A website on your own domain stays yours, shows up on Google Search and Google Maps, and gives customers the information they need: location, opening hours, your menu and a way to contact you.

What Pages Does a New Cafe Website Actually Need?

Keep it simple at first. You do not need a booking system or an online shop on day one. You need:

  1. Homepage. Your cafe name, a photo, your neighbourhood or address, and one clear line saying what makes you worth a visit.
  2. Menu page. Even a basic list or a clean photo of your menu board. People decide before they leave home.
  3. About page. A short story. Singapore customers like knowing who is behind the cafe.
  4. Contact and location page. Full address, opening hours, a Google Map embed and a WhatsApp button. Most Singapore customers will message you on WhatsApp before they call.
  5. A few blog articles. One article on 'best coffee in [your area] Singapore', one on your specialty drink or food item. These are the pages that appear in Google Search.

Google and AI assistants like ChatGPT read your website to decide whether to recommend you. The more clearly your site explains what you do and where you are, the better your chances. Read more about how to get your new business on Google in Singapore.

What Is a Google Business Profile and Do I Need One?

A Google Business Profile is the listing that appears in Google Maps and in the box on the right of a Google search result. It shows your cafe name, address, opening hours, photos and reviews. It is free to set up.

When someone types 'cafe near me' or 'cafe in Ang Mo Kio' on their phone, Google shows a map with three local listings first, before any websites. If your cafe is not on that map, you are invisible to those searches.

Setting up a Google Business Profile is one of the first things any new cafe owner in Singapore should do. You can learn how to appear on Google Maps for your small business step by step.

How Do I Get My Cafe Found by AI Assistants Like ChatGPT?

More Singapore customers are asking ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity things like 'good cafe in Bishan Singapore' or 'where to get matcha latte near Toa Payoh'. These AI tools read websites and Google listings to form their answers.

To appear in those answers, your website needs:

  • Structured data (also called schema markup): code that tells Google and AI tools your business name, address, opening hours and menu in a format they can read automatically.
  • An llms.txt file: a plain-text summary of your business that AI tools can read directly.
  • A sitemap: a list of all your pages so search tools index them completely.
  • Fast loading on mobile: most Singapore cafe searches happen on a phone.

These are technical things. Most website builders like Wix or Squarespace do not set them up for you by default. A developer or an SEO plan that handles them daily will save you a lot of manual work.

A Step-by-Step Launch Checklist for Your New Cafe Website

  1. Register a domain name in your own name. Use a .com.sg or .sg for local trust.
  2. Decide who builds your site: DIY builder, a freelancer, or a managed plan that does it for you.
  3. Add the five core pages: homepage, menu, about, contact and location, and at least one blog article.
  4. Set up your Google Business Profile with your full address, opening hours and photos.
  5. Connect Google Search Console so you can see which searches bring people to your site.
  6. Make sure your site has an SSL padlock (https://), loads fast on mobile and has a WhatsApp button.
  7. Add structured data for your business name, address, opening hours and menu.
  8. Add an llms.txt file and a sitemap so AI tools and Google can read every page.
  9. Publish new articles regularly targeting searches like 'coffee in [your area] Singapore'.

Does My Cafe Website Need to Be Perfect on Day One?

No. A simple, fast, technically correct website on day one beats a complicated one that takes three months to build. Google starts indexing new sites quickly. Every week you are not online is a week a competitor down the road is collecting your searches.

The most important thing is that Google and AI tools can find, read and understand your site. That means correct technical setup, a complete Google Business Profile and at least a few pages that answer real questions customers are typing into their phones.
