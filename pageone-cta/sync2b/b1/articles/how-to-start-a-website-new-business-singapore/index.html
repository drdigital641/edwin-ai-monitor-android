What Does a New Business Website in Singapore Actually Need?

Before you spend a cent, understand what the minimum viable website looks like for a Singapore small business.

1. A Domain Name

This is your address on the internet — for example, yourbusiness.com.sg or yourbusiness.com. Register it in your own name through a domain registrar. It usually costs S$15–30 a year. Make sure the receipt and login are yours; never let an agency or freelancer register a domain in their account on your behalf.

2. Hosting

Hosting is the server that keeps your website online 24 hours a day. You need an SSL certificate (the padlock in the browser address bar) so visitors and Google trust the site. Some builders include this; others charge separately.

3. Pages and Content

At minimum you need a home page, a services or about page, and a contact page with your WhatsApp number. For Google to rank you, each page needs clear, relevant text — not just photos and buttons.

4. Google Business Profile

This is the listing that appears when someone searches 'aircon servicing near me' or 'car workshop Ang Mo Kio' on Google Maps. It is free but requires setup and accurate information. See the full walkthrough in our guide on how to appear on Google Maps for small business Singapore.

5. Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools

These free tools tell Google and Bing (which feeds ChatGPT search results) that your site exists. Without them, a brand-new site can sit unnoticed for weeks.

6. Ongoing Updates

A website you launch and forget will slowly drop in rankings. Google rewards fresh, relevant content — meaning you need to publish new pages or articles regularly, especially answers to questions your customers are already typing into Google.

Step-by-Step: How to Start a Website for a New Business in Singapore

Follow these steps whether you go DIY or use a managed service.

Step 1: Register your domain. Choose a name that matches your business. A .com.sg domain signals a Singapore business to local searchers. Register it yourself through a reputable registrar and pay with your own card. Keep the login details somewhere safe.

Step 2: Choose a hosting solution. If you are using a managed plan like Page One, hosting is included. If DIY, pick a hosting provider with Singapore or Asia-Pacific servers for faster load times locally.

Step 3: Build or commission your pages. Focus on clear, plain language. Write for the customer first. Each service you offer should ideally have its own page — so a cleaning company might have separate pages for sofa cleaning, mattress cleaning and office cleaning.

Step 4: Set up your Google Business Profile. Go to Google Business Profile, claim or create your listing, add your address (even a service area), opening hours, photos and your WhatsApp number. Verify it by postcard or video. This is what gets you on Google Maps.

Step 5: Connect Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. Submit your sitemap so search engines can crawl every page. Bing Webmaster Tools matters because Bing's index feeds ChatGPT — so this step helps you get found by AI assistants too. For more on that, see how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT in Singapore.

Step 6: Add structured data. This is code (or a plugin) that tells Google your business name, address, phone number, opening hours and services in a format machines can read instantly. It helps your listing stand out in search results.

Step 7: Publish your first articles. Answer the questions your customers are already Googling. A tuition centre might publish 'What is the best way to prepare for PSLE maths?' A renovation contractor might publish 'How long does an HDB toilet renovation take?' Fresh, useful content is what keeps Google and AI assistants coming back to your site.

Step 8: Check your site regularly. Speed, mobile layout, broken links and security certificates can all affect rankings. A monthly technical audit catches issues before they cost you visibility.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a website if my new business already has a Facebook page or Instagram?

Social media pages are useful but they are not a substitute for a website. Google does not rank Facebook posts the way it ranks website pages, and you do not own your social media presence — the platform can change its algorithm or restrict your reach at any time. A website on your own domain is an asset you control.

How long does it take to get a new business website live in Singapore?

With a managed plan like Page One, a new website is typically live within seven days once you have registered your domain. DIY builders can be faster if you are comfortable with technology, but connecting all the Google tools properly usually takes longer than the build itself.

Do I need a .com.sg domain or will a .com work?

Either works, but a .com.sg signals to local searchers and Google that you are a Singapore business, which can help for location-based searches like 'near me' queries. A .com is fine too, especially if you plan to serve customers beyond Singapore eventually.

What is the cheapest way to start a website for a new business in Singapore?

The lowest upfront cost is a DIY website builder with a free tier, but you will still need to handle hosting, security, Google setup and content yourself — and those have hidden time costs. A managed monthly plan from S$100 covers all of that with no setup fee and no contract, making it genuinely affordable for a new business with a small budget.

Will my new website show up on Google Maps automatically?

No. Google Maps visibility comes from your Google Business Profile, which is a separate listing you need to create and verify. Your website and your Google Business Profile work together — one without the other limits how many customers can find you.
