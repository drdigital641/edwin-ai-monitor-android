How to Judge an SEO Proposal in Singapore: A Small Business Owner's Checklist

Not sure if that SEO quote is worth paying for? Here is an honest, practical checklist for Singapore small business owners evaluating SEO proposals.

Published 16 October 2026 · Updated 11 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

You have received one or more SEO proposals — maybe from an agency, a freelancer, or a monthly subscription service — and now you need to decide which, if any, is worth paying for. Knowing how to judge an SEO proposal in Singapore is one of the most useful skills a small business owner can have, because the gap between a good proposal and a bad one is not always obvious from the price tag.

How to Judge an SEO Proposal in Singapore

A good SEO proposal clearly explains what will be done each month, how results will be measured, what the contract terms are, and why the work will help your specific business get found on Google and AI assistants. If a proposal cannot answer those four things in plain language, treat that as a warning sign before you commit any money.

Key Takeaways

  • Look for clarity on deliverables: what happens every single month, written down.
  • Check the contract terms — long lock-ins and high cancellation fees are common red flags.
  • Ask how results are measured and when you will first see them.
  • A high price does not mean better results; a low price does not mean bad SEO.
  • Modern SEO should cover Google Search, Google Maps and AI assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini.
  • Always compare at least two proposals before deciding.

What Should an SEO Proposal Actually Include?

A proposal is only as useful as the detail behind it. Before you judge the price, check whether the proposal covers these essentials.

Monthly deliverables — A trustworthy proposal lists exactly what you receive every month. This typically includes new content written for your site (answering the questions your customers search for), a technical audit of your page, and updates to your Google Business Profile. If the proposal is vague — for example, it promises 'SEO work' or 'optimisation' without specifics — ask the provider to itemise it. Read our article on what a monthly SEO plan should include in Singapore for a benchmark checklist.

Measurement and reporting — The proposal should say which tool will track progress (Google Search Console is the standard), what metrics you will see, and how often. Rankings and clicks are the two most meaningful numbers for a small business. Beware of reports that only show traffic from paid ads when you are paying for organic (unpaid) SEO.

Coverage beyond Google Search — In 2026, your customers are also finding businesses through Google Maps and AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. A proposal that only mentions 'Google rankings' and ignores Maps and AI is already behind the times.

Timeline expectations — SEO takes time. Any proposal that promises first-page rankings within days or weeks is making a claim no one can honestly guarantee. A realistic proposal sets a timeframe of months, not days, and explains what drives that timeline. For more on this, see how long SEO takes for a new small business in Singapore.

What Are the Red Flags in an SEO Proposal?

Red flags are easier to spot once you know what to look for.

  1. Guaranteed rankings. Nobody can guarantee a Google position, including Google itself. If a proposal promises 'rank number one in 30 days', walk away.
  2. No contract details shown upfront. Some providers bury a 6- or 12-month lock-in in the fine print. Always ask: what is the minimum term, and what happens if I cancel early?
  3. Vague deliverables. Phrases like 'ongoing optimisation' or 'SEO strategy' without specifics are meaningless. You should know exactly what you are paying for each month.
  4. No mention of Google Search Console or tracking. If a provider cannot show you their results in Search Console, they cannot prove anything is working.
  5. Inflated jargon with no plain-language explanation. A good provider can explain what they do in terms a business owner understands. Jargon used to confuse rather than clarify is a warning sign.
  6. Setup fees with no clear justification. Setup fees are common, but if a provider charges a large one and cannot explain what is done during setup, ask questions.

What Questions Should You Ask Before Signing?

Ask these questions in writing (WhatsApp or email) and see how the provider responds:

  1. Can you show me Search Console results from a current client in a similar business type?
  2. What exactly happens in month one, month three and month six?
  3. Is there a minimum contract, and what is the cancellation process?
  4. How do you handle AI search visibility, not just Google Search?
  5. Who specifically will work on my account — a named person or a team?
  6. What happens to my website, domain and content if I cancel?

The quality and speed of the answers tell you as much as the answers themselves.

Is a No-Contract SEO Plan Better for a Small Business?

For most Singapore small businesses — especially HDB shopfront owners, home-based businesses and sole traders — a no-contract plan carries far less risk. You can stop if the results are not there, and you are not tied in during slow months. A provider confident in their results should have no problem offering month-to-month terms. If a provider insists on a long contract before showing any proof of results, that is worth questioning.

How to Judge the Proposal You Already Have

If you have a proposal in front of you right now, run through this checklist:

  1. Does it list specific monthly deliverables, not just 'SEO services'?
  2. Does it explain how results will be measured and shown to you?
  3. Is the contract length and cancellation policy clearly stated?
  4. Does it mention Google Maps and AI assistant visibility, not just Google Search rankings?
  5. Does the provider show real results from real clients, with a named tool like Search Console?
  6. Is the price justified by what is listed, compared to at least one other quote?

If you can answer yes to all six, the proposal is worth serious consideration. If two or more answers are 'no' or 'unclear', go back and ask those questions before signing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important thing to check in an SEO proposal in Singapore?

The most important thing is a clear list of monthly deliverables — what the provider will actually do each month, written down. Vague promises like 'ongoing SEO work' without specifics make it impossible to hold a provider accountable or measure whether you are getting value for money.

How do I know if an SEO price in Singapore is reasonable?

Compare what is included rather than the number alone. A lower-priced plan that includes monthly content, a technical audit, Google Business Profile management and Search Console reporting can outperform a higher-priced plan that delivers little of substance. Ask for a full breakdown of deliverables before judging the price.

Should I sign a long-term SEO contract with an agency in Singapore?

For most small businesses, a no-contract or month-to-month plan carries less risk, especially before you have seen any results. If a provider insists on a 6- or 12-month contract before showing proof of their work, ask why. Providers confident in their results generally offer flexible terms.

Can any SEO provider guarantee Google rankings in Singapore?

No. Nobody can guarantee a specific Google ranking, and any proposal that does is making a claim that cannot be backed up. Reputable providers document the work they do and show you real measurement data, but they do not promise positions because Google's algorithm is outside anyone's control.

What is the difference between SEO for Google Search and SEO for Google Maps?

Google Search SEO focuses on getting your website pages to rank in the standard blue-link results. Google Maps SEO focuses on your Google Business Profile appearing in the local map pack when someone searches 'near me' or in your area. Both matter for Singapore small businesses, and a good proposal should address both.

How long should I wait before judging whether an SEO plan is working?

Most SEO plans take at least three to six months to show meaningful results, especially for newer websites. The first month usually focuses on technical setup and content creation. Ask your provider what milestones to expect at month one, three and six so you have clear benchmarks rather than waiting indefinitely.

What happens to my website if I cancel an SEO plan?

This depends on the provider, so check before signing. With Page One Singapore, if you cancel, your site stays live until the end of the paid month, you keep your domain, and you can purchase the site files. Always confirm ownership of your domain and content before starting any SEO plan.
