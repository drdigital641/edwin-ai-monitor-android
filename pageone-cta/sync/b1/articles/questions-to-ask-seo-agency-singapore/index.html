What Questions Should I Ask an SEO Agency in Singapore?

Before you pay any SEO agency in Singapore, ask these questions first. A straightforward guide for small-business owners who want honest answers.

Published 19 October 2026 · Updated 11 November 2026 · By Page One Singapore

Shopping for SEO in Singapore can feel like buying a used car — everyone sounds confident, the jargon flows freely, and it is hard to tell a solid system from a smooth pitch. Knowing what questions to ask an SEO agency in Singapore before you commit puts you back in control. This guide gives you the exact questions, explains why each one matters, and flags the answers that should make you pause.

What Questions Should I Ask an SEO Agency in Singapore?

Ask about their process, their pricing structure, the contract length, what they actually do each month, how they measure results, and whether they have verifiable proof from real clients. Seven clear questions cover all of that. They take ten minutes to ask and can save you months of wasted spend.

Key Takeaways

  • Ask for real Search Console data from existing clients, not just screenshot rankings.
  • Find out exactly what work is done each month and who does it.
  • Understand the contract length and what happens if you cancel.
  • Treat guaranteed rankings as a red flag — nobody can promise them.
  • Compare what you get for the price, not just the price itself.
  • Check whether they cover Google Maps and AI search (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity), not just blue-link results.

Question 1: Can You Show Me Verified Results from a Real Client?

Any credible agency should be able to point to live Search Console or Analytics data from an actual Singapore client, not a cropped screenshot or a ranking position for a keyword nobody searches. Ask for the site URL, the data source, and the time period.

As a reference point, Page One's system — the same one used for every client — is documented publicly at /results/. Edwin Garage (sggarage.com) reached 2,280 clicks from Google in the 28 days to 5 October 2026, averaging position 6.1, and appearing on page one for over 4,290 different searches. Those are Google Search Console figures, not estimates. Results like that are not a promise for every business, but they show what real proof looks like.

If an agency cannot show you something similar — a URL, a data source, a date range — that is a red flag.

Question 2: What Exactly Do You Do Each Month?

SEO is ongoing work, not a one-time setup. A straight answer should list the actual tasks: technical audits, new content published, Google Business Profile updates, structured data, link activity. Vague answers like 'we optimise your site' or 'we monitor your rankings' tell you nothing.

Question 3: Is There a Contract, and What Happens If I Cancel?

Many agencies in Singapore require 6–12 month contracts. That is not automatically wrong — SEO takes time — but you should know what you are signing. Ask:

  1. What is the minimum commitment?
  2. What happens to your website and content if you leave?
  3. Do you keep the domain?
  4. Is there a penalty or notice period?

Contracts are worth scrutinising carefully. How to judge an SEO proposal covers the contract clauses that catch small-business owners off guard.

Question 4: How Do You Handle Google Maps and AI Search?

Most small Singapore businesses get found through Google Maps before they rank in the main results. Your customers search 'aircon servicing near me', 'car workshop Ang Mo Kio', 'sofa cleaning Singapore' — and Google shows them a map. If the agency does not mention Google Business Profile as part of the work, ask why.

AI search is also now part of the picture. ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity are answering business questions directly, and your business can appear in those answers if your content and structured data are set up correctly. Ask whether the agency's process includes structured data, llms.txt and Bing Webmaster Tools (which feeds ChatGPT's search). If they look blank, that tells you something.

For context on what AI search visibility involves, see how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT in Singapore.

Question 5: Who Actually Does the Work?

Small-business owners in Singapore often sign with a polished salesperson and then hand off to a junior executive or an overseas team they never speak to. Ask directly:

  • Who will work on my account?
  • Are they based in Singapore?
  • Will I have a single point of contact?
  • How do I reach them — email only, or WhatsApp?

WhatsApp is the default for business communication in Singapore. If an agency cannot communicate over WhatsApp, that is a practical mismatch worth thinking about.

Question 6: How Will You Measure Success, and How Often Do I See Reports?

Rankings are a vanity metric on their own. Ask what the agency tracks and how often you receive a report. The right answer is something like: clicks from Search Console, impressions, average position, which keywords you rank for, and month-on-month trends. A good agency shows you the before and the after, not just a current snapshot.

Also ask: will you see the raw data, or just a PDF they have assembled? Access to your own Search Console is non-negotiable — it is your data.

Question 7: How Long Before I See Results?

Honest answer: it varies. A brand-new site in a competitive category will take longer than an established domain adding content in a niche area. Be wary of any agency that promises page one within 30 days or guarantees specific positions. Nobody controls Google's algorithm.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed page one positions
  • No clear list of monthly deliverables
  • Long contracts with exit penalties
  • Reporting that relies on screenshots rather than live data access
  • No mention of Google Maps, Google Business Profile or structured data
  • Salesperson cannot explain what an SEO audit actually checks
  • Agency owns your domain or your website content

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important question to ask an SEO agency in Singapore?

Ask to see verified results from a real client — a live URL and Search Console data showing clicks, impressions and ranking positions over time. If an agency cannot provide that, you have no way to judge whether their system works.

Should I sign a long-term contract with an SEO agency?

Not without understanding the exit terms first. Some agencies require 6–12 month commitments with penalties for early cancellation. Ask what happens to your website, content and domain if you leave before the contract ends.

Can any SEO agency guarantee my business will rank on page one?

No. Nobody controls Google's algorithm, and any agency that guarantees specific rankings is overpromising. A trustworthy agency documents the work done and shows you the results over time rather than making promises upfront.

Do I need to ask about Google Maps separately, or is it included in SEO?

Ask explicitly. Some agencies focus only on organic search results and treat Google Maps as a separate service. For most Singapore small businesses, Maps visibility is where customers find you first, so it should be part of the monthly work.

How do I know if the price I am quoted is fair?

Compare what is included, not just the number. Ask for a written list of monthly deliverables, check whether reporting gives you access to your own data, and find out whether the price covers content, audits and Google Business Profile management. Page One's pricing page at /pricing/ shows one transparent benchmark.

What should an SEO agency do each month for my business?

At a minimum: a technical audit of your site, new content published targeting the searches your customers use, Google Business Profile kept up to date, and a results report drawing on real Search Console data. See the full breakdown at the monthly SEO plan article.

Is it safe to use a monthly subscription instead of a traditional agency?

Yes, if the subscription documents its work and shows real results. The key questions are the same: what is done each month, how are results measured, and can you cancel without penalty? A subscription with no contract and transparent reporting carries less financial risk than a long agency contract.
