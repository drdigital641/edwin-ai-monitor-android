Do you need a tread depth gauge or a laser tyre tread examiner in a tyre shop?

By CSKA Auto Enterprise · Updated 9 November 2026

A hand tread depth gauge measures one groove at a time: you put the probe in the groove and read the depth in millimetres. A laser tyre tread and brake disc examiner, like the one CSKA lists, reads tread and disc wear without taking the tyre off, and shares a QR report. In Singapore, tyres have to be replaced at 1.6 mm, so a tyre shop needs a quick, repeatable way to show customers where they stand.

What is the minimum tyre tread depth in Singapore?

Bridgestone Singapore states that the Land Transport Authority legally requires tyres to be replaced when they reach 1.6 mm, as of November 2026. Read its guide, how to check tyre tread depth, and confirm the current rule with LTA before you quote a customer. Bridgestone adds that some tyre types carry wear bars in the grooves at 1.6 mm, which is a quick visual check.

How does a hand tread depth gauge work?

You press the probe into a tread groove, with the shoulders of the gauge flat on the tread blocks, and read the depth in millimetres. Tyres can wear unevenly, so measure more than one groove across the tyre, and the inner and outer edges as well as the centre. It is a simple way to get one number per spot.

What does the laser tyre tread and brake disc examiner do?

CSKA lists a laser tyre tread and brake disc examiner that reads tread and disc wear without removing the tyre. It has a colour display, cameras to record damage and shares a QR report with the customer. These are the descriptions CSKA posts from the maker. Ask CSKA for a demo and to confirm the current version before you order.

Which suits a tyre shop: gauge or laser examiner?

The two do different jobs. Use this to decide what your counter needs:

QuestionHand gaugeLaser examiner (CSKA lists)
What it readsDepth in one groove, in millimetresTread and brake disc wear
Does the tyre come off?No, the probe goes in the grooveNo, it reads without removing the tyre
Record for the customerNone by itselfCameras record damage; QR report

A gauge is enough if you only need a number at one groove. An examiner suits a shop that wants to show a customer tread and disc wear at the counter and keep a record.

What should you tell a customer when tread is low?

Show the reading and the 1.6 mm limit, say which tyres are close, and note any damage you recorded. Let the customer decide. For sensor work on the same visit, see TPMS sensor programming: activate, program and relearn explained.
