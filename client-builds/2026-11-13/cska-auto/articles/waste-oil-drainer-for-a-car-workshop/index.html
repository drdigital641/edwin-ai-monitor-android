Waste oil drainer for a car workshop: drain-under or suction?

By CSKA Auto Enterprise · Updated 13 November 2026

A waste oil drainer catches used engine oil during a service. A drainer that sits under the car collects oil as it leaves the sump plug, and its adjustable height decides whether it fits under your lift or car. A mobile suction drainer instead draws the oil out by vacuum, so you can work faster without crawling under every car. Choose by the cars you service, the volume per day, how easily it moves and how you will dispose of the oil.

What does a waste oil drainer do?

It holds used oil safely during a service so it does not spill on the floor, and it lets you move the oil to a storage tank or drum afterwards. Almost every bay that does oil changes needs one, which is why it appears on most new-workshop equipment lists.

What is the difference between a drain-under and a suction drainer?

The two types do the same job in different ways:

  • Drain-under type sits below the car and catches oil from the sump drain plug. Its height has to suit the lift or the car, so adjustable height helps.
  • Suction type draws the oil out by vacuum, often through the dipstick tube, into a tank. It can work from above, so the car does not always need to be raised.

When does a suction drainer suit a workshop better?

When you do many quick oil changes, work on cars with low ground clearance, or want to avoid pulling the sump plug on every job. It also suits a bay that wants one mobile unit it can wheel from car to car. Some engines have no dipstick tube or a layout that makes suction slow, so check the cars you service, and ask the maker how the unit connects.

When does a drain-under type suit better?

When your bays have lifts and you already pull the plug on every service, because the oil leaves the sump the usual way and the adjustable height lets the drainer sit under different cars. It is also the simplest tool to understand and clean.

What should you check before you buy?

A short checklist helps you compare two drainers fairly:

  • Capacity the tank volume against the number of oil changes you do before you empty it.
  • Height range whether the drainer fits under your lift arms or your lowest cars.
  • Mobility wheels and a handle that cope with your workshop floor.
  • Emptying how the oil leaves the unit, so a full tank is easy to move to your storage drum.
  • Hoses and adapters what comes with the unit and whether it fits the cars you service.
  • Cleaning how easy it is to clean and to keep drips off the floor.

What about disposing of the used oil?

Used oil is not household waste. Check with the National Environment Agency (NEA) how your workshop must store and dispose of it, and keep the drainer and drum in good condition so oil does not leak.

Questions

Which is better for a car workshop, a suction or a drain-under waste oil drainer?

Neither is better for every shop. A suction drainer suits fast, mobile oil changes, while a drain-under type suits bays that already pull the sump plug on a lift. Choose by your cars, your volume and your space.

How big should a waste oil drainer be?

Compare the tank capacity with how many oil changes you do before you empty it. CSKA lists an HPMM HC2297 with an 80 L tank.

Can a suction drainer work on every car?

Not always. Suction usually goes through the dipstick tube, and some engines have no dipstick or an awkward layout. Check the cars you service and ask the maker how the unit connects.

Which waste oil drainers does CSKA list?

The THINKCAR TOR 218, listed with adjustable height, and the HPMM HC2297 80 L mobile suction drainer.

How should a workshop dispose of used oil?

Not with household waste. Check with the National Environment Agency (NEA) for the rules that apply to your workshop.
