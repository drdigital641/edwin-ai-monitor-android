Why Does My Automatic Car Clunk When I Shift Into Drive or Reverse?

Loud clunk or bang when you move the lever from Park or Neutral into Drive or Reverse? What causes it, what is harmless, what to check first, and when to see a gearbox specialist.

By Jacky Gearbox · 13 November 2026

In short: A soft, brief thump when an automatic car engages Drive or Reverse can be normal, especially when the engine is cold. A loud clunk or bang, a jolt that is getting worse, or a clunk together with a delay, slipping or a warning light is not normal. The cause may be in the gearbox, but it can also be a worn engine or gearbox mount, a high idle or low gearbox oil, so it is worth finding out which before the damage grows.

What is a normal engagement and what is a clunk?

When you move the lever from Park or Neutral into Drive or Reverse, the gearbox has to apply a clutch or band to start transmitting power. A gentle, short engagement that you feel more than hear is usually normal. A clunk is a sharp knock or bang that you can feel through the seat or hear from under the car, and that is a different thing. If it only happens occasionally on a cold start and then settles, mention it at the next service. If it happens every time or is getting stronger, have it checked.

What can cause a clunk when shifting into Drive or Reverse?

There is no single cause. The common ones a workshop looks at are:

  • Worn engine or gearbox mounts. The mounts hold the drivetrain in place. When they are worn, the engine and gearbox rock when torque is applied, and the movement can sound like a clunk even when the gearbox itself is fine.
  • Low or old gearbox oil. Oil that is low, burnt or very old can make engagement harsh. See how to check automatic gearbox fluid and how often to change gearbox oil.
  • A high engine idle. If the engine idles faster than it should, the gearbox engages with more force. This is an engine-side fault, not a gearbox one.
  • Worn gearbox internals. Worn clutches, a failing valve body or solenoid, or a pressure problem can make engagement harsh or late. Our guide on shift solenoids and the valve body explains how those parts work.
  • Driveline play. Worn driveshaft joints, differential parts or loose fasteners can clunk when load is applied and released. These sit outside the gearbox.

Is the clunk from the gearbox or from somewhere else?

This is the hard part, and it is why guessing is expensive. Some clues help, but none of them is proof:

  • A clunk that happens only when you change the lever, with the car standing still, often points at engagement or at a mount.
  • A clunk that you also feel when you lift off and press the accelerator while driving points more towards driveline play or a mount.
  • A clunk together with a flare of engine revs, a delay before the car moves or slipping points more towards the gearbox. See lack of response and car will not move in Drive or Reverse.
  • A transmission warning light together with the clunk means a fault may be stored. See transmission warning light.

What can you check yourself?

  • Does it happen cold, warm or both? Note it, because it helps the person who diagnoses it.
  • Is the car held on the brake when you shift? Shifting while the car is still rolling, or with your foot off the brake, can make a harsher engagement. The gear selector guide covers the habits to avoid.
  • Is the gearbox oil level and colour right? Only if your car has a dipstick and the owner manual shows how to check it. Many gearboxes are sealed.
  • Are there leaks or burning smells under the car? Both are worth mentioning.

Do not try to fix a clunk by adding oil or additives. The wrong oil or level can make a gearbox fault worse.

Is it safe to keep driving with a clunk?

A single soft thump that does not change is usually not urgent. A loud clunk that is getting worse, or any clunk with slipping, a delay, a warning light or a burning smell, is a reason to stop driving it hard and get it checked. If the car cannot move, free towing is available in office hours.

Questions

Is it normal for an automatic car to clunk when shifting into Drive?
A soft, brief engagement, especially on a cold start, can be normal. A loud clunk, or one that is getting worse, is not normal and should be checked.
Can worn engine mounts cause a clunk when shifting into Drive or Reverse?
Yes. Worn mounts let the engine and gearbox move when torque is applied, which can sound like a gearbox clunk. A workshop needs to check this before blaming the gearbox.
Can low or old gearbox oil cause a clunk?
It can make engagement harsher. Check the level and condition the way your owner manual describes, or have a workshop check it, since many gearboxes have no dipstick.
Should I keep driving if my car clunks into gear?
If it is a single soft thump that has not changed, you can arrange a check at your convenience. If it is loud, getting worse, or comes with slipping, a delay or a warning light, get it checked soon.
