Drove Your Automatic Car Through Flood Water? What to Do About the Gearbox

Drove your automatic car through flood water in Singapore? Why water in the gearbox is serious, what to do first, what not to do, and when to ask a gearbox specialist.

By Jacky Gearbox · 11 November 2026

In short: If an automatic car has been through deep flood water, treat the gearbox as a possible problem even if the car drives normally afterwards. Water can get in through the breather or seals and mix with the gearbox oil, and water in the oil can damage the clutches, bearings and valve body over time. Do not keep driving if the oil looks milky, if the gearbox behaves differently or if a warning light is on. Have the car checked, and tow it if you are unsure.

Why is flood water a risk for an automatic gearbox?

A gearbox is not sealed like a bottle. It has a breather that lets air in and out as the oil heats and cools, and the breather is often placed fairly low or tucked into the engine bay. When a car is driven through water that is deeper than usual, water can be drawn in through the breather or past worn seals. Water and gearbox oil do not mix well, and the friction material and metal parts inside depend on clean oil.

What should you do first after driving through deep water?

  • Stop in a safe place and switch the engine off if you notice any warning light, a strange smell, or the engine losing power.
  • Do not restart an engine that stalled in deep water. Water that has entered the engine can cause serious damage if the engine is cranked. Ask for the car to be towed instead.
  • Do not drive on to see if it is fine. A car can feel normal for a short while with water in the gearbox.
  • Take photos of the water level if you can, for your own record and for your insurer.

If the car is not running well afterwards, free towing is available in office hours when the car cannot move.

What does water in the gearbox oil look like?

Water usually turns the oil pale, milky or pink, and the dipstick on cars that have one may show a foamy or cloudy film. Many modern gearboxes are sealed with no dipstick, so the oil cannot be seen without a proper check. Our guide on milky or pink gearbox fluid explains what the colours mean, and how to check automatic gearbox fluid shows what you can and cannot check yourself.

What symptoms should make you stop driving?

Stop and get the car checked if you notice any of these after the water:

  • Harsh, delayed or slipping shifts, see gearbox slipping
  • A gearbox or transmission warning light, see transmission warning light
  • A lever that will not leave Park or a car that will not move in Drive or Reverse, see car will not move in Drive or Reverse
  • Whining, grinding or a burning smell, see burning smell

Some damage shows up days or weeks later, so keep an eye on how the car shifts.

Should the gearbox oil be changed after a flood?

If water has entered the gearbox, the oil needs to be dealt with properly, and often more than a simple top-up is involved, because the water can reach the valve body and clutches. A drain and refill alone may not remove it. Read flush versus drain and fill for how the methods differ. A specialist should look at the oil and the gearbox first, then recommend what is needed.

Does it matter for insurance and the warranty?

It can. Insurers usually want to know about flood damage promptly, and the terms differ between policies, so ask your insurer before repairs begin and keep the photos and any invoices. This site cannot say what a particular policy covers.

Questions

Can flood water damage an automatic gearbox?
Yes. Water can enter through the breather or seals and mix with the gearbox oil, which can damage the clutches, bearings and valve body. The risk is higher after deep water.
Should I start the car again after it stalled in flood water?
No. Water that has entered the engine can cause serious damage if the engine is cranked. Have the car towed and checked instead.
How do I know if there is water in my gearbox oil?
The oil often looks milky, pale or pink, and shifting may change. Many gearboxes have no dipstick, so a proper check is needed to be sure.
Is it safe to keep driving if the car feels normal after the flood?
Not necessarily. Water in the gearbox can cause damage over time even when the car feels normal at first, so have the oil and gearbox checked.
