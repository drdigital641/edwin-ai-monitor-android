What is the white powder on car battery terminals, and is it safe to clean?

By Battery Rescue SG (Motoserv) · Last updated 11 Nov 2026 · First published 11 Nov 2026

QUICK ANSWER
The white, blue or green crust on battery terminals is corrosion from acid vapour, and it can stop the car starting. To clean it, wear gloves and eye protection, disconnect the negative clamp first, scrub with a baking-soda and water paste, dry, and reconnect the negative last. If the battery case is cracked, swollen or wet, replace the battery instead.

What causes the white powder on battery terminals?

A car battery gives off tiny amounts of acid vapour and gas, especially when it is warm or being charged. The vapour reacts with the metal terminals and clamps and leaves a crust. White or grey powder is common; blue-green crust usually means copper parts are corroding. Heat, a slightly loose clamp or a battery that is ageing can all make it build up faster.

Why does it matter?

The crust is a poor conductor. It sits between the clamp and the battery post, so the starter may not get the current it needs. The result can look just like a flat battery: slow cranking, a clicking sound, or nothing at all. It can also stop the battery charging properly while you drive. Before you buy a battery, check the terminals first. Our guide to battery or alternator shows the simple voltage check.

How do you clean battery terminals safely?

  1. Park on level ground, switch the engine and all electrics off, and take the key out.
  2. Put on gloves and eye protection.
  3. Disconnect the negative (black, minus) clamp first, then the positive (red, plus).
  4. Mix baking soda with a little water into a paste and brush it over the crust. It fizzes as it neutralises the acid.
  5. Scrub the posts and the inside of the clamps with an old brush, then wipe with a damp cloth. Keep water away from the rest of the engine bay.
  6. Dry everything.
  7. Reconnect the positive clamp first, then the negative last, and tighten both so they cannot twist by hand.

Some cars, especially newer European models, need the battery left connected to keep settings, or the new battery registered after any change. Check your owner's manual before disconnecting. Disconnecting can reset the clock, radio presets and other settings; see what to reset after changing a car battery.

When should you not clean it, and replace the battery instead?

  • The battery case is cracked, swollen or wet. A leaking battery is a safety risk.
  • The crust comes back within days of cleaning.
  • The battery is old and the car is also slow to start. See signs a car battery is dying.
  • You are not comfortable working near the battery. Ask a technician instead.

Can you stop the corrosion coming back?

Keep the clamps tight, keep the top of the battery clean and dry, and check the terminals now and then. Some drivers apply a thin smear of petroleum jelly or terminal grease to the clean, dry posts after reconnecting. Never mix the paste or any liquid so that it runs into the battery vents.

Stuck now and the car will not start?

If the terminals are clean and tight and the car still will not start, a jump-start may get you going: see how to jump-start a car safely. Frequently asked questions

Is the white powder on my battery dangerous?

It is dried residue from battery acid, so treat it as a skin and eye irritant. Wear gloves and eye protection, avoid touching your face, and wash your hands after.

Can corroded terminals stop a car from starting?

Yes. A crust between the clamp and the post blocks current, so the car can crank slowly, click or not start even when the battery is charged. Cleaning the terminals often restores the connection.

What can I use to clean battery terminals?

A paste of baking soda and water neutralises the acid residue. Scrub with an old brush, wipe with a damp cloth, dry everything, and keep water away from the engine electrics.

When is corrosion a sign the battery needs replacing?

If the case is swollen, cracked or wet, or the crust keeps returning quickly after cleaning, the battery may be leaking or worn. Have it checked, and see signs a car battery is dying.

Which clamp do I disconnect first?

Disconnect the negative (black, minus) clamp first and reconnect it last. This avoids a spark if a spanner touches the car body while you work on the positive terminal.
