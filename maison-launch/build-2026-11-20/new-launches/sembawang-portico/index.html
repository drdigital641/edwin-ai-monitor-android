The 4-room plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    The living and dining area sits at one end of the flat, with corner windows and the household shelter. Put the shoe cabinet and a panel for the shelter door at the entry, the TV and storage wall on the longest solid wall, and a built-in window bench along the corner glazing so the walkway from the door stays clear.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The kitchen opens to the dining area, with the sink and hob on the outer run and a service yard behind it. Keep that run continuous, add a tall fridge and pantry column, and match the cabinet finish to the dining joinery because the two are seen together. Laundry goes to the yard.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom has its own bath. A full-width upholstered headboard wall with bedside ledges, a built-in platform bed and a full-height wardrobe on the longest wall give the most storage while keeping the bath door swing clear.

  4. Common bedrooms

    The two common bedrooms suit a child's room and a study or guest room. A platform bed with drawers and a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space use the floor best.

  5. Common bath

    The common bath serves the common bedrooms and guests. The flats come with sanitary fittings, and for the first three years toilet finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed, so plan overlays, a vanity cabinet and niches rather than hacking.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. HDB does not allow them to be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall. We confirm with your approved plan and the permit rules before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Whitewash Bungalow Batten Living

A white-painted batten TV wall with a black-steel frame and a floating teak console with cove lighting, plus a built-in window bench with cane drawers. Inspired by Sembawang's black-and-white bungalows. Window view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the living and dining area with corner windows and the household shelter. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Pewter Terrazzo Pantry Run

Pewter grey-green cabinets with slim edge pulls, a terrazzo-pattern sintered stone counter and full-height backsplash, a tall pantry and fridge column, and under-cabinet lighting, set along the 4-room kitchen run beside the service yard.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the kitchen open to the dining area, with the service yard behind it. A compact HDB kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single matte colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Clay Rose Cane Veranda Suite

A full-width clay-rose upholstered headboard wall framed in walnut, with bedside ledges and cove lighting, a built-in platform bed, and a full-height wardrobe with cane-inset doors. Evening view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the main bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Mist Grey Louvre Study Room

A full-height wardrobe with mist-grey louvred doors, a built-in platform bed with drawers, and a wall-to-wall white-oak study desk under the window, with open knee space and a lit shelf.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a common bedroom of the official 4-room plan. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the approved plan from HDB.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Choose a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors so the permit can be applied for properly.
  5. After keys, report defects within 30 days, then book the start of works.

Planning your Sembawang Portico renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Report defects within 30 days of key collection, before renovation starts (HDB move-in guide). The Defects Liability Period runs 1 year from key collection.
  • Check floor and wall tiles for hollow or chipped pieces, plaster for cracks, and paint for stains.
  • Test every door, window, lock, tap, floor trap, light point and power socket.
  • Report through the QR code in your Welcome Kit or your project's Building Service Centre, with photos.

What the rules allow

  • Use a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors; they can apply for the permit for you.
  • A permit is needed for works such as removing floor finishes, raising floor levels in wet areas, or hacking or altering walls.
  • For the first 3 years, toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed. New tiles can be overlaid.
  • General works: Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm. Noisy works: Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. No works on Sundays or public holidays.
  • Household shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled. Windows need a BCA-approved window contractor; air-conditioners need a BCA-trained installer.

Rules change, so we check the current version for your unit before we quote. Full bto key collection renovation guide

About Sembawang Portico

Sembawang Portico is an HDB BTO project in Sembawang, launched on 16 June 2026 in HDB's June 2026 sales exercise, with 875 Standard flats in three residential blocks of 26 storeys (433A, 433B and 434A), bounded by Admiralty Lane and Sembawang Drive. One block also has 50 rental flats. The official unit mix is 50 2-room Flexi Type 1 flats (approx. 40 sq m), 150 2-room Flexi Type 2 flats (approx. 48 sq m), 100 3-room flats (approx. 69 sq m), 300 4-room flats (approx. 93 sq m) and 275 5-room flats (approx. 113 sq m). 3-, 4- and 5-room flats are on a 99-year lease. HDB says the name comes from the portico on Sembawang's black-and-white colonial bungalows, and the brochure describes a multi-storey car park with a preschool and a rooftop garden. It is a Shorter Waiting Time project: HDB's estimated waiting time is 31 months, an estimate rather than a confirmed key date. Sembawang Brook is the sister June 2026 project and Sembawang Anchor a later November 2026 launch; neither is a guide to your stack or key date.

Sembawang Portico renovation FAQ

When was Sembawang Portico launched?

It was launched on 16 June 2026 in HDB's June 2026 BTO sales exercise, together with Sembawang Brook.

Is the key-collection date for Sembawang Portico confirmed?

No. HDB's Annex A gives an estimated waiting time of 31 months, counted from the median month of the flat selection exercise to the median projected completion month of the blocks, which makes it a Shorter Waiting Time project of under 3 years. HDB's project page did not show an estimated completion or delivery possession date when we checked on 18 November 2026. Use your HDB documents for your own appointment.

Do you have the Sembawang Portico floor plan?

Yes. HDB's official Layout Ideas drawing for the typical 4-room flat (approx. 93 sq m, including 90 sq m of internal floor area and the air-con ledge) is shown on this page, copied as-is. It is a typical type plan, not your stack. HDB also publishes Layout Ideas for the 2-room Flexi, 3-room and 5-room types. Please send us your approved unit plan.

How many units and which flat types are in Sembawang Portico?

HDB's Annex A lists 875 Standard flats: 50 2-room Flexi Type 1 (approx. 40 sq m), 150 2-room Flexi Type 2 (approx. 48 sq m), 100 3-room (approx. 69 sq m), 300 4-room (approx. 93 sq m) and 275 5-room (approx. 113 sq m), in 3 blocks of 26 storeys.

What does a Sembawang Portico 4-room flat come with?

HDB's brochure lists floor tiles in the living and dining area, bathrooms, household shelter, kitchen and service yard, vinyl strip flooring in the bedrooms, wall tiles in the bathrooms and kitchen, bedroom and bathroom doors, and sanitary fittings. Kitchen cabinets, wardrobes and lighting are yours to plan.

Which MRT station is near Sembawang Portico?

HDB's project notes mention Sembawang MRT station on the North-South Line and bus services along Sembawang Drive. We do not publish walking times because HDB does not state them.

What renovation ideas suit a 4-room at Sembawang Portico?

Plan a fitted kitchen with a tall pantry or fridge column, a laundry cabinet in the service yard, a wardrobe wall in the main bedroom, a living-room storage wall with an entry cabinet that panels the shelter door, and a built-in desk in a common bedroom. The kitchen is open to dining, so keep their finishes in one family.

How much does Sembawang Portico renovation cost?

The cost depends on your unit type and scope, mainly the amount of built-in carpentry. We have not published package prices, so WhatsApp us for a quote for your unit type. We give an itemised quotation so you can see what each part costs.

Can I remove the toilet tiles in a new BTO flat?

Not in the first three years. HDB's renovation guide says toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed in that period, but new tiles can be overlaid. Plan vanity cabinets, niches and overlays instead.

What should I send Maison?

Your approved floor plan, your unit type, and a key date if you already have one in writing. After keys, you need an HDB renovation permit before works start. WhatsApp us at https://wa.me/6593391185.
