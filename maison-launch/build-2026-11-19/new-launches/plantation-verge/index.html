3-room

Approx. 69 sq m (66 sq m internal)

Treat living and dining as one room with a shoe cabinet at the entry and a slim TV and display wall. In the main bedroom a wardrobe along the longest wall frees the most floor. If the second bedroom doubles as a study, a fold-down or wall-hung desk keeps it flexible.

Most 4-room owners ask for four things: a kitchen with a tall pantry run, a main bedroom wardrobe wall, a living-room feature wall with hidden storage, and a built-in desk in a common bedroom. Use the service yard for a laundry cabinet and keep the walkway from the door to the living area clear.

The 4-room plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    Living and dining run along the right-hand window wall of the 4-room plan, with the household shelter mid-plan. Zone it into an entry cabinet at the door, a dining bench near the kitchen and a TV and storage wall at the living end, and keep the walkway from the door clear.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The kitchen is enclosed and opens to a service yard. On HDB's plan the sink sits toward the yard with the hob further along the same exterior run, and there is a work counter against the shelter wall. Keep that run continuous, add a tall fridge and pantry column, and move laundry to the yard.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom sits in a corner of the plan and has its own bath. A full-width bed-head wall with lit niches, a platform bed with drawers and a full-height wardrobe on the longest wall give the most storage without crowding the window.

  4. Common bedrooms

    The two common bedrooms take a platform bed and a wall-to-wall window desk with knee space well. Plan one as a child's room and one as a study or guest room, and change the desk later rather than the wardrobe.

  5. Common bath

    The common bath serves the common bedrooms and guests. The flats come with sanitary fittings, and for the first three years toilet finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed, so plan overlays, a vanity cabinet and niches rather than hacking.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. HDB does not allow them to be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall. We confirm with your approved plan and the permit rules before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Umber Herringbone Oak Living

A full-height umber-stained oak TV wall with a herringbone veneer, slim cove lighting and a floating console, a built-in dining bench along the window wall toward the kitchen, and herringbone oak flooring. Window view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the living and dining along the window wall of HDB's 4-room plan, with the household shelter mid-plan. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Verdigris Soapstone Galley

An enclosed two-run kitchen in muted verdigris lacquer with honed dark-grey soapstone worktops, a tall oak pantry, under-cabinet lighting, the sink toward the service yard and the hob further along the same exterior run, plus a work counter against the household-shelter wall.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the enclosed kitchen that opens to a service yard. A compact HDB kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single matte colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Mulberry Mohair Evening Chamber

A full-width recessed headboard wall in deep mulberry mohair with lit bedside niches, a built-in platform bed, and a full-height dark-oak wardrobe with bronze-tinted glass doors and internal lighting, shown at dusk. Window view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the corner main bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Citron Poplar Homework Studio

A built-in platform bed with drawers, a full-height pale poplar wardrobe with citron lacquer accent doors, a soft citron upholstered panel, and a wall-to-wall study desk along the window with open knee space and a floating LED shelf. Window view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a common bedroom of the official 4-room plan. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the approved plan from HDB.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Choose a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors so the permit can be applied for properly.
  5. After keys, report defects within 30 days, then book the start of works.

Planning your Plantation Verge renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Report defects within 30 days of key collection, before renovation starts (HDB move-in guide). The Defects Liability Period runs 1 year from key collection.
  • Check floor and wall tiles for hollow or chipped pieces, plaster for cracks, and paint for stains.
  • Test every door, window, lock, tap, floor trap, light point and power socket.
  • Report through the QR code in your Welcome Kit or your project's Building Service Centre, with photos.

What the rules allow

  • Use a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors; they can apply for the permit for you.
  • A permit is needed for works such as removing floor finishes, raising floor levels in wet areas, or hacking or altering walls.
  • For the first 3 years, toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed. New tiles can be overlaid.
  • General works: Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm. Noisy works: Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. No works on Sundays or public holidays.
  • Household shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled. Windows need a BCA-approved window contractor; air-conditioners need a BCA-trained installer.

About Plantation Verge

Plantation Verge is an HDB BTO project in Tengah's Plantation district, along Plantation Close. HDB offered it in the May 2023 BTO sales launch as 971 units of 2-room Flexi, 3-, 4- and 5-room flats in seven residential blocks of 14 to 18 storeys. The brochure says the name refers to its location along the Forest Fringe, and describes a central courtyard and a multi-storey car park with a childcare centre on the second storey and a roof garden on the top floor. HDB's Layout Ideas give approximate floor areas of 40 and 48 sq m for the two 2-room Flexi types, 69 sq m for the 3-room, 93 sq m for the 4-room and 113 sq m for the 5-room. 2-room Flexi flats are on a 99-year lease or a short lease. The flats are built with PPVC and come with full floor finishes, internal doors and sanitary fittings. The brochure does not publish a project-wide completion or key month, so we do not give one.

Plantation Verge renovation FAQ

Is the completion or key date for Plantation Verge confirmed?

No. HDB's official May 2023 sales brochure does not publish a project-wide completion or key-collection month, and we do not use third-party estimates. If you have an HDB key-appointment letter, use that.

When was Plantation Verge launched?

It was offered in HDB's May 2023 BTO sales launch, as stated on the cover of HDB's Plantation Verge sales brochure.

Do you have the Plantation Verge floor plan?

Yes. HDB's official 4-room Layout Ideas plan (approx. 93 sq m, including 90 sq m internal floor area and the air-con ledge) is shown on this page, copied as-is from the May 2023 sales brochure. It is a typical type plan, not your stack. Layout Ideas for the 2-room Flexi, 3-room and 5-room types are in the same brochure. Please send your approved HDB unit plan.

How many units and which flat types are in Plantation Verge?

The brochure lists 971 units of 2-room Flexi, 3-, 4- and 5-room flats in 7 residential blocks of 14 to 18 storeys. It does not give a count for each flat type, so we do not publish one.

What are the Plantation Verge flat sizes?

HDB's Layout Ideas give approx. 40 sq m and 48 sq m for the two 2-room Flexi types, 69 sq m for the 3-room, 93 sq m for the 4-room and 113 sq m for the 5-room, each including internal floor area and the air-con ledge.

Which MRT station is near Plantation Verge?

The brochure says residents will be served by the upcoming Jurong Region Line stations and an upcoming bus interchange along Tengah Boulevard, and that the project is an HDB car-lite precinct. We do not publish walking times to any station.

What comes with a Plantation Verge flat?

The brochure says the flats are built with PPVC and come with full floor finishes, internal doors and sanitary fittings. Plan your carpentry, lighting and kitchen around those rather than replacing them.

What renovation ideas suit a 4-room at Plantation Verge?

Plan a fitted kitchen with a tall pantry or fridge run, a laundry cabinet in the service yard, a wardrobe wall in the main bedroom, a living-room storage wall and a built-in desk in a common bedroom. Keep the walkway from the door to the living area clear.

How much does Plantation Verge renovation cost?

The cost depends on your unit type and scope, mainly the amount of built-in carpentry. We have not published package prices, so WhatsApp us for a quote for your unit type. We give an itemised quotation so you can see what each part costs.

Do I need an HDB renovation permit?

Yes, for the usual HDB renovation works. After keys, inspect defects, then apply for the permit with a registered contractor before carpentry is fabricated.

Can I remove the toilet tiles in a new BTO flat?

Not in the first three years. HDB's renovation guide says toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed in that period, but new tiles can be overlaid. Plan vanity cabinets, niches and overlays instead.

Your approved floor plan, unit type if you have it, and a key date if one is already in writing. WhatsApp us at https://wa.me/6593391185.
