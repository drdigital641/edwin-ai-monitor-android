The Myst unit types and renovation ideas

1-Bedroom + Study

48 homes. Type A1Sa about 48 sq m (517 sq ft); A1Sa(d) with strata void 61 sq m (657 sq ft)

At about 517 sq ft the study has to work hard. Build it as a slim desk-and-shelf wall with closed storage above, keep the living room open, and put a full-height wardrobe on the bedroom's longest wall. A fold-down or extendable dining table saves floor space.

2-Bedroom

145 homes with the 2-Bedroom + Study. Type B1 63 sq m (678 sq ft), 2-bedroom types from 678 to 732 sq ft; 2-Bedroom + Study Type B3S 65 sq m (700 sq ft)

The Type B1 plan (63 sq m / 678 sq ft) has a living and dining area that opens to a balcony, a compact kitchen run, a main bedroom with its own bath and a second bedroom. Most owners ask for a living-room storage wall, a kitchen with a tall pantry column, a wardrobe wall in the main bedroom and a study desk in bedroom 2.

3-Bedroom

143 homes. 3-Bedroom 850 to 915 sq ft (Type C1 79 sq m), 3-Bedroom Premium 1,033 sq ft, 3-Bedroom Premium + Study 1,163 sq ft

The 3-bedroom types range from 850 to 1,163 sq ft and include Premium and Premium + Study versions. Plan storage room by room: wardrobes in every bedroom, a built-in TV console and a window bench free floor space, and a Premium + Study type should get a real work room with a wall-to-wall desk and task light.

4-Bedroom

49 homes. 1,453 to 1,518 sq ft (Type D2 141 sq m)

The 4-bedroom types (1,453 to 1,518 sq ft) suit a family that wants room to spread out. The Type D2 plan separates dry and wet kitchen zones, which suits a stone counter and a display wall on the dry side. In a (d) unit, the strata void above living and dining gives a 4.5 m ceiling that suits a full-height fluted or veneer feature wall.

23 homes. Type E1 157 sq m (1,690 sq ft)

The 5-bedroom Type E1 (1,690 sq ft) suits a multi-generation family. Agree who sleeps where first, keep routes to the bathrooms wide, give every bedroom a full-height wardrobe, and use the dry kitchen and living area for display and shared storage.

The Type B1 plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    Living and dining share one space that opens to the balcony. Put the TV and storage wall on the longest solid wall, keep the line to the balcony door clear, and give a dining bench the wall nearest the kitchen. Check the condo rules before you plan balcony screens or outdoor cabinets.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The Type B1 kitchen is a compact run beside the entrance. Build around the supplied appliances, add a tall fridge and pantry column at one end, and use lit wall cabinets so the counter stays clear.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom has its own bath. A full-width bed-head wall with lit shelves, a platform bed with drawers and a full-height wardrobe on the longest wall keep the window side open.

  4. Common bedrooms

    Bedroom 2 works as a child's room, a guest room or a study. A wall-to-wall desk under the window with a wardrobe run beside it, or a daybed corner, keeps it flexible.

  5. Common bath

    The common bath serves bedroom 2 and guests. Plan a vanity cabinet, a mirror cabinet and niches. Any change to wet-area finishes needs MCST approval first.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. They must not be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall, and anything seen from outside. We confirm with your approved plan, the condo by-laws and MCST approval before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Misty Forest Modern

A fluted pale-oak feature wall, a floating moss-green stone TV console, a built-in window bench and soft cove lighting bring the green outlook indoors. Window view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the Type B1 living and dining that opens to a balcony. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Champagne Marble Deco

Handleless champagne lacquer cabinets with brass edges, a calacatta-look sintered-stone counter and backsplash, and fluted bronze-glass wall cabinets give the kitchen a light, polished finish.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the compact Type B1 kitchen run. A compact condo kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit wall cabinets give light where the work happens. Keep the supplied Bosch and SMEG appliances in mind: the carpentry is built around them.

Main bedroom: Mocha Limewash Contemporary

A full-width limewash headboard wall with backlit floating shelves, a built-in platform bed and a ribbed walnut full-height wardrobe keep the main bedroom warm and uncluttered.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the main bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Navy Oak Heritage

A navy lacquer study-and-wardrobe run, an oak desktop under the window and a built-in daybed corner turn a compact bedroom into a study with room to rest.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for bedroom 2 of the Type B1 plan. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the floor plan from your sale and purchase documents.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Read the condo by-laws for renovation hours, deposits and balcony rules, and get MCST approval before works.
  5. At vacant possession, inspect and report defects through the developer's process, then book the start of works.

Planning your The Myst renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Your sale and purchase agreement sets the defects liability period, commonly 12 months from vacant possession. Check yours.
  • Inspect before your contractor starts, so new marks are not blamed on the renovation.
  • Check floors, walls, doors, windows, balcony glass, sealant, appliances, taps, water heater and air-conditioning.
  • Submit a list with photos through the developer's defects process and keep a copy.

What the rules allow

  • Renovation approval comes from the condo's management (the managing agent, then the MCST). Expect a form, a deposit and contractor registration.
  • Working hours are set by the condo's by-laws and are usually shorter than HDB's. Check them before booking work.
  • Changes seen from outside, such as balcony screens, grilles and the main door, need approval and must follow the approved design.
  • Structural changes need a professional engineer and building approvals.
  • Executive condominiums follow the condo's rules, not HDB's renovation permits.

About The Myst

The Myst is a private condominium at 800 and 802 Upper Bukit Timah Road in District 23, developed by CDL Aries Pte. Ltd., a wholly owned subsidiary of City Developments Limited. CDL's June 2023 news release describes 408 homes in two 24-storey towers on about 179,008 sq ft of land, with a 99-year lease from 11 May 2023, and a 5-minute walk to Cashew MRT station. CDL's construction page gives an indicative TOP of Q1 2027 and an expected vacant possession date of 31 March 2029; those are not key appointments. Renovation needs the management corporation's approval, not an HDB permit. The concepts below are styled on the Type B1 2-bedroom plan.
