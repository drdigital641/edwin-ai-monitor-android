The 4-room plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    Living and dining run along the window side. In the conventional 4-room arrangement the household shelter sits beside the main entrance; in the second arrangement it sits beside the kitchen and opens from it. Mark the front-door and shelter-door swings on the plan before you place an entry cabinet, then put the TV and storage wall on the longest unbroken wall.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The 4-room kitchen connects to the dining and entrance zone and has a separate service yard, with the bathrooms close by. Keep one continuous working run, put the fridge and a tall pantry column at the end, and move laundry into the yard. In the second arrangement the shelter wall is in the kitchen, so it cannot be treated as cabinet frontage.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom has its own bath. A full-width bed-head wall with lit niches, a platform bed with drawers and a full-height wardrobe on the longest wall give the most storage. Check the window and air-con positions on your stack before you fix the bed wall.

  4. Common bedrooms

    The two common bedrooms work as a child's room and a study or guest room. In the second arrangement one common bedroom sits apart from the others, which suits an enclosed study. A platform bed and a wall-to-wall desk with knee space keep each room flexible.

  5. Common bath

    The common bath is close to the kitchen area. For the first three years, toilet finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed, so plan tile overlays, a vanity cabinet and niches rather than hacking.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. HDB does not allow them to be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall. We confirm with your approved plan and the permit rules before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Warm Japandi

Warm oak, tactile cream fabrics and a sculptural stone-look table make a calm, welcoming living space, with closed oak storage along the wall. The window view is illustrative, not the view from a Towner stack.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the living and dining area of the 4-room plan, with its household shelter beside the entrance or the kitchen. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Modern Luxe

Graphite cabinetry, pale stone-look counters and warm task lighting bring a polished look to a compact kitchen. The fridge, door swings, pipework and sockets still need a measured design.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the 4-room kitchen with its separate service yard, sized like the compact kitchens in Maison's River Place and Commonwealth work. A compact HDB kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single matte colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Hotel Suite

Walnut tones, soft upholstery and layered lighting give the main bedroom a boutique-hotel mood, with fitted wardrobes keeping the room practical.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the main bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Soft Scandi

Sage walls, pale oak and integrated storage make a compact bedroom feel composed. A single bed and a window-side desk balance rest with a practical workspace.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a common bedroom that can double as a study. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Choose a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors so the permit can be applied for properly.
  5. After keys, report defects within 30 days, then book the start of works.

Planning your Towner Residences renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Report defects within 30 days of key collection, before renovation starts (HDB move-in guide). The Defects Liability Period runs 1 year from key collection.
  • Check floor and wall tiles for hollow or chipped pieces, plaster for cracks, and paint for stains.
  • Test every door, window, lock, tap, floor trap, light point and power socket.
  • Report through the QR code in your Welcome Kit or your project's Building Service Centre, with photos.

What the rules allow

  • Use a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors; they can apply for the permit for you.
  • A permit is needed for works such as removing floor finishes, raising floor levels in wet areas, or hacking or altering walls.
  • For the first 3 years, toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed. New tiles can be overlaid.
  • General works: Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm. Noisy works: Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. No works on Sundays or public holidays.
  • Household shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled. Windows need a BCA-approved window contractor; air-conditioners need a BCA-trained installer.

About Towner Residences

Towner Residences is an HDB BTO project along Towner Road in Kallang/Whampoa, offered in HDB's August 2021 sales launch. The precinct has two residential blocks of 25 and 40 storeys. The 316 sale flats are all in Block 153A: 90 three-room flats of approx. 68 sq m (65 sq m internal) and 226 four-room flats of approx. 93 sq m (90 sq m internal), shown in two layout arrangements. The other residential block is rental housing. Shared spaces at launch include a central green with play and fitness facilities, a 33rd-storey roof garden, and a multi-storey car park with a sixth-storey roof garden and a childcare centre. The brochure's map shows Boon Keng MRT station, Hong Wen School and Northlight School nearby. No project-wide key date is confirmed, so we do not give one.

Towner Residences renovation FAQ

Which floor areas are correct?

HDB’s brochure states 3-room: approx. 68 sqm including the air-con ledge (65 sqm internal), and 4-room: approx. 93 sqm including the air-con ledge (90 sqm internal). The ledge is not usable living space. Printed p.12 shows two 4-room arrangements; confirm your own stack and any mirroring.

How does the first 4-room arrangement work?

In the first arrangement on p.12, three bedrooms line the plan-top edge. Living/dining sits plan-left, the shelter beside the lower-left entrance, kitchen at the bottom and service yard to its right; bathrooms are beside this service zone. These are diagram-relative directions, not compass facings.

Which finishes are supplied?

The specifications name aluminium-framed tinted windows, glazed porcelain kitchen floor/wall tiles and an aluminium-framed glazed service-yard door. Polished porcelain living floors and vinyl bedroom floors are optional OCS items, not universal supply. Render colours, cabinetry, counters, furniture and light fittings are design proposals.

Are the pictures exact reconstructions?

No. They are AI-generated style concepts, not photos of a Towner Residences unit and not completed Maison projects. Room shapes, fittings, furniture and window views are illustrative. Individual room dimensions were not published, so check your approved owner plan and a measured survey before construction.

Are keys officially confirmed for a particular month?

No project-wide current key date was confirmed. Historical third-party estimates are not an appointment. Confirm your own HDB handover and defects process.

How should renovation be sequenced?

Inspect and report defects, coordinate rectification, survey the home, confirm the HDB renovation and permitted-work requirements, then procure and build. Any 8–12-week example is a conditional planning allowance, not a Maison guarantee. Never alter the household shelter or structural elements based on a concept image.

How does the second 4-room arrangement at Towner Residences work?

Living and dining sit more centrally, with one common bedroom on the opposite side from the main-bedroom group. The household shelter is beside the kitchen and opens from it, and the service yard adjoins the kitchen. This suits an enclosed study in the separated common bedroom.

Plan a fitted kitchen with a tall pantry or fridge run, a laundry cabinet in the service yard, a wardrobe wall in the main bedroom, a living-room storage wall and a built-in desk in a common bedroom. Mark the shelter door swing before you plan any cabinet near it.

How much does Towner Residences renovation cost?

The cost depends on your unit type and scope, mainly the amount of built-in carpentry. We have not published package prices, so WhatsApp us for a quote for your unit type. We give an itemised quotation so you can see what each part costs.

Can I remove the toilet tiles in a new BTO flat?

Not in the first three years. HDB's renovation guide says toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed in that period, but new tiles can be overlaid. Plan vanity cabinets, niches and overlays instead.
