Coastal Cabana unit types and renovation ideas

3-Bedroom Deluxe

Types C1a and C1b: 46 homes, 81 sq m (872 sq ft)

The most compact type here, so storage has to sit along whole walls. An entry cabinet, a slim TV and display wall and a kitchen run with a tall pantry column keep the living area open. Full-height wardrobes in all three bedrooms, with a fold-down desk in one, carry most families.

3-Bedroom Premium

Type C2: 139 homes, 85 sq m (915 sq ft). Premium + Study Type C3: 165 homes, 85 sq m (915 sq ft)

The extra space over the Deluxe suits a defined dining zone and a longer living-room storage wall. In the Premium + Study type, plan the study as a real work room: a wall-to-wall desk, shelving above and good task light, so it does not end up as a store room.

4-Bedroom Classic

Type D1: 59 homes, 92 sq m (990 sq ft)

Four bedrooms in 92 sq m means every room needs storage that does not eat floor space. Use full-height wardrobes with sliding doors where swing space is tight, a platform bed with drawers in the smaller rooms and a compact desk in one common bedroom.

4-Bedroom Deluxe

Type D2: 142 homes, 94 sq m (1,012 sq ft)

The Type D2 plan has a living room that opens to a balcony, dining inward toward a compact kitchen and yard, a corner master with its own bath and three common bedrooms. Most owners ask for a living-room storage wall, a fitted kitchen with a tall pantry run, a master wardrobe wall and a built-in desk in one common bedroom.

The larger 4-bedroom types add space, and in the Study and Flexi versions an extra room. Decide early what the study or flexi room is for, a home office, a helper's room or a guest room, because that sets its joinery, power points and lighting.

The largest homes here suit a multi-generation family. Plan who sleeps where first, keep routes to the bathrooms wide, and give every bedroom a full-height wardrobe so shared spaces stay clear. A long feature wall in the living area can carry both display and closed storage.

Coastal Cabana floor plan and space planning

This is the developer's Type D2 plan, the most common 4-bedroom type (142 of 748 homes), not a specific stack. The brochure says areas are approximate, not to scale and subject to final survey, and plans can be amended by the building authorities. Plans for the other 3-, 4- and 5-bedroom types are in the same brochure (pages 28 to 33). We do not assume a sea view for any stack.

The Type D2 plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    The living room opens to a balcony, with dining set further in toward the kitchen. Put the TV and storage wall on the solid living wall, keep the line to the balcony door clear, and give the dining bench the wall nearest the kitchen. Check the condo rules before you plan balcony screens or outdoor cabinets.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The kitchen is compact and opens to a yard, with the household shelter alongside. One continuous run with a tall fridge and pantry column keeps the floor free, and a laundry cabinet in the yard takes washing out of the kitchen.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The master bedroom sits at the corner with its own bath. A recessed bed-head wall with lit niches, a platform bed with drawers and a full-height wardrobe on the longest wall keep the window side open.

  4. Common bedrooms

    Bedrooms 2, 3 and 4 are the common rooms. Give each a full-height wardrobe, and plan one as a study with a wall-to-wall desk and knee space, so the home works for a growing family.

  5. Common bath

    The common bath serves the three common bedrooms and guests. Plan a vanity cabinet, a mirror cabinet and niches. Any change to wet-area finishes needs MCST approval first.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls next to the yard. They must not be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall, and anything seen from outside. We confirm with your approved plan, the condo by-laws and MCST approval before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Amber Slat Teak Living

A full-height amber-stained slatted teak TV wall with cove lighting and a floating console, and a built-in dining bench on the kitchen side, with the living opening to a balcony. Window view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the Type D2 living room that opens to a balcony. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Ochre Clay Galley Kitchen

Ochre-clay inset-panel cabinets with brass knobs, a warm quartz counter and full-height backsplash, and under-cabinet lighting, sized for the compact Type D2 kitchen that opens to a yard.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the compact Type D2 kitchen that opens to a yard. A compact condo kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Tobacco Hide Night Suite

A recessed tobacco-leather headboard wall with lit bedside niches, a built-in platform bed, and a full-height walnut wardrobe with bronze-tinted glass doors. Evening view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the corner master bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Wheat Oak Desk Cabin

A platform bed with drawers, a pale wheat-oak wardrobe with ochre accent doors, and a wall-to-wall study desk with open knee space under the window.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for one of the three common bedrooms in Type D2. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the floor plan from your sale and purchase documents.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Read the condo by-laws for renovation hours, deposits and balcony rules, and get MCST approval before works.
  5. At vacant possession, inspect and report defects through the developer's process, then book the start of works.

Planning your Coastal Cabana renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Your sale and purchase agreement sets the defects liability period, commonly 12 months from vacant possession. Check yours.
  • Inspect before your contractor starts, so new marks are not blamed on the renovation.
  • Check floors, walls, doors, windows, balcony glass, sealant, appliances, taps, water heater and air-conditioning.
  • Submit a list with photos through the developer's defects process and keep a copy.

What the rules allow

  • Renovation approval comes from the condo's management (the managing agent, then the MCST). Expect a form, a deposit and contractor registration.
  • Working hours are set by the condo's by-laws and are usually shorter than HDB's. Check them before booking work.
  • Changes seen from outside, such as balcony screens, grilles and the main door, need approval and must follow the approved design.
  • Structural changes need a professional engineer and building approvals.
  • Executive condominiums follow the condo's rules, not HDB's renovation permits.
