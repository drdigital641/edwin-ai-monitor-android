Brickland Weave flat types and renovation ideas

2-room Flexi

Type 1: approx. 38 sq m (36 sq m internal). Type 2: approx. 48 sq m (46 sq m internal)

Keep the flat open and light. A full-height wardrobe wall, a bench seat with drawers and a sliding or fluted-glass partition give a bedroom feel without closing the space. For senior owners, plan step-free floors, slip-resistant tiles and blocking in the walls for grab bars.

Free layout plan for 2-room Flexi

3-room

Approx. 69 sq m (66 sq m internal)

Treat living and dining as one room with a shoe cabinet at the entry and a slim TV and display wall. In the main bedroom a wardrobe along the longest wall frees the most floor. If the second bedroom doubles as a study, a fold-down or wall-hung desk keeps it flexible.

Free layout plan for 3-room

4-room

Approx. 93 sq m (90 sq m internal)

Most 4-room owners ask for four things: a kitchen with a tall pantry run, a main bedroom wardrobe wall, a living-room feature wall with hidden storage, and a built-in desk in a common bedroom. Use the service yard for a laundry cabinet and keep the walkway from the door to the living area clear.

Free layout plan for 4-room

5-room

Approx. 113 sq m (110 sq m internal)

The extra space suits a defined dining zone, a proper study nook and a longer feature wall that carries both display and closed storage. In the main bedroom a wardrobe wall plus a dressing counter works well. A full-height shoe and bag cabinet at the entry helps a larger household.

Brickland Weave floor plan and space planning

This is HDB's typical 4-room plan from the February 2023 brochure, not a specific stack. Areas are approximate and subject to final survey. Layout Ideas for the 2-room Flexi, 3-room and 5-room types are in the same brochure. Stack orientation and views are not verified.

The 4-room plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    Living and dining form one open area with the household shelter on the plan. Put the shoe cabinet at the entry, a full-height TV and storage wall on the longest living wall, and a built-in dining bench on the kitchen side so food travels the shortest route.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The kitchen is enclosed and opens to a service yard. A run with a tall fridge and pantry column, and lighting under the wall units, keeps the counter clear. Laundry goes to the yard.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom has its own bath. A recessed headboard wall with lit niches, a built-in platform bed and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set make the room read as a suite.

  4. Common bedrooms

    The two common bedrooms suit a child's room and a study or guest room. A platform bed with drawers and a wall-to-wall desk with knee space under the window keep the floor free.

  5. Common bath

    The common bath serves the rest of the household. Plan a vanity cabinet, niches and hooks, and keep to HDB's rules on toilet finishes in the first three years.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. HDB does not allow them to be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall. We confirm with your approved plan and the permit rules before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Khaki Teak Breeze Hall

A full-height khaki-stained teak TV wall with vertical fluted panels, a floating console and concealed cove lighting, a built-in dining bench and handleless full-height storage. Based on HDB's typical 4-room plan. View illustrative, not a balcony.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the open living and dining of the typical 4-room plan, with the household shelter. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Bottle Green Compact Galley

Bottle-green painted inset-panel cabinets with brushed-brass knobs, a pale stone counter and full-height backsplash, and LED lighting under the wall units, in the enclosed kitchen with a service yard beyond.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the enclosed kitchen that opens to a service yard. A compact HDB kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single matte colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Garnet Wool Night Alcove

A recessed headboard wall in deep garnet wool with lit bedside niches, a built-in platform bed, and a full-height walnut wardrobe with bronze-tinted glass doors. Dusk view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a main bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Canary Oak Desk Berth

A built-in platform bed with drawers, a full-height pale-oak wardrobe with canary-yellow lacquer accent doors, and a wall-to-wall study desk with open knee space and a floating lit shelf.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a common bedroom of the typical 4-room plan. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the approved plan from HDB.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Choose a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors so the permit can be applied for properly.
  5. After keys, report defects within 30 days, then book the start of works.

Planning your Brickland Weave renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Report defects within 30 days of key collection, before renovation starts (HDB move-in guide). The Defects Liability Period runs 1 year from key collection.
  • Check floor and wall tiles for hollow or chipped pieces, plaster for cracks, and paint for stains.
  • Test every door, window, lock, tap, floor trap, light point and power socket.
  • Report through the QR code in your Welcome Kit or your project's Building Service Centre, with photos.

What the rules allow

  • Use a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors; they can apply for the permit for you.
  • A permit is needed for works such as removing floor finishes, raising floor levels in wet areas, or hacking or altering walls.
  • For the first 3 years, toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed. New tiles can be overlaid.
  • General works: Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm. Noisy works: Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. No works on Sundays or public holidays.
  • Household shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled. Windows need a BCA-approved window contractor; air-conditioners need a BCA-trained installer.

About Brickland Weave

Brickland Weave is an HDB BTO project in the Brickland District of Tengah, bounded by Brickland Road and Tengah Boulevard. It was offered in the February 2023 BTO sales launch as 1,641 flats of 2-room Flexi, 3-, 4- and 5-room types in 12 residential blocks of 11 to 17 storeys, as published in HDB's sales brochure. The brochure's Layout Ideas give approximate floor areas of 38 sq m and 48 sq m for the two 2-room Flexi types, 69 sq m for the 3-room, 93 sq m for the 4-room and 113 sq m for the 5-room. It does not publish a project-wide completion or key-collection month. Plantation Dew is a November 2026 launch in the same town and is not a key-date guide for Brickland Weave.
