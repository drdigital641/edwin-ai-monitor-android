Plantation Dew flat types and renovation ideas

2-room Flexi

Type 1: 42 flats, 40 sq m. Type 2: 136 flats, 48 sq m

Keep the flat open and light. A full-height wardrobe wall, a bench seat with drawers and a sliding or fluted-glass partition give a bedroom feel without closing the space. For senior owners, plan step-free floors, slip-resistant tiles and blocking in the walls for grab bars.

3-room

84 flats, 69 sq m (66 sq m internal)

Treat living and dining as one room with a shoe cabinet at the entry and a slim TV and display wall. In the main bedroom a wardrobe along the longest wall frees the most floor. If the second bedroom doubles as a study, a fold-down or wall-hung desk keeps it flexible.

Free layout plan for 3-room

4-room

168 flats, 93 sq m (90 sq m internal)

Most 4-room owners ask for four things: a kitchen with a tall pantry run, a main bedroom wardrobe wall, a living-room feature wall with hidden storage, and a built-in desk in a common bedroom. Use the service yard for a laundry cabinet and keep the walkway from the door to the living area clear.

Free layout plan for 4-room

5-room

252 flats, 113 sq m (110 sq m internal)

The extra space suits a defined dining zone, a proper study nook and a longer feature wall that carries both display and closed storage. In the main bedroom a wardrobe wall plus a dressing counter works well. A full-height shoe and bag cabinet at the entry helps a larger household.

3Gen

28 flats, 121 sq m (115 sq m internal)

Plan around who sleeps where and who uses which bathroom first. Keep walking routes wide and floors slip-resistant for grandparents, and give children a study corner that can be seen from the living area. Built-in wardrobes in every bedroom keep the shared spaces clear.

Plantation Dew floor plan and space planning

Only the default 4-room plan is shown. HDB also publishes layout ideas for the other types and a white-flat option for 3-, 4- and 5-room flats. HDB's dates are estimates, not a key appointment.

The 4-room plan, room by room

  1. Living and dining

    The living and dining area has the household shelter at one corner and no balcony. HDB's plan shows no beams in the internal spaces, which gives clean headroom for a ceiling cove and full-height joinery. Keep the entry clear and put the TV and storage wall on the longest living wall.

  2. Kitchen and service yard

    The kitchen is enclosed and ends at a service yard. A compact run with a tall pantry or fridge column and lit niches suits it, and an opening or glazed door to the living area helps light travel.

  3. Main bedroom and bath

    The main bedroom has its own bath. A full-width bed-head alcove with side niches and a full-height wardrobe turns the room into a suite without hacking walls.

  4. Common bedrooms

    The two common bedrooms are well suited to a built-in platform bed and a wall-to-wall desk under the window. Use a wardrobe with tall doors so the floor stays free.

  5. Common bath

    HDB also offers a white-flat layout for 3-, 4- and 5-room units, with the same shell but no internal partitions between living area and bedrooms. If you choose it, you plan the whole layout from scratch.

Zoning and traffic flow

  • Public zone: entry, living and dining. Keep the line from the door to the kitchen clear.
  • Service zone: kitchen and service yard, close to the dining table and the household shelter.
  • Private zone: bedrooms and baths, away from the entry so the living area works as a buffer.

Keep or hack?

  • Keep: the household shelter walls. HDB does not allow them to be hacked or drilled.
  • Check first: any other wall. We confirm with your approved plan and the permit rules before we quote.
  • Prefer joinery over hacking: a fluted wall, bench or niche changes the room without touching the structure.

The design thinking behind the four concepts

Living room: Tawny Fluted Gallery

A full-height tawny fluted teak TV wall with cove lighting and a floating console, a built-in leather dining bench, and an opening through to the enclosed kitchen. Typical HDB casement windows; view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the living and dining of the default 4-room plan, with a household shelter at one corner. Think of the living room as one long elevation. A tall, fluted or reeded wall breaks the glare of a flat surface, hides cabling and shelving behind doors, and lets cove light wash the wall. Warm timber and stone keep it calm, and handleless doors stop the wall from looking busy.

Kitchen: Pale Jade Niche Run

Soft pale-jade matte lacquer handleless cabinets, walnut-lined inset niches with strip lighting, a sintered-stone counter and upstand, and a frosted-glass service-yard door at the end of a compact enclosed HDB kitchen.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the enclosed kitchen that ends at a service yard. A compact HDB kitchen works when every surface earns its place. One continuous run keeps the working triangle short, a tall pantry column takes the bulk storage off the counter, and lit niches or a lit shelf give light where the work happens. A single matte colour on the doors keeps a small room from feeling cut up.

Main bedroom: Espresso Leather Alcove

A full-width espresso leather headboard alcove with lit bedside niches, a platform bed built into the joinery, and a full-height smoked-oak wardrobe with bronze-tinted glass doors. Evening view illustrative.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for the main bedroom with its own bath. A bedroom should feel lower, darker and quieter than the living room. A full-width bed-head wall with lit side niches replaces bedside tables, a built-in platform bed hides drawers, and a full-height wardrobe in one joinery set keeps lines continuous. Dim, warm light does the rest.

Common bedroom: Fog Blue Study Cabin

A platform bed with drawers, a full-height pale-oak wardrobe with fog-blue inset doors, and a wall-to-wall study desk under the window with open knee space and a lit floating shelf.

Why it suits this flat. It is drawn for a common bedroom of the default 4-room plan. A shared or child's room has to change with age. Put the bed on a platform with drawers, a wall-to-wall desk under the window with open knee space, and a tall wardrobe. A single accent colour on a few doors gives the room character that can be repainted later.

Practical design rules we use

  • Light

    Use the window wall for the desk or the dining bench so natural light does the work, then add cove, niche and shelf lighting for the evening. Mixing warm light in the living room and bedrooms keeps the home restful.

  • Flow

    Keep a clear walking line from the door to the living area and from the kitchen to the dining table. Furniture that sits inside the joinery, not in front of it, keeps that line open.

  • Storage

    Plan storage along whole walls, floor to ceiling, rather than in small pieces. Shoe and bag storage at the door, a pantry column in the kitchen and a wardrobe wall in every bedroom carry most households.

  • Small-space tricks

    One material family across rooms, handleless doors, mirrors or reeded glass to bounce light, and built-in benches or platform beds with drawers all make a flat feel larger.

Checklist before key collection

  1. Confirm your unit type and keep a copy of the approved plan from HDB.
  2. List what you need built in: kitchen run, wardrobes, shoe cabinet, living-room wall, study desk.
  3. Ask for an itemised quotation and 3D drawings so you can compare scopes line by line.
  4. Choose a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors so the permit can be applied for properly.
  5. After keys, report defects within 30 days, then book the start of works.

Planning your Plantation Dew renovation

Defects check at handover

  • Report defects within 30 days of key collection, before renovation starts (HDB move-in guide). The Defects Liability Period runs 1 year from key collection.
  • Check floor and wall tiles for hollow or chipped pieces, plaster for cracks, and paint for stains.
  • Test every door, window, lock, tap, floor trap, light point and power socket.
  • Report through the QR code in your Welcome Kit or your project's Building Service Centre, with photos.

What the rules allow

  • Use a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors; they can apply for the permit for you.
  • A permit is needed for works such as removing floor finishes, raising floor levels in wet areas, or hacking or altering walls.
  • For the first 3 years, toilet wall and floor finishes in a new BTO flat cannot be removed. New tiles can be overlaid.
  • General works: Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm. Noisy works: Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. No works on Sundays or public holidays.
  • Household shelter walls must not be hacked or drilled. Windows need a BCA-approved window contractor; air-conditioners need a BCA-trained installer.
