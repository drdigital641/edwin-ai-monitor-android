Tyre Sidewall Bulge in Singapore: Can You Drive Safely?

Edwin Garage·Ang Mo Kio·7 October 2026

A rounded tyre-sidewall swelling can mean the internal casing cords are damaged and may fail under heat, load or speed. Do not drive on it to “see whether it gets worse”; park safely and arrange recovery or replacement. Our car servicing workshop in Ang Mo Kio inspects tyres by appointment, but an unsafe tyre must not be driven in for a check.

Here is how to recognise a bulge, respond safely in Singapore, understand a workshop check and budget for replacement. A sidewall bubble is not ordinary tread wear, and neither a plug nor wheel alignment can repair it.

What is a tyre sidewall bulge?

Reinforcing cords hold a tyre’s shape while the sidewall flexes. If the casing is damaged or separates, air can push the outer rubber outward at the weak point. A small bubble is not necessarily a minor fault.

The tyre can still hold air with no dashboard warning. Repeated flexing, heat, speed and load stress the weakened area, so treat a definite localised bulge as structural damage—not something to monitor for days.

How to recognise a dangerous bulge

With the car parked on level ground, look for a rounded area sticking out from the tyre’s even profile; it may be on the inner face too. Do not crawl under the vehicle or press the swelling.

  • A bubble, blister or lump on one section of the sidewall.
  • A new thumping, wobbling or rhythmic vibration that follows road speed.
  • A tyre that repeatedly loses pressure, or a pressure warning after an impact.
  • A cut, exposed cords, cracking or a bent wheel near the damaged area.
  • A sudden change after striking a pothole, road edge or carpark kerb.

Vibration may also come from balance, suspension or brakes, but do not drive on a tyre with a visible bulge to diagnose it. After replacement, compare with our guides to suspension noises and brake rotor condition if a separate vibration remains.

Illustrative example, not a record of a specific customer repair: imagine a Honda Vezel leaving an HDB carpark in Hougang after its front tyre clips a kerb. The driver later notices a small sidewall bubble. Even if the tyre still looks inflated and the journey is only a short one, the safer choice is to leave the car parked and arrange roadside assistance or a tow—not to take the CTE to a workshop.

Why do sidewall bulges happen?

A photograph cannot confirm the cause. A workshop checks the likely clues and the rest of the wheel rather than assuming the tyre only needs air.

  1. Impact damage: Hitting a pothole edge, road debris or a kerb can pinch the tyre against the rim and damage its internal cords. This is a common mechanism to consider in Singapore’s stop-start, kerbside and carpark driving, but a particular tyre’s cause still has to be assessed.
  2. Driving with low pressure or excess load: An underinflated tyre flexes more and can build heat; carrying more weight than the tyre or vehicle is designed for adds stress. This can make an impact more damaging.
  3. Age, wear or previous damage: A tyre that has already been weakened by cuts, deterioration, an earlier impact or a poor repair may be more vulnerable. A date code or age by itself does not diagnose a bulge; inspect the whole tyre and follow the vehicle and tyre maker’s guidance.
  4. Less common internal or manufacturing defects: A separation can sometimes develop without an obvious recent impact. Keep the tyre details and purchase records and ask the supplier or manufacturer about inspection if a defect is suspected.

A sharp carpark ramp or kerb can damage a tyre at low speed too. Heat and load add stress but do not identify the cause without inspection.

What should you do when you find one in Singapore?

  1. Stop in a safe place. If you are moving, ease off the accelerator and avoid sudden steering or hard braking. Do not stop in a live lane if you can reach a safer place without taking an obvious risk. On an expressway, follow road-safety guidance, use hazard lights and move occupants away from traffic only when it is safe.
  2. Do not continue the trip on the bulged tyre. Do not try to reduce pressure, add sealant or cover the area with a patch. A spare wheel may be an option only if the vehicle has one and you can change it safely away from traffic; follow the owner’s manual, including any temporary-spare speed and distance limits. If unsure, use roadside assistance.
  3. Arrange recovery or a proper replacement. If the car is in an HDB multi-storey carpark or a safe parking space, leave it parked. If stranded, contact your insurer or roadside-assistance provider to arrange help. A visibly unsafe tyre should not be driven to a workshop.
  4. Keep useful details. Photograph the bulge and tread from a safe position, note the tyre size and brand, and record when the impact or warning happened. Do not remove the tyre or discard it before the supplier has had a chance to inspect it if a warranty or defect claim may apply.

What does a workshop check, and can a bulge be repaired?

A technician checks both sidewalls, tread, bead, valve and pressure, and looks for cracks, bends or impact marks on the wheel. If removed, the tyre’s inner liner can be examined. The replacement must match the vehicle’s required size, load index and speed rating, including any run-flat or EV specification.

A plug or patch cannot restore broken sidewall cords; puncture repairs are limited to eligible tread-area damage under tyre-maker guidance. The damaged tyre is generally replaced. A wheel repair and whether to change its axle-mate depend on inspection, tread depth, drivetrain and manufacturer advice—not a blanket pair rule.

After fitting, the wheel is balanced and checked for seating and inflation. If impact damage may affect steering, ask whether another check is needed. Wheel alignment addresses direction and wear, not a bulge. Inspections are booked online except genuine urgent breakdowns.

How can you reduce the risk?

  • Check cold pressure against the vehicle’s door-jamb or manual specification, not the tyre-sidewall maximum.
  • Slow for potholes and carpark ramps; avoid kerb impacts where possible.
  • Look over the visible sidewalls and tread periodically and after a hard impact. Pressure monitoring cannot detect every structural injury.
  • Do not overload the car; use the specified size, load index and speed rating.

Rotation or alignment may help specific wear patterns, but cannot prevent every impact injury. If a pull remains after replacement, assess it separately. Our absorber wear guide and brake-pad guide cover other road-feel concerns.

Common misconceptions about a sidewall bulge

“It still holds air, so I can drive slowly.”

Pressure retention does not show whether the tyre’s internal cords are intact. A slow trip still puts repeated load on the weak spot. Do not drive on a visible bulge; arrange a spare change only if it can be done safely, or request recovery.

“A patch, tyre sealant or balancing will fix it.”

These do not rebuild damaged sidewall structure. A balance can reduce certain vibration problems, but it cannot turn a structurally compromised tyre into a safe one. Have the tyre replaced or assessed by a qualified tyre professional.

“A new tyre means the same mileage for everyone.”

Tyre life varies with compound, size, road conditions, pressure, load, driving and maintenance. There is no dependable fixed mileage that makes every tyre safe until that number—or unsafe immediately afterwards. Inspect condition and follow the vehicle and tyre manufacturer’s advice.

When should you seek professional help?

Arrange help promptly for a bubble, exposed cords, deep cut, sudden pressure loss, bent wheel or new thumping/vibration. If moving, reach a safe stop; do not drive to a workshop. Book non-urgent inspections online in advance.

FAQs

Can I drive with a small tyre sidewall bulge?

No. Even a small sidewall bulge may indicate structural damage, and there is no reliable way to judge safety from size alone. Park safely and arrange a spare change if it can be done safely or use roadside assistance; do not drive to a workshop on that tyre.

Can a mechanic patch or plug a tyre sidewall bulge?

A sidewall bulge is not a normal puncture repair. A plug or patch cannot restore damaged casing cords, so the affected tyre generally needs replacement. A tyre professional can inspect the wheel and tyre and confirm the correct replacement specification.

Can a tyre bulge burst without losing pressure first?

Yes. A damaged tyre may continue holding air until the weak area fails. No warning light or normal pressure reading can rule out internal sidewall damage, so treat a visible bubble as unsafe.

What causes a bulge on the side of a tyre?

Impact against a pothole edge or kerb is one possible cause. Low pressure, excess load, earlier damage, ageing or an internal defect may also contribute. The exact cause cannot be confirmed from a photo alone; a workshop should examine the tyre and wheel.

How much does a replacement tyre cost in Singapore?

Tyre replacement is quoted after inspection, because the cost depends on size, specification, brand, availability and fitting charges, so request an itemised quote before approving work.

Should I replace both tyres on the same axle?

Not automatically. The right choice depends on remaining tread, wear, drivetrain and the vehicle manufacturer’s advice. A technician can measure the other tyre and explain whether replacing one or a pair is appropriate for that vehicle.
