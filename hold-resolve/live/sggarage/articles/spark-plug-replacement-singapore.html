Spark Plug Replacement: When & Why It Matters

Edwin Garage·Ang Mo Kio·6 August 2026·Updated 14 November 2026

How often should you change spark plugs in Singapore? Change copper spark plugs about every 30,000 km and iridium or platinum plugs every 80,000 to 100,000 km, or as your owner’s manual says. Singapore’s stop-and-go traffic and heat can wear them sooner, so change them earlier if the car misfires, starts hard or uses more fuel. Edwin Garage quotes the job after inspection.

When it comes to car maintenance, spark plugs might not be the flashiest component, but they’re absolutely essential for your engine’s performance. At Edwin Garage in Ang Mo Kio, we’ve worked on cars of all shapes and sizes, and we’ve seen firsthand what happens when spark plugs are neglected. Whether you’re driving a reliable Toyota Wish or a sporty Mazda 3, keeping your spark plugs in good condition is something no car owner in Singapore should overlook.

What Are Spark Plugs and How Do They Work?

Spark plugs are small but mighty components that ignite the air-fuel mixture in your engine’s combustion chamber. This tiny explosion is what makes your engine run. Without functioning spark plugs, your car simply won’t start, let alone perform efficiently. Each spark plug generates a spark at precisely the right moment to ensure smooth engine operation. A single misfire can lead to performance issues, and over time, worn spark plugs can significantly impact your fuel economy and driving experience.

Symptoms of Worn-Out Spark Plugs

How do you know it’s time to replace your spark plugs? Here are the common warning signs to look out for:

  • Difficulty starting the car: Your engine cranks but struggles to start, especially on humid Singapore mornings.
  • Poor fuel economy: You notice you’re refueling more often than usual.
  • Engine misfiring: Jerking or uneven acceleration, especially when idling or at low speeds.
  • Reduced engine performance: Sluggish acceleration or a lack of power.
  • Check engine light: Ignoring this warning can lead to bigger problems later.

If your car is jerking or misfiring, don’t ignore it. We’ve covered engine misfiring causes and repairs in detail in another article.

What Causes Spark Plug Problems?

In Singapore, our climate and driving conditions can accelerate wear and tear on your spark plugs. Here are the most common reasons:

  1. Age and mileage: Spark plugs wear out over time because they experience constant heat and pressure inside the engine. Most cars need a replacement every 30,000 to 100,000 km, depending on the type of spark plug. Iridium and platinum plugs last longer than copper ones, but all eventually degrade.
  2. Stop-and-go traffic: Singapore’s traffic conditions, especially during peak hours on roads like the CTE, PIE, or even around Ang Mo Kio, mean frequent idling and restarts. This constant load increases spark plug wear and can lead to faster carbon buildup, reducing efficiency.
  3. Heat and humidity: The tropical climate in Singapore contributes to higher engine temperatures and can make combustion less efficient. This results in more carbon deposits on spark plugs, which can cause them to misfire or fail prematurely.
  4. Engine issues: Problems like a rich air-fuel mixture, where there’s too much fuel compared to air, or oil leaks into the combustion chamber can coat spark plugs with deposits. If left unchecked, these issues can severely shorten the lifespan of your spark plugs and lead to further engine damage.
  5. Low-quality fuel: Using poor-quality or contaminated fuel can lead to incomplete combustion, leaving behind more deposits on your spark plugs. This is a particular concern if you frequently refuel at lesser-known petrol stations.

These factors combine to make spark plug maintenance especially critical for Singaporean drivers. If you’re unsure about the condition of your spark plugs, our experienced team at Edwin Garage can help you assess and address the issue early.

How We Replace Spark Plugs at Edwin Garage

Replacing spark plugs may seem straightforward, but doing it right requires expertise to ensure no related issues are overlooked. Here’s how we handle it:

  1. Initial inspection: We start by checking your car’s overall engine health. This includes listening for misfires, checking for unusual noises, and scanning for any error codes if the check engine light is on.
  2. Removing old spark plugs: Using precision tools, we carefully remove each spark plug. During removal, we inspect each plug for signs of wear, such as carbon deposits, oil fouling, or damage. These observations can reveal underlying engine problems, like a leaky valve or poor combustion.
  3. Selecting the right replacement: We ensure the new spark plugs match the manufacturer’s specifications for your car. Iridium, platinum, or copper? Each has unique properties, and choosing incorrectly can affect your engine’s performance and lifespan. We source quality parts to ensure optimal results.
  4. Installing new plugs: We clean the spark plug threads and apply anti-seize compound (when needed) to prevent thread damage. Each spark plug is torqued to the manufacturer’s specifications to avoid over-tightening or under-tightening, both of which can lead to issues like poor contact or thread damage.
  5. Final checks: Once the new spark plugs are in place, we reconnect the ignition coils and test your engine. We check for smooth idling, proper acceleration, and ensure there are no remaining diagnostic codes. This step confirms that your car is ready to hit the road with improved performance.

Preventing Spark Plug Issues

Here are some actionable tips to extend the lifespan of your spark plugs:

  • Stick to your car’s maintenance schedule. Replace spark plugs as recommended in your owner’s manual. If you’re unsure, consult our team for advice tailored to Singapore’s conditions.
  • Reduce idling time. Long periods of idling, common in HDB carparks or during traffic jams, can cause unnecessary fouling. Turn off your engine if you expect to wait for more than a few minutes.
  • Use high-quality fuel to minimize deposits. Stick to trusted petrol stations like Shell, Caltex, or SPC for cleaner combustion.
  • Keep your engine healthy by addressing oil leaks or air-fuel ratio issues promptly. Check out our guide on Toyota Altis oil leaks for more information.
  • Drive smoothly. Avoid harsh acceleration and braking, as these can strain your engine and spark plugs unnecessarily.

By following these steps, you’ll not only extend the life of your spark plugs but also improve your car’s overall performance and efficiency, which is especially important given Singapore’s high fuel costs.

How Much Does Spark Plug Replacement Cost in Singapore?

The cost of spark plug replacement depends on several factors:

  • Type of spark plug: Iridium plugs, which last the longest, are more expensive than copper or platinum plugs.
  • Number of cylinders: A 4-cylinder engine will obviously cost less than a 6- or 8-cylinder engine requiring more spark plugs.
  • Labour costs: Some spark plugs are harder to access, especially on certain German cars like BMWs or Mercedes.

FAQs About Spark Plug Replacement

How often should I replace my spark plugs?

It depends on the type of spark plugs and your car. Iridium and platinum plugs last 80,000–100,000 km, while copper plugs may only last 30,000 km. Check your owner’s manual for specifics. Frequent stop-and-go driving, common in Singapore, might require more frequent replacements. If in doubt, our team can inspect your current spark plugs and advise on the best timeline for your car.

Can I drive with bad spark plugs?

Technically, yes, but it’s not a good idea. Worn spark plugs reduce fuel efficiency, harm engine performance, and can lead to costly damage if ignored for too long. Driving with bad spark plugs can cause misfires, which strain your engine and may even damage the catalytic converter. Over time, this neglect can result in a much higher repair bill compared to simply replacing the spark plugs early.

What happens if I don’t replace my spark plugs?

Neglecting spark plug replacement can lead to engine misfires, poor fuel economy, and even damage to your ignition coils or catalytic converter over time. Misfires place extra stress on other engine components, potentially causing overheating or long-term damage. It’s always more cost-effective to replace spark plugs on time rather than dealing with expensive repairs later.

Is spark plug replacement a DIY job?

It can be for experienced car enthusiasts, but if you’re unsure, leave it to the professionals. Incorrect installation can strip threads or damage your engine. Modern cars may also require special tools or diagnostic equipment to ensure proper replacement and calibration. At Edwin Garage, we make sure every spark plug is installed correctly for optimal performance.

How do I know if my spark plugs are the problem?

Common symptoms include misfiring, sluggish acceleration, and poor fuel economy. A diagnostic check at our Ang Mo Kio workshop can confirm the issue. If your check engine light is on or you notice your car struggling to start, it’s worth having your spark plugs inspected. Catching the issue early can save you money and prevent further complications.

Should all the spark plugs be changed together?

Yes, normally. All the plugs in an engine work in the same conditions and wear at a similar rate, so they are changed as a set. Replacing only one can leave the others close to the end of their life, and the misfire returns. Edwin Garage confirms the set and the plug type for your car after inspection.

Can worn spark plugs make the car shake or vibrate at idle?

Yes. A worn plug can misfire, and a misfiring cylinder makes the engine run rough and shake at idle or at traffic lights. Engine mounts can cause similar shaking, so a check is needed before parts are replaced. See car vibrates at idle and car stalls at traffic lights.

Can worn spark plugs cause a knocking or pinging noise?

They can contribute. Worn or wrong plugs can let fuel burn unevenly, which is heard as light pinging when you accelerate. A deep knock that follows engine speed is a different, more serious problem: stop driving and read our guide on engine knocking noise.

Common Misconceptions About Spark Plugs

“Spark plugs last forever.” Many drivers think they can ignore spark plugs entirely. While modern spark plugs are durable, they still have a lifespan and need replacement to maintain performance.

“All spark plugs are the same.” Not true. Using the wrong spark plugs can damage your engine. Stick to what your car manufacturer recommends.
