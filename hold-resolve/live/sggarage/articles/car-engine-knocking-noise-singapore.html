Why Is My Car Engine Knocking, and Is It Safe to Keep Driving?

Edwin·Master Automotive Technician·November 14, 2026·Edwin Garage, Ang Mo Kio

An engine knocking or tapping noise can be as minor as pinging under load, or as serious as worn internal parts. Common causes are low or old oil, fuel burning unevenly, carbon build-up, weak ignition, a loose belt component, or worn bearings. If the knock is deep and loud, or comes with the oil warning light, stop safely, switch off and arrange recovery.

We are Edwin Garage, a car workshop in Ang Mo Kio. This guide helps you describe the sound, decide whether to keep driving, and know what the workshop should check. It is a guide, not a diagnosis: the same sound can have different causes.

Is it safe to keep driving with an engine knock?

Stop and switch off if any of these happen:

  • A deep, heavy knock that gets faster as the revs rise.
  • The oil pressure warning light is on.
  • Burning smell, smoke, or the temperature gauge climbing.
  • Sudden loss of power.

Arrange recovery rather than driving on. If there is smoke or fire, get everyone out and call SCDF on 995. For a light tapping that fades once the engine is warm, you can usually drive to a workshop, but do not leave it for weeks. See also the engine oil pressure warning light.

What does the sound tell you?

Use the table to match what you hear. It narrows the possibilities, it does not confirm them.

Check the oil first

With the car on level ground and the engine off for a few minutes, check the dipstick. A low level is a common and fixable cause. Skipping oil changes also adds wear over time; see what happens if you skip an engine oil change.

Cold start or warm engine?

Note when the noise happens. A sound only on a cold start points in a different direction from one that appears when the engine is hot or under load. This detail helps the workshop a lot.

What should the workshop check?

  • Engine oil level, condition and pressure.
  • A scan for stored fault codes, including misfire.
  • Spark plugs and ignition parts.
  • Belts, tensioner and accessories that can tap or rattle.
  • Listening at the engine to locate where the sound comes from.

Frequently asked questions

What causes an engine knocking noise?

A knocking or tapping sound can come from low or wrong engine oil, fuel burning unevenly (often heard as pinging when you accelerate), carbon build-up, a weak ignition spark, a worn belt tensioner or accessory, or worn internal parts such as bearings. The sound alone is not enough to tell which, so it needs a proper check.

Is it safe to drive with an engine knocking?

Not if the knock is deep, loud or comes with the oil warning light, a burning smell or loss of power. Stop safely, switch off and arrange recovery. A light tapping that fades once the engine is warm is less urgent, but still book a check soon.

What is the difference between knocking and pinging?

Pinging is a light, metallic rattle heard mainly when you accelerate or climb a slope, and is linked to how the fuel burns. A deep knock that follows engine speed, and gets worse as revs rise, is more serious and can point to worn internal parts.

Can low engine oil cause a knocking noise?

Yes. Oil keeps moving parts apart. When the level is low or the oil is old, parts can tap or knock. Check the dipstick on level ground with the engine off, and do not drive far if the oil light is on. See our guide to the engine oil pressure warning light.

Will a spark plug change fix an engine knock?

Sometimes, if worn plugs cause misfiring or pinging. It will not fix a knock from worn internal parts. A diagnosis should come before replacing anything.

What should I tell the workshop about the noise?

Say when it happens (cold start, warm engine, accelerating, idle), whether it follows engine speed, whether any warning light is on, and when the oil was last changed. Ask for the cause and a quote before you approve any work.

Key Takeaways

  • A deep knock, the oil light or a burning smell means stop and arrange recovery.
  • Check the oil level first; low or old oil is a common cause.
  • Note when the noise happens: cold start, warm, accelerating or idle.
  • Get a diagnosis before replacing parts.
