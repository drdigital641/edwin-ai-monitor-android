What's Actually Going Wrong Inside Your Side Mirror

A power-folding side mirror is a small but fairly complex assembly. Inside the housing sits a folding motor and a set of nylon or metal folding gears that swing the mirror in and out when you lock or unlock the car, plus a separate motor for glass adjustment, wiring for the indicator, and on newer cars, blind-spot sensors, heating elements or even a camera. When people say "the mirror is spoiled," what has usually failed is just one small part of that system — the folding gear train — while everything else is fine.

Common Symptoms of a Faulty Folding Mechanism

These are the symptoms we see most often when a customer brings a car in for side mirror repair:

  • Side mirror does not fold when the car is locked
  • Mirror folds only halfway, or stops midway with a struggling sound
  • Mirror gets stuck in the open or closed position
  • Repeated clicking or knocking ("tak-tak-tak") sound from inside the mirror
  • Motor keeps running or whining even though the mirror has already folded
  • One side folds normally while the other side does not move at all
  • Mirror needs a manual push before it will move

Causes, From Most to Least Common

In our experience, Singapore's driving conditions play a big part in why these mirrors wear out. Ranked from what we see most to least often:

  1. Worn or slipping folding gears — years of folding and unfolding at every lock/unlock cycle wears down the nylon gear teeth until they start to slip, which is the source of that clicking sound.
  2. Heat and humidity fatigue — cars parked in open-air HDB carparks and under direct sun in Singapore's heat cause the plastic gear housing and lubricant inside to degrade faster than in cooler climates.
  3. Motor wear from constant stop-go use — commuters who fold and unfold mirrors multiple times a day (tight carpark lots, narrow lanes near Hougang and Serangoon) put more cycles through the mechanism.
  4. Physical impact — a mirror knocked while reversing or brushing against a pillar can shift the internal gear alignment even if the glass and housing look undamaged.
  5. Water ingress — corrosion on the small electrical contacts inside older mirrors, less common but does happen on cars that are washed with high-pressure jets aimed directly at the mirror.

What We Actually Do When You Bring the Car In

Rather than ordering a brand-new mirror assembly on the spot, we dismantle the housing and inspect the mechanism directly. If the folding gear, motor or internal linkage is the actual fault — which is the case more often than not — we replace only that component and rebuild the mirror using the original housing, glass, wiring and indicator. The result is a mirror that folds properly again, at a fraction of the cost of a full assembly, with nothing thrown away unnecessarily.

Prevention: How to Make Your Folding Mechanism Last Longer

A few habits can meaningfully extend the life of your folding mirror mechanism:

  • Avoid manually forcing the mirror to fold or unfold — let the motor do the work, even if it feels slow
  • Park under shade where possible to reduce heat fatigue on the plastic gear housing
  • Avoid pointing high-pressure car wash jets directly into the mirror housing
  • If you hear the first clicking sound, get it checked promptly rather than continuing to fold/unfold — the slipping gear wears further with every cycle
  • Have your mirror mechanism checked during your regular servicing, especially past the 5–6 year mark

Side Mirror Repair Cost Factors in Singapore

Repair cost depends on a few things: which brand and model the mirror is from, whether the mechanism, motor or wiring is the actual fault, and whether the specific internal parts are commonly stocked or need to be sourced. In general, replacing just the internal folding mechanism costs meaningfully less than a full mirror assembly replacement — sometimes just a fraction of the price — because you're keeping the glass, housing, indicator and wiring. We don't quote a fixed number without inspecting the mirror first, because the actual fault (and therefore the actual cost) can only be confirmed once we've opened it up.

Common Misconceptions About Side Mirror Repair

Misconception 1: "If it's clicking, the whole mirror motor is already dead." Not necessarily. A clicking or knocking sound usually means the motor is still running but the folding gear is slipping — the motor itself is often fine. That's exactly the kind of fault that's repairable without full replacement.

Misconception 2: "Once a side mirror stops folding, you must replace the entire assembly." This is the most common myth we hear, usually repeated by workshops that don't do internal mechanism repair. In many cases, the glass, housing, wiring and indicator are all still fully functional — only the folding gear or motor inside needs attention.

When You Should Bring the Car In Immediately

Don't wait until the mirror stops working completely. Bring it in if you notice any of the following:

  • A repeated "click-click-click" or "tak-tak-tak" sound when locking or unlocking the car
  • The mirror folding only partway before stopping
  • One mirror working while the other has gone completely silent
  • The motor still audibly running but the mirror isn't actually moving

Continuing to operate a mirror with a slipping gear usually causes further wear, which can eventually turn a simple gear replacement into a more involved repair.

Side Mirror Repair FAQs

My car side mirror makes a clicking sound but still folds — do I need to fix it now?

Yes, it's best to get it checked soon. A clicking or "tak-tak-tak" sound usually means the folding gear inside is starting to slip. The mirror may still fold for now, but continuing to use it will wear the gear further, and what's currently a small, affordable repair can become a bigger one if left too long.

Can you repair my side mirror even if it's a Continental car brand?

We mainly specialise in Japanese and Korean vehicles — Toyota, Honda, Mazda, Subaru, Nissan, Mitsubishi, Hyundai and Kia. For most Continental cars, we may not be able to perform this type of internal folding mechanism repair, so it's best to send us your car details first before assuming either way.

Is it cheaper to repair the folding mechanism than replace the whole mirror?

In most cases, yes. A full side mirror assembly includes the glass, housing, indicator, wiring and sometimes camera or blind-spot components. If only the folding gear or motor has failed, replacing just that component — while keeping everything else — is usually a fraction of the cost of a full mirror assembly.

Why does only one side mirror stop folding while the other one works fine?

This is very common and simply means the fault is isolated to that one mirror's internal mechanism — usually a worn folding gear or a failing motor. It doesn't mean anything is wrong with your car's central locking system if only one side is affected; it's almost always a localised mechanical issue inside that specific mirror.

How long does a side mirror mechanism repair take?

Many cases can be assessed and repaired within the same visit once the mirror is dismantled and the fault confirmed, though this depends on whether the specific internal part needs to be sourced. Sending us your car make, model, year and a short video of the problem beforehand helps us prepare before you arrive.

What information should I send before bringing my car in?

Please send us: (1) car make and model, (2) year of the vehicle, (3) which side is faulty — left or right, and (4) a short video showing the problem. This lets us give you an early indication of whether your side mirror is likely to be repairable before you make the trip down.
