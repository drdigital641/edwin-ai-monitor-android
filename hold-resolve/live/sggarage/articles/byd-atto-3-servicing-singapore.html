Edwin Garage·Ang Mo Kio·29 August 2026

The BYD Atto 3 has quickly become one of the most popular electric vehicles in Singapore — and for good reason. Competitive pricing, a healthy range, and a feature-rich cabin have made it a hit with local drivers switching away from petrol cars. But owning an EV doesn't mean zero maintenance. As your trusted car workshop in Singapore specialising in EV and hybrid servicing, we want to give BYD Atto 3 owners a clear, honest picture of what your car still needs — and when to bring it in.

We've been seeing more BYD Atto 3 vehicles coming through our workshop in Ang Mo Kio, and the most common question we hear is: "It's electric — what do I even need to service?" The answer might surprise you.

What the BYD Atto 3 Still Needs (That Isn't Battery-Related)

Electric vehicles eliminate a lot of traditional maintenance — no engine oil, no timing belt, no spark plugs, no transmission fluid (in most cases). But several key systems still wear out and need regular attention, especially in Singapore's climate.

Here's what we check on a BYD Atto 3 visit:

  • Cabin air filter — Singapore's air quality and haze seasons are real. The BYD Atto 3 uses a multi-layer cabin filter that captures PM2.5 particles. We recommend replacing it every 12–15 months, or sooner if you notice weaker aircon flow or unusual smells inside the car.
  • Brake pads and rotors — EVs use regenerative braking, which reduces wear significantly compared to petrol cars. But the brakes still work in emergency stops and at low speeds. In Singapore's stop-go traffic on the CTE and PIE, brake pads still need inspection every 12 months.
  • Tyres — This is the big one. EVs are heavier than their petrol equivalents, and the instant torque delivery of electric motors accelerates tyre wear. BYD Atto 3 owners should check tyre depth and condition every 6 months, and rotate tyres every 10,000–15,000 km.
  • Wiper blades — Simple but important, especially during monsoon season. We check both front and rear wipers.
  • Brake fluid — Even though regenerative braking reduces brake use, the hydraulic brake fluid still absorbs moisture over time. We recommend changing it every 2 years regardless of mileage.
  • Coolant for the battery thermal management system — The BYD Atto 3 uses a liquid-cooled battery pack. The coolant in this system degrades over time and needs to be topped up or replaced per the manufacturer's schedule — typically every 5 years or 100,000 km.
  • 12V auxiliary battery — Like most modern EVs, the Atto 3 has a conventional 12V battery for systems like lighting, locks, and entertainment. This battery has a normal lifespan of 3–5 years and can go flat — just like any petrol car's battery. We test it during every service visit.
  • Suspension and steering components — Bushings, ball joints, and tie rod ends wear out on EVs just as they do on petrol cars. In Singapore, HDB carpark kerbs and road imperfections contribute to suspension wear over time.

How Often Should You Service a BYD Atto 3 in Singapore?

BYD recommends annual servicing or every 10,000–20,000 km, whichever comes first. Given Singapore's urban driving patterns — shorter trips, lots of low-speed stop-go traffic, and the cumulative effect of humidity — we'd recommend aligning with the annual schedule rather than waiting for the mileage trigger.

We recently had a BYD Atto 3 owner come in from Hougang who had been putting off the annual check for 18 months because "it's electric, so nothing can be wrong." When we inspected the vehicle, we found the cabin filter was badly clogged (significantly reducing aircon efficiency), the rear brake pads were worn beyond safe limits due to an initial pad bedding-in issue, and one tyre was worn unevenly due to a wheel alignment drift. None of these are battery problems — they're standard vehicle maintenance that applies to any car, EV or not.

Common Misconceptions About EV Maintenance

"EVs don't need servicing." This is the most persistent myth we encounter. While EVs skip oil changes and spark plugs, the mechanical components — brakes, tyres, suspension, steering — are essentially identical to petrol cars. They wear out the same way.

"Regenerative braking means brakes last forever." Regenerative braking does reduce wear significantly, but it doesn't eliminate it. In heavy rain (which Singapore gets plenty of), regenerative braking is less effective and hydraulic brakes carry more load. We've seen Atto 3 brake pads wear down to the warning layer within 3 years in cases where the car was driven primarily in manual B-mode without regen.

"The traction battery is the only thing that matters." The 12V auxiliary battery is easy to forget — until it goes flat and the car won't power on at all, even with a fully charged traction battery. This happens and it's an inconvenient surprise. We test the 12V battery at every service visit.

Frequently Asked Questions: BYD Atto 3 Servicing Singapore

Does the BYD Atto 3 battery need servicing?

The high-voltage traction battery generally doesn't require routine servicing by a workshop. The battery thermal management coolant needs replacing per BYD's schedule (approximately every 5 years / 100,000 km), which an authorised centre handles. What your independent workshop can help with is checking the 12V auxiliary battery, which is separate and powers all the car's low-voltage electronics — locks, lights, displays, and so on. This typically lasts 3–5 years.

How often should I service my BYD Atto 3 in Singapore?

BYD's recommendation is every 12 months or every 10,000–20,000 km. In Singapore's urban driving conditions — short trips, lots of stopping and starting, humidity — we suggest sticking to the annual schedule rather than stretching it to the mileage limit. An annual check keeps all the mechanical wear items under observation and catches small issues before they become expensive ones.

Is the BYD Atto 3 warranty voided if I use an independent workshop?

Singapore's warranty terms for BYD generally require warranty-related repairs to go through the authorised network. However, routine maintenance (cabin filter, brake fluid, brakes, tyres, wipers) performed by a competent independent workshop does not void the warranty, provided the work meets OEM specifications and is documented. Keep your service records. If you're unsure, check your specific warranty terms with your BYD dealer.

Why are my BYD Atto 3 tyres wearing out faster than expected?

Electric vehicles are heavier than petrol equivalents (the Atto 3 weighs around 1,750 kg), and the instant torque delivery means the front tyres in particular take significant stress during acceleration. In Singapore's short-trip, stop-go urban environment, this effect is amplified. Proper tyre rotation every 10,000–15,000 km and correct tyre pressure maintenance are the most effective ways to extend tyre life.

What should I do if my BYD Atto 3 shows a warning light on the dashboard?

Battery-related warning lights (high-voltage system alerts, charging fault warnings) should be addressed by the authorised BYD service centre promptly. For other warning lights — ABS, brake system, tyre pressure, service reminder — an independent workshop can diagnose and resolve these. When to Seek Professional Help for Your BYD Atto 3

Bring your Atto 3 to a workshop immediately if you notice:

  • Any high-voltage battery or charging system warning light
  • Unusual noise when braking — squealing, grinding, or pulsating pedal
  • Pulling to one side when driving or braking
  • Tyre pressure warning light that re-triggers within a day of inflation (possible slow puncture)
  • Aircon not cooling effectively — the cabin filter may be severely blocked, or there may be an aircon system fault
  • 12V auxiliary battery warning or car failing to wake from sleep mode
  • Steering feeling heavier or less responsive than normal
  • Suspension clunking or knocking over bumps — especially relevant if you frequently park in HDB multi-storey carparks with speed bumps
