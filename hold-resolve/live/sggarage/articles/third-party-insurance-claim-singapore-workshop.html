What Is a Third-Party Car Insurance Claim in Singapore?

A third-party insurance claim means the repair costs are paid by the at-fault driver's insurer — not yours. This happens when another driver is responsible for the accident. In Singapore, all registered vehicles must carry third-party liability coverage under the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act. So theoretically, if someone drives into your car, their insurer should foot the bill for your repairs.

In practice, it's rarely that simple. Insurers have their own assessors, preferred workshops, and claim procedures. Without a workshop that understands the process, your claim can stall, get undervalued, or result in substandard repairs.

Step-by-Step: How the Workshop Handles Your Third-Party Claim

When you bring your car to Edwin Garage after an accident, here's what happens:

1. Accident documentation and scene photos

Before anything touches your car, we help you gather the evidence the insurer needs: photos of the damage, the accident scene if available, the other party's particulars, and the police report reference number. If you're coming straight from the accident, we'll guide you through exactly what you need.

2. Vehicle assessment and damage report

We carry out a thorough inspection — not just the visible panel damage, but structural integrity, mechanical components that may have been affected, and any hidden damage that only becomes apparent when panels are removed. This detailed report becomes the basis for your claim.

3. Claim submission and insurer liaison

We submit the repair report and supporting documents to the at-fault driver's insurer on your behalf. When the insurer sends their own assessor to inspect the car, we're there to walk them through our findings and make sure nothing is missed or undervalued.

4. Repair approval and commencement

Once the claim is approved, repairs begin. For panel beating, spray painting, or structural work, we have experienced technicians who follow proper repair procedures — no shortcuts, no skipping structural checks. For complex damage, we use the manufacturer's repair specifications.

5. Quality check and delivery

Before returning your car, we do a thorough quality check: paint match under different lighting, panel gaps, mechanical operation of doors and boot, and a road test if any mechanical work was involved. You collect a car that looks and drives as it should.

What Expenses Can Be Claimed Under a Third-Party Claim?

Many Singapore drivers don't realise how broad their entitlement is under a third-party claim. It's not just the repair cost. Depending on the circumstances, you may also be able to claim:

  • Cost of towing from the accident scene
  • Loss of use — a daily allowance while your car is in the workshop
  • Diminution in value (if the accident materially reduces your car's resale value)
  • Any other consequential losses related to the accident

A good workshop will flag these entitlements to you upfront and include them in the claim where applicable. Common Misconceptions About Third-Party Claims

Misconception 1: "I have to use the at-fault insurer's preferred workshop."

You do not. Under Singapore law, you have the right to choose your own repairer. The insurer may try to direct you to their authorised workshop for convenience, but this is not mandatory. Choose a workshop you trust — not one the insurer has a commercial arrangement with.

Misconception 2: "If it's the other party's fault, my NCD won't be affected."

If the claim is settled through the at-fault driver's insurer (and not your own policy), your No Claims Discount is generally preserved. However, you should always check with your own insurer before proceeding, especially if fault is disputed.

Misconception 3: "The assessor's first approval is final."

It's not. Insurer assessors sometimes under-approve repairs on first assessment. A detailed damage report from your workshop — with photos, measurements and written justification — can get the approval revised. This is part of what a proper workshop does for you.

Misconception 4: "Third-party claims take months."

Straightforward claims with clear liability can be resolved in one to three weeks. Complex structural repairs take longer. What slows claims down is incomplete documentation or poor communication between the workshop and the insurer — both of which we manage proactively.

What Should You Do Immediately After an Accident?

  • Stay calm and ensure everyone is safe — move vehicles to the road shoulder if possible
  • Exchange particulars with the other driver: name, NRIC/FIN, contact number, vehicle plate, insurer name and policy number
  • Take photos immediately: all vehicle damage, the accident scene, road positions, and any skid marks
  • Report to the police if there are injuries, road obstruction, or if the other driver refuses to cooperate
  • Call for towing if the car is not driveable — we can assist with accident towing in Singapore
  • Avoid admitting liability at the scene — let the insurers determine fault
  • Contact your insurer within 24 hours even if you intend to make a third-party claim

How Long Does Third-Party Claim Repair Take?

Timeline depends on the extent of damage and the speed of insurer approval:

  • Minor damage (bumper, panel dents): 3–7 working days once approved
  • Moderate damage (multiple panels, doors, structural): 1–2 weeks
  • Severe structural damage: 2–4 weeks or more

Cost Factors in Third-Party Repairs

If liability is clear and the third-party claim is accepted, you should not pay for the core repair costs. However, you may face out-of-pocket expenses if:

  • Liability is disputed and not fully resolved
  • The insurer approves a lower-quality repair method than what's required
  • There are damage items outside the claim scope (pre-existing conditions)
  • You choose repair upgrades beyond what the claim covers

FAQ: Third-Party Car Insurance Claims in Singapore

Can I choose my own workshop for a third-party claim?

Yes. You are entitled to choose your own repairer in Singapore. The at-fault driver's insurer cannot compel you to use their preferred workshop. However, your chosen workshop must be able to submit a proper repair report and liaise with the insurer's assessor professionally.

Do I need a police report for a third-party claim?

A police report is recommended if there are injuries or if the accident is complex. For straightforward damage-only cases, Singapore's e-DAI (electronic Damage Assessment Interface) system is used. Your workshop will advise on whether a police report is needed for your specific case.

What if the at-fault driver's insurer disputes liability?

Disputed liability is common when both parties give different accounts. In this situation, your own insurer handles the claim negotiation between parties. You may need to make a claim under your own policy first, with a later recovery process if fault is established. Your workshop can help document the damage thoroughly to support the case.

Will my insurance premiums go up if I make a third-party claim?

If the claim is made against the at-fault driver's insurer and your own policy is not involved, your NCD should be unaffected. Always confirm this with your own insurer, especially if you've been asked to report to them first.

Can I claim loss of use while my car is in the workshop?

Yes. A loss of use allowance (a daily rate for alternative transport) can typically be claimed against the at-fault party's insurer for the period your car is in for repairs. The rate varies and must be supported by documentation. We include this in our claim report where applicable.

When to Seek Professional Help

Don't delay getting your accident damage assessed and the claim process started if any of these apply:

  • The car is structurally damaged (frame, sill, firewall)
  • Airbags deployed — even partial deployment requires professional inspection
  • Any warning lights came on after the accident
  • Steering or suspension feel unusual after the collision
  • The other driver is uncooperative or their insurer is unresponsive
  • You suspect hidden damage that wasn't visible at the accident scene
