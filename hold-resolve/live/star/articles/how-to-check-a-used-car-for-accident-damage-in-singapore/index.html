How to check a used car for accident damage in Singapore

To check a used car for accident damage, walk around it in daylight and look for uneven panel gaps, paint that does not match, overspray on rubbers and trim, and uneven tyre wear. Check the boot floor and under the bonnet for reworked areas, ask the seller directly, then have a workshop inspect it before you pay.

No single sign proves a car was in an accident, and a repaired car is not always a bad car. The aim is to spot what needs a question. This is general advice, not a guarantee about any car.

What should you look at on the outside?

  1. Panel gaps. The gaps between the bonnet, doors, boot and bumpers should look even from side to side.
  2. Paint. View each panel from an angle in daylight. A different shade, texture or a slightly orange-peel finish can mean a repaint.
  3. Overspray. Look at rubber seals, trim and light edges for a fine paint mist.
  4. Lights and glass. Check that the headlights match, and that the glass markings look consistent.

What should you check inside, in the boot and under the bonnet?

Lift the boot floor carpet and look at the metal for ripples, fresh sealant or mismatched paint. Open the bonnet and look along the edges and bolts for signs they have been taken off. Inside, check that the seats, seat belts and dashboard look original and that no warning light stays on after the engine starts. For the rest of the walk-around, see what to check on a used car test drive.

What do the tyres tell you?

Tyres that wear unevenly, more on one edge or on one side only, can point to an alignment or suspension problem. That can come from an old knock, or simply from a worn part, so treat it as a question to ask, not a verdict.

What should you ask the seller?

  • Has the car been in an accident, or had any panel repainted?
  • Can you see the service records and any repair receipts?
  • Do the records fit the mileage? See how to check mileage and service history.

A straight answer, and paperwork that matches what you see on the car, is a good sign.

Is a repainted panel a deal-breaker?

Not by itself. A scrape or small dent is often repaired and repainted. What matters is whether the repair is sound and whether the seller was open about it. A concern about structure or safety is different, and that is where an inspection helps.

Why get a pre-purchase inspection?

A mechanic with the car on a lift can see the underside and structure, which you cannot judge on a test drive. Read what a pre-purchase inspection is. At Star Performance Motor you can have the car inspected at any workshop, and our team often goes along. See buying a used car from us.

Frequently asked questions

How can you tell if a used car has been in an accident?

Look for uneven gaps between panels, paint that differs in shade or texture, overspray on rubbers and trim, uneven tyre wear, and a boot floor or bonnet edge that looks reworked. None of these proves damage alone, so confirm with an inspection.

Does a repainted panel mean the car was in an accident?

Not always. A panel can be repainted after a scrape or minor dent. What matters is whether the repair is sound and whether the seller tells you about it, so ask directly.

Should I ask the seller about accident history?

Yes. Ask whether the car has been in an accident or had any panel work, and ask for the service records and repair receipts. A straight answer and paperwork that matches are a good sign.

Can a pre-purchase inspection find accident damage?

A mechanic with the car on a lift can look at things you cannot see on a test drive, such as the underside and structure. You can have the car inspected at any workshop, and our team often goes along.
